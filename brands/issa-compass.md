# Issa Compass

**Issa Compass** is an unregistered trade name used to advertise commercial visa and immigration-assistance services, particularly Thailand's Destination Thailand Visa (DTV).

The brand operates through two separately registered companies: [Issara Platforms Pte. Ltd.](../companies/issara-platforms-pte-ltd.md) in Singapore and [Issa Systems Co., Ltd.](../companies/issa-systems-co-ltd.md) in Thailand.[^1]

## Corporate Identity and Registration Claims

Issa Compass's Privacy Policy explicitly states **"We trade as Issa Compass."** The two companies have different registration numbers and legal responsibilities.[^2]

Nevertheless, the website's guide to identifying fraudulent visa agencies presents Issa Compass as a registered software company operating in Singapore and Thailand.[^3]

**The registered entities are Issara Platforms and Issa Systems, not a company named Issa Compass.** Their corporate registrations should not be confused with registration of the advertised trade name.

## Customer Information and Responsibility

The company's Privacy Policy identifies Issara Platforms as the controller of customers' digital visa files, while Issa Systems handles physical documents and in-person services in Thailand.[^2]

Passport copies, financial records, and other sensitive immigration documents are therefore processed through a cross-border arrangement involving two distinct legal entities.

The policy states that customer information is stored primarily in Singapore and accessed from both countries.[^2]

## November 2026 Money-Back Guarantee Restrictions

Issa Compass prominently advertises its money-back guarantee as evidence of its reliability. Its security guide describes a **"Full refund guarantee"**, while promotional articles emphasize protection against unsuccessful visa applications.[^3][^4]

However, its Terms of Service, updated November 13, 2026, impose substantial restrictions.

Under Section 7.3.7, applicable to plans first paid for on or after **November 14, 2026**, the company's legal team determines whether a customer's application qualifies for the guarantee. Customers may pay before receiving that determination.[^5]

If the company subsequently denies coverage, customers can proceed without the guarantee, switch visa categories, or abandon the application. Those who abandon it receive a **12-month credit toward another Issa Compass service instead of a cash refund**. The initial assessment fee remains non-refundable.

More remarkably, the company expressly reserves the right to **withhold its reasons for denying guarantee eligibility** when an explanation would reveal internal methods or proprietary knowledge.[^5]

The advertised guarantee is therefore not automatically secured when a customer purchases an eligible plan. The company can accept payment, subsequently determine that the application does not qualify, decline to fully explain that decision, and retain the customer's service payment as credit.

For qualifying purchases from August 29, 2026 onward, the ordinary guarantee also covers only the company's service fee, excluding government application fees and other third-party charges.[^5]

### Issa Compass Warns Against the Same Weak Refund Guarantees It Sells

Issa Compass's October 17, 2026 article *Document Pre-Checks, Money-Back Guarantees, and Expert Support* advises consumers how to identify misleading visa-service guarantees.[^6]

Its comparison table explicitly classifies:

- **Weak guarantee:** Service credit only.
- **Strong guarantee:** Refund of the service fee.

The article also warns that excessive conditions can make recovering money nearly impossible.

**Yet Issa Compass's own Terms of Service contain precisely the weak refund arrangement it warns consumers against.**

Under Section 7.3.7, applicable to purchases beginning November 14, 2026, customers can pay for plans advertised with money-back protection before the company confirms whether their applications qualify.[^5]

If Issa Compass's legal team subsequently denies guarantee eligibility, customers who abandon their applications do not receive an ordinary cash refund.

Instead:

- Issa Compass retains their service fees as credit toward another visa service.
- The credit expires 12 months after the original payment.
- The Initial Assessment Fee remains non-refundable.
- The company can withhold detailed reasons for rejecting guarantee eligibility when it considers those reasons proprietary.

**Issa Compass accepts payment while advertising refund protection, reserves the right to deny that protection afterward, and substitutes the exact service-credit remedy its own consumer guide labels a weak guarantee.**

The company's standard guarantee promises service-fee refunds for qualifying government refusals. But the contract allows Issa Compass to decide that a paying customer never qualified for that protection in the first place.

That distinction is central to the problem: **the company controls whether the advertised cash-refund guarantee applies after the customer has already paid.**

Its October consumer guide tells readers to scrutinize conditional guarantees and reject service-credit-only protection. Its November contract then authorizes both practices.

**Issa Compass's published consumer advice condemns refund arrangements that its own contractual terms expressly permit.**

Customers deserve to know before payment whether they are purchasing genuine cash-refund protection or merely the possibility of receiving it, subject to a later internal decision.

The contradiction directly undermines Issa Compass's marketing of its money-back guarantee as a reason to trust its paid visa services.

## Document Verification Claims Versus Contractual Disclaimers

Issa Compass advertises an AI-powered verification system that checks application documents against published and unpublished embassy requirements. Its promotional material describes comprehensive document verification as the foundation of its money-back guarantee.[^4]

However, Section 6.6(c) of its Terms of Service states:

> We do not independently verify, authenticate or investigate the information or documents the User provides, and we are under no obligation to do so.

The company also disclaims responsibility for inaccurate or falsified customer documents. Section 8.4 separately warns that its AI-generated document checks may contain errors and places responsibility on customers to review those outputs.[^5]

**Checking whether documents satisfy application requirements is not the same as independently authenticating those documents.** The distinction is important because the company's promotional emphasis on comprehensive verification is considerably broader than the responsibility it accepts under its contract.

## Prohibiting Customer Review of Visa Applications Before Submission

Issa Compass's Terms of Service, updated November 24, 2026, expressly prohibit customers using its submission services from reviewing application materials prepared on their behalf.

Section 6.8(a) prevents customers from inspecting, commenting on, or approving application forms, cover letters, declarations, and other submission materials before they are sent to government authorities.[^5]

Section 6.8(e) further establishes that being denied access to these materials before submission does not entitle customers to a refund.

### Contradiction With Official Thai Embassy Guidance

On November 19, 2026, the Royal Thai Embassy in Moscow published detailed consumer guidance specifically addressing commercial visa application services.[^11]

The embassy explicitly advises customers:

> Ask the company to show you the completed application before submission.

It also recommends checking personal information, passport details, travel dates, and the declared purpose of travel, while warning against allowing intermediaries to submit false information or altered documents.

**Issa Compass's contractual restrictions directly contradict this official consumer-protection recommendation.**

### Customer Responsibility Without Prior Approval

Section 6.6 of Issa Compass's contract places responsibility on customers for the accuracy, completeness, and authenticity of the information and documents they provide.

However, Section 6.8 prevents those customers from reviewing how that information is ultimately presented in applications prepared and submitted by the company.

This creates a significant accountability imbalance: customers remain responsible for their supplied information while being denied the opportunity to verify how the intermediary incorporates it into government submissions.

The contractual restriction excludes self-submission plans and expressly preserves applicable data-protection rights.

The available evidence does not establish that Issa Compass has submitted inaccurate or fraudulent applications, nor that its contractual restriction is itself unlawful.

Nevertheless, denying customers an opportunity to review immigration declarations made on their behalf is difficult to reconcile with the safeguards explicitly recommended by Thai authorities.

## Paid Scripted Social Media Promotions and Singapore Consumer-Protection Law

Issa Compass operates a commercial user-generated content (UGC) program that recruits individuals to produce and publish scripted promotional videos concerning Thailand's immigration requirements and the company's visa services.[^7]

The program does not require participants to have an established audience. Its public recruitment page does not identify previous experience using Issa Compass as an eligibility condition, and the company offers the same compensation rates to newly created social media accounts.

Issa Compass supplies scripts, tracks video performance, and compensates participants according to the number of views their content generates.

### Financial Incentives for Mass Promotional Content

Issa Compass advertises two alternative compensation schemes:

| Video views | Milestone payment | Performance payment |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2,000 | $30 | $6 |
| 10,000 | $75 | $30 |
| 100,000 | $250 | $300 |
| 1,000,000 | $2,000 | $1,600 |

The performance-based scheme also offers a one-time $25 launch bonus when a creator's first qualifying video exceeds 2,000 views.

Participants can publish unlimited videos, cross-post content to TikTok and Instagram, and receive compensation based on the better-performing platform.[^7]

**The program financially incentivizes the widespread distribution of company-scripted visa promotions through individually operated social media accounts.**

### Company-Scripted Content in a Personal Style

Issa Compass provides participants with a library of prepared scripts and encourages them to film videos casually using their phones.

The program does not require an established following or prior approval of individual videos before publication.

Participants are therefore not necessarily existing Issa Compass customers sharing independent experiences. They may be individuals recruited specifically to deliver commercially prepared promotional messages in exchange for compensation.

The publicly accessible recruitment page does not explain whether participants must prominently disclose their financial relationship with Issa Compass in every video.[^7]

Although the company links to a separate creator agreement, its contents could not be independently reviewed.

### Singapore Prohibits Misleading Commercial Endorsements

Issa Compass operates partly through [Issara Platforms Pte. Ltd.](../companies/issara-platforms-pte-ltd.md), a Singapore-registered company.

Section 4 of Singapore's Consumer Protection (Fair Trading) Act 2003 establishes that false claims and commercial representations or omissions capable of reasonably misleading consumers constitute unfair practices.[^8]

**Presenting compensated promotional content as independent customer recommendations can constitute an unlawful unfair trade practice under Singapore law.**

The geographical location of the advertised immigration services does not necessarily exclude Singapore's consumer-protection jurisdiction. Section 3 addresses transactions involving Singapore-resident suppliers or consumers, as well as offers or acceptances made in or sent from Singapore.

Where the statutory requirements are satisfied, businesses cannot avoid consumer-protection obligations merely by distributing their advertisements through individually operated social media accounts.

### Singapore Advertising Standards Require Sponsorship Disclosure

The Advertising Standards Authority of Singapore (ASAS) introduced its Guidelines for Interactive Marketing Communication and Social Media in August 2016.[^9]

These guidelines require marketers to distinguish commercial communications from independent personal opinions and editorial content.

They specifically address:

- Financial relationships between advertisers and endorsers.
- Commercially produced content presented as impartial commentary.
- Prominent and understandable sponsorship disclosures.
- Marketers' responsibilities when exercising control over promotional content.
- Fraudulent methods of artificially increasing social media engagement.

**Issa Compass's use of individually operated accounts, company-provided scripts, and casual video formats does not remove the commercial nature of its paid promotional content.**

These advertising standards complement Singapore's statutory consumer protections, although the advertising code itself is a self-regulatory framework rather than criminal legislation.

### Singapore Took Enforcement Action Against Paid Fake Reviews in 2026

On September 30, 2026, the Competition and Consumer Commission of Singapore (CCS) announced enforcement action against 45 businesses and a provider of fabricated online reviews.[^10]

The investigation documented an operation involving prepared review content, paid contributors, automated content generation, and an online dashboard for managing promotional activity.

The participating businesses had purchased reviews presented as genuine customer experiences despite those reviews not being authentic.

Following regulatory intervention, the businesses committed to stopping the practice, removing fabricated reviews, and publishing public apologies for six months.

**The enforcement action demonstrates that Singapore's consumer-protection authorities actively investigate commercial arrangements that manufacture apparently independent endorsements.**

Issa Compass was not identified as a participant in that enforcement case. Its creator program is not necessarily equivalent to the fake-review operation investigated by CCS.

Nevertheless, the case establishes the regulatory significance of concealing commercial relationships and manufacturing misleading customer endorsements.

### Relevance to Reported Social Media Manipulation

The creator program is particularly relevant to previously reported concerns about apparently independent social media recommendations promoting Issa Compass and [Thai Visa Centre](thai-visa-centre.md).

Unlike allegations concerning unidentified promotional accounts, **the existence of Issa Compass's paid, scripted content-production program is directly established by the company's own published recruitment material.**

The program provides a documented commercial mechanism through which large volumes of promotional content can be distributed by individually operated social media accounts, including newly created accounts without established audiences.

This raises several important questions:

- Does Issa Compass require prominent sponsorship disclosures in every promotional video?
- Are creators permitted to describe personal visa experiences they have never actually had?
- Are published videos monitored for misleading claims and undisclosed advertising?
- Which registered company administers creator contracts and payments?
- Are compensated recommendations subsequently presented as evidence of independent customer satisfaction?

Paid creator marketing is not inherently unlawful. However, concealing compensation, fabricating customer experiences, or presenting company-scripted advertising as independent recommendations could violate Singapore's Consumer Protection (Fair Trading) Act.

**Issa Compass should publicly explain how its financially incentivized content-production program complies with Singapore's advertising and consumer-protection requirements.**

The available evidence establishes the existence of the commercial program, but does not yet establish that individual participants have published undisclosed advertisements or fabricated testimonials.

### Independent Review Assurances and Paid Commercial Endorsements

Issa Compass's dedicated customer reviews page expressly states:

> We never pay for reviews, offer anything in return, or tell anyone what to write.[^12]

However, its separate creator recruitment program openly offers payments of up to $2,000 per video, supplies prepared scripts, and encourages participants to publish promotional content in a casual, personal style through independently operated social media accounts.[^7]

The company's review assurances do not explain how it distinguishes compensated social media recommendations from independent customer endorsements.

Its security guide also advertises more than 865 verified Google reviews as evidence of its legitimacy.[^3] However, it does not explain what verification procedure supports this designation.

Google prohibits compensated reviews and requires contributions to reflect genuine customer experiences.[^13] Trustpilot similarly prohibits incentivized reviews and reviews from individuals whose commercial relationships with a business could introduce bias.[^14]

Issa Compass should explain how it separates its paid creators from its independent review-collection activities and ensures that compensated endorsements are prominently identified as advertising.

**Independent customer feedback and company-scripted commercial endorsements represent fundamentally different forms of evidence.**
