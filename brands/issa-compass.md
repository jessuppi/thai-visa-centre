# Issa Compass

**Issa Compass** is an unregistered trade name used to advertise commercial visa and immigration-assistance services, particularly Thailand's Destination Thailand Visa (DTV).

The brand operates through two separately registered companies: [Issara Platforms Pte. Ltd.](../companies/issara-platforms-pte-ltd.md) in Singapore and [Issa Systems Co., Ltd.](../companies/issa-systems-co-ltd.md) in Thailand.[^1]

## Corporate Identity and Registration Claims

Issa Compass's Privacy Policy explicitly states **"We trade as Issa Compass."** The two companies have different registration numbers and legal responsibilities.[^2]

Nevertheless, the website's guide to identifying fraudulent visa agencies presents Issa Compass as a registered software company operating in Singapore and Thailand.[^3]

**The registered entities are Issara Platforms and Issa Systems, not a company named Issa Compass.** Their corporate registrations should not be confused with registration of the advertised trade name.

## Customer Information and Responsibility

The company's Privacy Policy identifies Issara Platforms as the controller of customers' digital visa files, while Issa Systems handles physical documents and in-person services in Thailand.[^2]

Passport copies, financial records, and other sensitive immigration documents are therefore processed through a cross-border arrangement involving two distinct legal entities.

The policy states that customer information is stored primarily in Singapore and accessed from both countries.[^2]

## November 2026 Money-Back Guarantee Restrictions

Issa Compass prominently advertises its money-back guarantee as evidence of its reliability. Its security guide describes a **"Full refund guarantee"**, while promotional articles emphasize protection against unsuccessful visa applications.[^3][^4]

However, its Terms of Service, updated November 13, 2026, impose substantial restrictions.

Under Section 7.3.7, applicable to plans first paid for on or after **November 14, 2026**, the company's legal team determines whether a customer's application qualifies for the guarantee. Customers may pay before receiving that determination.[^5]

If the company subsequently denies coverage, customers can proceed without the guarantee, switch visa categories, or abandon the application. Those who abandon it receive a **12-month credit toward another Issa Compass service instead of a cash refund**. The initial assessment fee remains non-refundable.

More remarkably, the company expressly reserves the right to **withhold its reasons for denying guarantee eligibility** when an explanation would reveal internal methods or proprietary knowledge.[^5]

The advertised guarantee is therefore not automatically secured when a customer purchases an eligible plan. The company can accept payment, subsequently determine that the application does not qualify, decline to fully explain that decision, and retain the customer's service payment as credit.

For qualifying purchases from August 29, 2026 onward, the ordinary guarantee also covers only the company's service fee, excluding government application fees and other third-party charges.[^5]

### Issa Compass Warns Against the Same Weak Refund Guarantees It Sells

Issa Compass's October 17, 2026 article *Document Pre-Checks, Money-Back Guarantees, and Expert Support* advises consumers how to identify misleading visa-service guarantees.[^6]

Its comparison table explicitly classifies:

- **Weak guarantee:** Service credit only.
- **Strong guarantee:** Refund of the service fee.

The article also warns that excessive conditions can make recovering money nearly impossible.

**Yet Issa Compass's own Terms of Service contain precisely the weak refund arrangement it warns consumers against.**

Under Section 7.3.7, applicable to purchases beginning November 14, 2026, customers can pay for plans advertised with money-back protection before the company confirms whether their applications qualify.[^5]

If Issa Compass's legal team subsequently denies guarantee eligibility, customers who abandon their applications do not receive an ordinary cash refund.

Instead:

- Issa Compass retains their service fees as credit toward another visa service.
- The credit expires 12 months after the original payment.
- The Initial Assessment Fee remains non-refundable.
- The company can withhold detailed reasons for rejecting guarantee eligibility when it considers those reasons proprietary.

**Issa Compass accepts payment while advertising refund protection, reserves the right to deny that protection afterward, and substitutes the exact service-credit remedy its own consumer guide labels a weak guarantee.**

The company's standard guarantee promises service-fee refunds for qualifying government refusals. But the contract allows Issa Compass to decide that a paying customer never qualified for that protection in the first place.

That distinction is central to the problem: **the company controls whether the advertised cash-refund guarantee applies after the customer has already paid.**

Its October consumer guide tells readers to scrutinize conditional guarantees and reject service-credit-only protection. Its November contract then authorizes both practices.

**Issa Compass's published consumer advice condemns refund arrangements that its own contractual terms expressly permit.**

Customers deserve to know before payment whether they are purchasing genuine cash-refund protection or merely the possibility of receiving it, subject to a later internal decision.

The contradiction directly undermines Issa Compass's marketing of its money-back guarantee as a reason to trust its paid visa services.

## Document Verification Claims Versus Contractual Disclaimers

Issa Compass advertises an AI-powered verification system that checks application documents against published and unpublished embassy requirements. Its promotional material describes comprehensive document verification as the foundation of its money-back guarantee.[^4]

However, Section 6.6(c) of its Terms of Service states:

> We do not independently verify, authenticate or investigate the information or documents the User provides, and we are under no obligation to do so.

The company also disclaims responsibility for inaccurate or falsified customer documents. Section 8.4 separately warns that its AI-generated document checks may contain errors and places responsibility on customers to review those outputs.[^5]

**Checking whether documents satisfy application requirements is not the same as independently authenticating those documents.** The distinction is important because the company's promotional emphasis on comprehensive verification is considerably broader than the responsibility it accepts under its contract.
