# THNIC Foundation

**Thai Network Information Center Foundation (THNIC Foundation)** is a Thai nonprofit organization responsible for administering Thailand's `.th` and `.ไทย` domain namespaces and establishing their registration policies. On April 1, 2008, the Foundation appointed [T.H.NIC Co., Ltd.](../companies/t-h-nic-co-ltd.md) as the official domain registrar and Thai Name Server Co., Ltd. as the registry operator.[^1]

The Foundation's Domain Name Registration Policy includes provisions governing complaints, bad-faith domain use, phishing, and domain suspension or revocation. These provisions have become central to questions concerning [THNIC](thnic.md)'s handling of abuse complaints submitted in April 2025 and October–November 2026, particularly its failure to explain why its published enforcement powers were not exercised or considered applicable.[^2][^3]

## Formal Complaint Against Registrar Enforcement (November 2026)

On November 12, 2026, Jesse Nickles submitted a formal complaint to the THNIC Foundation requesting an independent review of T.H.NIC Co., Ltd.'s handling of repeated domain abuse reports involving websites associated with [Thai Visa Centre](thai-visa-centre.md) and [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md). The complaint cited the registrar's handling of reports dating back to February 2025, including allegations involving `eta.in.th`, `dtv.in.th`, `asq.in.th`, `tdac.in.th`, and `tvc.co.th`.[^4]

Nickles challenged the registrar's November 10, 2026 position that it could not suspend `tvc.co.th` without an explicit court or government order, arguing that this interpretation had not been reconciled with Sections 12.4, 12.5, 14.1.4.2, 14.2, and 17 of the Foundation's published Domain Name Registration Policy. He requested that the Foundation independently examine the registrar's decisions, determine whether the relevant bad-faith-use and information-security provisions had been considered, clarify the circumstances requiring a court order, and explain its oversight procedures.[^4]

**The complaint specifically requested a written determination addressing the registrar's handling of the April 2025 phishing report concerning `tdac.in.th`, as well as the broader pattern of complaints, referrals, and unresolved enforcement questions.** It represented a formal escalation to the organization responsible for the policies the registrar had been asked to enforce.[^4]

## International Delegation and Public-Interest Responsibilities

The Internet Assigned Numbers Authority (IANA) identifies the THNIC Foundation as the designated manager of both Thailand's `.th` and `.ไทย` country-code top-level domains.[^5][^6]

Neither T.H.NIC Co., Ltd. nor Thai Name Server Co., Ltd. is independently identified as the designated manager in these delegation records.

This distinction is significant because the Foundation retains its policy-setting role while delegating operational responsibilities to its appointed commercial entities.

### Commitments Made During the 2010 Delegation

On July 16, 2010, IANA published its evaluation of the Foundation's application to administer Thailand's Thai-language top-level domain.

The report documented support from the Thai government, the Ministry of Information and Communication Technology, the Thai Webmaster Association, the Thai Internet Service Provider Association, and the Office of Higher Education.

Under its public-interest assessment, IANA recorded the following commitment:

> The proposed sponsoring organisation undertakes to continue to operate the domain in a fair and equitable manner, using the same policies used for the “.TH” domain today.

The assessment identified equitable treatment of the local Internet community as one of the criteria governing delegation.[^7]

### Responsibility Remains With the Designated Manager

IANA's published guidance distinguishes the organization responsible for administering domain policies from commercial companies contracted to perform technical operations.

It also characterizes country-code domain managers as trustees acting on behalf of their local Internet communities.[^8]

The Foundation's appointment of a separate registrar and registry operator therefore does not eliminate its own accountability as the designated manager of Thailand's national domain namespaces.

### The Foundation's Own Public Assurances

The Foundation's current website features a statement from its chairman, Dr. Khotom Areeya, emphasizing the role of strict registration policies in preventing internet-related crime and maintaining confidence in the authenticity of Thai online businesses.[^9]

These assurances are directly relevant to complaints involving privately operated immigration websites that allegedly resemble official government services.

The unresolved complaints provide a practical reason to examine how the Foundation supervises the registrar's application of its published registration policies.

### Limits of International Oversight

IANA's published revocation guidance expressly distinguishes its responsibility for the security and stability of the Domain Name System from matters that should be resolved through local accountability mechanisms.[^10]

IANA does not ordinarily adjudicate disputes concerning fairness, honesty, or individual domain registrations.

Consequently, the documented complaints do not independently establish grounds for revoking the Foundation's delegation.

Nevertheless, the Foundation's historical commitments and its current status as Thailand's designated domain manager provide relevant institutional context for requests that it review the conduct of its appointed registrar.

## September 2025 Anti-Phishing Guidance and Unresolved Complaints

On September 9, 2025, the THNIC Foundation published an educational article concerning deceptive website addresses, featuring explanations attributed to its director, Dr. Pensri Arunwatanamongkol.[^11]

The article identifies several warning signs associated with deceptive websites:

- Domain names resembling legitimate websites.
- Recently registered domains.
- Unclear domain ownership.
- Requests for unnecessary personal information or important documents.
- Misleading assurances of security based on SSL certificates.

The Foundation recommends examining domain registration records and verifying the authenticity of websites before submitting personal information.

### Relevance to Earlier Immigration Website Complaints

These recommendations are directly relevant to the complaints submitted to THNIC between February and May 2025 concerning privately operated immigration websites associated with Thai Visa Centre.

Those complaints raised questions about misleading website presentation, domain ownership, government affiliation, and the collection of travelers' personal information.

The Foundation's September publication demonstrates institutional awareness of these general cybersecurity risks.

However, the available complaint records do not establish whether equivalent criteria were applied when examining the reported immigration websites.

### Domain Registration Versus Government Authorization

The Foundation also promotes Thai domain registrations as a means of enhancing commercial credibility, including through its participation in a Department of Business Development event on February 27, 2025.[^12]

Nevertheless, its registration policy distinguishes general-purpose .in.th domains and commercial .co.th domains from government-specific .go.th domains.

Eligibility to register a Thai domain does not independently establish government authorization, professional competence, or the legitimacy of every service subsequently advertised through that domain.

The Foundation's public cybersecurity guidance provides an additional benchmark against which to evaluate its oversight of the registrar's complaint-handling practices.

The September article does not specifically identify Thai Visa Centre or establish that the reported immigration websites constitute phishing operations.

## April 2023 Public Assurances Regarding Fraudulent Domain Use

On April 5, 2023, the THNIC Foundation published an announcement concerning its partnership with the Digital Council of Thailand.[^13]

Discussing fraudulent websites and the benefits of verified Thai domain registrations, executive director Dr. Pensri Arunwatanamongkol stated:

> In case of misuse or fraudulent activities, it is possible to contact the domain holder or administrators to suspend the usage.

The Foundation presented domain-holder verification as a mechanism for reducing deceptive registrations and protecting internet users.

### Relevance to Subsequent Abuse Complaints

Between February 2025 and November 2026, Jesse Nickles submitted multiple complaints concerning immigration-related domains associated with Thai Visa Centre and Agents Co., Ltd.[^3][^4]

These complaints raised precisely the types of concerns addressed in the Foundation's earlier public statements, including potentially misleading website identities and the collection of personal information through privately operated immigration websites.

The registrar subsequently stated that suspension of tvc.co.th would require an explicit court or competent government order.

**The Foundation's earlier assurances make it particularly important to establish what investigative and intervention procedures are available when suspected domain misuse is reported.**

The April 2023 statement does not establish that every abuse complaint warrants suspension or that the Foundation can independently adjudicate defamation disputes.

Nevertheless, it provides additional grounds for requesting clarification of the registrar's enforcement procedures and the Foundation's oversight responsibilities.
