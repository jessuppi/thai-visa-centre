# ASEAN NOW

ASEAN NOW, formerly ThaiVisa.com, is a Thailand-focused English-language news and discussion forum acquired by [The Thaiger](the-thaiger.md) in April 2024. The platform hosts extensive discussions about Thai immigration procedures, visa agencies, and expatriate services while selling advertising and sponsorship opportunities to businesses operating in those sectors.[^1]

## Sponsor-Protection Rules and Suppression of Criticism

ASEAN NOW's published forum rules explicitly distinguish paying sponsors from ordinary members. **Rule 24 exempts current forum sponsors from restrictions against commercial self-promotion**, while Rule 32 prohibits other members from publishing commercial topics or posts without sponsorship. Rule 37 restricts comments about advertisers and sponsors, and Rule 13 prohibits members from publicly questioning moderation decisions. Together, these policies give paying businesses privileged access to forum discussions while limiting how other participants can scrutinize the resulting content.[^2]

This protection is longstanding. In an April 13, 2020 forum-support announcement, administrator CharlieH reproduced Rule 37 as an explicit prohibition against **negative comments concerning advertisements or sponsors**. Following The Thaiger's April 2024 acquisition of ASEAN NOW, CharlieH published another notice on September 30, 2024, criticizing members who made derogatory remarks about sponsors and advertorials. He instructed dissatisfied members that **"if you have nothing positive to say, move on"**, reinforcing the platform's preference for protecting commercial content from unfavorable public discussion.[^3][^4]

The consequences became apparent in a November 4, 2025 discussion about [Thai Visa Centre](thai-visa-centre.md). Global moderator DrJack54 directed readers toward the agency's online reviews and described it as Bangkok's largest visa agent. Another global moderator, BrandonJT, stated that he had encountered consistently favorable feedback. Meanwhile, the agency's official **ThaiVisaCentre** account participated under the designation **"Forum Sponsor"**, promoting its immigration services and subsequently advertising paid 90-day reporting through `90day.in.th`.[^5]

On November 5, administrator **Captain Flack explicitly confirmed removing a post criticizing the forum sponsor**, citing Rule 37 and its prohibition against negative comments about advertisers. Moderator DrJack54 separately announced that multiple posts and replies had been removed. The original critical posts are no longer available for independent assessment, but Captain Flack's own explanation directly establishes that sponsor protection was invoked as a reason for removing criticism. Notably, the currently published version of Rule 37 uses narrower language concerning disrespectful, dismissive, or off-topic comments; the available records do not establish when that wording changed.[^2][^5]

**The arrangement presents a structural conflict between ASEAN NOW's role as an immigration-advice community and its commercial obligations to paying sponsors.** Ordinary members face restrictions on business promotion, criticism of sponsors, and public challenges to moderation, while sponsored businesses can advertise directly within discussions where potential customers seek independent recommendations. The documented removal of criticism concerning Thai Visa Centre demonstrates how these policies operated in practice. Although the evidence does not establish that Thai Visa Centre requested the deletions or purchased favorable moderator statements, it establishes that ASEAN NOW's own administrators enforced sponsor-protective restrictions in a discussion about a paying advertiser.[^2][^5]

## May–August 2025 TDAC Promotion and Unsupported Security Claims

On May 24, 2025, ASEAN NOW administrator **CharlieH** published a promotional discussion titled *Apply for your TDAC early - Here*, advertising the Thailand Digital Arrival Card platform operated by [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md), a business associated with [Thai Visa Centre](thai-visa-centre.md). The announcement described the commercial platform as **"our platform"**, promoted advantages over Thailand's official government registration system, and advertised early-submission fees ranging from **US$0.50 to US$8 per applicant**. The promotion appeared directly within ASEAN NOW's immigration-advice forum rather than exclusively through a conventional advertising placement.[^6]

On May 25, forum participant Maestro announced that he had **removed a post containing false information**, along with a reply. However, on July 9, global moderator **BrandonJT** responded to a traveler concerned about the commercial platform's handling of personal information by claiming that submitting information through Thailand's official government TDAC system carried a **"50% chance of being stolen by hackers"**. He attributed this extraordinary probability to earlier government data breaches without identifying any investigation, technical assessment, or statistical evidence supporting the figure.[^6]

Another participant, Maestro, responded that he could not confidently demonstrate that BrandonJT was exaggerating. Neither participant supplied evidence supporting the claimed probability. This is particularly significant because **ASEAN NOW's published Rule 5 prohibits false, inaccurate, misleading, and scaremongering content**, while instructing members to support factual claims with reputable sources. The unsupported numerical allegation about the government's immigration system contrasts sharply with those published standards.[^2][^6]

On August 9, BrandonJT again defended the commercial offering when another member questioned the value of paying for a government procedure available free of charge. He described the approximately **US$8 fee as an optional convenience**, offering examples of travelers who might prefer to arrange submissions earlier. On August 19, Thai Visa Centre's official account, identified as a **Forum Sponsor**, joined the discussion to promote the platform's claimed security safeguards, regulatory compliance, and personal-data deletion capabilities. Thailand's Immigration Bureau, meanwhile, continued providing TDAC registration through its official government portal without charging a submission fee.[^6][^7]

**The episode exposes a discrepancy between ASEAN NOW's stated misinformation standards and the conduct of individuals exercising moderation authority in commercially sponsored discussions.** A moderator made an unsupported claim suggesting that the free government immigration system presented an exceptionally high risk of data theft, while forum administrators and moderators promoted a commercial alternative collecting the same categories of personal information. The surviving discussion does not establish that Thai Visa Centre directed these statements or that the moderator had a financial interest in the service. It does, however, document how unsupported security allegations and favorable commercial messaging appeared together in a sponsored immigration discussion without comparable evidentiary scrutiny.[^2][^6]

## November 2025 Moderator Claims About Retirement Visa Bank Deposits

On November 10, 2025, an ASEAN NOW member using the name **Blueman1** acknowledged using [Thai Visa Centre](thai-visa-centre.md) for retirement extensions because he did not maintain the required money in his Thai bank account. Another participant directly asked whether the agency deposited the required 800,000 baht on customers' behalf. Global moderator **DrJack54** responded: **"All agents deposit 800k. Obtain bank letter showing required balance. The funds then instantly withdrawn."**[^8]

The moderator's description conflicts with Thailand's published financial requirements for annual retirement extensions. Applicants relying on the 800,000-baht bank-deposit route must maintain the required balance for at least **two months before submitting an application and three months after approval**, with a minimum balance of 400,000 baht thereafter. Obtaining a bank certificate after a temporary deposit and immediately withdrawing the funds would not, by itself, satisfy these conditions.[^9]

Other participants questioned the mechanics of the purported arrangement, including how an agent could withdraw money from a customer's personal bank account. The moderator did not supply bank statements, transaction records, immigration documentation, or other evidence establishing that Thai Visa Centre followed the procedure he described. His sweeping assertion that all agents used this method was similarly unsupported.[^8]

The exchange occurred within the same discussion where **Thai Visa Centre participated as a paying Forum Sponsor** and ASEAN NOW administrators had already acknowledged removing criticism of the agency under sponsor-protection rules. Consequently, readers encountered an explanation of potentially noncompliant financial arrangements from a moderator exercising authority over the discussion, while criticism of the commercial sponsor was subject to removal.[^5][^8]

**The incident raises questions about the reliability and independence of immigration advice provided by ASEAN NOW moderators.** The published statements do not establish that Thai Visa Centre actually arranged temporary deposits, that a particular visa was obtained unlawfully, or that the moderator participated in improper transactions. They do establish that a moderator publicly described a practice inconsistent with the financial conditions governing ordinary retirement extensions, without producing evidence or distinguishing it from legitimate immigration procedures.[^8][^9]

## Historical Advertiser Protection and Continuity Following The Thaiger Acquisition

ASEAN NOW's restrictions on criticism of commercial sponsors substantially predate its sponsorship relationship with Thai Visa Centre and its acquisition by The Thaiger.

### November 2010: Advertising Revenue Used to Justify Restrictions

On November 21, 2010, ThaiVisa.com member **fridgemagnet** reported that a post criticizing a commercial sponsor had been removed and publicly challenged the forum's policy.[^10]

Responding to the complaint, forum contributor **cdnvic** defended the arrangement by emphasizing that sponsors financed the infrastructure required to operate the discussion platform.

The contributor directed dissatisfied customers toward the advertising sales department or private communication with the sponsor.

He also cited Thailand's strict defamation laws and the possibility of competitors abusing anonymous discussion accounts.

### June 2012: Contractual Restrictions on Advertiser Criticism

On June 10, 2012, during a discussion concerning the legality of a sponsored television service, forum member **SaNim** reproduced the following provision attributed to ThaiVisa's then-current forum rules:

> Our contracts with our advertisers prohibit comments with regard to advertisements which appear on our forums.

The provision additionally required such comments to be removed.[^11]

The contributor questioned whether the restriction prevented members from publicly examining the legality of services advertised by commercial sponsors.

This historical exchange indicates that advertiser protection was presented as a contractual obligation rather than merely a general moderation preference.

### April 2024: Sponsor Protection Reaffirmed After Acquisition

On April 23, 2024, just four days after The Thaiger announced its acquisition of ASEAN NOW, longtime forum member **simon43** criticized increasingly intrusive advertising and anti-ad-blocking notifications.[^1][^12]

The forum account **george** responded by citing three rules, including Rule 37:

> You will not post negative comments concerning advertisements on ASEAN NOW or our sponsors.

The discussion was subsequently closed to further replies.

### Relevance to Thai Visa Centre

On November 5, 2025, ASEAN NOW administrator Captain Flack invoked the same rule when acknowledging the removal of criticism concerning Thai Visa Centre.[^5]

**These historical records demonstrate that commercially motivated restrictions on public criticism existed long before The Thaiger's acquisition and remained in operation following the ownership transition.**

Their continued enforcement is particularly significant given The Thaiger's subsequent marketing of sponsored community participation, search-engine visibility, and AI-generated recommendations.

The historical advertising contracts themselves have not been obtained, and the available evidence does not establish that Thai Visa Centre negotiated specific censorship provisions.

## Commercial Sale of Community Authority and Sponsor Participation

On September 4, 2025, ASEAN NOW's parent publisher, The Thaiger, published an advertising guide promoting commercial partnership packages within its expatriate discussion community.[^13]

The advertised benefits included sponsored accounts, direct participation in community discussions, pinned advertorials, premium business-directory placements and backlinks intended to improve search-engine rankings.

### Commercially Acquired Community Credibility

The publisher marketed sponsored participation using the phrase:

> Become a trusted voice within the community.

This establishes that perceived authority within the discussion platform was explicitly presented as a commercially obtainable benefit.

The arrangement is particularly significant within immigration discussions, where prospective customers frequently seek independent assessments of visa agencies and immigration procedures.

### Application to Thai Visa Centre

Thai Visa Centre subsequently participated as a designated Forum Sponsor in the November 2025 discussion concerning its immigration services.[^5]

During that discussion, moderators published favorable assessments of the agency, while an administrator acknowledged removing criticism under the forum's sponsor-protection rules.

These documented events illustrate how commercially privileged participation and restrictions on criticism can operate simultaneously within discussions intended to provide immigration advice.

### Commercial and Editorial Accountability

**ASEAN NOW markets community credibility to advertisers while maintaining moderation rules that restrict ordinary members' ability to scrutinize commercial sponsors.**

Consequently, favorable discussion content should not automatically be treated as representative of independent customer experiences or unrestricted community opinion.

The available evidence does not establish that Thai Visa Centre purchased favorable moderator statements or that its sponsorship agreement included specific censorship provisions.

## May 2026 Sponsor Referrals and Restrictions on Visa Legality Discussions

On May 13, 2026, an ASEAN NOW member sought recommendations concerning Thai Visa Centre, explicitly acknowledging that he lacked the 800,000 baht ordinarily required under the bank-deposit route for retirement extensions.[^14]

The following day, global moderator DrJack54 recommended contacting Thai Visa Centre directly and indicated an approximate price of 16,000 baht.

Thai Visa Centre subsequently participated as a designated Forum Sponsor, advertising its annual extension services for approximately 16,000 baht and referring the prospective customer to its private communication channels for an individualized assessment.

### Moderators Discourage Scrutiny of Legal Compliance

On May 15, another forum participant suggested that the applicant should satisfy the applicable legal requirements before pursuing an extension.

DrJack54 responded:

> Thread is not about legality or otherwise of use of agent.

The moderator subsequently announced the removal of off-topic posts.

On May 17, another participant questioned whether the agency could lawfully assist someone who did not satisfy the financial requirements.

DrJack54 acknowledged that some customers used agents to avoid financial requirements, while continuing to direct participants back toward the original question.

Global moderator BrandonJT separately asserted that immigration officers possessed full discretion to approve extensions without normally required financial evidence. Another participant challenged that interpretation.

### Commercial and Editorial Accountability

**ASEAN NOW permitted its commercial sponsor to advertise immigration assistance to a prospective customer who acknowledged a financial shortfall, while a moderator discouraged discussion of the legality of the underlying arrangements.**

The exchange provides further evidence that the concerns documented in November 2025 continued into May 2026.

Some critical comments remained publicly visible. The available evidence does not establish that all removed posts concerned Thai Visa Centre, that the agency obtained an extension for this particular applicant, or that any immigration officer accepted an improper payment.

Nevertheless, questions concerning legal compliance are directly relevant to prospective customers evaluating paid immigration assistance and should not automatically be treated as irrelevant to commercial recommendations.

## August 2026 Customer Concerns and Additional Immigration Service Referrals

On August 4, 2026, an ASEAN NOW member using the name TonBrow questioned whether retirement extensions arranged through Thai Visa Centre were accepted without difficulty when obtaining other immigration services.[^15]

Thai Visa Centre responded through its sponsored forum account, offering to arrange a certificate of residence on the customer's behalf and explaining that it might already possess the necessary passport documentation.

### Conflicting Customer Experiences

Another participant claimed that Thai Visa Centre included an initial 90-day report and TM30 documentation within its extension services.

On August 5, TonBrow disputed whether this reflected his own experience:

> They've never sent me a tm 30 and always charge 500 baht to make a 90 day report.

He also expressed concern about passports reportedly being sent to immigration offices in northeastern Thailand and whether his extension would attract scrutiny elsewhere.

Other participants discussed potential complications involving extensions issued outside the customer's province of residence.

### Continued Reliance on Commercial Assistance

**The discussion illustrates how uncertainty concerning an agent-arranged extension can encourage customers to purchase additional immigration assistance from the same agency rather than approach immigration independently.**

The surviving discussion also contains favorable customer experiences and legitimate questions from a moderator concerning immigration-office jurisdiction.

Unlike the previously documented November 2025 and May 2026 discussions, this exchange does not independently establish that moderators removed critical comments.

The available evidence does not establish that the customer's extension was fraudulent, that Thai Visa Centre improperly filed residence notifications, or that immigration authorities rejected any subsequent application.

## October 2026 Same-Day Retirement Visa and Banking Packages

On October 10, 2026, Thai Visa Centre advertised two retirement visa packages through its sponsored ASEAN NOW account, responding to an inquiry concerning a prospective applicant entering Thailand without a visa.[^16]

The agency advertised the following prices and processing periods:

- 45,000 baht for its standard two-to-three-week service.
- 55,000 baht for same-day completion within approximately six to seven hours.

Both packages included assistance opening a Thai bank account.

### Financial Documentation and Personal Attendance

Thailand's published government guidance concerning in-country conversion to Non-Immigrant O status for retirement purposes identifies financial evidence requirements, including an 800,000-baht deposit with evidence of overseas remittance, qualifying pension income, or an applicable combination of funds and income.[^17]

The guidance also requires applicants to attend personally and establishes a government application fee of 2,000 baht.

Thai Visa Centre's advertisement does not explain how its accelerated service satisfies these documentary and attendance requirements or identify which additional charges account for the substantial difference between its advertised prices and the government application fee.

### Continued Commercial Promotion

**Thai Visa Centre continues advertising accelerated retirement immigration services through ASEAN NOW, including packages combining bank account opening and visa issuance within a single day.**

This provides additional commercial context for the previously documented discussions concerning financial eligibility and agent-arranged retirement extensions.

The advertisement does not independently establish that Thai Visa Centre circumvents financial requirements, makes improper payments, or obtains immigration approvals unlawfully.
