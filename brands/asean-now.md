# ASEAN NOW

ASEAN NOW, formerly ThaiVisa.com, is a Thailand-focused English-language news and discussion forum acquired by [The Thaiger](the-thaiger.md) in April 2024. The platform hosts extensive discussions about Thai immigration procedures, visa agencies, and expatriate services while selling advertising and sponsorship opportunities to businesses operating in those sectors.[^1]

## Sponsor-Protection Rules and Suppression of Criticism

ASEAN NOW's published forum rules explicitly distinguish paying sponsors from ordinary members. **Rule 24 exempts current forum sponsors from restrictions against commercial self-promotion**, while Rule 32 prohibits other members from publishing commercial topics or posts without sponsorship. Rule 37 restricts comments about advertisers and sponsors, and Rule 13 prohibits members from publicly questioning moderation decisions. Together, these policies give paying businesses privileged access to forum discussions while limiting how other participants can scrutinize the resulting content.[^2]

This protection is longstanding. In an April 13, 2020 forum-support announcement, administrator CharlieH reproduced Rule 37 as an explicit prohibition against **negative comments concerning advertisements or sponsors**. Following The Thaiger's April 2024 acquisition of ASEAN NOW, CharlieH published another notice on September 30, 2024, criticizing members who made derogatory remarks about sponsors and advertorials. He instructed dissatisfied members that **"if you have nothing positive to say, move on"**, reinforcing the platform's preference for protecting commercial content from unfavorable public discussion.[^3][^4]

The consequences became apparent in a November 4, 2025 discussion about [Thai Visa Centre](thai-visa-centre.md). Global moderator DrJack54 directed readers toward the agency's online reviews and described it as Bangkok's largest visa agent. Another global moderator, BrandonJT, stated that he had encountered consistently favorable feedback. Meanwhile, the agency's official **ThaiVisaCentre** account participated under the designation **"Forum Sponsor"**, promoting its immigration services and subsequently advertising paid 90-day reporting through `90day.in.th`.[^5]

On November 5, administrator **Captain Flack explicitly confirmed removing a post criticizing the forum sponsor**, citing Rule 37 and its prohibition against negative comments about advertisers. Moderator DrJack54 separately announced that multiple posts and replies had been removed. The original critical posts are no longer available for independent assessment, but Captain Flack's own explanation directly establishes that sponsor protection was invoked as a reason for removing criticism. Notably, the currently published version of Rule 37 uses narrower language concerning disrespectful, dismissive, or off-topic comments; the available records do not establish when that wording changed.[^2][^5]

**The arrangement presents a structural conflict between ASEAN NOW's role as an immigration-advice community and its commercial obligations to paying sponsors.** Ordinary members face restrictions on business promotion, criticism of sponsors, and public challenges to moderation, while sponsored businesses can advertise directly within discussions where potential customers seek independent recommendations. The documented removal of criticism concerning Thai Visa Centre demonstrates how these policies operated in practice. Although the evidence does not establish that Thai Visa Centre requested the deletions or purchased favorable moderator statements, it establishes that ASEAN NOW's own administrators enforced sponsor-protective restrictions in a discussion about a paying advertiser.[^2][^5]

## May–August 2025 TDAC Promotion and Unsupported Security Claims

On May 24, 2025, ASEAN NOW administrator **CharlieH** published a promotional discussion titled *Apply for your TDAC early - Here*, advertising the Thailand Digital Arrival Card platform operated by [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md), a business associated with [Thai Visa Centre](thai-visa-centre.md). The announcement described the commercial platform as **"our platform"**, promoted advantages over Thailand's official government registration system, and advertised early-submission fees ranging from **US$0.50 to US$8 per applicant**. The promotion appeared directly within ASEAN NOW's immigration-advice forum rather than exclusively through a conventional advertising placement.[^6]

On May 25, forum participant Maestro announced that he had **removed a post containing false information**, along with a reply. However, on July 9, global moderator **BrandonJT** responded to a traveler concerned about the commercial platform's handling of personal information by claiming that submitting information through Thailand's official government TDAC system carried a **"50% chance of being stolen by hackers"**. He attributed this extraordinary probability to earlier government data breaches without identifying any investigation, technical assessment, or statistical evidence supporting the figure.[^6]

Another participant, Maestro, responded that he could not confidently demonstrate that BrandonJT was exaggerating. Neither participant supplied evidence supporting the claimed probability. This is particularly significant because **ASEAN NOW's published Rule 5 prohibits false, inaccurate, misleading, and scaremongering content**, while instructing members to support factual claims with reputable sources. The unsupported numerical allegation about the government's immigration system contrasts sharply with those published standards.[^2][^6]

On August 9, BrandonJT again defended the commercial offering when another member questioned the value of paying for a government procedure available free of charge. He described the approximately **US$8 fee as an optional convenience**, offering examples of travelers who might prefer to arrange submissions earlier. On August 19, Thai Visa Centre's official account, identified as a **Forum Sponsor**, joined the discussion to promote the platform's claimed security safeguards, regulatory compliance, and personal-data deletion capabilities. Thailand's Immigration Bureau, meanwhile, continued providing TDAC registration through its official government portal without charging a submission fee.[^6][^7]

**The episode exposes a discrepancy between ASEAN NOW's stated misinformation standards and the conduct of individuals exercising moderation authority in commercially sponsored discussions.** A moderator made an unsupported claim suggesting that the free government immigration system presented an exceptionally high risk of data theft, while forum administrators and moderators promoted a commercial alternative collecting the same categories of personal information. The surviving discussion does not establish that Thai Visa Centre directed these statements or that the moderator had a financial interest in the service. It does, however, document how unsupported security allegations and favorable commercial messaging appeared together in a sponsored immigration discussion without comparable evidentiary scrutiny.[^2][^6]

## November 2025 Moderator Claims About Retirement Visa Bank Deposits

On November 10, 2025, an ASEAN NOW member using the name **Blueman1** acknowledged using [Thai Visa Centre](thai-visa-centre.md) for retirement extensions because he did not maintain the required money in his Thai bank account. Another participant directly asked whether the agency deposited the required 800,000 baht on customers' behalf. Global moderator **DrJack54** responded: **"All agents deposit 800k. Obtain bank letter showing required balance. The funds then instantly withdrawn."**[^8]

The moderator's description conflicts with Thailand's published financial requirements for annual retirement extensions. Applicants relying on the 800,000-baht bank-deposit route must maintain the required balance for at least **two months before submitting an application and three months after approval**, with a minimum balance of 400,000 baht thereafter. Obtaining a bank certificate after a temporary deposit and immediately withdrawing the funds would not, by itself, satisfy these conditions.[^9]

Other participants questioned the mechanics of the purported arrangement, including how an agent could withdraw money from a customer's personal bank account. The moderator did not supply bank statements, transaction records, immigration documentation, or other evidence establishing that Thai Visa Centre followed the procedure he described. His sweeping assertion that all agents used this method was similarly unsupported.[^8]

The exchange occurred within the same discussion where **Thai Visa Centre participated as a paying Forum Sponsor** and ASEAN NOW administrators had already acknowledged removing criticism of the agency under sponsor-protection rules. Consequently, readers encountered an explanation of potentially noncompliant financial arrangements from a moderator exercising authority over the discussion, while criticism of the commercial sponsor was subject to removal.[^5][^8]

**The incident raises questions about the reliability and independence of immigration advice provided by ASEAN NOW moderators.** The published statements do not establish that Thai Visa Centre actually arranged temporary deposits, that a particular visa was obtained unlawfully, or that the moderator participated in improper transactions. They do establish that a moderator publicly described a practice inconsistent with the financial conditions governing ordinary retirement extensions, without producing evidence or distinguishing it from legitimate immigration procedures.[^8][^9]
