# THNIC

**T.H.NIC Co., Ltd. (THNIC)** provides domain registration services for Thailand's `.th` namespace and handles complaints concerning domain registrations and alleged abuse.

## Earlier Abuse Complaints (2025)

On February 27, 2025, Jesse Nickles submitted an abuse complaint to THNIC concerning three immigration-related domains: [eta.in.th](../domains/eta.in.th.md), [dtv.in.th](../domains/dtv.in.th.md), and [asq.in.th](../domains/asq.in.th.md). He alleged that the first two websites misleadingly presented themselves as official Thai government immigration resources, while `asq.in.th` reproduced names, photographs, and comments from private Facebook groups without authorization. He also reported months of unsolicited visa-related marketing emails, including messages allegedly continuing after recipients requested removal. The complaint questioned the businesses' identities and domain-registration arrangements and explicitly requested that THNIC investigate and consider suspending the reported domains. **No response or investigation outcome from THNIC concerning this February complaint was identified in the available correspondence.**[^8]

On April 13, 2025, Jesse Nickles contacted THNIC concerning allegedly defamatory material published on [tvc.co.th](../domains/tvc.co.th.md) and [asq.in.th](../domains/asq.in.th.md), including accusations of criminal conduct and a purported arrest warrant. On April 17, THNIC representative **Orapin H.** acknowledged receipt and confirmed that the complaint had been forwarded to relevant contacts. However, she did not identify those contacts, explain whether THNIC had initiated an investigation under its domain registration policies, or specify what action would follow.[^1]

Nickles subsequently asked whether THNIC could forward the complaint to Thai police or provide an appropriate police contact. Another representative, [Pimracha Tukjak](../people/pimracha-tukjak.md), directed him to Thailand's Online Complaint Center 1212 and the Technology Crime Suppression Division (TCSD). Neither response disclosed whether THNIC's unnamed contacts had examined the allegations or whether the registrar intended to take any independent action concerning the reported domain use.[^1]

On April 18, THNIC representative [Wassamon Treekul](../people/wassamon-treekul.md) acknowledged a separate complaint concerning [tdac.in.th](../domains/tdac.in.th.md), which Nickles alleged was misleading travelers by presenting itself as an official Thai government resource. Treekul stated that THNIC would review the information and provide updates if further developments occurred. The correspondence confirms THNIC received these complaints but does not establish that it independently verified the allegations or took enforcement action.[^2]

## Further Complaints and Police Escalation (April–May 2025)

On April 15, 2025, Jesse Nickles followed up with THNIC seeking an update on his original complaint concerning [tvc.co.th](../domains/tvc.co.th.md) and [asq.in.th](../domains/asq.in.th.md). On April 17, after representative [Pimracha Tukjak](../people/pimracha-tukjak.md) directed him to the Technology Crime Suppression Division (TCSD), Nickles submitted a complaint directly to that police unit, copying THNIC. He alleged that Thai Visa Centre had published death threats, offered a monetary reward targeting him, and falsely presented material as originating from Thai police. He also supplied an archived copy of the disputed publication.[^6]

On May 1, 2025, Nickles sent another warning to THNIC, ThaiCERT, and TCSD, providing links to public reports of Thai police warnings about fraudulent Thailand Digital Arrival Card websites. He again identified [tdac.in.th](../domains/tdac.in.th.md), [dtv.in.th](../domains/dtv.in.th.md), [eta.in.th](../domains/eta.in.th.md), and [asq.in.th](../domains/asq.in.th.md), alleging government impersonation, misleading domain registrations, unauthorized publication of personal photographs, and unsolicited commercial advertising.[^7]

**These follow-ups demonstrate that THNIC received additional allegations and supporting references after its initial April 2025 responses.** Nickles had also followed its recommendation to approach Thai law enforcement directly, while continuing to request action concerning the reported domains. The available THNIC correspondence contains no substantive response to the May 1 warning or explanation of whether the additional information prompted an investigation or enforcement decision.[^6][^7]

### Unanswered Domain Registration and Identity Questions

Nickles's April–May 2025 complaints raised concerns extending beyond website content. He alleged that `tdac.in.th` and related immigration-themed websites used personal WHOIS registration details while conducting commercial activities under inadequately identified business names and presenting themselves as official Thai government resources. His May 1 follow-up specifically challenged THNIC's continued registration of these domains despite the reported conduct.[^2][^7]

THNIC's Domain Name Registration Policy distinguishes between the registered domain holder and the person authorized to manage a domain. Although Section 5.3.2.2 permits eligible individuals to register `.in.th` domains, **Section 16 expressly authorizes suspension or revocation when false registration information is discovered and verified**. Section 18.3 additionally requires registration information to be accurate and treats false information as a violation of the registration agreement.[^5]

Despite receiving complaints concerning the identity and representations of the businesses behind these websites, **THNIC provided no documented explanation of whether it verified the domain holders, examined the accuracy of their registration information, or considered the relevant policy provisions**. Its response also did not address the distinction between eligibility to register an `.in.th` domain and the subsequent use of that domain to suggest affiliation with Thai government immigration services.[^2][^5][^7]

## October–November 2026 Domain Abuse Complaint

On October 21, 2026, Jesse Nickles submitted another complaint concerning [tvc.co.th](../domains/tvc.co.th.md), alleging the unauthorized publication of private photographs and defamatory material. In subsequent correspondence, he alleged that corrupt Thai immigration officers had unlawfully leaked photographs from restricted airport CCTV surveillance systems to [Chad Scira](../people/chad-scira.md), who manipulated them using AI and published them to harass and defame him.[^3]

THNIC representative [Suthida Tepaloon](../people/suthida-tepaloon.md) initially directed Nickles to Thailand's Online Complaint Center 1212, operated under the Ministry of Digital Economy and Society, and the Technology Crime Suppression Division (TCSD) of the Royal Thai Police. After Nickles cited THNIC's own registration policies, she confirmed on October 22 that the complaint would be reviewed under those rules. On October 29, she stated that THNIC had notified the domain registrant and requested that the disputed content be reviewed and updated. On November 5, she confirmed that THNIC's executive management and Legal Team were jointly reviewing the matter.[^3]

On November 10, Tepaloon stated that the domain's registration complied with THNIC's policies because it matched the registered company name [TVC. Visa Agent Co., Ltd.](../companies/tvc-visa-agent-co-ltd.md). She maintained that THNIC lacked authority to adjudicate website content disputes and would not suspend the domain without an explicit court or competent government order. Nickles challenged this interpretation, citing Sections 12.4, 12.5, 14.1.4.2, and 14.2 of THNIC's Domain Name Registration Policy, which address investigations and action concerning bad-faith domain use. As of November 12, THNIC had not provided a substantive response explaining why those provisions were inapplicable or confirming whether the registrant had complied with its earlier request.[^4][^5]

## Enforcement Powers and Unanswered Policy Questions

THNIC's November 10, 2026 response raises questions about how it interprets and applies its own Domain Name Registration Policy. Although THNIC stated that it could not suspend `tvc.co.th` without an explicit court or competent government order, Sections 12.4 and 12.5 expressly authorize the registrar to investigate complaints concerning domain use and, in specified circumstances, suspend services. Section 12.11 requires domain holders to act in good faith, while Sections 14.1.4.2 and 14.2 provide additional authority concerning domains used in bad faith.[^4][^5]

THNIC's conclusion that `tvc.co.th` was properly registered under TVC Visa Agent Co., Ltd. addressed the domain's eligibility for registration but did not resolve the separate question of its subsequent use. THNIC had initially agreed to review Nickles's complaint under its registration policies, later confirmed that its executive management and Legal Team were examining the matter, and requested that the registrant review the disputed material. Nevertheless, its final response did not identify which bad-faith provisions had been considered, whether the registrant had responded, or why the registrar's discretionary enforcement powers were considered unavailable.[^3][^4]

The policy also contains provisions governing dispute resolution, including Section 15.2, which contemplates awaiting a court judgment or another dispute-resolution process. However, THNIC did not explain how it reconciled that provision with its separate authority to investigate and act against bad-faith domain use. Nickles explicitly requested that explanation on November 10, but no substantive answer had been received as of November 12.[^4][^5]

Similar questions arise from the April 2025 complaint concerning `tdac.in.th`. Nickles alleged that the website was misleading travelers about its relationship with the Thai government and collecting personal information. Section 17 of THNIC's policy expressly addresses phishing and other information-security threats. Although representative Wassamon Treekul acknowledged the complaint and said THNIC would review it, the available correspondence does not establish whether THNIC assessed those allegations under Section 17, reached a determination, or took enforcement action.[^2][^5]

### Repeated Referrals to External Authorities

THNIC's handling of the 2025 and 2026 complaints reveals a recurring response. On April 17, 2025, [Pimracha Tukjak](../people/pimracha-tukjak.md) directed Nickles to Thailand's Online Complaint Center 1212 and the Technology Crime Suppression Division (TCSD) after he reported allegedly defamatory material on `tvc.co.th` and `asq.in.th`. On October 21, 2026, [Suthida Tepaloon](../people/suthida-tepaloon.md) referred him to those same two authorities after receiving a new complaint concerning unauthorized photographs and allegedly defamatory material on `tvc.co.th`.[^1][^3]

Despite the eighteen-month interval and involvement of different representatives, both initial responses directed the complainant toward external authorities without addressing THNIC's own enforcement powers. In October 2026, THNIC agreed to review the allegations under its registration policies only after Nickles explicitly cited Sections 12.4 and 12.5. The recurrence is significant because **referring suspected criminal conduct to government authorities does not itself answer whether the registrar has independently assessed possible violations of its domain registration policies**.[^3][^5]
