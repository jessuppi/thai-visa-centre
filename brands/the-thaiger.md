# The Thaiger

The Thaiger is a Thailand-based English- and Thai-language digital news publisher. In April 2024, it acquired ASEAN NOW, formerly ThaiVisa.com, expanding its operations into one of Thailand's largest English-language expatriate discussion communities.[^1]

## 2024 Acquisition of ASEAN NOW and Cross-Platform Advertising

On April 19, 2024, The Thaiger announced its acquisition of **ASEAN NOW from Choice Group Asia Co., Ltd.**, placing two prominent English-language news and immigration-information platforms within the same media operation. The acquisition announcement described a combined audience of approximately **5 million monthly users**, expanding the publisher's influence over information consumed by foreigners living in or traveling to Thailand.[^1]

On May 24, 2024, The Thaiger began advertising **joint promotional packages starting at 30,000 baht**, offering coordinated exposure across both publications. Advertisers could purchase pinned ASEAN NOW forum discussions, newsletter placements, sponsored announcements, promotional articles, social-media distribution, and press-release publication on The Thaiger. These arrangements explicitly commercialized access to both editorial-style publications and community discussions within the same media business.[^2]

During 2025, both platforms promoted commercial immigration services associated with [Thai Visa Centre](thai-visa-centre.md) and [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md). On June 13, The Thaiger published a press release advertising the company's paid TDAC early-submission service, using the descriptions **"Official TDAC form"** and **"Official TDAC information page"** for privately operated websites. Another promotional article appeared on September 24, followed by a November 11 publication defending the company's commercial TDAC platform, criticizing competing providers, and circulating disputed criminal allegations against critic Jesse Nickles.[^3][^4][^6]

Meanwhile, Thai Visa Centre participated in ASEAN NOW immigration discussions under the designation **"Forum Sponsor."** On November 5, 2025, administrator Captain Flack explicitly confirmed removing a post criticizing the sponsor, citing Rule 37, which prohibited negative comments concerning forum advertisers and sponsors. This provides direct evidence that moderators suppressed criticism of a commercial sponsor within a platform owned by the same media business publishing favorable material concerning that sponsor's associated services.[^5]

**The relationship raises significant questions about editorial independence and commercial influence.** The Thaiger publicly marketed coordinated advertising across both platforms, while its news website distributed promotional immigration material and its affiliated forum enforced rules protecting advertisers from criticism. The available evidence does not establish that Thai Visa Centre purchased a specific joint advertising package or directed The Thaiger's editorial decisions. Nevertheless, the documented combination of shared ownership, paid promotional opportunities, and sponsor-protective moderation presents a significant conflict-of-interest concern for readers seeking independent immigration advice.[^1][^2][^5][^6]

## September 2026 Editorial Standards and Commercial Immigration Coverage

The Thaiger's published Editorial Standards, last reviewed in September 2026, establish commitments concerning editorial independence, advertising transparency, factual accuracy, corrections, and rights of reply.[^7]

These commitments are particularly relevant to its previously documented promotion of commercial immigration services operated by AGENTS CO., LTD.

### Commercial Disclosures Appear After Promotional Content

An examination of three existing publications on November 25, 2026, identified the following presentation:

| Publication date | Article category | Disclosure at end |
| --- | --- | --- |
| June 13, 2025 | Thailand Travel | Press release |
| September 24, 2025 | Thailand Travel | Sponsored |
| November 11, 2025 | Visa Information | Press Release |

All three publications use the generic Thaiger byline rather than identifying an individual journalist or the commercial organization responsible for preparing the material.[^3][^4][^6]

Although the articles contain commercial-content disclosures, those disclosures appear after the substantive text rather than prominently beside the headline.

This presentation is particularly consequential for the November 11 article, whose headline presents the publication as general consumer-protection guidance while its contents extensively promote AGENTS' services and criticize competing operators.

### The Publisher's Own Editorial Commitments

The September 2026 standards promise to distinguish sponsored material from news coverage, disclose relevant commercial relationships, and attribute unverified claims to their sources.[^7]

They also establish procedures for offering criticized parties an opportunity to respond and correcting inaccurate information.

The three immigration publications predate the latest review of these standards. Their continued availability, however, raises questions about whether the publisher has assessed its historical promotional content against its current editorial commitments.

### Singapore Advertising Disclosure Standards

The Advertising Standards Authority of Singapore establishes principles for distinguishing commercial advertisements from editorial material through the Singapore Code of Advertising Practice and associated digital-marketing guidelines.[^8]

These standards provide a relevant benchmark because The Thaiger identifies its Singapore-registered company as the publisher responsible for its operations.

A disclosure appearing only after an extensive promotional article may not provide readers with the same immediate understanding of its commercial nature as a prominent disclosure beside the headline.

The available evidence does not establish that Singapore's advertising regulator has assessed these particular publications or determined that they violate applicable standards.

### Editorial Accountability Following the Ownership Change

The Thaiger's 2026 ownership transfer to Dot Property Pte. Ltd. provides additional context for reviewing historical commercial publications.

The current publisher should clarify whether previously published promotional immigration articles have undergone an editorial review, whether their commercial relationships are adequately disclosed, and whether disputed factual assertions have been independently examined.

**The issue is not the mere publication of sponsored material, but whether readers can readily distinguish commercial immigration promotions from independently researched consumer guidance.**

### Advertising Verification Versus Contractual Disclaimers

The Thaiger's separate Content and Advertising Guidelines establish mandatory editorial review of commercial material before publication.[^18]

Section 5 requires advertisers to provide valid documentary evidence of applicable licences and permits. It also authorizes the publisher to remove misleading advertisements and report serious violations to relevant authorities.

However, Section 2.1 of The Thaiger's Terms of Use expressly disclaims verification, endorsement, and responsibility for third-party content, including advertisements.[^10]

**The publisher therefore promises editorial scrutiny in its advertising guidelines while disclaiming verification of advertising content in its agreement with readers.**

### Application to AGENTS' Promotional Claims

The Thaiger's June 13, 2025 promotional article concerning AGENTS' commercial TDAC platform claimed that more than 99% of customers received their arrival cards without delay during a government-system outage. It also asserted full compliance with Thailand's Personal Data Protection Act.[^3]

Its September 24, 2025 sponsored article similarly claimed that AGENTS' customer information was hosted on ISO 27001-certified infrastructure and that its telecommunications integration guaranteed access to Thailand's fastest networks.[^4]

Neither article identifies an independent performance assessment, security auditor, certification number, or regulatory determination supporting these assurances.

The distinction between certified hosting infrastructure and certification of the commercial application itself is also left unexplained.

These claims concern the reliability and security of a private intermediary collecting travelers' passport information and immigration declarations.

Under The Thaiger's currently published advertising guidelines, such material is subject to editorial review rather than automatic acceptance of advertiser-supplied assurances.

The publisher should clarify what documentary evidence supported these promotional claims, whether its current advertising standards have been applied retrospectively to existing publications, and how its editorial review requirements are reconciled with its contractual disclaimers.

**Readers should not mistake the publication of commercial security and compliance assurances by a news organization for independent verification of those assurances.**

## June 2019 Investigation Into Undisclosed Visa-Agent Commissions

On June 13, 2019, The Thaiger published an article examining the commercial network surrounding Thai visa applications in Penang, Malaysia.[^9]

The article documented the experience of an anonymized applicant who reportedly spent more than 40,000 baht on a visa application process that could have been completed independently for substantially less.

According to the applicant, his case had passed through four additional agents, each collecting a commission.

The publisher criticized the resulting network of undisclosed intermediaries, additional charges, expensive accommodation packages, and commercial exploitation of inconsistent immigration requirements.

It also warned readers that advice supplied by visa agents could reflect their own financial interests rather than those of their customers.

### Contrast With Subsequent Commercial Activities

The Thaiger subsequently incorporated visa referrals into its own commercial operations.

Its published Terms of Use expressly identify visa services among its third-party referral programs, while disclaiming responsibility for the quality, accuracy, legality, and outcomes of services provided by those businesses.[^10]

Its 2026 Destination Thailand Visa guide actively solicits prospective applicants' contact information for referral to an immigration specialist whose identity is not specified on the enquiry form.[^11]

**The publisher has therefore transitioned from investigating undisclosed commercial intermediaries within Thailand's immigration industry to operating its own immigration referral service.**

Commercial referrals are not inherently improper. However, the publisher's earlier reporting provides an important benchmark against which to evaluate its own transparency concerning referral compensation, intermediary relationships, and commercial recommendations.

The 2019 article does not identify Thai Visa Centre as a participant in the reported commission arrangements, and its allegations concerning individual agents have not been independently verified.

## Historical Visa Agency Partnerships and Undisclosed Referral Recipients

The Thaiger's commercial involvement in immigration services predates its acquisition of ASEAN NOW and subsequent promotion of AGENTS CO., LTD.

### 2021 Partnership With Tadoo and TMT Visa Services

On August 25 and September 1, 2021, sponsored articles authored by Ann Carter promoted business and Thailand Elite visa services through The Thaiger's commercial partner Tadoo.[^12][^13]

On November 3, 2021, The Thaiger announced a partnership between Tadoo and TMT Visa Services Phuket involving education visa packages.[^14]

Tadoo CEO Sapir Maton publicly endorsed TMT's inclusion in the company's service-provider network and expressed plans to introduce additional products through the partnership.

On April 11, 2022, The Thaiger published another promotional article extensively advertising TMT's immigration services, using first-person language from the visa agency itself.[^15]

These publications establish a historical commercial relationship connecting the publisher, a comparison and referral platform, and a specific immigration agency.

### Confidential Immigration Inquiries in 2026

The Thaiger's current overstay guide solicits names, email addresses, telephone numbers, and descriptions of applicants' immigration circumstances.[^16]

The form describes the assistance as confidential and identifies the intended recipient only as a vetted specialist partner.

However, neither this form nor the equivalent Destination Thailand Visa enquiry form identifies the receiving agency or explains the criteria used to vet its qualifications.[^11]

The publisher's Privacy Policy also permits automated or manual analysis of communications between users and service providers for purposes including product development and regulatory compliance.[^17]

These provisions are particularly relevant when prospective customers disclose potentially incriminating immigration circumstances under assurances of confidentiality.

### Commercial Accountability

The publisher's historical articles explicitly identified Tadoo and TMT as commercial partners. Its current enquiry forms provide less transparency concerning the identities of participating immigration agencies.

The reviewed forms do not disclose whether the publisher receives referral commissions, fixed lead-generation payments, or other compensation.

The available evidence does not establish that Tadoo, TMT, Thai Visa Centre, or Issa Compass currently receives these inquiries.

Nevertheless, prospective customers should be able to identify the businesses receiving their personal immigration information and understand the commercial arrangements underlying the publisher's recommendations.

## Thaiger AI and Automated Commercial Customer Acquisition

The Thaiger's commercial activities extend beyond advertising, sponsored publications, and conventional customer referrals.

Its associated technology business, `thaiger.ai`, explicitly identifies its origins within Thaiger Media's technology department and lists Michael Kenner as a board member.[^19]

Its Privacy Policy identifies [The Thaiger Pte. Ltd.](../companies/the-thaiger-pte-ltd.md) as the website's owner and operator.[^20]

### Integration With Associated Property Businesses

Thaiger AI publishes several case studies documenting its commercial and technical relationships with businesses associated with The Thaiger's wider corporate network.[^21]

| Business | Advertised services |
| --- | --- |
| Dot Property | Automated WhatsApp customer engagement and appointment scheduling |
| FazWaz | Automated telephone calls, recorded responses, and property database updates |
| AA Insurance Brokers | Automated customer acquisition combined with Thaiger Media sponsorship |

These arrangements predate The Thaiger's disclosed 2026 acquisition by Dot Property.

The case studies demonstrate that the publisher's commercial operations include direct involvement in customer communications and transaction-related workflows, rather than merely selling advertising space.

### Integration of Media Sponsorship and Customer Acquisition

The AA Insurance Brokers case study explicitly describes combining automated appointment generation with sponsored exposure through Thaiger Media.

Thaiger AI separately promotes its proprietary ThaigerGo customer-management platform, which facilitates automated customer classification, personalized communications, appointment scheduling, and advertising optimization.[^22]

Its advertised target industries include healthcare, finance, and legal services.

**The publisher therefore operates an integrated commercial infrastructure combining media exposure, customer acquisition, automated communications, and service-provider referrals.**

### Data Protection and Commercial Accountability

Thaiger AI's Privacy Policy, last updated December 10, 2024, describes collecting contact information, technical usage records, and customer communications.[^20]

However, it does not specifically explain the retention and processing arrangements governing automated telephone recordings, AI-generated customer profiles, or customer information processed on behalf of commercial clients.

These omissions are relevant given The Thaiger's subsequent introduction of immigration enquiry forms and account-associated immigration records.

The reviewed documents do not establish that Thaiger AI processes immigration inquiries on behalf of Thai Visa Centre or AGENTS CO., LTD.

Nevertheless, they establish a broader commercial infrastructure capable of connecting publishing operations with automated customer acquisition and service delivery.

## Commercial Influence Over Search Rankings and AI-Generated Recommendations

On July 2, 2026, The Thaiger published an article explaining how businesses can increase their visibility within AI-generated recommendations, specifically identifying ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini.[^23]

The publication recommends high placements in listing advertorials and repeated coverage through press releases published by reputable third-party websites.

The article expressly identifies commercial rankings as a mechanism for influencing the frequency with which artificial intelligence systems recommend particular businesses.

### Manufacturing the Appearance of Independent Consensus

The Thaiger's article explains that repeated favorable coverage across multiple publications can increase the perceived credibility of commercial information within AI-generated responses.

This strategy is particularly relevant to The Thaiger's ownership of ASEAN NOW.

The publisher can distribute favorable commercial material through its news website, discussion forums, newsletters, and associated social media channels.

These publications may subsequently appear to search engines and AI systems as multiple sources supporting the same commercial claims, despite originating from coordinated promotional arrangements.

### Selling Community Authority and Search-Ranking Links

On September 4, 2025, The Thaiger published an advertising guide promoting ASEAN NOW's commercial partnership packages.[^24]

The advertised benefits included:

- Pinned advertorials within relevant discussion forums.
- Sponsored accounts with opportunities to answer community questions.
- Premium business-directory placements.
- Social media and newsletter promotions.
- Do-follow backlinks intended to improve search-engine rankings.

The publisher explicitly marketed sponsored status using the phrase:

> Become a trusted voice within the community.

This establishes that perceived community authority is itself presented as a commercially purchasable benefit.

### Conflict With Google's Published Search Policies

Google's published spam policies prohibit purchasing or selling links primarily to manipulate search rankings.[^25]

Paid advertising links remain permissible when appropriately qualified using sponsored or nofollow attributes.

Google also distinguishes legitimate advertorial publication from commercial content hosted primarily to exploit an established website's search-ranking reputation.

ASEAN NOW's explicit promotion of paid do-follow backlinks for ranking purposes therefore warrants examination under these policies.

### Relevance to Thai Visa Centre

These commercial strategies are particularly significant given the previously documented promotion of Thai Visa Centre and AGENTS CO., LTD. across both media platforms, alongside moderation rules restricting criticism of commercial sponsors.

**The Thaiger explicitly markets techniques intended to influence search-engine rankings, AI-generated recommendations, and perceived authority within online communities.**

The combination of favorable commercial coverage and restricted criticism creates a risk that search engines and AI systems encounter a disproportionately favorable representation of sponsoring businesses.

The available evidence does not establish that Thai Visa Centre purchased a specific AI optimization campaign or that Google has imposed penalties against either publication.

### Contradictory Positions on AI-Generated Information

In its November 11, 2025 press release defending AGENTS CO., LTD., The Thaiger explicitly attributed unfavorable AI-generated information concerning the company to online criticism.[^6]

The publication claimed that the volume of critical posts had caused travelers and artificial intelligence systems to misunderstand the company's commercial activities.

However, The Thaiger's July 2, 2026 marketing guide subsequently encouraged businesses to use repeated favorable press coverage and prominent advertorial rankings to influence AI-generated recommendations.[^23]

**The publisher portrays repeated unfavorable online coverage as a source of AI misinformation while promoting repeated favorable commercial coverage as an effective marketing strategy.**

This contradiction is particularly significant given ASEAN NOW's documented restrictions on criticism of commercial sponsors.

### Google News Distribution and Sponsorship Transparency

The November 2025 press release appears within The Thaiger's Visa Information category, uses the publisher's generic byline, and displays a Google News subscription link.

Its press-release disclosure appears after the substantive promotional content and disputed allegations against a critic.

Google News expressly prohibits presenting sponsored content as independent editorial material and requires clear disclosure of financial, ownership, and affiliate interests.[^26]

Google may exclude noncompliant publications from its news surfaces.

Although the existence of a Google News subscription link does not establish that a particular article appeared within Google News, the publisher's presentation raises questions about how commercial immigration promotions are distinguished from independent reporting within news aggregation and AI-generated information systems.
