# Atthaphon Sirilatthayakorn

Police Lieutenant Colonel Atthaphon Sirilatthayakorn (อรรถพล ศิริลัทธยากร) is a Royal Thai Police officer who served as head of Chainat Provincial Immigration. A January 2023 Immigration Bureau transfer order assigned him to Chainat from the investigation and suppression division of Immigration Division 2. Official Chainat Immigration records confirm that he remained in charge in December 2024 and January 2025, when he oversaw meetings concerning the office's annual integrity and transparency assessment.[^1][^2]

## Family and Business Interests

Atthaphon is a son of Itthi Sirilatthayakorn, a former Thai parliamentarian and deputy minister, and a brother of politician Atthakorn Sirilatthayakorn, who has held several ministerial positions.[^3][^4]

In January 2025, investigative outlet Isra News reported that Atthaphon held a 15% ownership stake in Atthasiri Co., Ltd. (บริษัทอรรถศิริ จำกัด), a family-owned real estate company in Chachoengsao Province, as of April 30, 2024. His brothers Atthakan and Atthaphong held the remaining 85%, with stakes of 45% and 40%, respectively.[^3]

Isra News also obtained a loan agreement dated July 10, 2015, documenting a 19-million-baht loan from their father to Atthasiri Co., Ltd. The agreement identified Atthakan as representing the borrowing company and specified that the money would be used to purchase land for subdivision and resale. It provided for no interest between July 10 and December 31, 2015, with repayment due upon the lender's demand. The loan remained listed in their father's September 2024 asset declaration, nearly a decade after the agreement was signed. Atthaphon was a shareholder but was not identified as a party to the contract.[^3][^5]

On January 27, 2025, Isra News published a further investigation into Atthasiri Co., Ltd.'s financial statements filed with Thailand's Department of Business Development. The outlet reported that the company had recorded no revenue from 2015 through 2023, with a net loss of 14,630 baht in 2023. Its latest accounts also listed 19.2 million baht in interest-free short-term loans from a company director and a real estate development project valued at approximately 20.08 million baht. The report did not establish that Atthaphon personally provided any of those loans.[^20]

## Family Political Positions and Financial Disclosures (2011–2026)

In January 2025, investigative outlet Isra News examined an asset declaration submitted by Atthaphon's father, Itthi Sirilatthayakorn, following his appointment as deputy minister of agriculture and cooperatives on September 6, 2024. Itthi and his wife declared combined assets of 225,894,914 baht and liabilities of 16,266,106 baht. Itthi's reported assets included 92 land plots in Chonburi and Chachoengsao provinces valued at approximately 165.16 million baht, along with 157 buildings and structures, including a hotel, cinema, market, and convenience store. The declaration also included the previously documented 19-million-baht loan to Atthasiri Co., Ltd., in which Atthaphon held a 15% ownership stake.[^3][^28]

Atthaphon's brother, Atthakorn Sirilatthayakorn (อรรถกร ศิริลัทธยากร), began his parliamentary career in 2011, winning election as a Pheu Thai Party-list candidate ranked 57th. Before entering politics, he studied communication arts at Bangkok University and marketing management at Middlesex University in the United Kingdom and worked in private business. In the 2019 general election, he switched to the Palang Pracharath Party, winning another parliamentary seat as its 18th-ranked party-list candidate.[^4]

Atthakorn subsequently became an assistant secretary to the government's parliamentary coordination committee and served on parliamentary budget committees. In June 2021, he joined Palang Pracharath's executive committee and became the party's spokesperson. In the 2023 general election, he won Chachoengsao Constituency 2, transitioning from party-list representation to a directly elected constituency seat.[^4][^31]

His first ministerial appointment came in April 2024, when he became deputy minister of agriculture and cooperatives under Prime Minister Srettha Thavisin. Following a cabinet reshuffle later that year, his father, Itthi Sirilatthayakorn, assumed the deputy minister position in September 2024. This placed both father and son in the same ministerial portfolio at different stages of the government's tenure.[^4][^28]

On July 1, 2025, another cabinet reshuffle removed Itthi from his deputy minister position while appointing Atthakorn as the new minister of agriculture and cooperatives under Prime Minister Paetongtarn Shinawatra. Atthakorn therefore returned to the ministry in a more senior position previously occupied by other political figures, including Thammanat Prompao. During a July 4 policy briefing, Atthakorn publicly credited Thammanat's support for his return and described his agricultural program as continuing policies developed during Thammanat's earlier leadership.[^40][^41]

On July 22, 2025, Itthi was appointed director of the Kla Tham Party under Party Order No. 11/2568, signed by party leader Narumon Pinyosinwat. Itthi had previously represented Chachoengsao in Parliament for five terms between 1992 and 2007 and served as deputy minister of transport before returning to government as deputy minister of agriculture and cooperatives in 2024. His appointment to the party leadership established his continued involvement in national politics after leaving his ministerial position.[^29]

Despite leaving the cabinet, Itthi continued participating in the ministry's activities during his son's tenure. An official Department of Agriculture report dated September 3, 2025, identified Itthi as chairman of the advisory committee to the minister of agriculture and cooperatives. The report concerned agricultural-export policies being pursued under Minister Atthakorn and confirms that the father held an advisory role while his son headed the ministry.[^42]

Atthakorn subsequently became minister of tourism and sports in September 2025 under Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul, remaining in that portfolio until March 2026. After leaving the cabinet, he continued as a Chachoengsao parliamentarian. On April 28, 2026, Kla Tham Party leader Thammanat Prompao appointed him party spokesperson under Order No. 4/2569, a position he continued holding in October 2026.[^30][^31][^38][^39]

The Sirilatthayakorn family's involvement in the Kla Tham Party continued into 2026. Following a party leadership reorganization on April 28, 2026, Thailand's Election Commission listed Atthaphon's father, Itthi Sirilatthayakorn, as a serving member of the party's executive committee. An October 2, 2026 report in *Ban Muang* additionally identified Itthi as a deputy leader of the party.[^36][^37]

The father and son also appeared together in public political activities. On October 2, 2026, *Ban Muang* reported that Itthi and Atthakorn visited flood-affected communities in Chachoengsao Province, helping organize relief supplies and temporary assistance facilities. The report identified Itthi as deputy party leader and Atthakorn as a Kla Tham parliamentarian.[^37]

## Atthasiri Company: Updated Financial Filings (2024–2025)

Corporate-data provider Creden identifies Atthasiri Co., Ltd. (บริษัทอรรถศิริ จำกัด), registration number 0245558000234, as an active real estate company incorporated on January 26, 2015. Its updated listing identifies financial statement submissions for 2024 and 2025, registered capital of 1 million baht, and shareholders' equity of 854,690 baht, equivalent to approximately 85.47% of registered capital.[^32]

These filings extend beyond the financial statements examined by Isra News in January 2025. That investigation found that Atthasiri had reported no revenue from 2015 through 2023, while carrying approximately 19.2 million baht in interest-free loans from a company director and a real estate development project valued at approximately 20.08 million baht. Atthaphon Sirilatthayakorn was identified as a 15% shareholder in the family business.[^20]

The newer financial statements could clarify whether the company subsequently generated revenue, repaid any of its outstanding loans, or changed its ownership structure. Creden's publicly accessible summary confirms the additional filing years but does not disclose those detailed financial figures.[^32]

## Immigration Career and Departure from Chainat

An earlier Royal Thai Police personnel order dated July 21, 2017, identifies Atthaphon as a staff officer attached to the commander of Immigration Division 2. The order transferred him to Pathum Thani Provincial Immigration as a deputy inspector under Immigration Division 3, establishing another documented posting before his subsequent assignments to Nakhon Pathom and Chainat.[^8]

An *Inspector 234* training yearbook also lists Atthaphon as a police captain and deputy inspector at Nakhon Pathom Provincial Immigration, with a portrait in uniform.[^12]

A November 30, 2021 Immigration Bureau personnel order identifies Atthaphon as a deputy inspector at Nakhon Pathom Provincial Immigration and records his transfer to an inspector position in the Investigation and Suppression Division of Immigration Division 2. He remained there until his January 2023 reassignment to Chainat Provincial Immigration.[^1][^6]

A Chainat Immigration expenditure plan for the first two quarters of fiscal 2024, reporting data through March 31, 2024, identifies him as the provincial immigration inspector in its verification section.[^13]

Official Chainat Immigration records identify Atthaphon Sirilatthayakorn as the provincial immigration inspector on January 9, 2025, when he chaired a meeting concerning the office's annual Integrity and Transparency Assessment.[^2] His successor was Police Lieutenant Colonel Chanaiyaporn Chatphum (ชไนยพร ฉัตรภูมิ), who had previously served as an inspector at Surat Thani Provincial Immigration. An official Surat Thani Immigration report identifies Chatphum in that position as late as January 29, 2025, when she participated in an immigration enforcement operation.[^34]

By March 6, 2025, Chatphum was heading Chainat Provincial Immigration and chairing its transparency-assessment meetings.[^7] She continued in that position throughout 2025 and remained the provincial immigration inspector in April 2026, according to Chainat Immigration's subsequent announcements and published leadership roster.[^35] These records narrow the leadership transition to early 2025 and establish that Chatphum was Atthaphon's replacement.

Atthaphon's next documented posting was the Bangkok Port Immigration Checkpoint. On May 27, 2025, the Duang Prateep Foundation identified him as an immigration inspector stationed at Bangkok Port when he and fellow officers visited the foundation to donate rice and adult diapers.[^15] This is the latest independently documented posting located for Atthaphon; no subsequent transfer or appointment has been established from publicly available records reviewed through November 2026.

## Police Rank Progression (2017–2025)

Royal Thai Police personnel records document Atthaphon Sirilatthayakorn's advancement from police captain to police lieutenant colonel over approximately eight years. On July 21, 2017, Royal Thai Police Order No. 409/2560 identified him as a police captain serving as a staff officer attached to the commander of Immigration Division 2. The order reassigned him as a deputy inspector at Pathum Thani Provincial Immigration.[^8]

On November 30, 2021, Immigration Bureau Order No. 291/2564 continued to identify Atthaphon as a police captain, this time serving as a deputy inspector at Nakhon Pathom Provincial Immigration. The order transferred him to an inspector position in the Investigation and Suppression Division of Immigration Division 2.[^6]

By January 31, 2023, Immigration Bureau Order No. 27/2566 identified him as an acting police major (ว่าที่ พ.ต.ต.) when he was transferred to head Chainat Provincial Immigration. Chainat Immigration records subsequently identified him as an acting police lieutenant colonel (ว่าที่ พ.ต.ท.) in December 2024 and January 2025.[^1][^2][^23]

On May 27, 2025, the Duang Prateep Foundation identified Atthaphon as a police lieutenant colonel serving at the Bangkok Port Immigration Checkpoint. Together, these records establish his progression from captain in 2017–2021 to major by 2023 and lieutenant colonel by 2025.[^15]

## Academic Research and Publications

In addition to his immigration-police career, Atthaphon Sirilatthayakorn has contributed to academic research in public administration. In July 2020, the *Research Community and Social Development Journal* published a study he co-authored examining how Thai organizations could become learning organizations within the country's cultural context. The journal identified him as affiliated with Rattana Bundit University.[^21]

In December 2022, he co-authored another paper, "Applying Qualitative Research in Public Administration," published in the *Journal of the Public Administration Association of Thailand*. The paper identified him as "Pol. Maj. Dr. Artthapol Sirilatthayakorn," affiliated with Rattana Bundit University, and provided an official Royal Thai Police email address.[^22]


On July 7, 2023, the *Journal of the Public Administration Association of Thailand* published a further study co-authored by Atthaphon, Jamnean Joungtrakul, and Klahan Na-Nan. The paper examined the application of systematic literature reviews to public administration research in Thailand, particularly their potential use in government development planning, policy implementation, and evaluation. The journal again identified Atthaphon as affiliated with Rattana Bundit University.[^26]

In December 2023, the *Journal of MCU Social Science Review* published "Creating Sound Labor Relations to Prevent and Settlement of Labor Dispute," listing Artthapol Sirilatthayakorn among five co-authors, including Jamnean Joungtrakul, who collaborated with him on earlier public administration research. The paper examined workplace conflicts in Thailand and proposed a 12-step approach to preventing and resolving labor disputes. Although the English author name matches Atthaphon's other publications, the journal uses a different Thai spelling of his surname, leaving some uncertainty about the identification.[^27]
## May 2024: Anti-Trafficking Operations in Chainat

On May 15, 2024, while serving as head of Chainat Provincial Immigration, Atthaphon directed officers from its investigation and suppression unit to participate in the province's eighth labor anti-trafficking committee meeting of the year. The meeting was held at the provincial Ministry of Labour government complex, and officers also inspected two businesses considered potentially at risk in Wat Sing District. Chainat Immigration reported that neither inspection uncovered any violations. The announcement identifies Atthaphon as supervising the operation but does not establish whether he personally attended the meeting or inspections.[^17]

## December 2024: Joint Immigration Enforcement Operation

On December 17, 2024, while Atthaphon headed Chainat Provincial Immigration, he was named among officers involved in a joint immigration enforcement operation in Si Prachan District, Suphan Buri. According to *Ban Muang*, highway police stopped a van on Highway 340 after noticing apparent mechanical problems and discovered eight undocumented migrants inside. The operation involved immigration personnel from Chainat, Suphan Buri, and Sing Buri, along with highway police.[^16]

Police arrested the driver on suspicion of facilitating unlawful entry into Thailand and detained the eight migrants for further proceedings. Although the report identifies Atthaphon among the participating officers, it does not specify his individual role in the initial stop or subsequent arrests.[^16]

## August 2024: Chainat Immigration Transparency Assessment Results

On August 16, 2024, Thailand's National Anti-Corruption Commission (NACC) announced the results of its fiscal 2024 Integrity and Transparency Assessment (ITA). Chainat Provincial Immigration, headed by Atthaphon Sirilatthayakorn, received a score of 99.27 out of 100, ranking third nationally among provincial immigration offices and immigration checkpoints. The office received the assessment's highest rating, *Excellent Pass* (ผ่านดีเยี่ยม).[^33]

Uthai Thani Immigration ranked first with 99.41 points, followed by Samut Sakhon Immigration with 99.33 points. Chainat's result exceeded the Immigration Bureau's overall score of 95.88 points. The assessment was conducted during Atthaphon's tenure as provincial immigration inspector, approximately five months before his departure from Chainat in early 2025.[^33]

## December 2024–January 2025: Transparency Oversight

On December 12, 2024, Atthaphon chaired a meeting at Chainat Provincial Immigration to prepare for the office's fiscal 2025 Integrity and Transparency Assessment (ITA). The office issued Order No. 31/2567 establishing a working group to oversee the assessment.[^23]

On January 8, 2025, Atthaphon publicized the assessment's external-stakeholder questionnaire (EIT) to visitors receiving services at Chainat Immigration. The following day, he conducted an internal briefing to review assessment requirements, identify areas needing improvement, assign responsibilities, and inform officers about the internal-stakeholder questionnaire (IIT). These activities documented his involvement in both the public-facing and internal components of the assessment shortly before his departure from Chainat.[^2][^23]

The accompanying plan called for monthly publication of immigration enforcement and service statistics, bribery-risk assessments, public feedback channels, and improved information about immigration services, including electronic extension applications. These measures were documented during Atthaphon's final months heading Chainat Immigration, although the report does not establish how effectively each measure was implemented.[^23]

## August 2023: Questions About Agent-Arranged Extensions in Chainat

On August 17, 2023, while Atthaphon headed Chainat Provincial Immigration, ASEAN NOW member JustAnotherFarang reported obtaining retirement extensions through a visa agent despite living in southern Thailand. He stated that his extension was stamped in Chainat and acknowledged having sufficient money available but not maintaining it in the required Thai bank account. He also described using the same agent for approximately three years, with his annual renewal fee increasing to 18,000 baht.[^9]

Thailand's published retirement-extension rules required applicants using the bank-deposit method to maintain at least 800,000 baht in a Thai bank account for two months before applying and three months after approval, with a minimum balance of 400,000 baht thereafter. Applicants could alternatively qualify through monthly income of at least 65,000 baht or a combination of income and savings.[^24] The forum user's admission that he had sufficient funds but did not keep them in the bank therefore raised questions about the financial evidence supporting his Chainat extension. However, he did not publish his application documents or disclose which financial criteria the immigration office had applied.[^9][^24]

Another forum member questioned the arrangement's legality and warned that obtaining extensions outside an applicant's province of residence could leave customers dependent on agents for subsequent renewals. Thailand's Immigration Bureau handbook directs retirement-extension applicants to the office responsible for their area of residence, while Chainat Immigration's own jurisdictional description covers eight districts, 51 subdistricts, and 505 villages within Chainat Province. The customer's account of receiving a Chainat-stamped extension despite living in southern Thailand therefore raises questions about the residence address used for his application and why that office processed it. The forum discussion does not provide the application documents needed to resolve those questions.[^9][^10][^25]

On August 18, 2023, ASEAN NOW global moderator [DrJack54](../moderators/drjack54.md) responded to the discussion after the customer disclosed that his agent-arranged retirement extension had been stamped in Chainat despite living in southern Thailand. DrJack54 agreed with another member's assessment that the customer could either continue using the agent or leave Thailand and restart the visa process. He recommended continuing with the agent for the time being, comparing increased agent fees to rising rent, without addressing why the extension had been processed outside the customer's province of residence.[^9]

The account was published during Atthaphon's tenure, raising questions about agent-arranged extensions processed through Chainat Immigration. However, the discussion does not establish the extension's exact issuance date, identify the approving officer, or conclusively identify the agency involved as Thai Visa Centre.[^9]

## Visa-Extension Processing During His Tenure

Official Chainat Immigration statistics recorded 131 extension-of-stay applications in October 2024—more than twice November's 48 and December's 62, and over four times January 2025's 31. February recorded 42 applications, while March rose to 157, the only month in the six-month reporting period to exceed October. The report offers no explanation for these substantial fluctuations.[^11]

The office also recorded two applications per month for obtaining or changing visa categories from October through December, followed by one in January. Atthaphon was still heading Chainat Immigration on January 9, 2025, although the exact date of his subsequent transfer remains unclear.[^2][^11]

These figures document considerable variation in extension-processing activity around the end of his tenure. However, the published statistics do not identify individual applicants, their provinces of residence, participating visa agents, or the officers approving each application. Consequently, they cannot establish whether the agent-arranged extension discussed on ASEAN NOW in August 2023 was an isolated case or part of a broader practice.[^9][^11]

## 2024 Bribery-Risk Assessment for Extension Applications

A fiscal-year 2024 risk-management plan published by Chainat Provincial Immigration classified temporary extensions of stay as a high bribery-risk activity, with a risk score of 8. It identified failure to remit collected extension fees to the state as a potential avenue for corruption and assigned oversight responsibilities to the provincial immigration inspector and the deputy inspector responsible for services to foreigners.[^14]

The plan called for supervisory checks, anti-bribery briefings, public complaint channels, and disciplinary investigations if misconduct was detected. The assessment describes institutional risks, not proven wrongdoing by Atthaphon or any other named officer. It provides additional context for the office's handling of extension applications during his tenure.[^14]
