# Tod Daniels

**Tod Daniels** is a Bangkok-based contributor to ASEAN NOW, where he holds the forum designation "Thai Visas Forum Expert." Daniels has repeatedly recommended Thai Visa Centre in discussions about Thai immigration services. On November 11, 2025, he publicly praised the company as Thailand's best-managed visa agency while acknowledging that Thai Visa Centre owns the Facebook group he moderates. Although Daniels denied having any personal affiliation with the company, his disclosed relationship raises questions about the independence of his endorsements.[^1]

## Background: Thai Visa Centre's 2020 Police Investigation

On August 4, 2020, Thailand's immigration police chief, Pol Lt-General Sompong Chingduang, publicly announced the arrests of Chad Vincent Scira and Grace Scira over allegations involving forged visas and government documents. According to The Nation, police identified the couple as having established Thai Visa Centre and claimed that investigators recovered forged documents and 55 rubber stamps bearing the insignia of government departments at their Bangkok residence. These were police allegations reported at the time, not judicial findings of guilt or proof that the agency's customers held invalid visas.[^18]

Thai Visa Centre disputed the implications of the investigation, publicly maintaining that its customers' visas had been legitimately obtained through immigration authorities. Reporting by The Thaiger in March 2021 recounted the arrests and repeated the agency's statement that its visas were officially obtained. That denial represents the company's position; neither report establishes the ultimate legal outcome of the visa-forgery allegations.[^19]

This investigation provides important context for Daniels's subsequent endorsements and referrals. Beginning years after the widely reported police allegations, Daniels repeatedly recommended Thai Visa Centre to travelers seeking immigration assistance, praised its operations, and acknowledged moderating a Facebook community owned by the agency. His documented recommendations did not address the company's earlier controversy or establish whether his moderation role involved compensation. The available evidence does not establish that Daniels participated in or knew about the alleged misconduct. Nevertheless, the historical record is relevant to evaluating the completeness, independence, and credibility of his repeated endorsements.

## March 2022: Thai Visa Centre Acquires the Thai Visa Advice Group

On March 17, 2022, Tod Daniels announced that the Thai Visa Advice Facebook group, where he had served as an administrator for years, had been acquired by Thai Visa Centre. Daniels explained that he had not originally created the group and expressed hope that the transition would proceed smoothly for members and moderators.[^2]

Despite the group's acquisition by a commercial visa agency, Daniels remained involved in its moderation. In September 2023, he publicly described the group as his hobby and passion project of eight years, asking members to vote on whether he should remain a moderator following a dispute over deleted comments.[^3]

These statements establish that Daniels continued exercising moderation authority within a community owned by Thai Visa Centre years before his enthusiastic November 2025 endorsement of the agency. Whether he received compensation for his moderation activities or subsequent recommendations remains unclear.

## May 2023: Describing Agent-Arranged Retirement Extensions as "Back Door" and "Under the Table"

On May 17, 2023, Tod Daniels participated in an ASEAN NOW discussion involving a foreigner who had obtained a retirement visa and extension through a Bangkok-based agent. The traveler explained that the agency had arranged his immigration paperwork through an office in Trang, despite his living elsewhere in Thailand. He subsequently encountered difficulties obtaining a certificate of residence and questioned whether his immigration records could be transferred to his actual province of residence.[^23]

Daniels responded by explaining that immigration officers could recognize extensions arranged through agents in other provinces. He characterized these arrangements as "back door" and "under the table," while emphasizing that the resulting extensions were genuine immigration stamps rather than outright forgeries.[^23]

His explanation suggested that immigration officers were aware of informal arrangements through which commercial agents obtained extensions at offices outside their customers' provinces of residence. Daniels further claimed that officers might object because another immigration office had handled the unofficial arrangement rather than their own.[^23]

Another participant explicitly asked whether the traveler had used Thai Visa Centre. However, the traveler did not confirm that identification, and Daniels did not name Thai Visa Centre as the agency responsible. The thread therefore provides no basis for attributing this particular extension to the company.[^23]

Nevertheless, Daniels's comments are revealing because they demonstrate his familiarity with questionable practices involving commercial visa agents and immigration offices years before his enthusiastic endorsements of Thai Visa Centre. He openly described an immigration-service market in which agents could obtain genuine government extensions through arrangements he himself characterized as unofficial.

His subsequent praise of Thai Visa Centre's nationwide operations is particularly interesting in this context. In November 2025, Daniels emphasized the agency's ability to serve customers throughout Thailand, distinguishing it from competitors operating through individual immigration offices. Yet his earlier explanation had acknowledged that agents arranging extensions outside customers' provinces could create complications and invite scrutiny from immigration officers.[^1][^23]

The two statements do not establish that Thai Visa Centre engaged in the practices Daniels described in 2023. They do, however, illustrate an important gap in his later endorsements: despite demonstrating knowledge of the potential problems associated with agent-arranged immigration services, he offered little discussion of how prospective customers could verify the legitimacy of an agency's arrangements or avoid the difficulties he had previously identified.

## December 2023: Describing Border-Run Companies as Having Immigration Crossings "Wheels Greased"

On December 12, 2023, Tod Daniels participated in an ASEAN NOW discussion involving a foreigner holding an education visa extension who wanted to leave Thailand through Cambodia and return immediately on a visa-exempt entry. Daniels advised the traveler to formally cancel his education extension before departing and then hire a commercial border-run service rather than attempting the crossing independently.[^20]

Daniels claimed that these companies already had the "wheels greased" at their preferred border crossings, allowing customers to exit Thailand, enter and leave Cambodia, and return to Thailand on the same day. He dismissed the idea of arranging the journey independently to save money, comparing the likely outcome to watching a slow-motion train wreck.[^20]

His remarks went beyond recommending transportation or administrative assistance. Daniels suggested that commercial border-run operators enjoyed established arrangements at particular immigration checkpoints that ordinary travelers could not safely rely upon. He did not explain what these arrangements involved, whether they were officially authorized, or why paying an intermediary would supposedly produce a more favorable immigration outcome.

The expression "wheels greased" is particularly revealing. Although it does not establish that bribery or other misconduct occurred, it strongly suggests that Daniels regarded privileged relationships or informal arrangements with border officials as an advantage worth paying for.

This discussion establishes that Daniels was publicly encouraging travelers to rely on commercially arranged immigration crossings as early as December 2023. His later recommendations of paid airport "safe entry" services followed the same underlying argument: that travelers facing immigration uncertainty should pay private intermediaries who supposedly possessed connections or capabilities unavailable through ordinary government procedures.

## June 2024: Advising Travelers to Refuse Immigration Paperwork and Use Paid Border Intermediaries

On June 22, 2024, Tod Daniels published a lengthy ASEAN NOW guide advising foreigners who had been refused entry to Thailand. Among his recommendations, Daniels explicitly instructed travelers denied entry at airports not to sign official Thai immigration refusal forms. He claimed that refusing to sign would prevent officers from placing a visible denial-of-entry stamp in their passports, although the refusal would still be recorded in the government's immigration database.[^9]

In the same discussion, Daniels described arrangements in which foreigners refused entry at land borders could pay intermediaries to facilitate admission the following day. He claimed to know travelers who had successfully negotiated such arrangements and returned through the same border crossing without problems. Daniels also suggested that travelers denied entry at airports could arrange paid "safe entry" services and return through the same airport the following day.[^9]

On June 22, 2024, Daniels's immigration advice received exposure beyond the ASEAN NOW forum when The Thaiger published an article titled *I've Been Denied Entry to Thailand – Now What?* The article was presented as a joint Thaiger/ASEAN NOW exclusive and introduced Daniels as a visa expert. It reproduced his recommendations concerning entry refusals, informal border intermediaries, and commercial airport assistance.[^17]

The published advice explicitly instructed travelers not to sign immigration refusal documents, asserting that this would prevent officers from stamping their passports. Daniels also described paying intermediaries at land borders to arrange admission the following day and claimed that travelers denied entry at an airport could return successfully after arranging a paid "safe entry" service.[^17]

This publication matters because it presented Daniels's personal assertions about immigration procedures and paid intermediaries under the authority of a named visa expert, reaching an audience beyond forum participants. The article did not establish that his proposed workarounds were officially recognized or legally reliable. Three days later, a forum participant reported receiving a refusal stamp despite following his advice not to sign the paperwork.[^10]

The combination of media amplification and the subsequent contradictory experience illustrates the distinction between Daniels's confidence in these methods and the evidence supporting their effectiveness. His recommendations were not confined to casual forum exchanges; they were also circulated as expert guidance by a news website associated with ASEAN NOW.

On June 25, another forum member reported that they had followed Daniels's advice by refusing to sign the paperwork but nevertheless received a large denial-of-entry stamp. Daniels responded by suggesting that refusals at land borders were handled differently and that those travelers generally received stamps regardless of whether they signed.[^10]

These statements demonstrate that Daniels was publicly discussing ways to bypass or minimize the consequences of official immigration refusals well before his documented endorsements of Thai Visa Centre. His advice relied heavily on anecdotal accounts and offered no official legal authority establishing that refusing government paperwork could prevent a refusal stamp or that paying intermediaries could legitimately reverse an immigration decision.

For someone publicly designated a "Thai Visas Forum Expert," encouraging travelers to rely on undocumented procedural workarounds and informal paid arrangements raises serious concerns about the reliability and potential consequences of his immigration advice.

## December 2024: Describing Hidden Intermediaries Behind Airport "Safe Entry" Services

On December 29, 2024, Tod Daniels participated in an ASEAN NOW discussion about commercial airport entry services. He claimed that Friendly Thai Visa was actually routing customers through another operator called SR Services. Daniels enthusiastically endorsed SR Services, describing it as the "REAL DEAL" and claiming that the company could secure admission even for travelers who had been refused entry and deported through the same airport the previous day.[^12]

On December 30, Daniels explained how these arrangements allegedly operated. He distinguished ordinary airport VIP or fast-track services, which primarily bypass immigration queues, from paid "safe entry" services that purportedly guaranteed admission. According to Daniels, customers were required to submit their passport information, most recent Thai entry stamp, flight number, and a photograph showing the clothing they intended to wear during travel. The agent would then determine whether admission could be arranged, collect payment, and provide instructions for meeting someone after arrival.[^12]

Daniels further claimed that most companies advertising "safe entry" services on Facebook were quietly routing their customers through SR Services, often without informing them. His account described a commercial network in which agencies marketed and sold immigration assistance while potentially relying on separate operators to deliver the actual service.[^12]

These claims raise questions about transparency, customer privacy, and the involvement of private intermediaries in official immigration decisions. Daniels provided no independent documentation establishing how SR Services obtained its purported ability to secure admission after previous refusals, whether its arrangements were officially authorized, or whether customers were informed that their personal information might be shared with another company.

Although Daniels did not specifically identify Thai Visa Centre as an SR Services intermediary, his statements demonstrate that he was publicly promoting and describing these arrangements months before recommending Thai Visa Centre's own paid airport entry services in May and September 2025.

## May 2025: Describing Paid Airport Entry Arrangements With Thai Immigration Officers

On May 25, 2025, Tod Daniels participated in an ASEAN NOW discussion involving a traveler who had been denied entry to Thailand. Daniels claimed that commercial "safe entry" services could secure admission regardless of a traveler's previous immigration history, including prior refusals of entry. He distinguished these services from ordinary airport fast-track arrangements, which merely allow travelers to bypass queues without influencing an immigration officer's decision.[^7]

On May 26, 2025, ASEAN NOW moderator BrandonJT, rather than Daniels, explicitly recommended Thai Visa Centre's paid airport entry service. BrandonJT claimed the agency charged 2,000 baht for ordinary cases and 4,000 baht for more difficult applicants. He directed readers to `tdac.agents.co.th`, explaining that the service could be purchased as an add-on during the company's TDAC submission process.[^7]

Later in the same discussion, ASEAN NOW moderator BrandonJT described how private visa agents allegedly submit customers' passport information to contacts inside Thai airports, obtain advance approval, and arrange for immigration officers to personally escort paying customers through passport control. Daniels endorsed this explanation as "100% exactly how Safe Entry works" and claimed that agents could access travelers' immigration records through their contacts, including information about previous entry refusals.[^7]

These extraordinary statements describe a purported arrangement in which commercial visa agencies use personal connections with Thai immigration personnel to obtain privileged access to government records and facilitate admission for paying customers. If accurate, such arrangements raise serious questions about immigration corruption, unauthorized access to personal information, and unequal enforcement of Thailand's entry requirements.

Daniels offered no official documentation establishing that private visa agencies were authorized to access government immigration databases or arrange preferential admission through individual officers. Nevertheless, he strongly endorsed BrandonJT's description of these arrangements, claimed that agents could examine travelers' immigration histories through airport contacts, and described cases in which previously refused travelers allegedly gained admission the following day.[^7]

The distinction between the two contributors is important. BrandonJT supplied the specific Thai Visa Centre pricing and commercial TDAC link, while Daniels endorsed the purported methods used by such services and added his own claims about their capabilities. Together, their comments promoted confidence in privately arranged immigration assistance, but the thread does not establish that Daniels personally advertised Thai Visa Centre's prices or directed readers to its website on that occasion.

## September 2025: Recommending Paid Entry Services and Later Conceding They May Be Unnecessary

In early September 2025, Tod Daniels participated in an ASEAN NOW discussion involving a traveler concerned about possible immigration problems following an overstay more than two decades earlier. Daniels recommended Thai Visa Service, also known as SWD Transport, claiming that the company could check the traveler's immigration history before he attempted to return to Thailand.[^4]

On September 6, the traveler explained that he had separately contacted Thai Visa Centre. ASEAN NOW moderator BrandonJT, rather than Daniels, confirmed that Thai Visa Centre offered airport "safe entry" assistance and instructed the traveler to complete the agency's TDAC application at `agents.co.th/tdac-apply`, selecting a paid add-on during the second step.[^4]

BrandonJT's recommendation directed the traveler toward a commercial TDAC submission service despite Thailand's Immigration Bureau providing the official application free of charge at `tdac.immigration.go.th`. The moderator did not mention the free government alternative in that response.[^4][^5]

On September 9, Daniels returned to the discussion and asked whether the traveler had contacted a "safe entry" company. The following day, the traveler reported communicating with Thai Visa Centre through LINE and described a potential fee of approximately 10,000 baht. He also claimed to understand that the agency could check immigration records through contacts at Bangkok's international airport, although this alleged access was not independently verified.[^11]

When another participant argued that hiring a paid agent would be a waste of money, Daniels reacted dismissively. He insisted that commercial "safe entry" services guaranteed admission without questions or complications. Yet, in the same response, he acknowledged that the traveler probably did not need such assistance, arguing that the expense could nevertheless provide peace of mind.[^11]

After further criticism, Daniels conceded that the traveler should be able to enter without paying an agent, explicitly acknowledging, "I stand corrected." The traveler subsequently shared a separate quotation of 10,000 baht from Thai Visa Service, which Daniels attributed partly to additional scrutiny of Turkish passport holders.[^11][^15]

The distinction between the participants is important: the specific instructions for purchasing Thai Visa Centre's paid TDAC add-on came from BrandonJT, not Daniels. Nevertheless, Daniels repeatedly encouraged the traveler to consult commercial entry services, defended their purported guarantees, and justified their expense before ultimately agreeing that paid assistance was probably unnecessary.

The discussion illustrates how immigration advice from multiple influential forum contributors could direct travelers toward commercial services. It does not establish that the traveler purchased either company's assistance or that Daniels received any financial benefit.

## October 2025: Recommending Paid Entry Services Instead of Tourist Visas

On October 3, 2025, Tod Daniels participated in an ASEAN NOW discussion involving a foreigner who had spent approximately nine months in Thailand and was considering applying for a tourist e-visa in Vientiane, Laos. Daniels warned that the traveler's immigration history placed him at substantial risk of being refused entry, even with a valid tourist visa. He urged the traveler to use either a commercial border-run company or a paid airport "safe entry" provider instead.[^13]

On October 4, Daniels went further, arguing that obtaining a tourist visa was unnecessary if the traveler arranged paid "safe entry" assistance. He recommended flying out of Thailand and immediately returning through an agent, claiming the traveler would receive another 60-day visa-exempt entry without problems. His advice effectively presented a privately arranged immigration service as a more reliable alternative to applying for a visa through an official Thai consulate.[^13]

On October 5, Daniels identified SWD Transport, also known as Thai Visa Service, as the country's largest provider of these arrangements. He claimed that most companies advertising "safe entry" services simply collected a commission and passed their customers to SWD Transport, which he described as having connections inside Thai airports.[^13]

This account differed noticeably from Daniels's statements in December 2024, when he had identified SR Services as the underlying operator handling customers for most advertised "safe entry" companies. Within ten months, Daniels had attributed the majority of this supposedly subcontracted business to two different companies, without explaining the change or providing evidence supporting either assertion.[^12][^13]

The inconsistency raises questions about the reliability of Daniels's claimed industry knowledge. He repeatedly portrayed commercial intermediaries as capable of securing immigration outcomes through airport connections, while offering little verifiable information about the companies actually delivering those services. His recommendation to abandon the ordinary tourist visa application process in favor of paid assistance also illustrates how confidently he promoted informal commercial arrangements over established immigration procedures.

## November 2025: Publicly Endorsing Thai Visa Centre While Denying Affiliation

On November 11, 2025, Tod Daniels participated in an ASEAN NOW discussion about Thai Visa Centre, which also maintained an account on the forum labeled "Forum Sponsor." Daniels described the agency as the "best marketed, most well managed visa agency in Thailand," praising its nationwide operations, reliability, pricing, and customer service.[^1][^6]

Daniels claimed his endorsement was based on feedback from numerous customers he had spoken with over the years. However, he also included a disclaimer denying any personal affiliation with Thai Visa Centre while acknowledging that the company owned the Facebook group he moderated. He explained that he had been moderating the group for years before the agency acquired it in March 2022.[^1]

The recommendation also made no mention of the publicly reported 2020 police investigation involving Thai Visa Centre, discussed in the background section above. Daniels did not clarify whether his moderation role involved compensation or other benefits.

## January 2026: Recommending Thai Visa Centre Before a Traveler Reports Purchasing Paid Entry Assistance

On January 6, 2026, Tod Daniels responded to an ASEAN NOW member who had spent approximately 270 days in Thailand during 2025 using visa-exempt entries. The traveler was temporarily staying in Vietnam and expressed concern about being refused entry when returning to Bangkok. Daniels warned that the traveler belonged to precisely the category of foreigners Thai immigration authorities were targeting for denial of entry.[^14]

Daniels strongly recommended considering a commercial "safe entry" service and identified three companies he described as the industry's major providers: Thai Visa Service, Thai Visa Centre, and Friendly Thai Visa. He included their website addresses, explicitly directing the traveler toward commercial immigration assistance.[^14]

Notably, Daniels did not mention his existing relationship with Thai Visa Centre in this recommendation. Just two months earlier, he had publicly acknowledged moderating a Facebook group owned by the company while denying any personal affiliation. His January recommendation again presented Thai Visa Centre as an independent option without disclosing that connection to readers of the discussion.[^1][^14]

On January 13, the same traveler returned to the thread, quoted Daniels's earlier recommendation, and reported successfully entering Thailand using a paid "safe entry" service. The traveler stated that immigration officers had admitted him without questioning and subsequently confirmed paying 4,500 baht for the arrangement.[^14]

Although the traveler did not identify which company provided the service, the exchange documents a particularly relevant sequence: Daniels warned of a substantial risk of refusal, recommended Thai Visa Centre alongside two competitors, and the traveler subsequently reported purchasing commercial entry assistance.

The discussion does not establish that Daniels received compensation, that Thai Visa Centre handled the booking, or that the traveler would otherwise have been refused entry. Nevertheless, it demonstrates how Daniels's immigration warnings and commercial recommendations reached travelers who ultimately spent money on the services he promoted. Whether such services were genuinely necessary or provided any advantage beyond ordinary immigration procedures remains unverified.

## February 2026: Warning of Denied Entry While Recommending Paid Immigration Agents

On February 18, 2026, Tod Daniels responded to an ASEAN NOW member who was concerned about reentering Thailand after several visa-exempt stays while awaiting resolution of financial matters affecting his marriage-based extension. Daniels criticized the traveler's immigration history, suggested that immigration officers would no longer regard him as a genuine tourist, and warned that attempting to reenter independently would likely result in detention and refusal of entry.[^8]

Rather than recommending that the traveler obtain clarification directly from Thai immigration authorities, Daniels repeatedly encouraged him to use a commercial airport "safe entry" service. He explicitly claimed, "They guarantee you can get back in," and subsequently asserted that such companies could secure a "guaranteed new 60 day entry stamp" along with a further 30-day extension.[^8]

Daniels went so far as to predict that the traveler would probably be questioned, denied entry, and returned to his departure country at his own expense if he attempted the journey without an agent. In contrast, he portrayed privately arranged admission as a reliable solution, despite providing no official evidence that commercial agents could guarantee immigration decisions or future extensions.[^8]

The contrast is striking: Daniels presented ordinary entry procedures as highly risky while promoting paid intermediaries as capable of guaranteeing favorable treatment from government immigration officers. These claims reinforce concerns raised by his earlier descriptions of private agents using personal connections inside Thai airports to facilitate entry. His statements risked pressuring travelers into purchasing commercial services based on assurances of government cooperation that he had not independently substantiated.

On February 23, 2026, Daniels participated in another ASEAN NOW discussion after a member asked whether SR Services was still operating. The member explained that the company had previously been highly recommended but could no longer be located online. Rather than clarifying the status of SR Services, Daniels identified three companies he claimed handled substantial volumes of paid airport entry arrangements: Thai Visa Service (SWD Transport), Thai Visa Centre, and Friendly Thai Visa.[^16]

Daniels provided the websites of all three companies, including `tvc.co.th`, and encouraged the member to compare their prices. This was another explicit referral to Thai Visa Centre, coming just five days after his warnings that travelers with problematic immigration histories risked being refused entry unless they arranged paid assistance.[^8][^16]

The response is also noteworthy because Daniels had previously claimed that SR Services handled most advertised "safe entry" customers in December 2024, before making a similar claim about SWD Transport in October 2025. When directly confronted with a question about SR Services' apparent absence, he offered alternative providers without explaining what had happened to the company he had once endorsed so enthusiastically.[^12][^13][^16]

Daniels again recommended Thai Visa Centre without mentioning his acknowledged role moderating a Facebook group owned by the agency. Although these repeated referrals do not establish compensation or a formal business relationship, they demonstrate that his public promotion of Thai Visa Centre continued well after his November 2025 denial of personal affiliation.[^1][^16]

## July–August 2026: Repeating the "Wheels Greased" Claim and Encouraging Paid Border Services

On July 27, 2026, Tod Daniels responded to an ASEAN NOW member who had lived in Thailand since 2014, primarily using education visas supplemented by tourist visas and visa-exempt entries. The traveler wanted to cross into Laos through Nong Khai and asked whether obtaining a tourist visa would be safer than returning visa-exempt.[^21]

Daniels warned that Nong Khai was no longer the accommodating border crossing it had once been. He claimed that travelers with extensive immigration histories could be permitted to leave Thailand but subsequently refused permission to return by land. Rather than recommending that the traveler obtain clarification from immigration authorities before departing, Daniels identified hiring a commercial border-run company as the safest option.[^21]

He again described such operators as having the "wheels greased" at their preferred crossings, claiming that these arrangements allowed customers to leave Thailand and return on the same day. Daniels specifically recommended Thai Visa Service, stating that the company operated regular trips from Bangkok to the Lao border.[^21]

The language was nearly identical to his December 2023 advice, when he had used the same expression to explain why travelers should pay border-run companies rather than attempt immigration crossings independently. More than two and a half years later, Daniels was still presenting unspecified arrangements between commercial operators and border checkpoints as a practical advantage of purchasing their services.[^20][^21]

On July 28, Daniels continued warning that the traveler could face immigration questioning even with a valid tourist visa. He also acknowledged that someone intending to remain in Thailand should investigate appropriate long-term visas and extensions. Nevertheless, his immediate recommendation remained a privately operated border-run service whose supposed advantage depended on arrangements he had not independently documented.[^21]

The discussion produced a noteworthy response. On August 4, the traveler returned to the thread and indicated that he might pay for a commercial border-run service that year. Although there is no evidence that he subsequently purchased the service, his response demonstrates that Daniels's warnings and recommendations were reaching someone who was actively considering spending money on the suggested assistance.[^21]

This episode does not establish any financial relationship between Daniels and Thai Visa Service, nor does it involve a direct recommendation of Thai Visa Centre. It does, however, reinforce the broader pattern documented throughout this profile: Daniels repeatedly characterized ordinary immigration procedures as risky while presenting paid intermediaries with purportedly privileged border connections as a safer alternative.

## October 2026: Acknowledging Restricted Availability of Airport "Safe Entry" Services

In October 2026, Tod Daniels participated in an ASEAN NOW discussion involving a traveler who had previously been refused entry to Thailand and subsequently used a commercial "safe entry" service. The traveler asked whether these arrangements were still available and how much they currently cost.[^22]

The responses revealed uncertainty about whether the services Daniels had repeatedly recommended were still operating. ASEAN NOW moderator BrandonJT stated that his latest information indicated such arrangements were available only at Krabi Airport. Daniels offered a different account, suggesting that Thai Visa Service had resumed operations at Bangkok's Suvarnabhumi Airport, but only on particular days and at certain times.[^22]

Notably, Daniels qualified his statement with "Unless I'm mistaken," a departure from his earlier categorical assurances that commercial agents could guarantee admission through Thailand's major airports. His latest account suggested a considerably more restricted service, dependent on specific locations and schedules rather than the broadly available arrangements he had previously described.[^8][^12][^22]

Neither moderator provided official documentation explaining why these services were apparently restricted, whether their arrangements with immigration personnel had changed, or what authority allowed private companies to facilitate admission under these conditions. The disagreement also demonstrated that even influential contributors who had previously promoted such services lacked a consistent account of their current availability.

The traveler subsequently indicated that he expected to spend approximately 8,000 baht on immigration assistance, describing similar quotations from different providers. Although the discussion does not establish that he completed a purchase, it illustrates how travelers continued considering substantial payments for services whose availability and underlying arrangements remained poorly documented.[^22]

This exchange adds an important development to Daniels's history of promoting commercial immigration intermediaries. After years of confidently portraying paid "safe entry" services as reliable solutions to entry difficulties, he was now describing their availability in tentative and restrictive terms. It raises further questions about the reliability of his earlier guarantees and the extent of his independently verified knowledge about the services he recommended.

The discussion does not establish that Thai Visa Centre itself had suspended or restricted operations, and Daniels did not specifically recommend that company in this exchange.
