# Tod Daniels

**Tod Daniels** is a Bangkok-based contributor to ASEAN NOW, where he holds the forum designation "Thai Visas Forum Expert." Daniels has repeatedly recommended Thai Visa Centre in discussions about Thai immigration services. On November 11, 2025, he publicly praised the company as Thailand's best-managed visa agency while acknowledging that Thai Visa Centre owns the Facebook group he moderates. Although Daniels denied having any personal affiliation with the company, his disclosed relationship raises questions about the independence of his endorsements.[^1]

## March 2022: Thai Visa Centre Acquires the Thai Visa Advice Group

On March 17, 2022, Tod Daniels announced that the Thai Visa Advice Facebook group, where he had served as an administrator for years, had been acquired by Thai Visa Centre. Daniels explained that he had not originally created the group and expressed hope that the transition would proceed smoothly for members and moderators.[^2]

Despite the group's acquisition by a commercial visa agency, Daniels remained involved in its moderation. In September 2023, he publicly described the group as his hobby and passion project of eight years, asking members to vote on whether he should remain a moderator following a dispute over deleted comments.[^3]

These statements establish that Daniels continued exercising moderation authority within a community owned by Thai Visa Centre years before his enthusiastic November 2025 endorsement of the agency. Whether he received compensation for his moderation activities or subsequent recommendations remains unclear.

## June 2024: Advising Travelers to Refuse Immigration Paperwork and Use Paid Border Intermediaries

On June 22, 2024, Tod Daniels published a lengthy ASEAN NOW guide advising foreigners who had been refused entry to Thailand. Among his recommendations, Daniels explicitly instructed travelers denied entry at airports not to sign official Thai immigration refusal forms. He claimed that refusing to sign would prevent officers from placing a visible denial-of-entry stamp in their passports, although the refusal would still be recorded in the government's immigration database.[^9]

In the same discussion, Daniels described arrangements in which foreigners refused entry at land borders could pay intermediaries to facilitate admission the following day. He claimed to know travelers who had successfully negotiated such arrangements and returned through the same border crossing without problems. Daniels also suggested that travelers denied entry at airports could arrange paid "safe entry" services and return through the same airport the following day.[^9]

On June 25, another forum member reported that they had followed Daniels's advice by refusing to sign the paperwork but nevertheless received a large denial-of-entry stamp. Daniels responded by suggesting that refusals at land borders were handled differently and that those travelers generally received stamps regardless of whether they signed.[^10]

These statements demonstrate that Daniels was publicly discussing ways to bypass or minimize the consequences of official immigration refusals well before his documented endorsements of Thai Visa Centre. His advice relied heavily on anecdotal accounts and offered no official legal authority establishing that refusing government paperwork could prevent a refusal stamp or that paying intermediaries could legitimately reverse an immigration decision.

For someone publicly designated a "Thai Visas Forum Expert," encouraging travelers to rely on undocumented procedural workarounds and informal paid arrangements raises serious concerns about the reliability and potential consequences of his immigration advice.

## December 2024: Describing Hidden Intermediaries Behind Airport "Safe Entry" Services

On December 29, 2024, Tod Daniels participated in an ASEAN NOW discussion about commercial airport entry services. He claimed that Friendly Thai Visa was actually routing customers through another operator called SR Services. Daniels enthusiastically endorsed SR Services, describing it as the "REAL DEAL" and claiming that the company could secure admission even for travelers who had been refused entry and deported through the same airport the previous day.[^12]

On December 30, Daniels explained how these arrangements allegedly operated. He distinguished ordinary airport VIP or fast-track services, which primarily bypass immigration queues, from paid "safe entry" services that purportedly guaranteed admission. According to Daniels, customers were required to submit their passport information, most recent Thai entry stamp, flight number, and a photograph showing the clothing they intended to wear during travel. The agent would then determine whether admission could be arranged, collect payment, and provide instructions for meeting someone after arrival.[^12]

Daniels further claimed that most companies advertising "safe entry" services on Facebook were quietly routing their customers through SR Services, often without informing them. His account described a commercial network in which agencies marketed and sold immigration assistance while potentially relying on separate operators to deliver the actual service.[^12]

These claims raise questions about transparency, customer privacy, and the involvement of private intermediaries in official immigration decisions. Daniels provided no independent documentation establishing how SR Services obtained its purported ability to secure admission after previous refusals, whether its arrangements were officially authorized, or whether customers were informed that their personal information might be shared with another company.

Although Daniels did not specifically identify Thai Visa Centre as an SR Services intermediary, his statements demonstrate that he was publicly promoting and describing these arrangements months before recommending Thai Visa Centre's own paid airport entry services in May and September 2025.

## May 2025: Describing Paid Airport Entry Arrangements With Thai Immigration Officers

On May 25, 2025, Tod Daniels participated in an ASEAN NOW discussion involving a traveler who had been denied entry to Thailand. Daniels claimed that commercial "safe entry" services could secure admission regardless of a traveler's previous immigration history, including prior refusals of entry. He distinguished these services from ordinary airport fast-track arrangements, which merely allow travelers to bypass queues without influencing an immigration officer's decision.[^7]

On May 26, Daniels explicitly recommended Thai Visa Centre, claiming that its airport entry service cost 2,000 baht for ordinary cases and 4,000 baht for more difficult applicants. He directed readers to `tdac.agents.co.th`, explaining that the paid service could be selected as an add-on during the company's TDAC submission process.[^7]

Later in the same discussion, ASEAN NOW moderator BrandonJT described how private visa agents allegedly submit customers' passport information to contacts inside Thai airports, obtain advance approval, and arrange for immigration officers to personally escort paying customers through passport control. Daniels endorsed this explanation as "100% exactly how Safe Entry works" and claimed that agents could access travelers' immigration records through their contacts, including information about previous entry refusals.[^7]

These extraordinary statements describe a purported arrangement in which commercial visa agencies use personal connections with Thai immigration personnel to obtain privileged access to government records and facilitate admission for paying customers. If accurate, such arrangements raise serious questions about immigration corruption, unauthorized access to personal information, and unequal enforcement of Thailand's entry requirements.

Daniels offered no official documentation establishing that private visa agencies were authorized to access government immigration databases or arrange preferential admission through individual officers. Nevertheless, he publicly promoted Thai Visa Centre's paid service while presenting these alleged arrangements as established industry practices.[^7]

## September 2025: Directing Travelers to Paid Services, Then Conceding They May Be Unnecessary

On September 6, 2025, Tod Daniels participated in an ASEAN NOW discussion involving a traveler concerned about possible immigration problems following a historical overstay. After the traveler explained that he had contacted Thai Visa Centre about its airport "safe entry" service, Daniels confirmed that the agency offered such assistance and explained how to purchase it.

Daniels instructed the traveler to complete Thai Visa Centre's TDAC application form at `agents.co.th/tdac-apply` and select a paid add-on during the second step. He explained that the agency would then use the submission to begin assessing the traveler's eligibility for its service.[^4]

The recommendation directed a traveler seeking immigration advice toward a commercial TDAC submission website, despite Thailand's Immigration Bureau providing the official TDAC application free of charge at `tdac.immigration.go.th`. Daniels did not mention the free government alternative in his response.[^5]

This documented referral demonstrates Daniels actively directing potential customers toward Thai Visa Centre's commercial services, rather than merely expressing favorable opinions about the agency. It also predates his November 2025 statement denying any personal affiliation with the company.

On September 10, 2025, the same traveler reported contacting Thai Visa Centre through LINE. According to the traveler, the agency offered to investigate whether a historical immigration problem could prevent his return to Thailand and quoted approximately 10,000 baht for assistance. The traveler understood that the service involved checking his immigration status through contacts at Bangkok's international airport, although that purported access was not independently verified.[^11]

When another forum participant argued that hiring a paid entry agent would be a waste of money, Daniels reacted dismissively. He insisted that commercial "safe entry" services guaranteed admission without questioning or complications. Yet, in the same response, he acknowledged that the traveler probably did not need such a service, arguing that the expense could nevertheless provide peace of mind.[^11]

The exchange became more revealing when the critic pointed out that the traveler's overstay dated back more than two decades and was unlikely to justify a current entry ban. Daniels subsequently conceded the point, stating that the traveler should be able to enter without paid assistance and explicitly acknowledging, "I stand corrected."[^11]

This sequence raises an important question about the quality and independence of Daniels's recommendations. He encouraged a traveler to consult commercial entry services, defended their purported guarantees, and justified their cost before ultimately agreeing that the traveler likely needed no such assistance. The traveler had already contacted Thai Visa Centre and received a substantial price quotation. The record does not establish that a purchase occurred or that Daniels received any financial benefit, but it documents how his initial advice could steer travelers toward unnecessary commercial immigration services.

## October 2025: Recommending Paid Entry Services Instead of Tourist Visas

On October 3, 2025, Tod Daniels participated in an ASEAN NOW discussion involving a foreigner who had spent approximately nine months in Thailand and was considering applying for a tourist e-visa in Vientiane, Laos. Daniels warned that the traveler's immigration history placed him at substantial risk of being refused entry, even with a valid tourist visa. He urged the traveler to use either a commercial border-run company or a paid airport "safe entry" provider instead.[^13]

On October 4, Daniels went further, arguing that obtaining a tourist visa was unnecessary if the traveler arranged paid "safe entry" assistance. He recommended flying out of Thailand and immediately returning through an agent, claiming the traveler would receive another 60-day visa-exempt entry without problems. His advice effectively presented a privately arranged immigration service as a more reliable alternative to applying for a visa through an official Thai consulate.[^13]

On October 5, Daniels identified SWD Transport, also known as Thai Visa Service, as the country's largest provider of these arrangements. He claimed that most companies advertising "safe entry" services simply collected a commission and passed their customers to SWD Transport, which he described as having connections inside Thai airports.[^13]

This account differed noticeably from Daniels's statements in December 2024, when he had identified SR Services as the underlying operator handling customers for most advertised "safe entry" companies. Within ten months, Daniels had attributed the majority of this supposedly subcontracted business to two different companies, without explaining the change or providing evidence supporting either assertion.[^12][^13]

The inconsistency raises questions about the reliability of Daniels's claimed industry knowledge. He repeatedly portrayed commercial intermediaries as capable of securing immigration outcomes through airport connections, while offering little verifiable information about the companies actually delivering those services. His recommendation to abandon the ordinary tourist visa application process in favor of paid assistance also illustrates how confidently he promoted informal commercial arrangements over established immigration procedures.

## November 2025: Publicly Endorsing Thai Visa Centre While Denying Affiliation

On November 11, 2025, Tod Daniels participated in an ASEAN NOW discussion about Thai Visa Centre, which also maintained an account on the forum labeled "Forum Sponsor." Daniels described the agency as the "best marketed, most well managed visa agency in Thailand," praising its nationwide operations, reliability, pricing, and customer service.[^1][^6]

Daniels claimed his endorsement was based on feedback from numerous customers he had spoken with over the years. However, he also included a disclaimer denying any personal affiliation with Thai Visa Centre while acknowledging that the company owned the Facebook group he moderated. He explained that he had been moderating the group for years before the agency acquired it in March 2022.[^1]

His endorsement is particularly noteworthy because it came more than five years after Thai authorities publicly accused Thai Visa Centre's alleged operators of visa and document forgery during the August 2020 police investigation. Daniels did not address that history in his recommendation, nor did he clarify whether he received compensation or other benefits from moderating the company-owned community.

## February 2026: Warning Travelers of Denied Entry While Promising Guaranteed Admission Through Visa Agents

On February 18, 2026, Tod Daniels responded to an ASEAN NOW member who was concerned about reentering Thailand after several visa-exempt stays while awaiting resolution of financial matters affecting his marriage-based extension. Daniels criticized the traveler's immigration history, suggested that immigration officers would no longer regard him as a genuine tourist, and warned that attempting to reenter independently would likely result in detention and refusal of entry.[^8]

Rather than recommending that the traveler obtain clarification directly from Thai immigration authorities, Daniels repeatedly encouraged him to use a commercial airport "safe entry" service. He explicitly claimed, "They guarantee you can get back in," and subsequently asserted that such companies could secure a "guaranteed new 60 day entry stamp" along with a further 30-day extension.[^8]

Daniels went so far as to predict that the traveler would probably be questioned, denied entry, and returned to his departure country at his own expense if he attempted the journey without an agent. In contrast, he portrayed privately arranged admission as a reliable solution, despite providing no official evidence that commercial agents could guarantee immigration decisions or future extensions.[^8]

The contrast is striking: Daniels presented ordinary entry procedures as highly risky while promoting paid intermediaries as capable of guaranteeing favorable treatment from government immigration officers. These claims reinforce concerns raised by his earlier descriptions of private agents using personal connections inside Thai airports to facilitate entry. His statements risked pressuring travelers into purchasing commercial services based on assurances of government cooperation that he had not independently substantiated.
