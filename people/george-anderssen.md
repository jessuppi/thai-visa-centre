# George Anderssen

George Anderssen founded [ThaiVisa.com](../domains/thaivisa.com.md) in 2002, establishing one of Thailand's largest English-language expatriate discussion forums. The platform was later rebranded as [ASEAN NOW](../brands/asean-now.md).

On November 19, 2004, Anderssen published a second-anniversary retrospective explaining that ThaiVisa.com's forum had launched on November 20, 2002, using Ikonboard software, with just two initial members: himself and **dr_pat_pong**. Within two years, the community had grown to more than **14,000 members** and over **30,000 newsletter subscribers**.[^12]

By November 2004, ThaiVisa.com had expanded into classifieds, blogs, chat rooms, photo galleries, and a news service. Anderssen also reported that the website sponsored **Radio Bangkok**, demonstrating how the original forum had developed into a broader expatriate media operation.[^12]

Anderssen remained involved following the website's acquisition by Choice Group Asia in 2015. When [The Thaiger](../brands/the-thaiger.md) acquired ASEAN NOW in April 2024, he was publicly identified as its Chief Operating Officer (COO).

In September 2015, [Choice Group Asia Co., Ltd.](../companies/choice-group-asia-co-ltd.md) acquired ThaiVisa.com from Thaivisa Expat Group Co., Ltd. By then, the website founded by Anderssen had grown to more than **1.5 million monthly visitors** and over **300,000 email newsletter subscribers**, expanding well beyond its original discussion forum into a major English-language news and media platform for expatriates in Thailand.[^1]

Choice Group Asia appointed **Dan Cheeseman** as managing director of the acquired ThaiVisa Media Group, incorporating the business into its broader media portfolio alongside Pattaya Channel TV and Inspire Digital Media Group.[^1]

## Forum Management

On January 4, 2004, ThaiVisa.com administrators published proposed forum rules that explicitly protected commercial advertisers from public criticism. The first rule stated:

> Our contracts with our advertisers prohibit comments regarding advertisements which appear on our forums.

The rule further specified that such comments would be removed. In the same discussion, an administrator posting as **IT Manager** stated that he had spent six hours working with Anderssen and another administrator to develop the rules.[^10]

The exchange establishes that Anderssen participated in shaping ThaiVisa.com's moderation policies during its earliest years, when restrictions on criticism of paying advertisers were already being introduced. These restrictions foreshadowed ASEAN NOW's later **Rule 37**, which protects forum sponsors from negative commentary.

On April 19, 2024, Anderssen responded to ASEAN NOW members questioning how The Thaiger's acquisition would affect the forum. Posting under the username **george**, he confirmed that the existing team would remain and that improvements to moderation and content were planned.[^3]

He also described plans to reorganize the forum, improve navigation and the homepage, and upgrade its cloud hosting infrastructure. Anderssen indicated that further announcements would follow in May 2024.

On February 8–9, 2024, ASEAN NOW members complained that intrusive full-screen advertisements were obscuring forum content and making the website increasingly difficult to use. Posting under the username **george**, Anderssen acknowledged the complaints and explained that the website was testing advertising configurations to meet revenue targets while balancing the user experience.[^5]

During the same discussion, Global Moderator **metisdead** confirmed removing a post under **Rule 22**, which prohibited discussion of methods for blocking or bypassing advertisements. The exchange documented both the forum's commercial advertising priorities and its enforcement of restrictions on discussing ways to avoid those advertisements.[^5]

In November 2023, ASEAN NOW experienced approximately six days of downtime. On November 22, Anderssen attributed the outage to a cyberattack affecting a US datacenter hosting parts of the forum. He announced that the website had migrated to another datacenter, stated that no user data or content had been compromised, and acknowledged continuing performance problems following the migration.[^8]

On November 26, members questioned the explanation, with one suggesting the outage might have resulted from a problematic server migration rather than an attack. Anderssen responded that the datacenter had experienced problems and that the forum's software vendor was upgrading its templates. He also rejected speculation about an internal power struggle and **personally closed the discussion**, describing it as defamatory.[^9]

The incident documents Anderssen's direct involvement in ASEAN NOW's technical operations and his willingness to shut down public discussion challenging the platform's management.

## Advertising Sales

On June 16, 2006, Anderssen advertised two positions at ThaiVisa.com: a **content manager/webmaster/PHP programmer** and an **advertising sales assistant**. The technical role involved managing the website's TYPO3 content management system, developing new content, redesigning travel websites, and improving search-engine optimization.[^13]

The sales position focused on recruiting sponsors and selling advertising throughout Thailand, with commission or revenue-sharing arrangements offered. Anderssen also invited companies to provide the services on an outsourced basis, demonstrating ThaiVisa.com's efforts to expand its commercial operations.[^13]

In February 2023, Anderssen publicly advertised a sales position at [ASEAN NOW](../brands/asean-now.md) through his personal LinkedIn account. The recruitment post sought candidates to sell the forum's advertising packages to businesses throughout Thailand, emphasizing its large expatriate audience, social media presence, and daily email newsletter.[^6]

Applicants were instructed to contact an email address at Choice Group Asia, which owned ASEAN NOW at the time. The recruitment provides direct evidence of Anderssen's involvement in the forum's commercial advertising operations before its acquisition by The Thaiger.

## Editorial Involvement

On October 2, 2009, Anderssen's **george** account published an announcement explaining that the *Bangkok Post* had instructed ThaiVisa.com to stop republishing its articles outside an approved RSS-feed arrangement. The newspaper's publisher, Post Publishing PCL, had adopted a policy restricting how other websites could reproduce its reporting.[^11]

ThaiVisa.com responded by prohibiting members from posting or quoting Bangkok Post articles, warning that offending posts would be deleted and members could receive moderation warnings. The announcement also criticized the newspaper's declining web traffic while emphasizing ThaiVisa.com's continuing relationships with *The Nation* and *The Phuket Gazette*.[^11]

On June 22, 2024, [The Thaiger](../brands/the-thaiger.md) published *I've Been Denied Entry to Thailand – Now What?* under Anderssen's byline, branded as a **The Thaiger / ASEAN NOW Exclusive**. The article featured advice from [Tod Daniels](tod-daniels.md), whom the publication described as a visa expert.[^4]

Daniels advised travelers refused entry to Thailand not to sign immigration refusal forms, claiming this could prevent denial stamps in their passports. He also described paid intermediaries who could allegedly arrange entry after a refusal, including commercial **"safe entry" services** at airports.

The article demonstrates how Anderssen's editorial contributions helped bring immigration advice from ASEAN NOW contributors to The Thaiger's wider readership.

## Retrospective Interview (2026)

On February 2, 2026, Anderssen appeared on the *Hua Hin Today Podcast* in a 52-minute interview titled *George Anderssen: How ThaiVisa Became the #1 Expat Site in Thailand*.[^7]

The discussion revisited ThaiVisa.com's beginnings as a single webpage in 2002 and its subsequent growth into a major expatriate forum. Topics included early monetization through **Google AdSense**, reliance on volunteer moderators, management of trolls and viral discussions, and the forum's role during the 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami.

The interview provides a retrospective account of how Anderssen developed ThaiVisa.com's advertising business and moderation structure, both of which remained important aspects of ASEAN NOW following its ownership changes.
