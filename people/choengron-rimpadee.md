# Choengron Rimpadee

Police Major General Choengron Rimpadee (เชิงรณ ริมผดี) is a senior Royal Thai Police officer who commanded Immigration Division 2 at Suvarnabhumi Airport during the 2025 rollout of Thailand's Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) system before becoming deputy commissioner and spokesperson for the Immigration Bureau. On March 29, 2026, he publicly warned that at least 10% of foreign arrivals had registered through fraudulent TDAC websites that were not connected to the government's immigration system, with some charging travelers for a service that is officially free. Rimpadee directed travelers to the legitimate government portal, `tdac.immigration.go.th`, warned that personal information submitted to fraudulent websites could be misused, and confirmed that immigration authorities were coordinating efforts to shut down these operations and pursue legal action.[^1][^2]


## April 2025: Launch of Thailand's Digital Arrival Card

In April 2025, Choengron Rimpadee, then commander of Immigration Division 2 at Suvarnabhumi Airport, publicly explained Thailand's new Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) system ahead of its nationwide launch on May 1. The system replaced paper arrival cards with mandatory online registration for foreign travelers entering Thailand by air, land, or sea. Registration opened on April 28 for travelers arriving from May 1 onward.[^1][^4]

Rimpadee explained that TDAC registrations would be integrated with the Immigration Bureau's biometric database, allowing authorities to screen arriving foreigners against criminal records and identify potential transnational security threats. He also stated that travelers' declared accommodation details would automatically enter immigration databases, replacing portions of the previous manual data-entry process.[^1]

According to Rimpadee, the new system was intended to improve immigration screening while making arrivals more convenient for travelers. Its integration with official immigration databases would later become an important distinction in his March 2026 warnings about fraudulent third-party TDAC websites that were not connected to government systems.[^1][^2]

## March 2026: Warning About Fraudulent TDAC Websites

On March 29, 2026, Choengron Rimpadee warned that unofficial Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) websites were collecting travelers' personal information and charging unnecessary registration fees. He explained that these websites were not connected to the Immigration Bureau's registration system, meaning travelers could pay for a submission that did not reach immigration authorities.[^2][^3]

Public reporting of his warning identified websites including `tdac.info`, `ivisa.com/thailand/digital-arrival-card`, `thailandarrivalcardtourist.com`, `thailandarrivalcard2025.com`, `thailandonarrivalweb.com`, and `australiaasiagroup.com/thailand`. These were presented as examples of unofficial or paid TDAC services, although their inclusion did not establish criminal wrongdoing by every operator.[^3]

Rimpadee also warned that personal information collected through fraudulent registration websites could be exploited by scammers. He said the Immigration Bureau was coordinating with other government agencies to identify and shut down unlawful operations, but enforcement was complicated by websites operating outside Thailand. Immigration authorities had also sought assistance from Thai consular offices and international airlines to warn travelers about fraudulent registration services and direct them to the official government portal.[^2][^3]
