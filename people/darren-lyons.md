# Darren Lyons

Darren Lyons is a British media executive and CEO of [The Thaiger](../brands/the-thaiger.md), a Thailand-focused English-language news outlet. In September 2020, Khaosod English filed a police complaint against The Thaiger's management, alleging unauthorized reproduction of articles and photographs.[^1] In February 2024, The Verge investigated the website's publication of apparently AI-generated obituaries and suspicious author profiles, reporting that Lyons did not respond to requests for comment.[^2] In April 2024, Lyons announced The Thaiger's acquisition of [ASEAN NOW](../brands/asean-now.md), formerly ThaiVisa.com, from Choice Group Asia Co., Ltd.[^3]

## Business and technology background

Before becoming CEO of The Thaiger, Darren Lyons worked in foreign-exchange technology at the Royal Bank of Scotland. According to his biography on RE Marketing's website, he left banking in 2017 to establish MyMediTravel, an online medical-tourism marketplace that subsequently expanded into Thailand, Indonesia, and Vietnam under the MyMediGroup brand.[^4]

The biography also identifies Lyons as managing director of RE Marketing, a digital marketing agency serving businesses across Southeast Asia. He took over its management from founder Michael Kenner and was listed as an adviser to several other online businesses, including FazWaz, BoatCrowd, and YonderTours.[^4]

## Digital Broker Ventures and related businesses

[Digital Broker Ventures](../brands/digital-broker-ventures.md) identifies Darren Lyons as its chief marketing officer, alongside CEO Michael Kenner and co-founder Paul Trayman. The venture builder's portfolio includes [The Thaiger](../brands/the-thaiger.md), MyMediTravel, RE Marketing, and FazWaz, establishing a network of overlapping business interests rather than unrelated ventures.[^5]

Digital Broker Ventures states that it helped transform the Phuket Gazette's publishing operation into The Thaiger's digital platform. The Thaiger's own company history identifies Digital Broker Ventures as the new owners behind the newspaper's transition in 2018.[^5][^6]

The relationships extend beyond publishing. Digital Broker Ventures identifies Lyons as the owner of MyMediTravel, while RE Marketing's company biography identifies him as its managing director. Michael Kenner, who founded RE Marketing, is also listed as CEO of Digital Broker Ventures, connecting the two businesses through shared leadership.[^4][^5]

## Khaosod English copyright complaint

On September 16, 2020, Khaosod English filed a police complaint against The Thaiger's management at Prachachuen Police Station in Bangkok, alleging repeated copyright infringement. The newspaper identified at least 20 instances in which its articles and photographs were allegedly reproduced without permission, with The Thaiger's staff credited as the authors. Khaosod English also accused the website of monetizing the copied material.[^1]

The complaint was publicly announced the following day, with Khaosod English stating that a police investigation was underway. The report did not identify Darren Lyons individually as an accused party or establish the eventual outcome of the investigation.[^1]

## AI-generated obituaries and author profiles

In February 2024, The Verge investigated The Thaiger's publication of obituary-style articles about private individuals, including people who died by suicide or in accidents. The articles frequently contained generic, repetitive language, lacked original reporting, and appeared to have been generated using artificial intelligence.[^2]

The investigation also identified several staff profiles featuring apparently AI-generated photographs, including visible inconsistencies in clothing and accessories. Neither The Thaiger nor CEO Darren Lyons responded to The Verge's requests for comment. After reporters questioned the photographs, the publication removed the affected author profiles and their article archives from its website.[^2]

## Acquisition of ASEAN NOW

On April 19, 2024, The Thaiger announced its acquisition of [ASEAN NOW](../brands/asean-now.md), the expatriate news and discussion community formerly known as ThaiVisa.com, from [Choice Group Asia Co., Ltd.](../companies/choice-group-asia-co-ltd.md). As CEO, Darren Lyons publicly presented the acquisition as part of a strategy to expand The Thaiger's English-language digital publishing reach in Thailand.[^3]

The announcement stated that ASEAN NOW's existing staff and moderators would be retained, with plans to upgrade its technical infrastructure and expand its content offerings. The Thaiger also claimed that the combined properties would reach approximately five million monthly users, although this figure was presented by the acquiring company rather than independently verified.[^3]
