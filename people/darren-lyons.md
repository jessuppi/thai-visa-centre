# Darren Lyons

Darren Lyons is a British media executive and CEO of [The Thaiger](../brands/the-thaiger.md), a Thailand-focused English-language news outlet. In September 2020, Khaosod English filed a police complaint against The Thaiger's management, alleging unauthorized reproduction of articles and photographs. In February 2024, The Verge investigated the website's publication of apparently AI-generated obituaries and suspicious author profiles, reporting that Lyons did not respond to requests for comment. In April 2024, Lyons announced The Thaiger's acquisition of [ASEAN NOW](../brands/asean-now.md), formerly ThaiVisa.com, from Choice Group Asia Co., Ltd.

## Khaosod English copyright complaint

On September 16, 2020, Khaosod English filed a police complaint against The Thaiger's management at Prachachuen Police Station in Bangkok, alleging repeated copyright infringement. The newspaper identified at least 20 instances in which its articles and photographs were allegedly reproduced without permission, with The Thaiger's staff credited as the authors. Khaosod English also accused the website of monetizing the copied material.

The complaint was publicly announced the following day, with Khaosod English stating that a police investigation was underway. The report did not identify Darren Lyons individually as an accused party or establish the eventual outcome of the investigation.

## AI-generated obituaries and author profiles

In February 2024, The Verge investigated The Thaiger's publication of obituary-style articles about private individuals, including people who died by suicide or in accidents. The articles frequently contained generic, repetitive language, lacked original reporting, and appeared to have been generated using artificial intelligence.

The investigation also identified several staff profiles featuring apparently AI-generated photographs, including visible inconsistencies in clothing and accessories. Neither The Thaiger nor CEO Darren Lyons responded to The Verge's requests for comment. After reporters questioned the photographs, the publication removed the affected author profiles and their article archives from its website.
