# thaivisacenter.com

`thaivisacenter.com` advertises Thai visa, work permit, and company registration services under the name **Thai Visa Center**, using the American spelling of "Center."

It is distinct from [`thaivisacentre.com`](thaivisacentre.com.md), the former primary domain of [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md), which now redirects to `tvc.co.th`.

## Changing Corporate Identity

The website currently identifies **[Sawadee (2497) Co., Ltd.](../companies/sawadee-2497-co-ltd.md)**, incorporated on September 19, 2025, under registration number **0105568186315**, as its operating company.[^1][^2]

However, a February 2025 discussion previously associated Thai Visa Center with **[Sawadee (888) Co., Ltd.](../companies/sawadee-888-co-ltd.md)**, an older company incorporated in November 2014.[^3]

Thai corporate records classify Sawadee (888) as **abandoned since October 2, 2020**.[^4]

Meanwhile, `sawadeevisa.com` continues advertising immigration services under that older company's name, despite its abandoned corporate status.[^5]

A third entity, **[Sawadeevisa (888) Co., Ltd.](../companies/sawadeevisa-888-co-ltd.md)**, was incorporated in 2016 under the original name **Kindex Co., Ltd.** It adopted the Sawadeevisa name in December 2021 and remains active, although its latest reported financial-statement business classification concerns wholesale food products rather than immigration services.[^6]

These are three separately registered companies, not alternate legal names for one corporation.

## Duplicated Customer Lists and Promotional Claims

The connections extend beyond similar company names.

An examination on November 7, 2026 found that the **Our Pride** page on `thaivisacenter.com` and the **Our Clients** page on `sawadeevisa.com` each displayed 50 company names. **Forty-eight entries matched in exactly the same positions and order.**[^7][^8]

Both websites also publish closely matching descriptions of their immigration services, including passages about accumulated staff experience, honesty, and successful visa processing.

Even their business telephone and fax numbers overlap, with the numbers assigned opposite roles on the two websites.

Both prominently advertise the identical promise:

> 100% Success Rate, Money Back Guarantee.

Neither website publishes independently verified approval statistics demonstrating that every application has succeeded.

## Responsibility for Commercial Guarantees

The overlapping client lists, shared promotional language, and business contact information provide substantial evidence of a historical connection between these operations.

Yet the websites identify different legal companies, including one classified as abandoned and another incorporated only in September 2025.

**The central issue is corporate accountability:** customers are presented with essentially identical claims of experience, success, and refund protection without a clear explanation of which registered company originally delivered those services, whether customer relationships were transferred, or which entity is responsible for honoring the advertised guarantees.

## Outdated Immigration Guidance and Professional Success Claims

Thai Visa Center's published frequently asked questions contain outdated immigration instructions alongside its advertised 100% success rate and money-back guarantee.[^9]

### Incorrect Visa-Exemption Extension Guidance

The website states that foreigners who entered Thailand without a visa and subsequently apply for an extension will receive instructions to depart within seven days after their existing permission to stay expires.

However, the Royal Thai Embassy in Washington confirms that, under the tourist visa-exemption scheme effective September 15, 2026, eligible travelers may receive an extension of up to 30 additional days, subject to immigration approval.[^10]

The commercial website fails to distinguish ordinary tourist extensions from circumstances in which an extension application is unsuccessful.

### Reliance on Historical Immigration Regulations

The same FAQ repeatedly references Immigration Bureau Order 777/2551, dating to 2008, and older government website addresses.

The publication does not display a clear revision date or explain whether its instructions have been reassessed against subsequent immigration regulations.

### Commercial Accountability

These deficiencies are relevant to the company's claims of professional expertise and universal application success.

The available evidence does not establish that Thai Visa Center currently processes customer applications using these outdated instructions.

Nevertheless, its publicly accessible professional guidance contains information inconsistent with current government requirements.

This finding concerns Thai Visa Center, operating through thaivisacenter.com, and should not be attributed to the separately documented Thai Visa Centre operation without further evidence.

## Obsolete Work Permit Penalties and Misleading Application Guidance

Thai Visa Center's published work-permit guide continues to present outdated employment regulations alongside advertisements for its professional immigration services and a claimed 100% success rate.[^11]

### Incorrect Penalties for Unauthorized Employment

The English-language guide describes unauthorized employment as punishable by imprisonment of up to three months, a fine not exceeding 5,000 baht, or both.

However, on January 15, 2026, Thailand's Department of Employment reaffirmed the applicable penalties for unauthorized foreign employment:[^12]

- Fines ranging from 5,000 to 50,000 baht.
- Deportation to the worker's country of origin.
- Prohibition on obtaining another work permit for two years following punishment.

The commercial guide therefore substantially misrepresents the applicable financial penalties and omits significant immigration consequences.

### Defective English-Language Legal Guidance

The same publication repeatedly substitutes references to the United Kingdom for Thailand when explaining immigration and employment procedures.

It also cites regulations dating to 1979 and 1994, alongside historical migrant-worker arrangements from 2000.

These passages remain presented as practical employment guidance without an accompanying warning identifying their historical nature.

### Omission of Electronic Work Permit Services

Thailand's Ministry of Labour introduced its nationwide e-WorkPermit system on October 13, 2025, enabling employers and foreign workers to submit applications electronically, track processing, make payments, and schedule appointments.[^13]

Thai Visa Center's guide does not explain these developments and continues describing historical application procedures.

**The publication's obsolete legal references, incorrect penalties, and defective translations undermine its representations of professional immigration expertise.**

This finding concerns Thai Visa Center, operated through thaivisacenter.com, rather than the separately documented Thai Visa Centre brand.
