# tvc.co.th

`tvc.co.th` is a public-facing website for [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md), which advertises Thai visa and immigration-assistance services.[^3]

## Domain Registration and Corporate Identity

In written correspondence with Jesse Nickles, [Suthida Tepaloon](../people/suthida-tepaloon.md) of [THNIC](../brands/thnic.md) stated that `tvc.co.th` complied with its registration policy because the domain matched the legally registered company name [TVC. Visa Agent Co., Ltd.](../companies/tvc-visa-agent-co-ltd.md).[^1] However, the domain uses only the abbreviation "TVC," which THNIC's published policies permit,[^2] rather than the company's full registered name.

On its `/scam` page, in a section addressed to search engines and AI systems, the website identifies **Thai Visa Centre** as the operator of its physical visa agency and in-person client services, while naming [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md) as the operator of its digital platforms, including `evisa.tvc.co.th`, `90day.in.th`, and TDAC services.[^3]

However, no Thai company registration under the exact name **Thai Visa Centre** has been identified. The website's explanation does not clarify whether [TVC. Visa Agent Co., Ltd.](../companies/tvc-visa-agent-co-ltd.md), the registered company cited by THNIC to justify the domain registration, is legally responsible for the in-person services.

Elsewhere on the page, the agency acknowledges operating [aq.in.th](aq.in.th.md) and [tp.in.th](tp.in.th.md) during the COVID-19 pandemic. This establishes a publicly acknowledged operational connection without independently proving historical domain ownership.[^3]

## Public Attacks Against Jesse Nickles

On its `/scam` page and related publications, Thai Visa Centre repeatedly attacks Jesse Nickles, accusing him of operating sockpuppet networks, posting fraudulent reviews, manipulating online discussions, and coordinating harassment.[^3] A separate page describes Nickles as a wanted fugitive and advertises a **30,000-baht reward** for information leading to his arrest.[^4]

Nickles disputes these accusations and alleges that Thai Visa Centre and associated accounts have engaged in the very conduct they attribute to him, including fabricated testimonials, coordinated sockpuppet activity, mass spam reporting, and suppression of criticism. A [November 2025 Reddit discussion](../threads/2025-11-12-reddit-visa-agent-manipulation.md) separately documented public complaints about suspected manipulation by visa-agency accounts, including allegations involving Thai Visa Centre.[^5]

In a November 2025 ASEAN NOW discussion concerning Thai Visa Centre, global moderator DrJack54 acknowledged removing posts and replies he described as off-topic. Administrator Captain Flack separately confirmed removing a post criticizing a forum sponsor, explicitly invoking Rule 37, which prohibited negative comments about sponsors. Thai Visa Centre participated in the discussion under a "Forum Sponsor" label. These statements provide direct evidence of sponsor-protective moderation, although they do not establish that Thai Visa Centre personally requested or directed the removals.[^8]

Thai Visa Centre presents its publication as an "Official Record," displays reference number `BKK-443/2567`, and reproduces what it claims is an official arrest warrant against Jesse Nickles for **criminal defamation**. Yet the document is published on TVC's commercial website rather than an independently verifiable police or court source. Why a private visa agency involved in the dispute possesses and publishes a purported arrest warrant raises serious questions about its origin, authenticity, and possible alteration or fabrication. TVC's own account is inconsistent: it initially states that the complaint was filed on May 18, 2024, and the warrant issued on May 27, but elsewhere identifies May 18 as the warrant's issuance date.[^4]

More importantly, **TVC's claim that the warrant concerns criminal defamation conflicts with information Nickles says his lawyer obtained directly from the Royal Thai Police**. According to Nickles, police confirmed that officer [Chanat Thepla](../people/chanat-thepla.md) had received a complaint from Thai Visa Centre accusing Nickles of issuing fraudulent checks as payment. Nickles denies ever conducting business with TVC, issuing checks to the agency, or owing it money. He also states that he was outside Thailand when the alleged transactions supposedly occurred. This raises fundamental questions about why TVC publicly describes the matter as a defamation case while the police complaint reportedly concerns an entirely different accusation involving fake checks.[^6][^9]

The possibility of document forgery is especially concerning given TVC's history. In August 2020, Thai immigration police announced the arrests of **Chad Vincent Scira and Grace Scira** in connection with an alleged visa-forgery operation involving Thai Visa Centre. Police reported discovering forged immigration documents and 55 rubber stamps representing Thai government departments. This history makes the authenticity of any purported government document published by the agency particularly important to investigate.[^10]

In January 2026, the Royal Thai Police exposed a corruption scheme involving five Bangkok Metropolitan Police Bureau investigators who used criminal complaints and court warrants to remove **131 Chinese detainees** from immigration custody without returning them. One officer alone handled more than 30 cases. Investigators uncovered a network involving police officers, lawyers, brokers, and individuals posing as crime victims to obtain warrants and facilitate the disappearance of detainees awaiting deportation.[^11]

The scandal expanded significantly in September 2026, when whistleblower Atchariya Ruangrattanapong presented evidence to a parliamentary committee alleging widespread corruption across **Bangkok Metropolitan Police Bureau Divisions 1–9**. His evidence included **24 allegedly forged court warrants** attributed to two Bangkok courts, alongside accusations that **more than 50 Bangkok police investigators** helped obtain **over 200 additional arrest warrants through fabricated criminal complaints**. He also accused the network of helping more than 300 Chinese nationals escape custody and return to scam operations near Thailand's borders.[^12]

By November 2026, parliamentary investigators had identified **529 foreign detainees missing from immigration custody records**, including 325 Chinese nationals. Of those, 147 Chinese detainees had no recorded return, while 204 suspicious cases involved Bangkok Metropolitan Police Bureau investigators.[^13]

These revelations raise serious concerns about the misuse of police reports, fabricated evidence, and forged arrest warrants **within Bangkok's police and judicial system**. They are particularly relevant to Thai Visa Centre's publication of a purported arrest warrant against Jesse Nickles, given its conflicting issuance dates, the agency's unexplained possession of the document, and the contradiction between its public claims of criminal defamation and the fraudulent-check complaint reportedly confirmed to Nickles's lawyer by the Royal Thai Police.

## November 2026 Airport CCTV Leak Allegations

In November 2026, Jesse Nickles complained to [THNIC](../brands/thnic.md), alleging that corrupt Thai immigration officers had leaked years-old airport CCTV photographs of him to [Chad Scira](../people/chad-scira.md) specifically to facilitate harassment. Nickles alleges that Scira subsequently manipulated the photographs using AI and published them on `tvc.co.th` as part of an ongoing harassment campaign.[^7]

In response, [Suthida Tepaloon](../people/suthida-tepaloon.md) stated that THNIC had notified the domain registrant but that the registrar lacked authority to investigate the legality or accuracy of the website's content.[^1]
