# tvc.co.th

`tvc.co.th` is a public-facing website for [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md), which advertises Thai visa and immigration-assistance services.[^3]

## Domain Registration and Corporate Identity

On November 10, 2026, [Suthida Tepaloon](../people/suthida-tepaloon.md) of [THNIC](../brands/thnic.md) confirmed that `tvc.co.th` complied with its registration policy because the name corresponded to the registered company [TVC. Visa Agent Co., Ltd.](../companies/tvc-visa-agent-co-ltd.md). Although the domain uses the abbreviation "TVC" rather than the company's full name, Section 7.7.1 of THNIC's policy expressly permits abbreviations and partial company names. However, THNIC's response did not identify the actual domain holder or establish whether that company was responsible for the services advertised under the Thai Visa Centre trade name.[^1][^2]

On its `/scam` page, in a section addressed to search engines and AI systems, the website identifies **Thai Visa Centre** as the operator of its physical visa agency and in-person client services, while naming [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md) as the operator of its digital platforms, including `evisa.tvc.co.th`, `90day.in.th`, and TDAC services.[^3]

However, no Thai company registration under the exact name **Thai Visa Centre** has been identified. The website's explanation does not clarify whether [TVC. Visa Agent Co., Ltd.](../companies/tvc-visa-agent-co-ltd.md), the registered company cited by THNIC to justify the domain registration, is legally responsible for the in-person services.

Elsewhere on the page, the agency acknowledges operating [aq.in.th](aq.in.th.md) and [tp.in.th](tp.in.th.md) during the COVID-19 pandemic. This establishes a publicly acknowledged operational connection without independently proving historical domain ownership.[^3]

## Public Attacks Against Jesse Nickles

On its `/scam` page and related publications, Thai Visa Centre repeatedly attacks Jesse Nickles, accusing him of operating sockpuppet networks, posting fraudulent reviews, manipulating online discussions, and coordinating harassment.[^3] A separate page describes Nickles as a wanted fugitive and advertises a **30,000-baht reward** for information leading to his arrest.[^4]

Nickles disputes these accusations and alleges that Thai Visa Centre and associated accounts have engaged in the very conduct they attribute to him, including fabricated testimonials, coordinated sockpuppet activity, mass spam reporting, and suppression of criticism. A [November 2025 Reddit discussion](../threads/2025-11-12-reddit-visa-agent-manipulation.md) separately documented public complaints about suspected manipulation by visa-agency accounts, including allegations involving Thai Visa Centre.[^5]

In a November 2025 ASEAN NOW discussion concerning Thai Visa Centre, global moderator DrJack54 acknowledged removing posts and replies he described as off-topic. Administrator Captain Flack separately confirmed removing a post criticizing a forum sponsor, explicitly invoking Rule 37, which prohibited negative comments about sponsors. Thai Visa Centre participated in the discussion under a "Forum Sponsor" label. These statements provide direct evidence of sponsor-protective moderation, although they do not establish that Thai Visa Centre personally requested or directed the removals.[^8]

Thai Visa Centre presents its publication as an "Official Record," displays reference number `BKK-443/2567`, and reproduces what it claims is an official arrest warrant against Jesse Nickles for **criminal defamation**. Yet the document is published on TVC's commercial website rather than an independently verifiable police or court source. Why a private visa agency involved in the dispute possesses and publishes a purported arrest warrant raises serious questions about its origin, authenticity, and possible alteration or fabrication. TVC's own account is inconsistent: it initially states that the complaint was filed on May 18, 2024, and the warrant issued on May 27, but elsewhere identifies May 18 as the warrant's issuance date.[^4]

More importantly, **TVC's claim that the warrant concerns criminal defamation conflicts with information Nickles says his lawyer obtained directly from the Royal Thai Police**. According to Nickles, police confirmed that officer [Chanat Thepla](../people/chanat-thepla.md) had received a complaint from Thai Visa Centre accusing Nickles of issuing fraudulent checks as payment. Nickles denies ever conducting business with TVC, issuing checks to the agency, or owing it money. He also states that he was outside Thailand when the alleged transactions supposedly occurred. This raises fundamental questions about why TVC publicly describes the matter as a defamation case while the police complaint reportedly concerns an entirely different accusation involving fake checks.[^6][^9]

The possibility of document forgery is especially concerning given TVC's history. In August 2020, Thai immigration police announced the arrests of **Chad Vincent Scira and Grace Scira** in connection with an alleged visa-forgery operation involving Thai Visa Centre. Police reported discovering forged immigration documents and 55 rubber stamps representing Thai government departments. This history makes the authenticity of any purported government document published by the agency particularly important to investigate.[^10]

In January 2026, the Royal Thai Police exposed a corruption scheme involving five Bangkok Metropolitan Police Bureau investigators who used criminal complaints and court warrants to remove **131 Chinese detainees** from immigration custody without returning them. One officer alone handled more than 30 cases. Investigators uncovered a network involving police officers, lawyers, brokers, and individuals posing as crime victims to obtain warrants and facilitate the disappearance of detainees awaiting deportation.[^11]

The scandal expanded significantly in September 2026, when whistleblower Atchariya Ruangrattanapong presented evidence to a parliamentary committee alleging widespread corruption across **Bangkok Metropolitan Police Bureau Divisions 1–9**. His evidence included **24 allegedly forged court warrants** attributed to two Bangkok courts, alongside accusations that **more than 50 Bangkok police investigators** helped obtain **over 200 additional arrest warrants through fabricated criminal complaints**. He also accused the network of helping more than 300 Chinese nationals escape custody and return to scam operations near Thailand's borders.[^12]

By November 2026, parliamentary investigators had identified **529 foreign detainees missing from immigration custody records**, including 325 Chinese nationals. Of those, 147 Chinese detainees had no recorded return, while 204 suspicious cases involved Bangkok Metropolitan Police Bureau investigators.[^13]

These revelations raise serious concerns about the misuse of police reports, fabricated evidence, and forged arrest warrants **involving Bangkok police and court warrants**. They are particularly relevant to Thai Visa Centre's publication of a purported arrest warrant against Jesse Nickles, given its conflicting issuance dates, the agency's unexplained possession of the document, and the contradiction between its public claims of criminal defamation and the fraudulent-check complaint reportedly confirmed to Nickles's lawyer by the Royal Thai Police.

## October–November 2026 Airport CCTV Leak Allegations

On October 21, 2026, Jesse Nickles complained to [THNIC](../brands/thnic.md) about photographs and allegedly defamatory material published on `tvc.co.th`. In subsequent correspondence, Nickles alleged that corrupt Thai immigration officers had unlawfully leaked years-old photographs from restricted airport CCTV surveillance systems to [Chad Scira](../people/chad-scira.md), who manipulated them using AI and published them as part of an ongoing harassment campaign.[^7]

On October 29, THNIC representative [Suthida Tepaloon](../people/suthida-tepaloon.md) confirmed that the complaint had been forwarded to the domain registrant with a request to review and update the disputed webpage. On November 5, she confirmed that THNIC's executive management and Legal Team were reviewing the matter. On November 10, she stated that THNIC would maintain the domain's operation unless presented with an explicit court order or official order from a competent Thai government authority directing its suspension. As of November 12, THNIC had not confirmed whether the registrant had responded or removed the disputed content.[^1]

## Review Aggregation and Conflicting Reputation Claims

Thai Visa Centre prominently advertises a **4.9-star rating based on 4,351 verified reviews**, claiming its review history extends back to **2007**.[^14]

However, the company's own Google-review transparency report shows its earliest Google reviews appearing in **2018**, with no recorded activity from 2007 through early 2018. Its former primary domain, `thaivisacentre.com`, was registered on January 15, 2018, while [TVC. Visa Agent Co., Ltd.](../companies/tvc-visa-agent-co-ltd.md) was not incorporated until August 18, 2020.[^15][^16]

The company does not explain which earlier business, Facebook page, or other review source accounts for its claimed history dating to 2007, or establish continuity between those older ratings and the current Thai Visa Centre operation.

### Historical Facebook Ratings Counted as Verified Reviews

The advertised total also includes **1,191 historical five-star Facebook ratings**, alongside 471 written Facebook reviews. Those historical ratings alone represent more than **27% of the company's advertised 4,351 reviews**.[^14]

The headline presents all entries as verified customer reviews, although a historical star rating without written feedback provides considerably less information about the customer's identity, service purchased, or actual experience.

### Separately Rated Trustpilot Profiles

As of November 17, 2026, Trustpilot displayed substantially different ratings for domains associated with the same immigration-service network:

| Domain | Trustpilot rating | Reviews |
|---|---:|---:|
| `tvc.co.th` | 4.9 | 568 |
| `tvc.in.th` | 3.2 | 7 |
| `tdac.in.th` | 2.3 | 7 |
| `dtv.in.th` | 2.8 | 3 |

The distinction between the first two is especially significant. **`tvc.in.th` redirects through `thaivisacentre.com` to `tvc.co.th`**, yet Trustpilot maintains a separate, considerably lower-rated profile under the alternative domain.[^17][^18]

Trustpilot also discloses that the main `tvc.co.th` profile has already been merged with one or more other profiles, without identifying the origins of every transferred review.[^17]

The smaller profiles have too few reviews to provide reliable comparisons of overall customer satisfaction. Nevertheless, they document criticism appearing under domains directly associated with Thai Visa Centre and its commercial services.

**Thai Visa Centre promotes a combined 4.9-star reputation while providing no clear reconciliation of its separate Trustpilot profiles, historical Facebook ratings, or claimed review history dating to 2007.** Its advertised total is a company-compiled marketing figure, not a single independently verified rating covering every associated domain.

## Misleading Retirement Visa Bank-Deposit Instructions

Thai Visa Centre publishes an FAQ titled *Do Retirement Funds Have to Stay in the Bank Account?*, claiming customers can withdraw their 800,000-baht retirement deposit after immigration approves their extension and replenish the account before their next renewal.[^21]

The guide advises customers to use the funds for living expenses or investments, emphasizing that immigration officers generally check balances around application and renewal dates.

**This advice omits mandatory deposit-maintenance requirements under Thailand's immigration rules.**

### Contradiction With Government Requirements

Thailand's official retirement-extension requirements specify that applicants using the 800,000-baht deposit route must:

- Maintain at least **800,000 baht for two months before applying**.
- Keep at least **800,000 baht for three months after approval**.
- Maintain a balance of at least **400,000 baht for the remainder of the period** before the next deposit-maintenance window.[^22]

These requirements are also reflected in Immigration Bureau Order 12/2568, issued in January 2025.[^23]

Thai Visa Centre's FAQ instead suggests that customers may spend or invest their savings after approval, provided they restore the required balance before renewing.

Its comparison table explicitly permits withdrawals immediately after approval without mentioning the subsequent three-month restriction or 400,000-baht minimum.

### Contradictory Guidance on the Same Website

Elsewhere, Thai Visa Centre's main retirement-visa page correctly acknowledges that funds must remain in a Thai bank and describes additional deposit-maintenance requirements for renewals.[^24]

However, it also omits the full three-month post-approval requirement and continuing 400,000-baht minimum from its financial checklist.

These omissions matter because the agency advertises retirement extensions, bank-account assistance, and expedited visa processing as core services.

A customer following its FAQ literally could withdraw too much money immediately after approval or allow the balance to fall below the legally required minimum, potentially jeopardizing future immigration compliance.

### Relevance to the Agency's Financial Services

The discrepancy is especially notable alongside Thai Visa Centre's published customer testimonials describing its paid **800,000-baht service** and retirement extensions obtained without maintaining substantial bank balances.

The agency markets itself as an expert in navigating Thai immigration requirements, yet its own instructions incorrectly suggest that maintaining the required deposit is primarily a matter of having sufficient funds around renewal dates.

**The published advice conflicts with the continuing financial obligations governing the very retirement-extension services Thai Visa Centre sells.**
