# 90day.in.th

In a [May 2026 Reddit discussion](../threads/2026-05-reddit-thailand-90-day-online-reporting.md), [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md) publicly acknowledged providing paid 90-day immigration reporting services through `90day.in.th`.[^1][^2]

The agency described fees of 375–500 baht for in-person submissions and document delivery. The website identifies [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md) as the service operator.[^1][^2][^3]

The company's [agents.co.th](agents.co.th.md) website also lists `90day.in.th` among its immigration-related services, providing a separate public acknowledgment of the connection.[^3]

The discussion also identified `tm47.immigration.go.th` as Thailand's official government portal for 90-day reporting, distinguishing it from the privately operated paid service.[^1][^2]

## Formal THNIC Abuse Complaint (November 2026)

On November 12, 2026, Jesse Nickles submitted a formal complaint to [THNIC](../brands/thnic.md) concerning the publication of disputed criminal allegations on `https://90day.in.th/scam`. The website identifies [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md), registration number 0115562031107, as its operator.[^4]

Nickles reported that the page accused him of operating fraudulent online accounts and conducting harassment campaigns, while asserting that an active criminal arrest warrant existed against him. The page also directed readers to a separate publication on [tvc.co.th](tvc.co.th.md) containing purported criminal-case documentation. Nickles disputed these allegations and raised concerns about the accuracy and authenticity of the associated claims and documents.[^4]

**The complaint specifically requested an independent investigation into whether `90day.in.th` was being used in bad faith under Sections 12.4, 12.5, and 14.1.4.2 of THNIC's Domain Name Registration Policy.** Nickles emphasized that he was requesting an assessment of domain use rather than asking the registrar to adjudicate the underlying criminal accusations.[^4]

Nickles also requested that THNIC register the matter as a separate complaint from his existing dispute concerning `tvc.co.th`, identify whether the domain holder had been notified, explain which enforcement provisions applied, preserve the complaint records, and provide an independent case reference and written determination.[^4]

The complaint extended the registrar's examination beyond `tvc.co.th` to a separately registered domain allegedly distributing the same disputed criminal narrative. It raised the question of whether THNIC would independently assess potentially abusive domain use across multiple websites associated with the same business network.[^4]

## Non-Expiring Reporting Credits and Restrictive Refund Terms

[Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md), the operator of `90day.in.th`, markets prepaid immigration-reporting credits that **never expire** and can be transferred between customers. Its published pricing charges **500 baht per individual report**, reduced to **375 baht per report** when purchasing packages of four or more. The service handles Thailand's routine 90-day immigration reporting through in-person submissions and tracked delivery of the resulting documents.[^5][^6]

However, the company's Refund Policy imposes substantial restrictions on recovering money from partially used credit packages. Customers who have not used any credits may request a full refund, but customers who have used even one credit are entitled to receive only **50% of the value of their remaining unused credits**. The company attributes this reduction to discounted pricing and administrative expenses.[^6]

The policy provides a concrete example: a customer purchasing four credits for **1,500 baht** and using one would have three unused credits worth 1,125 baht. Instead of refunding their remaining value, the operator would return just **562.50 baht**, retaining the other 562.50 baht despite those three reporting services not having been performed. Separately, the policy states that individual reporting services become non-refundable once the company begins gathering information, preparing documents, or visiting immigration.[^6]

These conditions are significant because the company promotes advance purchases and unlimited credit validity as customer benefits while imposing a substantial financial penalty when customers discontinue partially used packages. Although the restrictions are disclosed in its Refund Policy, the prominent service descriptions emphasize pricing discounts and non-expiring credits without explaining the reduced refund value. **The arrangement raises questions about whether customers are adequately informed of the financial consequences of purchasing multiple reports in advance**, particularly when the underlying government reporting procedure is available free of charge.[^5][^6]

## Late Reporting Promises and Contradictory Service Guarantees

[Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md) promotes its `90day.in.th` service as particularly useful for foreigners who have missed their 90-day immigration-reporting deadlines. Its About page claims that the company's representatives can handle late reports in person, avoiding technical rejections and unnecessary immigration-office visits. The same page advertises **"No Rejections"** as a service benefit and states that reports may be submitted to an alternative immigration office when the operator considers that approach appropriate.[^7]

However, Thailand's Immigration Bureau distinguishes ordinary reporting through an authorized representative from reporting after the permitted deadline. Its official handbook allows reporting from **15 days before until seven days after the scheduled reporting date**, but specifies that foreigners whose reporting period has expired must **appear personally at immigration and pay a 2,000-baht fine**. Authorized representatives may handle routine reporting, but the handbook explicitly excludes overdue cases from that arrangement.[^9]

This distinction is particularly important because the company's advertising does not clearly explain when an allegedly late report can still be handled by an agent and when the customer must appear personally. The operator also reserves the right to select an alternative immigration office, whereas Thailand's published government instructions generally direct foreigners to report to the office responsible for their area of residence. The company's documentation does not identify the circumstances under which an alternative office would have jurisdiction over a customer's reporting obligation.[^7][^9][^10]

**The company's contractual terms also contradict the impression of guaranteed success conveyed by its advertising.** Although its promotional website promises to eliminate rejections, its Terms & Conditions expressly state that AGENTS CO., LTD. cannot guarantee particular outcomes from immigration authorities. Customers remain ultimately responsible for compliance with Thai immigration laws, while the company disclaims responsibility for immigration officials' decisions and limits its financial liability to the greater of **US$1 or payments received during the preceding month**.[^7][^8]

**These discrepancies raise questions about whether customers receive accurate information concerning the limitations of paid immigration assistance.** A traveler who has genuinely exceeded the permitted reporting window may still be required to attend immigration personally and pay a fine, regardless of purchasing the company's service. The published material does not establish that AGENTS CO., LTD. has improperly processed overdue reports, but its broad advertising promises are not adequately reconciled with official reporting restrictions or its own contractual disclaimers.[^7][^8][^9]

## Conflicting Data-Use Promises and Affiliated-Company Marketing

[Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md), the operator of `90day.in.th`, publishes a Data Protection Policy specifically addressing its 90-day immigration-reporting services. The document identifies the personal information collected as including **passport numbers, passport biographical pages, visa details, residential addresses, contact information, and passport photographs**. Crucially, its section on TM47 reporting states that this information is processed **solely for submitting customers' 90-day reports to Thai immigration authorities** under the limited Power of Attorney granted to the company.[^11]

However, the same website's Privacy Policy expressly authorizes using personal information for **marketing the products and services of AGENTS CO., LTD. and its sister companies**. It additionally permits sharing information with businesses under common ownership or control, including for promotional purposes. The company's Terms & Conditions separately require customers to acknowledge that their information may be shared with these affiliated businesses for marketing.[^8][^12]

Even the Data Protection Policy containing the specific TM47 processing restriction includes a broader provision permitting personal information to be shared with sister companies for marketing and promotional purposes. **Neither policy clearly explains whether passport and immigration information collected specifically for 90-day reporting is excluded from these wider permissions.** The documents also fail to identify the sister companies authorized to receive customer information or distinguish between information needed to complete an immigration filing and information available for subsequent commercial marketing.[^11][^12]

These ambiguities are especially significant given the documented relationship between AGENTS CO., LTD. and [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md), whose associated services include visa applications, paid arrival-card submissions, and other immigration assistance. Customers purchasing a routine reporting service may not expect their information to be available for marketing unrelated services across an affiliated business network. The published policies do not establish that such transfers have occurred, but they provide conflicting explanations of the purposes for which reporting customers' personal information may be processed.[^11][^12]

**The discrepancy raises questions about the company's representations concerning purpose limitation, informed consent, and personal-data handling.** A service-specific promise to process immigration records solely for government reporting is difficult to reconcile with broadly worded permissions for affiliated-company marketing unless the company clearly identifies which information is covered by each provision. The published policies leave that distinction unresolved.[^8][^11][^12]

## Contradictory Claims About Government Online Reporting

Thai Visa Centre publishes conflicting explanations of how its paid 90-day immigration-reporting service operates.

Its guide *90-Day Report Online* describes the company's service as using Thailand's official TM47 online portal, with deadline reminders and address verification. The same page advertises online submission through the government system for **375–500 baht per report**.[^13]

Another guide, *Important Immigration Forms in Thailand*, lists `90day.in.th` as an online reporting method alongside postal and in-person submissions.[^14]

However, the actual operator, [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md), describes an entirely different procedure.

### The Operator Explicitly Denies Online Filing

On its own `90day.in.th/scam` page, AGENTS CO., LTD. explicitly states that it is **not simply submitting the government's online form on behalf of customers**.[^15]

Instead, the company describes a physical proxy service:

- Customers purchase reporting credits and submit their information through a private dashboard.
- Company representatives visit immigration offices to file TM47 reports in person.
- Original reporting receipts are returned to customers through tracked delivery.

The service's About page confirms this arrangement and identifies the government's free online reporting system as a separate service.[^16]

### Contradiction With the Free Government Service

Thailand's Immigration Bureau operates its own online 90-day reporting system at `tm47.immigration.go.th`. Eligible foreigners can submit TM47 notifications directly through that portal without purchasing reporting credits from a commercial intermediary.[^17]

**Submitting information through AGENTS CO., LTD.'s private dashboard is not equivalent to submitting a TM47 application through the government's online system.**

The distinction matters because physical proxy submissions and online government applications involve different procedures, eligibility requirements, and processing arrangements.

Thai Visa Centre's promotional material blurs that distinction by describing its paid service as online filing through the official system, while the actual operator explicitly identifies the service as physical representation.

### Misrepresentation of the Service Being Sold

The evidence does not establish whether AGENTS also processes some applications through the official online portal.

However, **the companies' published explanations contradict one another**, and neither explains whether the advertised online-filing option is a separate service from the physical proxy arrangement.

Customers should not have to reconcile contradictory descriptions across affiliated websites to determine what immigration service they are purchasing.

The inconsistency is particularly significant because Thai Visa Centre promotes its immigration expertise while charging customers for assistance with a government reporting obligation that already has a free online submission option.

## Misidentifying Immigration Customers as Data Controllers

AGENTS CO., LTD., the operator of `90day.in.th`, publishes a Privacy Policy that attempts to assign data-controller responsibility to customers submitting their personal information through its immigration-reporting service.

The policy also describes circumstances in which AGENTS acts as a data controller.

However, its explanation of these roles conflicts with the definitions established by Thailand's Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA) and with the company's own statements concerning how customer information is processed.

**The discrepancy matters because the company handles passport details, immigration records, residential addresses, and other identifying information supplied by foreigners purchasing its services.**

### Customers Described as Data Controllers

The Privacy Policy initially acknowledges that AGENTS acts as a data controller when requesting personal information necessary to provide access to its website and services.[^12]

However, the same document subsequently states:

> We act in the capacity of a data processor in situations when you submit Personal Information through the Website and Services.

It then claims that the company does not control or make decisions about submitted information and that the user providing it acts as the data controller.[^12]

This language creates a misleading distinction between information requested by the company and information submitted by the customer.

For an ordinary immigration-reporting customer, these are often part of the same transaction.

The customer supplies passport details and other information because AGENTS requires those records to perform a paid service.

**Submitting one's own passport details does not automatically make the customer a data controller responsible for the commercial operator's handling of those records.**

### Thailand's PDPA Defines Responsibility Differently

Section 6 of Thailand's Personal Data Protection Act distinguishes between two roles:

- **Data controller:** The person or legal entity making decisions concerning the collection, use, or disclosure of personal information.
- **Data processor:** The person or legal entity processing information under the instructions of a data controller.[^18]

An individual supplying personal information about themselves is ordinarily the *data subject*, rather than becoming a data controller merely by completing a form.

A company can legitimately act as a processor when carrying out instructions for another controller, including in certain business-to-business arrangements.

However, that relationship cannot be established simply by declaring every submitting user to be a controller.

Section 40 of the PDPA also establishes obligations for processors acting under a controller's instructions and addresses situations in which a processor acts outside those instructions.[^18]

**The legal distinction depends on who actually determines how the information is used, not on which party typed it into the website.**

### AGENTS Makes Independent Decisions About Customer Information

The company's own policies demonstrate that AGENTS determines substantial aspects of its data-processing arrangements.

Its Privacy Policy permits collecting information through customer forms, public databases, and joint marketing partners.[^12]

It also identifies several company-selected processing purposes, including:

- Managing customer accounts and purchases.
- Operating and improving its services.
- Requesting customer feedback.
- Marketing AGENTS products and services.
- Marketing products and services offered by sister companies.
- Sharing information with affiliated businesses under common ownership or control.

These activities involve commercial decisions made by the operator rather than instructions independently determined by an individual purchasing a 90-day reporting service.

**A company deciding to use customer records to promote its affiliated businesses is exercising control over that processing purpose.**

The policy does not explain how those independent commercial decisions can be reconciled with its separate assertion that AGENTS merely processes customer-submitted information on the customer's instructions.

### A More Specific Promise of Limited Immigration Use

The contradiction becomes sharper when the Privacy Policy is compared with the company's separate Data Protection Policy.

Under its section addressing TM47 immigration reporting, AGENTS identifies the information collected as including passport details, visa information, residential addresses, contact information, and passport photographs.[^11]

It expressly states that this information is processed solely to complete customers' 90-day reporting obligations through their authorized representative.

Yet the broader Privacy Policy permits affiliated-company marketing, and the Terms & Conditions similarly authorize information sharing with sister companies for promotional purposes.[^8][^12]

The existing investigation documents the inconsistency between these processing purposes.

The additional issue is **which entity assumes legal responsibility for determining and enforcing those purposes**.

AGENTS cannot adequately explain its responsibilities by treating an ordinary customer as the controller of information that AGENTS independently decides to use for its own commercial activities.

The published documents do not establish that immigration records have actually been transferred to sister companies or used for unrelated advertising.

They establish conflicting explanations of who determines the permissible uses of those records.

### The Terms and Privacy Policy Identify the Wrong Website

A separate documentation error reinforces the concern.

Although customers access these legal documents through `90day.in.th`, both the Terms & Conditions and Privacy Policy expressly identify **`agents.co.th`** as the website governed by their provisions.[^8][^12]

The registered company is the same: AGENTS CO., LTD.

However, the two websites perform different public-facing functions.

`agents.co.th` is the company's corporate website, while `90day.in.th` provides a dedicated paid immigration-reporting service involving customer accounts, prepaid credits, and sensitive immigration documents.

The terms refer broadly to related services, which may be intended to include the reporting platform.

Nevertheless, neither document clearly explains why a customer entering an agreement through `90day.in.th` is presented with legal provisions expressly identifying another domain as the website being used.

**The operator has published contractual and privacy documents that do not consistently identify either the relevant website or the parties' data-protection roles.**

This is particularly problematic for a service whose operation depends on customers entrusting their passport and immigration records to a private intermediary.

### Why the Classification Matters

Data-controller and data-processor classifications determine important responsibilities under Thailand's privacy framework.

These include the lawful purposes of processing, disclosure obligations, handling of access and deletion requests, responses to objections to direct marketing, and accountability for third-party processing arrangements.

An immigration customer should be able to determine:

1. Which legal entity decides how their passport and immigration records are used.
2. Whether those records are restricted to completing their TM47 submission.
3. Whether separate information may be used for affiliated-company marketing.
4. Who is responsible for responding to personal-data requests or objections.
5. Which website and contractual provisions govern their paid reporting service.

The company's current documents do not resolve these questions consistently.

### What the Documents Establish

The published statements do not independently prove unlawful processing, unauthorized marketing, or a violation of the PDPA.

The company might act as a processor for some customers and as a controller for other processing activities.

However, its Privacy Policy fails to explain those distinctions with reference to the actual purposes and relationships involved.

**AGENTS tells immigration customers that they become data controllers by submitting personal information while reserving broad powers to determine how customer data may be used across its commercial network.**

Its separate reporting policy promises restricted immigration-related processing, while its general contractual documents contemplate affiliated-company marketing and identify a different website as the service.

These are substantive accountability questions, not merely differences in legal terminology.

The operator should clarify which processing activities it controls, identify any genuine controller–processor relationships, reconcile its marketing permissions with its TM47 purpose limitation, and correct the website identification in its customer agreements.

**For a business handling sensitive immigration records, unclear allocation of responsibility undermines its published assurances that customers' personal information receives appropriate legal protection.**

## Misleading Government System Comparisons and Rejection-Resolution Advertising

AGENTS CO., LTD. publishes a dedicated landing page targeting foreigners whose online 90-day immigration reports have been rejected.[^19]

The page reproduces the government's rejection-message wording before directing visitors toward its paid reporting service, starting at 375 baht per report.

### Government Application Tracking

The company claims that online applications can remain pending indefinitely without any means of checking their progress.

However, the Immigration Bureau's published instructions expressly describe an application-status feature within its free online reporting portal.[^20]

The instructions also explain several functions available directly through the government system:

| Feature | Official government system |
| --- | --- |
| Application tracking | Available through the applicant's account |
| Application results | Delivered by email |
| Reporting reminders | Sent 15 days before the next reporting date |
| Previous submissions | Accessible through the status interface |

The government's instructions describe a normal processing period of three working days.

Technical failures and administrative delays may occur, but **the company's categorical claim that applicants cannot check pending applications contradicts the government's published system documentation.**

### Presenting Ordinary Eligibility Restrictions as Technical Failures

The commercial landing page attributes online rejection problems to technical errors, document-format restrictions, and address-verification difficulties.

However, some applicants are expressly ineligible to use the online reporting system.

For example, the Immigration Bureau requires foreigners who have obtained replacement passports to complete their next report through the immigration office responsible for their residence, either personally or through an authorized representative.[^20]

Subsequent reports can ordinarily resume through the online system.

This restriction is an established administrative requirement rather than necessarily a technical malfunction.

The company's rejection-resolution page does not clearly distinguish between system failures and legitimate procedural restrictions.

### Unsupported Claims About Immigration Consequences

The same page warns that missed reports can result in airport departure difficulties, refused visa extensions, and adverse visa decisions in other countries.[^19]

Although overdue reporting can result in financial penalties, the company does not provide official documentation establishing the broader consequences it describes.

Its advertising also promises that purchasing the service will prevent missed deadlines and maintain a clean immigration record.

These assurances should be considered alongside the previously documented contractual disclaimer concerning immigration outcomes.

**The commercial landing page exaggerates deficiencies in the free government system while presenting paid assistance as protection against a wider range of immigration consequences than its supporting evidence establishes.**

The available evidence does not establish that the government's online reporting system operates without problems or that commercial proxy assistance provides no legitimate benefit.

It establishes specific discrepancies between the operator's promotional representations and the government's documented reporting features.

## Perpetual Content Rights and Contradictory Data-Deletion Promises

The Disclaimer published by AGENTS CO., LTD., dated April 2, 2025, contains a broad provision granting the company an indefinite license over information supplied through its website.[^21]

Under its Content and Postings section, the company claims an "unlimited, perpetual right" to reproduce, distribute, publish, and reuse submitted information.

Although the provision appears in a section discussing user-generated content, it does not expressly exclude private immigration documents uploaded through the reporting service.

### Contradictory Retention and Deletion Provisions

The company's published policies provide three different descriptions of its rights over customer information:

| Document | Published provision |
| --- | --- |
| Disclaimer | Perpetual rights over submitted information. |
| Data Protection Policy | Retention limited to service delivery and applicable legal obligations. |
| Privacy Policy | Permanent account and personal-data deletion, with an exception for required payment transaction identifiers. |

The Data Protection Policy additionally acknowledges that certain immigration records may require continued retention under legal or regulatory obligations.[^11][^12]

The account-deletion promise does not expressly identify those additional exceptions.

### Relevance to Immigration Customers

AGENTS collects passport information, residential addresses, visa records, and identification photographs to perform paid immigration-reporting services.

A general content-publication license may be appropriate for public comments or voluntarily submitted testimonials. Its application to confidential immigration documentation would raise substantially different privacy considerations.

The operator should explicitly exclude confidential customer records from its content-publication license, identify applicable retention obligations, and reconcile its account-deletion assurances with those obligations.

The available evidence does not establish that AGENTS has exercised its content-publication rights over private immigration documents or failed to honor a deletion request.

The concern is the unresolved scope of its published contractual provisions.

## Misleading Reporting Options Advertised to DTV Holders

AGENTS CO., LTD. publishes a dedicated landing page targeting Destination Thailand Visa (DTV) holders with its paid 90-day reporting service.[^22]

The page presents two alternatives for DTV holders unable to use the government's online reporting system:

- Personally visiting an immigration office.
- Purchasing the company's reporting service.

However, Thailand's published government instructions recognize four reporting methods, including submission by an authorized representative and registered mail.[^10][^23]

The company's promotional material omits these alternatives, presenting its commercial service as the only way to avoid personally attending an immigration office.

Customers can authorize another person without necessarily employing a commercial immigration agency. Registered mail also provides an alternative where permitted by the responsible immigration office.

**AGENTS uses restrictions on government online reporting to promote its paid service while omitting other officially recognized reporting methods.**

## Misleading Reporting Eligibility and Immigration Office Jurisdiction

The homepage of `90day.in.th` provides general instructions for foreigners wishing to complete their immigration reporting independently.

However, these instructions contain substantive inaccuracies concerning both the responsible immigration office and the reporting obligations applicable to certain long-term visa categories.[^24]

### Incorrect Immigration Office Instructions

AGENTS instructs foreigners to visit the immigration office where their visa was obtained.

Thailand's published immigration procedures instead generally assign reporting responsibility according to the foreigner's place of residence.[^20][^23]

The distinction is particularly relevant to foreigners who obtained immigration permissions in another province or subsequently relocated within Thailand.

Following the company's instructions could result in unnecessary travel to an immigration office that no longer has jurisdiction over the applicant's residence.

### Omission of Annual Reporting Privileges

The website also categorically describes 90-day reporting as a requirement for foreigners holding long-term visas.

However, Thailand's Board of Investment expressly identifies annual reporting privileges for certain visa categories:

| Visa category | Ordinary reporting interval |
| --- | --- |
| Retirement visa | 90 days |
| Destination Thailand Visa | 90 days |
| Long-Term Resident Visa (LTR) | One year |
| SMART Startup Visa | One year |

The official LTR guidance specifically requires eligible holders to submit form TM95 instead of TM47 after one year of continuous residence.[^25]

The SMART Startup Visa program similarly provides annual reporting privileges.[^26]

These exceptions are absent from the commercial homepage's explanation.

### Contradictory Guidance Across Affiliated Websites

AGENTS separately operates `ltr.in.th`, which promotes Thailand's LTR visa program.

Its commercial network therefore advertises both immigration categories carrying annual reporting privileges and a paid reporting service whose general instructions omit those privileges.

**The reporting website should accurately distinguish customers who require TM47 submissions from foreigners who benefit from alternative statutory reporting arrangements before encouraging them to purchase prepaid reporting credits.**

The available evidence does not establish that AGENTS has actually sold unnecessary reporting services to LTR or SMART visa holders.

## Contradictory Customer Age Requirements and Financial Data Collection

As of November 27, 2026, AGENTS CO., LTD. continued publishing contradictory eligibility requirements for customers purchasing its 90-day immigration reporting services.

### Customers Aged 16–19 Receive Conflicting Instructions

The website's Terms & Conditions establish a minimum service age of 16.[^8]

However, its Privacy Policy states:

> If you are under the age of 20, please do not submit any Personal Information through the Website and Services.[^12]

These instructions are incompatible for customers aged 16–19 seeking to use the reporting service independently.

The company's Data Protection Policy confirms that completing an immigration report requires processing passport information, visa details, residential addresses and contact information.[^11]

Customers cannot realistically purchase and complete the advertised reporting service without submitting personal information.

### Thai Law Establishes More Nuanced Consent Requirements

Section 20 of Thailand's Personal Data Protection Act establishes specific requirements concerning consent obtained from minors, including circumstances requiring parental authorization and exceptions relating to legal capacity.[^27]

The legislation does not establish a universal prohibition against processing personal information belonging to everyone younger than 20.

AGENTS should distinguish its commercial minimum-age requirement from statutory consent requirements and explain how eligible younger customers or their legal representatives may complete immigration reporting.

### Collection of Financial Documents

The company's Terms & Conditions also authorize AGENTS to demand government-issued photographic identification and potentially recent bank statements when it considers a transaction to present an elevated risk.[^8]

Its Privacy Policy additionally permits collecting personal information from public databases and joint marketing partners.[^12]

These provisions extend beyond the information ordinarily required to complete a TM47 immigration notification.

Section 22 of Thailand's Personal Data Protection Act limits personal-data collection to what is necessary for the controller's lawful purposes.[^27]

Although payment-fraud prevention may justify additional verification in appropriate circumstances, AGENTS does not clearly identify separate retention periods or processing restrictions for financial documents collected through this procedure.

**AGENTS advertises professional immigration assistance while maintaining contradictory customer eligibility requirements and broadly worded permissions to collect additional financial information.**

The company should reconcile its age restrictions, explain its procedures for parental authorization, and distinguish immigration documentation from payment-verification records.

The available evidence does not establish that AGENTS has improperly processed minors' personal information or collected unnecessary bank statements from particular customers.

## Non-Expiring Credits and Asymmetric Contractual Obligations

As of November 27, 2026, AGENTS CO., LTD. continued advertising non-expiring immigration-reporting credits while maintaining contractual provisions that could substantially restrict customers' remedies long after their initial purchases.[^5][^6][^8]

### Prepaid Services and the One-Month Liability Window

The company's commercial model encourages customers to purchase multiple reporting credits in advance.

However, its Terms & Conditions calculate aggregate liability using the greater of US$1 or qualifying payments made during the month preceding the event giving rise to liability.

Consider a customer purchasing four reporting credits for 1,500 baht and using them throughout the following year.

If an agency error occurs several months after purchase, the original payment could fall entirely outside the contractual liability calculation.

**The company's financial liability could therefore be contractually limited to US$1 even when the customer has prepaid for the affected service.**

The enforceability of this restriction remains subject to applicable law.

### Unilateral Amendments to Existing Agreements

The Terms & Conditions also permit AGENTS to modify its agreement at its discretion, ordinarily making revised terms effective upon publication.

Individual notification is discretionary rather than mandatory.

Continued use of the service constitutes acceptance of the revised agreement.[^8]

This creates uncertainty for customers holding unused credits purchased under earlier contractual conditions.

The published terms do not expressly guarantee that those credits will remain governed by the conditions applicable when they were purchased.

### Revoking the Power of Attorney

Customers may revoke the limited Power of Attorney granted to AGENTS before their reports are submitted.

However, the agreement makes previously performed services billable, while the separate Refund Policy excludes refunds once information gathering or document preparation has commenced.[^6][^8]

Consequently, customers exercising their contractual right to revoke authorization may still be unable to recover the corresponding service payment.

### Thailand's Unfair Contract Terms Act

Section 4 of Thailand's Unfair Contract Terms Act B.E. 2540 identifies several categories of potentially unfair contractual provisions, including restrictions on liability, unjustified nonperformance and provisions imposing additional obligations beyond those originally agreed.[^28]

Unfair provisions are enforceable only to the extent considered fair and reasonable under the circumstances.

The legislation is particularly relevant where consumers purchase services in advance but the business retains considerable discretion over subsequent contractual conditions.

### Commercial Significance

AGENTS should clarify whether prepaid credits retain their original contractual protections, explain the consequences of subsequent amendments, and establish liability provisions that account for advance payments.

The available evidence does not establish that AGENTS has retroactively altered existing credit packages, improperly refused refunds following revocation, or successfully enforced its US$1 liability restriction.

Nevertheless, the combination of indefinite credit validity, a short liability calculation period, unilateral amendment rights and restrictive refund conditions creates material uncertainty for customers purchasing long-term immigration assistance.

## Omitted First-Extension Reporting Provisions and Incomplete Penalty Guidance

As of November 27, 2026, AGENTS CO., LTD. continued promoting prepaid immigration-reporting services without explaining several important qualifications to Thailand's reporting requirements.[^22][^24]

### First Extension of Stay Can Satisfy Initial Reporting

The Royal Thai Consulate-General in Los Angeles expressly identifies a foreigner's first application for an extension of stay as equivalent to the initial 90-day residence notification.[^23]

However, AGENTS' general reporting instructions do not explain this provision.

Foreigners whose initial reporting obligation has already been satisfied through an applicable extension procedure may not require a separate TM47 submission at that time.

This distinction is commercially relevant because AGENTS encourages customers to purchase reporting credits in advance.

Applicants should confirm their applicable reporting dates with the responsible immigration office rather than assuming that a separate paid submission is necessary.

### Incomplete Explanation of Statutory Penalties

AGENTS' dedicated DTV reporting page describes fines of up to 5,000 baht for foreigners apprehended with overdue reports.[^22]

However, official consular guidance also identifies the possibility of additional fines of up to 200 baht per day until the reporting obligation is fulfilled.[^23]

Although these additional penalties may not ordinarily be imposed, they form part of the published legal framework.

The company's explanation therefore does not fully describe the potential statutory consequences of continued noncompliance.

### Commercial Significance

**AGENTS markets professional reporting assistance while omitting a provision that may eliminate the need for a separate initial submission and providing an incomplete explanation of applicable penalties.**

The company should distinguish initial reporting obligations from subsequent notifications and accurately communicate the statutory penalty framework.

The available evidence does not establish that AGENTS has sold unnecessary reporting credits or that any customer has incurred additional fines because of its published guidance.
