# dtv.in.th

`dtv.in.th` is an unofficial Destination Thailand Visa (DTV) information website associated with [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md) and [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md). Its published legal policies identify AGENTS CO., LTD. as the contracting business, while the main website promotes Thai Visa Centre's immigration services.[^1][^2][^3]

## Private Visa Qualification Website and Commercial Referrals

The website publishes extensive guidance concerning Thailand's Destination Thailand Visa, including eligibility requirements, visa fees, financial documentation, and application procedures. It also reproduces government announcements and offers an **unofficial DTV qualification test**. A prominent disclaimer states that the website is not operated by the Thai government and directs applicants to the official Thai e-Visa system.[^1]

Despite its informational presentation, `dtv.in.th` also functions as a promotional channel for [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md). Its homepage displays customer testimonials, advertises a **"Trusted DTV Visa Agent"** linking to `tvc.co.th`, and makes prominent claims that the agency has served more than **100,000 expatriates** and is Thailand's leading visa agent. These marketing assertions are presented by the website itself and have not been independently substantiated.[^1]

The website's Privacy Policy and Terms & Conditions identify **AGENTS CO., LTD.** as the contracting company. The Privacy Policy permits collecting names, contact details, account information, and other personal information submitted through website forms. It also states that information may be obtained from public databases and joint marketing partners, and identifies product updates, customer feedback, and testimonials among its permitted processing purposes.[^2][^3]

The Privacy Policy additionally permits sharing personal information with affiliated companies and service providers when necessary to provide services. Its affiliate-marketing provisions describe tracking purchases through referral links. The Terms & Conditions separately acknowledge that some website links may generate affiliate commissions. Neither document establishes whether the website's referral to Thai Visa Centre is commission-based or precisely how information submitted through the qualification test is subsequently used.[^2][^3]

This commercial relationship is significant because travelers researching visa eligibility may encounter a website presenting detailed immigration information alongside promotional material for a private agency. The website discloses its unofficial status, but **the corporate identity of its operator is primarily established through its legal policies rather than the prominent qualification-test and visa-information material**.[^1][^2][^3]

Thailand's Ministry of Foreign Affairs provides a separate official electronic visa system through which applicants can submit DTV applications. Thai embassies and consulates retain responsibility for assessing eligibility, requesting additional documents, and deciding whether applications are approved. The qualification test offered by `dtv.in.th` therefore has no established authority to determine an applicant's official visa eligibility.[^4]

**The central concern is the use of an immigration-information website to channel prospective applicants toward related commercial services while collecting information that may support subsequent marketing.** The available evidence establishes the website's contractual connection to AGENTS CO., LTD. and its promotion of Thai Visa Centre. It does not establish unauthorized data sharing, fraudulent visa processing, fabricated testimonials, or undisclosed commission payments.

Nevertheless, the arrangement warrants scrutiny of **operator transparency, referral relationships, customer-data handling, and the independence of immigration advice presented alongside commercial advertising**.

## Conflicting Service Disclaimers, Refund Terms, and Customer Data Protections

Although `dtv.in.th` prominently describes itself as an unofficial immigration-information website offering a visa qualification test, its published legal policies establish contractual provisions governing paid visa applications, financial transactions, personal information, and limitations of liability.

These documents contain several inconsistencies that raise questions about the services offered by AGENTS CO., LTD. and the protections available to its customers.

### Refund Policy Describes Services the Website Claims Not to Provide

The website's prominent disclaimer directs applicants to Thailand's official electronic visa system and states that its own service is limited to an unofficial qualification test.[^1]

However, its Refund Policy describes submitting visa applications to embassies and consulates on behalf of paying customers.[^5]

The policy establishes refund arrangements involving government application fees, unsuccessful visa applications, incomplete documentation, and commercial service charges.

**These provisions describe a paid visa-processing operation rather than merely an informational website offering an eligibility questionnaire.**

Substantially identical provisions appear on the affiliated [ltr.in.th](ltr.in.th.md) website, despite the significant procedural differences between the Destination Thailand Visa and Long-Term Resident Visa programs.[^9]

Both refund policies identify July 16, 2024, as their last revision date.

Neither website clearly establishes whether these provisions govern services sold directly by AGENTS CO., LTD., services provided through Thai Visa Centre, or commercial functionality that is no longer available.

### Contradictory Cancellation and Refund Conditions

The Refund Policy describes full reimbursement as available when customers cancel before their applications are submitted to an embassy or consulate.

However, a separate cancellation provision introduces a 12-hour and same-day condition and transaction deductions of between 2% and 7%.[^5]

These provisions overlap without clearly establishing which takes precedence.

The policy also promises complete reimbursement of visa-agent service fees following unsuccessful applications.

**The website advertises contractual refund protections without clearly identifying the commercial services or transactions to which those protections apply.**

This is particularly relevant because `dtv.in.th` directs prospective applicants toward Thai Visa Centre, whose commercial services are governed by separately published legal policies.

### Contradictory Age Requirements

The website's Terms & Conditions permit individuals aged 16 and older to use its services.[^3]

However, its Privacy Policy instructs individuals younger than 20 not to submit personal information through the website.[^2]

| Published policy | Age requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Terms & Conditions | Minimum age of 16 |
| Privacy Policy | Direct submission of personal information restricted to individuals aged 20 and older |

The Privacy Policy also acknowledges that parents may submit children's information during visa applications.

This distinction is particularly relevant because the Destination Thailand Visa program provides an application route for qualifying dependent children.[^1]

Thailand's Personal Data Protection Act establishes specific consent requirements involving minors rather than a universal prohibition on processing information belonging to individuals under 20.

**AGENTS permits individuals aged 16–19 to use its services while simultaneously instructing them not to submit the personal information potentially necessary to access those services.**

Comparable inconsistencies appear across other websites associated with Thai Visa Centre.

### Contractual Liability Potentially Limited to One Dollar

The Terms & Conditions attempt to restrict the aggregate financial liability of AGENTS CO., LTD. and its affiliates to the greater of:

- One dollar.
- Qualifying amounts actually paid in cash by the customer to AGENTS during the month preceding the first event giving rise to liability.

The agreement also excludes numerous categories of indirect and consequential damages, subject to applicable law.[^3]

Consequently, the contractual formula could produce a liability limit of only one dollar where the customer has made no qualifying payment during the relevant period.

This is significant because the same agreement requires government-issued identification and potentially a recent bank statement when AGENTS considers a purchase high-risk.

Thailand's Unfair Contract Terms Act limits the enforceability of contractual provisions that give businesses unreasonable advantages over consumers.[^6]

Section 373 of Thailand's Civil and Commercial Code separately invalidates advance exemptions from liability for a debtor's own fraud or gross negligence.[^7]

**Acceptance of the website's Terms & Conditions does not necessarily make its exceptionally restrictive liability provisions enforceable under Thai law.**

The same liability formula appears in legal documents associated with the [Thai Visa Centre client portal](evisa.tvc.co.th.md) and other immigration-related websites operated by AGENTS.

### Incorrect Classification of Customers as Data Controllers

The Privacy Policy recognizes that AGENTS acts as a data controller when determining why and how personal information is processed.

However, it separately classifies users submitting information through its services as data controllers and describes AGENTS as acting solely according to their instructions.[^2]

This explanation conflicts with other provisions authorizing AGENTS to independently determine how customer information is used, including for:

- Product and service improvements.
- Promotional communications.
- Customer testimonials.
- Feedback collection.
- Account administration.
- Enforcement of company policies.

The Privacy Policy additionally permits obtaining information from public databases and marketing partners.

Section 6 of Thailand's Personal Data Protection Act defines data controllers according to their authority to make decisions concerning the collection, use, or disclosure of personal information.[^8]

An individual does not automatically become a data controller merely by submitting their own personal information to a commercial service.

**Where AGENTS independently determines the purposes of processing customer information, its legal responsibilities cannot simply be transferred to customers through a contractual classification.**

The company's precise role must instead be assessed according to the processing activity concerned.

The discrepancies documented here are not unique to `dtv.in.th`.

Related investigations identify substantially similar contractual provisions across:

| Website | Documented concerns |

| [ltr.in.th](ltr.in.th.md) | Inconsistent service disclaimers and refund policies |

The repetition indicates that AGENTS and Thai Visa Centre use substantially similar legal templates across websites serving different immigration procedures, without consistently adapting those documents to the services being offered.

**The problem extends beyond outdated legal wording: prospective customers cannot reliably determine from these documents which services are being sold, which refund protections apply, or which responsibilities the operator accepts when handling personal information.**

The published documents warrant scrutiny of contractual transparency, consumer protections, and data-processing responsibilities. They do not independently establish improper fee collection, unauthorized data sharing, or actual customer losses.

## November 2026 Corrections to Previously Published Immigration Guidance

The English-language homepage of `dtv.in.th`, displaying an update date of November 24, 2026, explicitly acknowledges several deficiencies in its previously published Destination Thailand Visa guidance.[^1]

Among the acknowledged changes are:

| Subject | Previous website guidance, as described by the operator | Revised guidance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Employment documentation | Six months of salary slips | Documents specified by the current application checklist |
| Company registration | Embassy-legalized corporate documentation | Not listed as a universal national requirement |
| Spousal eligibility | Six months of residence in Thailand | Not listed as a national requirement |
| Dependent finances | Dependents could avoid the financial qualification requirement | Financial evidence or sponsorship required for visa applications |
| Extension fees | Potentially up to 10,000 baht | Visa issuance and extension fees are separate |

The revised website distinguishes national application requirements from additional documentation that individual embassies and consulates may request. It also distinguishes dependent visa applications from the Bangkok immigration checklist for dependent extensions, which does not repeat the 500,000-baht requirement.[^1]

### Revised Application Requirements

The website also incorporates the government's August 2026 changes requiring evidence of permanent residence and criminal-record clearance for DTV applications submitted from August 31, 2026 onward.[^10][^11]

These government changes should be distinguished from corrections to the website's earlier interpretation of existing requirements.

### Relevance to Commercial Immigration Advice

The corrections are significant because `dtv.in.th` simultaneously promotes Thai Visa Centre as a trusted specialist immigration agency and directs prospective applicants toward its commercial services.

**The operator's own revised guidance acknowledges that its earlier publications contained requirements and fee information that were not supported by the relevant national documentation.**

The revisions represent an improvement in the accuracy and transparency of the website's immigration guidance.

However, the available material does not establish how long the earlier statements remained published, whether customers received commercial advice based on them, or whether affected applicants were subsequently notified.

The corrections should therefore be preserved as part of the historical record without presenting the superseded guidance as information that remains currently published.

## Misleading 90-Day Reporting Guidance and Commercial Referrals

The dedicated 90-day reporting guide published on `dtv.in.th` promotes the affiliated [90day.in.th](90day.in.th.md) service, charging 500 baht for individual reports or 375 baht per report through prepaid packages.[^12]

The guide presents paid assistance as particularly beneficial for DTV holders who frequently travel internationally.

### Omitting Alternative Reporting Methods

The website presents personal attendance at an immigration office and its own commercial service as the alternatives available to DTV holders unable to report online.

However, the Immigration Bureau identifies four reporting methods:

- Personal attendance at immigration.
- Submission through an authorized representative.
- Submission by registered post.
- Online submission, subject to eligibility.[^13]

The official instructions also describe the documentation and postage requirements for postal submissions.

Although individual immigration offices may impose procedural conditions, the commercial guide does not explain these additional alternatives.

### Unsupported Restrictions on Online Reporting

The website claims that every departure and subsequent re-entry into Thailand resets online reporting eligibility.

It further identifies an in-country DTV extension as the sole exception allowing subsequent online reporting.

However, the reviewed Immigration Bureau instructions distinguish the resetting of the 90-day reporting period from eligibility to use the online system.

They also describe procedures for restoring online reporting eligibility following a passport replacement without requiring a DTV extension.[^13]

The operator does not identify an official DTV-specific regulation establishing its claimed exclusive exception.

Actual online reporting eligibility may depend on immigration records, local procedures, and system limitations. Nevertheless, the website's categorical restrictions are not substantiated by the official instructions reviewed.

### Frequent Travelers May Not Need to Report

The Immigration Bureau confirms that departure from Thailand resets the calculation of the 90-day reporting period upon re-entry.[^13]

Consequently, DTV holders who leave Thailand before their reporting obligation becomes due may not need to submit a report during that particular stay.

This qualification is particularly relevant because the website markets prepaid reporting packages to frequent international travelers without clearly explaining how their travel patterns affect the reporting obligation.

### Commercial Significance

The guide demonstrates how the DTV information website directs prospective immigration customers toward additional paid services operated within the same commercial network.

The available evidence does not establish that commercial proxy reporting is unlawful or that the service provides no legitimate convenience.

However, the omission of alternative reporting methods and the unsupported restrictions on online eligibility create an incomplete presentation of customers' available options.

## Questionable Traveler Discussions Contradict Government Border Closure Warnings

As of November 27, 2026, `dtv.in.th` advertised a live DTV discussion containing 576 comments alongside its promotion of Thai Visa Centre as a trusted immigration agency.[^14]

However, several recently published comments describe apparently successful international travel through the Poipet land border crossing between Cambodia and Thailand, despite the continuing suspension of ordinary cross-border travel.

### Purported November 2026 Border Crossing

A participant displaying an email-verification designation described plans to cross at Poipet approximately three weeks earlier.

Another purported traveler responded by describing a successful crossing during the preceding month.

The original participant subsequently reported:

> The Poipet crossing is settled: they checked the ticket, the cash stayed in the bag.

The website displayed this follow-up as having been posted approximately two weeks before November 27, 2026.

### Multiple Governments Confirm Continuing Border Closures

These accounts conflict with published government travel advisories.

The United Kingdom's Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office, in guidance current on November 26, 2026, confirmed that land crossings between Thailand and Cambodia remained suspended.[^15]

Australia's official Smartraveller advisory similarly confirmed that the land crossings remained closed.[^16]

Canada's government travel advisory, updated October 14, 2026, also identified the continuing closure.[^17]

**The purported traveler experiences therefore describe ordinary immigration crossings during a period when multiple governments reported that those crossings remained closed.**

### Reliability of the Website's Traveler Discussions

The discrepancy raises questions about the authenticity, accuracy and moderation of the discussions published by AGENTS CO., LTD.

The website displays email-verification designations beside participants' names, but verification of an email address does not independently establish that a person's claimed travel experiences are genuine.

This distinction is particularly important because the discussion platform operates alongside commercial immigration advertising and includes contributions from Thai Visa Centre's designated administrator account.

### Potential Safety Consequences

Inaccurate accounts of successful border crossings could encourage foreign residents to arrange unnecessary travel to a restricted border region.

The United Kingdom and Australia also maintain significant safety warnings concerning areas near the Thailand–Cambodia border.

AGENTS should investigate the contradictory travel accounts, explain how its discussion content is moderated, and prominently identify continuing border restrictions.

The available evidence does not establish that AGENTS generated these comments, fabricated the participating accounts, or knowingly published false travel experiences.

Nevertheless, the documented contradiction warrants scrutiny of the reliability of the website's purported traveler discussions.

## Incorrect Irish Visa Fees and Omitted November 2026 Consular Requirements

On November 12, 2026, the Royal Thai Embassy in London published dedicated guidance concerning Destination Thailand Visa applications under the workcation category. The embassy updated this guidance on November 24, 2026.[^18]

However, AGENTS' DTV information website continues publishing several contradictory or incomplete application requirements.

### Incorrect Application Fee for Ireland

The website's dedicated Irish application guide identifies the government visa fee as €350.[^19]

However, the Royal Thai Embassy in London processes applications from residents of Ireland and specifies a £300 workcation visa fee.[^18][^20]

AGENTS' own main DTV guide correctly identifies London's £300 fee, while its country-specific Irish guide continues advertising a different amount in euros.[^1]

### Omitted Sponsorship Restrictions

The website repeatedly presents financial sponsorship as an alternative to demonstrating personal funds of at least 500,000 baht.[^1][^19][^21]

However, London's updated workcation guidance restricts sponsorship of principal applicants to children younger than 16. Older applicants are required to provide individual bank statements.[^18]

Although AGENTS acknowledges that individual diplomatic missions may impose additional requirements, its British and Irish application guides do not identify this specific restriction.

### Criminal-Record Certificates From Multiple Countries

AGENTS' guidance describes criminal-record certificates from either the applicant's country of nationality or country of application.

However, London's updated requirements specify certificates from both countries where applicants hold permanent residence outside their country of nationality.[^18]

The distinction is significant because obtaining additional criminal-record documentation may require considerable preparation before submitting a visa application.

### Commercial Significance

The website prominently promotes Thai Visa Centre's commercial immigration assistance alongside its country-specific application guidance.

**The continued publication of incomplete country-specific requirements undermines the practical value of these guides, particularly when the website displays recent update dates and promotes professional visa assistance.**

AGENTS should distinguish national minimum requirements from the specific requirements imposed by the diplomatic mission responsible for each country's applications.

The available evidence does not establish that applicants have lost application fees or received visa refusals because of these discrepancies.

## Incomplete United States Application Requirements

As of November 27, 2026, AGENTS continued publishing a dedicated Destination Thailand Visa application guide for the United States alongside its unofficial qualification test and promotion of Thai Visa Centre's commercial immigration services.[^22]

However, the guide omits several important requirements published by the Royal Thai Consulate-General in Los Angeles, whose official instructions were updated on October 1, 2026.[^23]

### FBI Criminal-Record Certificates

AGENTS describes the criminal-record requirement without identifying the specific issuing authority or applicable document validity period.

The Los Angeles consulate expressly requires an FBI criminal-record certificate issued within the preceding three months.

A generic criminal-record certificate does not necessarily satisfy this requirement.

### Additional Muay Thai Certification Requirements

AGENTS' United States guide describes confirmation of participation in an eligible soft-power activity as supporting evidence for DTV applications.

However, the Los Angeles consulate specifically requires Muay Thai applicants to provide an official acceptance and support letter from the Sports Authority of Thailand, together with documentation from a gym certified by that authority.

AGENTS discusses government certification in connection with extensions of stay inside Thailand but does not identify this additional requirement for initial visa applications processed in Los Angeles.

Consequently, prospective applicants could incorrectly assume that an ordinary gym enrollment letter is sufficient.

### Foreign Document Certification

The consulate additionally requires supporting documents issued outside Thailand and the United States to undergo certified English translation and specified official notarization procedures.

AGENTS' country-specific guide does not explain these documentary requirements.

### Commercial Significance

Although AGENTS acknowledges that individual diplomatic missions may impose additional requirements, its dedicated United States guide primarily reproduces general national eligibility criteria.

**The website promotes professional visa assistance and unofficial qualification testing without adequately distinguishing general eligibility from the specific documentary requirements imposed by individual American consular offices.**

These omissions are particularly significant for applicants purchasing Muay Thai training packages or obtaining time-sensitive criminal-record documentation.

The available evidence does not establish that any customer has purchased an unsuitable training package, submitted unacceptable documentation or suffered a visa refusal because of these omissions.
