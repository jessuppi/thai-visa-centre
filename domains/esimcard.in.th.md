# esimcard.in.th

`esimcard.in.th` is a commercial retail telecommunications website operated by [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md) and explicitly associated with [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md).[^1][^2]

The website identifies AGENTS CO., LTD. as its operator and publishes the company's travel-business licence number, 11/10602.

## February 2023 Commercial Promotion

On February 7, 2023, Thai Visa Centre used its designated Forum Sponsor account on ASEAN NOW to advertise discounted DTAC eSIM products through `esimcard.in.th`.[^2]

The agency advertised a price of 180 baht using the promotional code `THAIVISACENTRE` and claimed to have a special commercial arrangement.

Forum participants **isaanistical** and **SuperSilverHaze** objected to the advertising. Following a complaint that the discount code did not work, Thai Visa Centre corrected the code and explained that the discount was ordinarily offered to its existing clients.

**The exchange establishes that Thai Visa Centre was commercially promoting telecommunications products to its immigration customers by February 2023.**

This predates the 2025 wholesale eSIM distribution milestone published on AGENTS' corporate website.[^3]

## Retail and Wholesale Telecommunications Operations

The retail website advertises a ten-day unlimited 5G eSIM for US$19, discounted from US$29.[^1]

The separate [esim.agents.co.th](esim.agents.co.th.md) platform targets hotels, travel agencies, tour operators, and other commercial distributors.

Together, these websites document AGENTS' expansion into both direct-to-consumer telecommunications sales and wholesale distribution.

## Customer Identification and Financial Documents

The retail website's Terms & Conditions authorize AGENTS to request government-issued photographic identification and potentially a recent bank statement when it considers a purchase to present an elevated transaction risk.[^4]

Its Privacy Policy also permits collecting personal information from public databases and joint marketing partners.[^5]

These provisions demonstrate that the company's telecommunications operations may involve collecting customer information beyond what is necessary to deliver an electronic SIM card.

The published documents do not clearly distinguish payment-fraud verification from telecommunications subscriber-registration requirements.

## Commercial Claims and Regulatory Transparency

The website describes itself as Thailand's leading eSIM provider without publishing comparative sales figures or independent market-share evidence supporting that position.

It prominently displays its travel-business licence, but that licence does not itself establish authorization to operate a telecommunications service.

The contractual relationships between AGENTS, its telecommunications suppliers, and retail customers warrant clarification, particularly in light of the company's subsequent expansion into wholesale distribution.

## Restrictive Consumer Terms and Conflicting Personal Data Protections

As of November 27, 2026, `esimcard.in.th` continued advertising a US$19 unlimited 5G eSIM package while prominently identifying itself as a trusted service from the team behind Thai Visa Centre.[^1]

However, the website's contractual documents provide considerably less reassuring explanations of customers' rights.

### Potential One-Dollar Liability Limit

AGENTS CO., LTD.'s Terms & Conditions attempt to limit its aggregate liability to the greater of US$1 or qualifying payments received from the customer during the preceding month.[^4]

This restriction could be particularly significant for travelers purchasing extendable eSIM services or arranging connectivity well before their arrival in Thailand.

The agreement also excludes numerous categories of damages, permits order cancellations after submission, and reserves broad powers to terminate customer access without notice or liability.

The publicly accessible storefront does not prominently explain customers' refund entitlements when an eSIM fails to activate, provides inadequate connectivity, or is cancelled by the supplier.

### Thai Consumer Protection Law

Section 4 of Thailand's Unfair Contract Terms Act B.E. 2540 provides that contractual provisions conferring excessive advantages on businesses may be enforceable only to the extent that they are fair and reasonable.[^6]

Furthermore, Section 373 of the Civil and Commercial Code invalidates advance agreements excluding a debtor's liability for its own fraud or gross negligence.[^7]

AGENTS' contractual limitations are therefore not necessarily enforceable in every circumstance merely because customers accepted its online terms.

### Perpetual Rights Over Submitted Information

The website's separate Disclaimer grants AGENTS an unlimited and perpetual licence to reproduce, distribute, publish and reuse information submitted by users.[^8]

Although this provision appears within a section addressing user-generated content, its wording does not expressly exclude confidential documents or information submitted privately.

This ambiguity is particularly concerning because AGENTS' Terms & Conditions permit the company to demand government-issued identification and recent bank statements when reviewing transactions.

Meanwhile, its Privacy Policy describes limited retention periods and the eventual deletion of personal information.[^5]

The company does not clearly reconcile those retention commitments with the perpetual information-reuse rights contained in its Disclaimer.

### Relevance to Thai Visa Centre's Commercial Network

AGENTS operates immigration reporting, arrival-card submission, travel and telecommunications platforms associated with Thai Visa Centre.

The expansion of these operations into retail telecommunications creates additional opportunities to collect identification, financial and contact information from international travelers.

**While promoting the trustworthiness of its association with Thai Visa Centre, AGENTS maintains broadly worded contractual provisions that raise significant questions concerning customer remedies and personal-data protection.**

The company should clarify its refund obligations, identify the circumstances governing service termination, and expressly exclude confidential customer documents from its perpetual content licence.

The available evidence does not establish that AGENTS has improperly retained bank statements, refused legally required refunds, or successfully enforced its liability limitations against any particular customer.
