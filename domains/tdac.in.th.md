# tdac.in.th

In a [May 2025 ASEAN NOW discussion](../threads/2025-05-01-asean-now-fake-tdac-emerges.md), [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md) publicly acknowledged operating `tdac.in.th` and identified [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md) as the operator of the website and [tdac.agents.co.th](tdac.agents.co.th.md).

The company's [agents.co.th](agents.co.th.md) website also lists `tdac.in.th` among its Thailand Digital Arrival Card resources.

The agency initially described `tdac.in.th` as an informational website offering no paid services. It subsequently announced an $8 early-application service for Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) submissions outside the government's standard 72-hour registration window.

These statements document the agency's publicly claimed operations and pricing, but do not independently establish legal domain ownership or government affiliation.

As of November 2026, `tdac.in.th` redirects to `tdac.agents.co.th`. On its `tdac.in.th/scam` page, the operator identifies AGENTS CO., LTD., registration number 0115562031107, and publishes a list of 29 competing TDAC websites characterized as scams or untrusted operators. The same website promotes its own $8 (270 THB) early-submission service while acknowledging that the official Thai government TDAC portal is free and that its platform is privately operated. This practice is particularly notable given Thai Visa Centre's May 2025 ASEAN NOW statements, in which the agency criticized another commercial TDAC provider as misleading and potentially unlawful, initially described its own website as purely informational, and subsequently announced paid submissions. The company therefore uses public scam warnings to discredit competing intermediaries while promoting its own paid alternative.

## Registration and Launch Timeline

Public WHOIS records date the registration of `tdac.in.th` to March 27, 2025, approximately five weeks before Thailand's Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) requirement took effect on May 1, 2025. On its corporate website, [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md) states that its TDAC Information Portal launched on April 2, 2025, followed by its TDAC Agents Portal on May 1, 2025.

This timeline shows that the company registered a domain using the name of Thailand's forthcoming mandatory immigration form and, according to its own published launch date, opened its information website on April 2, 2025, nearly four weeks before the official government portal began accepting TDAC submissions on April 28. During the subsequent rollout, [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md) initially characterized `tdac.in.th` as an informational resource without paid services, before publicly announcing an $8 early-submission service. The sequence documents how the domain evolved from a pre-launch information website into an entry point for the company's commercial TDAC services.

## Official Government Warnings

On June 18, 2025, Thailand's Immigration Bureau issued a public warning, published by the Tourist Police Bureau, urging foreign travelers to register their Thailand Digital Arrival Card exclusively through the official government portal at `tdac.immigration.go.th`. The announcement warned against fraudulent websites seeking payments or personal information and emphasized that TDAC registration was entirely free, with no money transfers required.

On November 24, 2025, the Royal Thai Embassy in Canberra issued another warning about fraudulent TDAC websites, reiterating that `tdac.immigration.go.th` was the only official registration website and that submitting the arrival card was free of charge.

These government warnings contrast sharply with the commercial practices of [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md), which operates `tdac.in.th` and promotes an $8 early-submission service. The company's website goes further by characterizing its own early-submission offering as the only legitimate paid TDAC service, despite Thai immigration authorities directing travelers to use the free government portal. This claimed legitimacy for paid intermediary submissions comes from the commercial operator itself, not from the Thai government.

### Government Fact-Checks of Commercial TDAC Websites

Thailand's Anti-Fake News Center published several fact-checks, verified with the Immigration Bureau, addressing unauthorized websites claiming to provide Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) registration services.

On **May 2, 2025**, the center rejected claims that the Immigration Bureau had launched a fee-charging arrival-card registration website for VIP travelers. The Immigration Bureau confirmed that the website featured in the claim was not affiliated with the agency and reiterated that its official TDAC registration service was free.

On **May 9, 2025**, another fact-check addressed a purported backup TDAC registration website. The Immigration Bureau identified the website shown in that claim as fake, warned travelers against entering personal information, and emphasized that it operated only one official TDAC registration website.

On **January 17, 2026**, the Anti-Fake News Center published a further warning concerning claims that the Immigration Bureau had introduced a new TDAC registration website charging service fees. The bureau again confirmed that `tdac.immigration.go.th` was its only official portal, that registration was free, and that other websites offering equivalent services were unaffiliated with the bureau and considered fake.

These repeated official statements contrast with the marketing of `tdac.in.th` and the commercial TDAC platform operated by [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md). The company's own scam-comparison page describes its paid early-submission offering as the only legitimate paid TDAC service, despite the Immigration Bureau consistently directing travelers to use its free government website.

The government fact-checks do not specifically identify `tdac.in.th` by name or independently establish that AGENTS CO., LTD. committed fraud. They do, however, demonstrate that the company's claims about the legitimacy of commercial TDAC submissions are not endorsements from the Immigration Bureau and must be distinguished from official government authorization.

## Deferred Government Submission and Data Retention

The $8 early-submission service promoted through `tdac.in.th` does not allow travelers to register their Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) with the Immigration Bureau before the government's permitted submission window. According to [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md)'s own website, applications received more than 72 hours before arrival remain pending until the company automatically submits them when the official registration window opens.

**Consequently, customers paying for early submission are purchasing a scheduling service, not earlier government processing or authorization.** The company collects their application information in advance, retains it in its private system, and subsequently attempts submission on their behalf.

This arrangement also has implications for personal-data handling. TDAC applications contain passport information, travel itineraries, accommodation details, and health declarations. Travelers submitting weeks or months in advance entrust those details to a commercial intermediary for a substantially longer period than would be necessary when registering directly through the official government portal.

The company's website advertises saved application drafts that do not expire unless deleted. Its privacy policy permits retaining personal information for purposes including legal compliance, contractual obligations, and dispute resolution, but does not establish a specific default retention period for pending TDAC applications.

Although AGENTS CO., LTD. offers automatic submission attempts and a data-removal function, these features do not eliminate the distinction between submitting information to a private intermediary and registering with Thailand's Immigration Bureau. The service's actual scheduling mechanism and associated data-retention arrangements are important considerations for travelers deciding whether advance submission justifies the additional fee and disclosure of sensitive personal information.

## Personal Data Collection and Marketing

The privacy policy linked from the `tdac.in.th` submission service states that [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md) collects visitor information including IP addresses, browsing activity, and personal information submitted through online forms. The policy also permits obtaining personal information from external sources, including public databases and joint marketing partners.

The policy explicitly allows using personal information to market products and services belonging to AGENTS CO., LTD. and its sister companies. Its terms and conditions further authorize sharing customer information with companies under common ownership or control for marketing purposes. However, **neither document identifies these sister companies, their associated websites, or which businesses may receive information collected through the TDAC service**.

Thai Visa Centre's own mailing-list policy acknowledges operating `tdac.in.th` as an informational website offering email subscriptions, while claiming that subscriber addresses are collected through explicit consent and are not shared. This creates questions about how those assurances relate to the broader cross-company marketing permissions published by AGENTS CO., LTD., particularly given the history of public complaints alleging unsolicited promotional emails from Thai Visa Centre.

These disclosures are significant because travelers using `tdac.in.th` to complete a government-required arrival card are providing personal information to a private commercial operator without a clear identification of the affiliated businesses that may benefit from its marketing and data-sharing arrangements.

### Contradictory Third-Party Data-Sharing Assurances

The Privacy Policy published by [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md) contains apparently conflicting assurances about disclosure of customers' personal information. Its *Disclosure of Information* section states that the company will not share personally identifiable information with third parties. However, the surrounding provisions explicitly authorize sharing information with subsidiaries, joint venture partners, affiliates, contracted companies, service providers, and sister companies for marketing purposes.

The policy separately identifies payment processors as third parties that may collect customers' contact and financial information. Although the company may intend its no-sharing assurance to exclude authorized service providers and affiliated businesses, **it does not clearly explain that distinction or reconcile the assurance with its stated sharing practices**.

This ambiguity is particularly relevant to travelers submitting passport details, contact information, and travel itineraries through the commercial TDAC service. The policy leaves readers uncertain about which recipients qualify as third parties and which disclosures are permitted despite the broad no-sharing statement.

The inconsistent wording does not establish that customer information has been disclosed unlawfully. It does, however, raise questions about the clarity and reliability of the privacy assurances presented to travelers.

### Data-Deletion Assurances

In a September 24, 2025 sponsored article, [The Thaiger](../brands/the-thaiger.md) promoted [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md)'s Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) platform while assuring travelers that they could request immediate removal of their personal information after approval. The [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md) account, posting as a *Forum Sponsor* on [ASEAN NOW](../brands/asean-now.md), similarly told travelers that they could delete all their personal information using a button on the application status page.

The same privacy policy promises that using its *Data Removal* function permanently deletes personal information and account details from the company's systems, retaining only payment transaction identifiers required for regulatory compliance. It does not explain whether this deletion extends to copies of information previously disclosed to sister companies, marketing partners, or other third-party recipients.

This distinction matters because travelers may reasonably interpret promises of immediate or complete data removal as covering all personal information collected through the TDAC service. The published documents leave unanswered whether affiliated businesses must also delete information they previously received.

A further ambiguity appears in AGENTS CO., LTD.'s separately published *Disclaimer*. Under *Content and postings*, the company grants itself an **unlimited, perpetual right to distribute, publish, reproduce, and reuse information** uploaded or otherwise made available by users. Because this provision appears alongside rules concerning posts and comments, it may be intended only for publicly submitted content rather than private TDAC applications. However, the Disclaimer does not expressly establish that distinction or explain whether exercising the company's *Data Removal* function affects any previously granted reuse rights. This leaves the relationship between its promises of permanent data deletion and its broadly worded content-reuse permissions unclear.

These unanswered questions do not establish that AGENTS CO., LTD. has failed to honor a deletion request. They do, however, expose a transparency gap between its public assurances of complete data removal and its permissions for sharing personal information with affiliated businesses.

### Unclear Data-Controller and Processor Responsibilities

The Privacy Policy published by [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md) provides potentially confusing explanations of the company's responsibilities for customers' personal information. It states that AGENTS CO., LTD. acts as a *data controller* when requesting personal information, but also claims to act as a *data processor* when users submit information through its services. In the latter situation, the policy identifies the user submitting the information as the data controller and states that the company does not make decisions about that information.

**This distinction is not clearly explained for individual travelers completing their own TDAC applications.** These customers provide passport details and travel information to a commercial service whose operator establishes its own policies for data retention, marketing, and disclosure to affiliated businesses. The Privacy Policy does not explain how an individual applicant would exercise the decision-making authority attributed to them.

Thailand's Personal Data Protection Act distinguishes between a data controller, which determines how personal information is collected, used, or disclosed, and a data processor, which handles that information under a controller's instructions. The company's published wording leaves unclear how those roles apply to ordinary TDAC customers and which party is responsible for decisions concerning their personal information.

The language does not establish that AGENTS CO., LTD. has violated data-protection law. However, it raises questions about whether the company's general-purpose Privacy Policy adequately explains its responsibilities when collecting sensitive information from individual travelers.

### Conflicting Age Rules and Children's TDAC Data

The Terms & Conditions published by [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md) establish a minimum age of **16** for using its website and services. However, the Privacy Policy instructs anyone under **20** not to submit personal information through those same services.

**These provisions create an unexplained inconsistency for travelers aged 16–19.** Although the Terms & Conditions permit them to use the platform, the Privacy Policy effectively discourages them from providing the personal information required to complete a TDAC submission.

The Privacy Policy acknowledges that parents may submit information concerning their children, but describes this possibility in the context of a *visa application*. Thailand's Digital Arrival Card is not a visa, and the Immigration Bureau confirms that children, including infants, must have their own TDAC submissions.

The company's documentation does not clearly explain how the different age thresholds apply to its TDAC service, or distinguish between minors submitting their own information and parents submitting information on their behalf.

These inconsistencies do not establish unlawful collection of children's information. They do, however, raise legitimate questions about the clarity and reliability of the company's published privacy protections for families using its immigration-related services.

## Payment Terms and Customer Protections

### Payment and Identity Verification

The terms and conditions linked from `tdac.in.th` state that [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md) may require additional identity and financial documentation when processing payments. If the company classifies a purchase as a high-risk transaction, its terms require customers to provide a copy of valid government-issued photo identification and potentially a recent bank statement associated with the payment card. The terms do not disclose the criteria used to classify transactions as high-risk.

The company also reserves the right to change prices, refuse orders, and cancel purchases at its discretion. These provisions apply to a private TDAC submission service advertising an $8 early-application fee, despite the underlying government registration being free.

Consequently, travelers using the paid intermediary could face requests for additional identity and financial documents beyond the information required for official TDAC registration. Such payment-verification demands originate from the commercial operator's terms, not from Thailand's Immigration Bureau.

### Misleading Refund Policy and Visa Terminology

The refund policy linked from `tdac.in.th` describes the company's TDAC Early Submission Service as allowing customers to submit their "visa applications" well before their travel dates. **This description is factually incorrect.** Thailand's Immigration Bureau explicitly states that the Thailand Digital Arrival Card is not a visa. It is a mandatory arrival declaration, and completing it does not constitute applying for or obtaining permission to enter Thailand.

The error is particularly concerning because [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md) markets itself as a professional immigration and travel-services provider while charging customers $8 for early TDAC submissions. Confusing an arrival card with a visa application in the service's published refund policy raises legitimate questions about the accuracy and reliability of the company's customer-facing documentation.

The same refund policy promises a full service-fee refund if a traveler cancels their trip entirely or cancels the service more than seven days before arrival. However, it does not explain whether customers are entitled to refunds when a TDAC submission is delayed, processed incorrectly, or otherwise fails to deliver the promised service. The policy was last updated on May 15, 2025, after the TDAC system became mandatory.

These omissions leave important questions unanswered about the protections available to travelers paying a private intermediary for a government form that can otherwise be submitted free of charge.

### Liability Limits and Early TDAC Submissions

The Terms & Conditions published by [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md) place substantial limitations on the company's financial responsibility to customers. They exclude numerous categories of indirect and consequential damages and limit the company's aggregate liability to whichever is greater: **US$1 or the amount the customer paid during the preceding one-month period**.

This limitation is particularly relevant to the company's paid TDAC Early Submission Service. Customers may submit applications weeks or months before arriving in Thailand, while AGENTS CO., LTD. postpones submission to the Immigration Bureau until the official registration window opens. Consequently, **a customer who paid $8 several months earlier could encounter a submission failure after that payment falls outside the contractual one-month calculation**. Under the company's stated formula, the applicable liability cap could then be only $1.

The Terms & Conditions also require customers to indemnify AGENTS CO., LTD. and its affiliates against certain third-party claims, losses, and legal expenses arising from their use of the services. These provisions place additional contractual obligations on travelers purchasing an intermediary service for a government arrival declaration that is otherwise available free of charge.

The existence of these clauses does not establish that the company has enforced them against customers or that every limitation would be legally enforceable. Nevertheless, the combination of advance payments, deferred government submission, broad liability exclusions, and a potentially minimal compensation limit raises legitimate questions about the protections available when the service fails to perform as expected.

## Comparison Claims About the Official TDAC Portal

### Approval-Time Terminology

The scam-comparison page published by [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md), which identifies `tdac.in.th` as one of its services, advertises an **“Approval Time” of “0–5min”** for both its own service and the official Thai government TDAC portal. However, the same page explicitly states that TDAC submissions are issued instantly, require no review, and cannot receive priority approval.

Thailand's Immigration Bureau identifies the TDAC as an arrival card, not a visa. Official government guidance describes the result of submission as a confirmation or acknowledgment email that travelers present to immigration officers upon arrival. Receiving that confirmation does not constitute immigration approval or guarantee permission to enter Thailand.

The terminology is particularly inconsistent with a November 11, 2025 article published by [The Thaiger](../brands/the-thaiger.md) promoting AGENTS CO., LTD. That article identifies “fast approval” messaging as a warning sign associated with misleading TDAC websites, while the company's own comparison table uses approval time as a selling point.

Although the comparison table applies the same terminology to the government portal, describing TDAC confirmation as “approval” risks confusing routine arrival-card registration with an immigration authorization process. The discrepancy illustrates a lack of precision in the company's marketing and its criticism of competing services.

### Editing and Resubmission

The scam-comparison page operated by [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md) presents its TDAC platform as offering superior editing and resubmission capabilities compared with Thailand's official government website.

The comparison marks *Resubmission Functionality* as unavailable on `tdac.immigration.go.th`, while presenting it as supported by the company's own platform. It also describes the government portal's editing capabilities as *Partial Support*, compared with *Full Support* through AGENTS CO., LTD.

**However, Thailand's Immigration Bureau explicitly confirms that travelers can submit a TDAC multiple times and that the most recent valid submission takes precedence.** Its official guidance also explains that travelers can update information such as flight and accommodation details. If certain personal information cannot be edited, including passport numbers or names, travelers can submit a replacement application containing the correct information.

The government's system does have limitations, including restrictions on editing certain fields and the inability to resume an unfinished application. However, these limitations should not be confused with an inability to submit corrected or replacement TDAC forms.

The company's comparison does not explain whether *Resubmission Functionality* refers specifically to reusing previously entered information or to the ability to submit another application. Without that distinction, its negative assessment of the government portal risks misleading travelers about capabilities already available through the free official service.

This is particularly relevant because the comparison promotes AGENTS CO., LTD.'s additional features as reasons to use its privately operated platform instead of submitting directly to Thailand's Immigration Bureau.

### Government Email Support

On its *About our TDAC services* page, [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md) compares its commercial platform with Thailand's official Immigration Bureau website. The comparison labels the government's email support **“Unresponsive”** while describing the company's own email support as *Available*.

However, the company provides no supporting evidence for this assessment, such as documented inquiries, response times, testing dates, or independently verified customer-service comparisons.

Thailand's Immigration Bureau publishes an official TDAC support email address in its FAQ and directs travelers to its user guide and immigration personnel for assistance. Although the existence of these support channels does not establish how quickly inquiries are answered, it demonstrates that the government provides assistance options.

**Describing government email support as unresponsive is a claim about service performance, not an objective feature comparison.** Without supporting evidence or a disclosed testing methodology, readers cannot determine whether the assessment reflects representative experience, isolated incidents, or the commercial operator's own judgment.

The unsupported characterization is particularly relevant because AGENTS CO., LTD. uses the comparison to promote its privately operated TDAC submission service over the free government alternative.

### LTR Visa Documents

On its *About our TDAC services* page, [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md) promotes the individual TDAC documents generated by its commercial platform as an advantage over Thailand's official government system. The company describes individual documents as crucial for Long-Term Resident (LTR) visa applications, specifically claiming that Thailand's Board of Investment (BOI) requires them.

The BOI's official guidance does identify the Thailand Digital Arrival Card as a relevant document for certain LTR visa issuance procedures. Applicants who entered Thailand after May 1, 2025, may need to provide their TDAC information when completing visa issuance in Thailand. The BOI also instructs applicants to enter their TDAC number in the field previously used for the TM.6 arrival card.

**However, the BOI does not state that applicants must obtain separately generated TDAC documents through a commercial intermediary or that confirmations issued through the government's group-submission process are unacceptable.** Thailand's Immigration Bureau expressly allows families and groups of up to ten travelers to submit their TDAC information together through its free official portal.

AGENTS CO., LTD.'s marketing therefore connects a legitimate immigration documentation requirement with an asserted advantage of its own service without establishing that the government's free submission process is insufficient. The distinction matters because travelers could interpret the company's description as suggesting that its individual-document service is necessary for an LTR visa, rather than an optional convenience.

This does not establish that separately generated TDAC documents lack practical value. It does, however, raise questions about whether the company's comparison accurately distinguishes government requirements from features offered by its commercial platform.

### Group Submission Limits

On its *About our TDAC services* page, [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md) compares the official Thai government TDAC portal's traveler limit of **10** with its own platform's supposedly *Unlimited* capacity. The company promotes unlimited group submissions as a particular advantage for travel agents and tour operators, including an example of processing 30 travelers three weeks before arrival for $16.

**However, Thailand's Immigration Bureau specifies a maximum of ten travelers per application, not a maximum of ten travelers per group, tour operator, or entire trip.** Its official guidance also confirms that travel agents and tour leaders may submit TDAC applications on behalf of their clients.

Consequently, larger groups are not inherently excluded from using the free government portal. They can be divided into separate submissions of up to ten travelers each during the permitted registration window. The company's comparison does not clearly distinguish this per-application limit from an overall restriction on group size.

AGENTS CO., LTD. may offer genuine administrative conveniences by handling larger groups through a single interface and accepting information before the government's submission window opens. However, **those conveniences should not be confused with an exclusive ability to register groups exceeding ten travelers**.

The published comparison risks exaggerating a limitation of the official government system while promoting the commercial platform as a necessary alternative for larger groups.

## Claims and Inaccuracies on the TDAC Scam-Warning Page

### Competitor Blacklist and Unsupported Fraud Classifications

The scam-warning page operated by [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md) publishes a blacklist of 29 competing Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) websites, categorizing them as scams or untrusted operators. Several entries are explicitly labeled *CONFIRMED SCAM*, while others carry warnings such as *FOREIGN ENTITY AVOID* or accusations involving fake advertisements and misleading fees.

Although the page includes selected advertising records and links to external reports, it does not establish a consistent methodology for distinguishing confirmed fraud from expensive, unreliable, or otherwise undesirable commercial services. Nor does it identify official findings supporting every fraud classification.

**The company's own pricing claims also contain a demonstrable contradiction.** Its warning states that all untrusted operators charge between 900 and 4,000 THB for services available free through official channels. However, the same comparison table lists `tdac.asia` as an untrusted foreign operator charging just $10 (340 THB) for TDAC submissions.

Charging substantial fees for a free government form can legitimately raise consumer-protection concerns, particularly where pricing or government affiliation is misrepresented. Nevertheless, operating from another country, advertising on Google, or charging a service fee does not independently establish fraud.

The distinction is especially important because AGENTS CO., LTD. presents its own commercial platform alongside Thailand's government portal as a trusted submission option while categorizing competing intermediaries negatively.

These classifications represent the commercial operator's assessments and should not be mistaken for an official Thai government blacklist or independently verified findings of criminal wrongdoing.

### Misleading Domain-Name Trust Signals

On its TDAC scam-warning page, [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md) advises travelers that legitimate Thai services use `.go.th`, `.co.th`, or `.in.th` domains, describing the latter two as domains for registered Thai businesses. It also warns against websites without a `.th` extension while identifying its own `tdac.in.th` and `agents.co.th` platforms as trusted submission options.

**This characterization is misleading, particularly regarding `.in.th`.** According to the Thai Network Information Center (THNIC) Foundation's published registration policy, `.in.th` domains are available to eligible individuals and non-juristic entities, as well as registered companies. Registration of an `.in.th` domain therefore does not, by itself, establish that its holder operates a registered Thai business.

More importantly, neither `.co.th` nor `.in.th` registration demonstrates authorization, endorsement, or recognition by Thailand's Immigration Bureau. Conversely, the absence of a `.th` extension does not independently establish that a TDAC intermediary is fraudulent.

By presenting Thai domain extensions as indicators of legitimacy while operating websites under those extensions, AGENTS CO., LTD. promotes a trust criterion that favors its own services without establishing any corresponding authority to process government immigration submissions.

The claimed Trustpilot business profile for `tdac.in.th` lists **United States** under its *Contact info*, despite the operator's own TDAC scam-comparison page identifying **AGENTS CO., LTD.** as a registered business in Samut Prakan, Thailand. This discrepancy is noteworthy because the company promotes Thai registration and domain extensions as indicators of legitimacy while warning travelers against competing TDAC websites described as foreign entities. Trustpilot does not disclose whether the displayed country was supplied by the operator or determined through another process, so the listing does not independently establish where the company operates. Nevertheless, the U.S. designation conflicts with the Thai location emphasized in the operator's public marketing.

### Incorrect Thai-Language TDAC Deadline Instructions

The Thai-language TDAC scam-warning page operated by [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md) repeatedly tells travelers that official TDAC submissions are free **“ภายใน 72 ชั่วโมงหลังจากการมาถึง”**, meaning *within 72 hours after arrival*. This wording appears in the page's explanation of legitimate services, its scam-prevention checklist, and its closing promotion of the company's own platform.

**The instruction reverses the actual submission deadline.** Thailand's Immigration Bureau requires travelers to complete their TDAC before entering Thailand, within three days before arrival, including the arrival date. Official Thai government guidance similarly specifies the 72-hour period *before* travel, not afterward.

The inconsistency is particularly notable because another paragraph on AGENTS CO., LTD.'s same Thai-language page correctly refers to submissions within 72 hours *before* arrival. The website therefore presents contradictory instructions about a basic immigration requirement.

AGENTS CO., LTD. advertises support for 76 languages and identifies poor translations as a warning sign associated with fraudulent TDAC websites. Yet its own Thai-language consumer-protection guidance contains a substantive translation error concerning when travelers must submit their arrival cards.

AGENTS CO., LTD.'s published Terms & Conditions introduce an additional concern. Under *Accuracy of information*, the company acknowledges that its website may contain errors or omissions and reserves the right to correct them. However, it also states that it has no general obligation to update, amend, or clarify published information except where required by law. **This reservation is particularly noteworthy alongside demonstrably incorrect immigration instructions on a page that promotes the company's reliability while criticizing competing TDAC websites for providing inaccurate information.**

The mistake does not establish deliberate deception or demonstrate that any traveler actually missed the deadline. Nevertheless, providing incorrect timing instructions on a website marketed as a reliable TDAC information resource raises legitimate concerns about the accuracy of its multilingual guidance.

The article also advised travelers to look for `.co.th` or `.in.th` domains when assessing third-party TDAC providers, presenting these extensions as indicators of legitimate Thai business registration. The article additionally included a section titled *Legal notice regarding defamation activity*, which named **Jesse Nickles**, accused him of using numerous anonymous accounts to criticize AGENTS CO., LTD., and asserted that a criminal defamation case and arrest warrant existed against him. Notably, AGENTS CO., LTD.'s October 2026 Trustpilot responses cited **BKK-443/2567**, sharing the **443/2567** identifier used in The Thaiger article. This overlap connects the allegations made in the article with the operator's later responses to consumer criticism, although it does not independently establish that both references concern the same legal proceeding, who authored the article, or whether the criminal allegations are substantiated.

The publication illustrates how The Thaiger's ostensibly informational TDAC coverage combined commercial promotion, criticism of competing intermediaries, and personal allegations against a named critic. Its presentation as a travel guide raises questions about whether readers could clearly distinguish independently verified reporting from material advancing the interests of AGENTS CO., LTD.

These publications document repeated promotion of the privately operated TDAC platform through The Thaiger's commercial publishing channels and ASEAN NOW's sponsored forum environment. That coverage included misleading *official* terminology and unsupported criticism of the government system, even after Thai immigration authorities warned travelers to use the free official portal.

## Trustpilot Review Responses

### Accusations Against Negative Reviewers

The claimed Trustpilot profile for `tdac.in.th` contains several negative reviews describing the website as misleading, criticizing its charges for Thailand's free Digital Arrival Card, and warning travelers about submitting personal information to unofficial intermediaries.

On October 17, 2026, the operator posted substantially identical responses to multiple negative reviews, alleging that the complaints were part of a coordinated harassment campaign involving fabricated accounts. These responses invoked a purported criminal case, **BKK-443/2567**, and claimed that the individual responsible was subject to an arrest warrant and was evading authorities. The responses provided no review-specific evidence connecting the individual reviewers to those allegations.

After a reviewer challenged the accusations and stated that their criticism reflected a genuine experience, the operator acknowledged that the review might have been mistakenly classified as fraudulent and apologized. That reversal is significant because the operator had publicly associated negative reviews with alleged criminal harassment without providing evidence connecting those specific reviewers to the allegations.

### Conflicting Published Prices

In its October 2026 response to the same reviewer, the operator stated that its maximum early-submission charge was **US$7**. However, the company's own `tdac.in.th/scam` page advertised early submissions at **US$8 (270 THB)**, and its corporate website also listed the $8 price.

This was not a newly advertised $8 rate: a June 13, 2025 press release published by The Thaiger had already stated that AGENTS CO., LTD.'s early-submission pricing reached US$8 per applicant.

These contradictory statements concern the same commercial TDAC service and undermine the operator's repeated claims of transparent pricing.

The combination of inconsistent pricing information and aggressive accusations against critical reviewers is especially concerning for a private service that collects travelers' sensitive immigration information while promoting itself as a trusted alternative to the free government system.
