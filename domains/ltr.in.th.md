# ltr.in.th

`ltr.in.th` is a privately operated Long-Term Resident (LTR) visa information website associated with [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md) and [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md). Its published legal policies identify AGENTS CO., LTD. as the contracting business, while its homepage promotes Thai Visa Centre's immigration services and compares the agency with businesses certified by Thailand's Board of Investment (BOI).[^1][^2][^3]

## Commercial Promotion and Certified-Agent Comparisons

The website provides extensive information about Thailand's LTR visa program, reproduces government announcements, and offers an unofficial visa qualification test. It prominently advertises Thai Visa Centre as a **"Trusted LTR Visa Agent"**, accompanied by customer testimonials and claims that the agency has served more than **100,000 expatriates** and is Thailand's leading visa agent. These promotional assertions are made by the commercially connected website and have not been independently substantiated.[^1]

Thailand's Board of Investment maintains an official list of certified agencies authorized to provide LTR visa application assistance. Its published list includes organizations such as Siam Legal, Thai Relo Services, Star Visa Service 14, and other approved providers. **Neither Thai Visa Centre nor AGENTS CO., LTD. appears on the current published list.** The distinction matters because BOI certification involves a formal approval process and defined responsibilities rather than simply possessing experience assisting visa applicants.[^4]

Notably, `ltr.in.th` itself acknowledges this distinction. An October 2026 comparison table explicitly identifies Thai Visa Centre as **"Not BOI certified"** while describing it as the leading uncertified agent by customer reviews. The website claims the agency has **4,240 reviews across Google, Facebook, and Trustpilot**, compared with a combined **4,112 Google reviews** attributed to selected certified agencies.[^1]

**This comparison uses inconsistent measurement criteria.** Thai Visa Centre's total combines three review platforms, while the competing certified agencies are primarily assessed through Google business listings. The figures also concern general business reviews rather than independently verified LTR visa applications, approvals, customer outcomes, or service quality. The website excludes True Digital Park's broader Google business-review total from its calculation because those reviews do not specifically concern its visa services, but it does not demonstrate that Thai Visa Centre's own aggregate reviews specifically concern LTR visa assistance.[^1]

The presentation therefore risks implying that review volume establishes superiority over officially certified competitors. **The BOI's certification status and a commercial agency's customer-review totals measure fundamentally different things.** The available evidence does not establish that Thai Visa Centre provides inferior services or that any reviews are fabricated. It does establish that the website promotes a commercially associated agency through a comparison whose underlying figures are not directly equivalent.[^1][^4]

The website also operates a traveler discussion forum containing hundreds of comments, including contributions from an account identified as **"THAI VISA CENTRE - visa agent"** with a verified administrator designation. This gives the agency a prominent position within a discussion platform where prospective customers seek immigration advice. The authenticity and independence of other participants' testimonials cannot be established from the published material alone.[^1]

The website's Privacy Policy identifies AGENTS CO., LTD. as the responsible contracting entity and permits collecting contact information, account details, and information submitted through website forms. It also identifies public databases and joint marketing partners as potential sources of personal information. Its Terms & Conditions acknowledge that some external links may generate affiliate commissions, although they do not establish whether referrals to Thai Visa Centre are commission-based.[^2][^3]

Thailand's BOI allows eligible applicants to submit LTR applications directly through its official online system without paying an endorsement fee. Approved applicants generally pay **50,000 baht for visa issuance in Thailand**, while optional commercial assistance may involve additional charges. The BOI also publishes its own list of certified agents for applicants seeking professional representation.[^4][^5]

**The central concern is the promotion of an uncertified commercial visa agency through an information website that uses government program material, customer testimonials, and uneven competitor comparisons.** Although the website discloses its unofficial status and Thai Visa Centre's lack of BOI certification, its promotional rankings are not supported by independently verified, comparable measures of LTR visa service performance.

The arrangement warrants further scrutiny of **advertising transparency, corporate relationships, review authenticity, and the distinction between official accreditation and commercially promoted reputation**.

## Altered Google Review Presented as LTR Visa Endorsement

The promotional website `ltr.in.th` displays a five-star Google testimonial attributed to **Luca G.**, stating that the customer used Thai Visa Centre for an **LTR Visa**.[^6]

However, the same customer's testimonial appears on the affiliated `dtv.in.th` website with the visa type changed to **DTV Visa**.[^7]

Both websites link to the same Google Maps review location.

### Thai Visa Centre's Own Review Confirms DTV

Thai Visa Centre's official review archive identifies the reviewer as Luca G., dates the review to **September 25, 2024**, and reproduces the full testimonial.[^8]

The original testimonial displayed there explicitly concerns a **DTV visa**, which the customer says was obtained in approximately one week.

It contains no reference to an LTR visa.

This establishes that `ltr.in.th` presents a visa-specific endorsement that conflicts with the same review as reproduced by Thai Visa Centre itself.

**A customer testimonial about obtaining a DTV visa has been repurposed to imply that the customer obtained an LTR visa.**

### Misleading Evidence of Specialist Experience

The distinction is significant because DTV and LTR visas are different immigration programs, with different qualification requirements and government processing procedures.

A successful DTV application provides no evidence that the same agency successfully processed an LTR application.

The altered testimonial appears prominently beside the website's **"Trusted LTR Visa Agent"** promotion and claims that Thai Visa Centre is Thailand's leading visa agency.

This directly reinforces the site's existing comparisons favoring Thai Visa Centre over officially BOI-certified LTR service providers.

**The issue is not whether Luca G. genuinely reviewed Thai Visa Centre. The documented problem is that the review's subject was changed when used to advertise a different immigration service.**

Such editing misrepresents the customer's reported experience and undermines the reliability of the site's visa-specific testimonials.

## Visa Refund Policies Contradict Qualification-Test Disclaimers

AGENTS CO., LTD. publishes substantially identical visa-refund policies across its privately operated LTR and DTV information websites, despite both websites prominently stating that they provide only unofficial visa qualification tests.

The discrepancy concerns more than recycled legal wording. The policies describe paid immigration representation, government application fees, and customer refund rights for services that the websites' own disclaimers say they do not provide.

### Qualification Tests Versus Paid Visa Representation

The homepage of `ltr.in.th` expressly states that visitors should use Thailand's official Board of Investment website for visa applications and that the private website offers only an unofficial qualification test.[^1]

However, its published Refund Policy describes the operator submitting applications to Thai embassies or consulates on customers' behalf.[^9]

The policy promises refunds when an application has not yet been submitted, distinguishes government fees from visa-agent service fees, and addresses unsuccessful applications.

It also states that customers may be charged transaction fees of 2–7% when requesting certain refunds more than 12 hours after payment.

**These are contractual provisions for a paid visa-processing service, not merely a free immigration-information website offering an unofficial eligibility questionnaire.**

The website does not clearly explain whether these terms apply to an actual service available through `ltr.in.th`, an affiliated agency's services, or a visa-processing operation that is no longer offered.

### The Same Refund Policy Appears on DTV.in.th

The affiliated `dtv.in.th` website publishes the same disclaimer stating that it provides only an unofficial qualification test.

Nevertheless, its Refund Policy reproduces the same provisions concerning embassy submissions, unsuccessful applications, nonrefundable government charges, and 12-hour cancellation periods.[^10]

Both documents display July 16, 2024, as their last-updated date.

Their substantive refund terms are effectively identical, even though the LTR and DTV programs have different qualification requirements, processing procedures, and government authorities.

Both websites identify AGENTS CO., LTD. as the contracting company in their published Terms & Conditions.[^3][^11]

**AGENTS operates two supposedly informational immigration websites while publishing customer-payment and refund provisions that describe direct visa-processing services.**

The duplicated terms demonstrate a shared contractual template, but neither website adequately explains which services those provisions actually govern.

### LTR Government Application Fees Are Not Charged Before Endorsement

The discrepancy becomes more significant when the refund policy is compared with the official LTR application process.

Thailand's Board of Investment explicitly confirms that:

- LTR applications must be submitted through its official online system.
- The qualification assessment and endorsement process are free.
- The 50,000-baht visa issuance fee is payable only after approval when collecting the visa in Thailand.
- Applicants obtaining their visas through overseas embassies or consulates follow the applicable consular payment procedures.[^12]

The LTR refund policy, however, describes applications being submitted to embassies or consulates and government application fees becoming nonrefundable following submission.

It does not distinguish the BOI's free qualification-endorsement stage from the subsequent visa-issuance stage.

**An applicant rejected during the free BOI endorsement process would not have incurred a BOI government application fee.**

The possibility of a separate, nonrefundable embassy visa fee arises later in the process, after the applicant obtains the required qualification endorsement.

This distinction is essential when determining which charges a commercial agent may legitimately describe as government fees and which amounts represent its own services.

### Contradictory Cancellation Language

The refund policy also contains an internal inconsistency.

One provision promises a full refund if the customer cancels before the operator submits the application.

Another provision addresses customers who cancel before processing has begun, but introduces a 12-hour deadline after which transaction charges of 2–7% may apply.

These provisions overlap without clearly explaining when an unsubmitted application qualifies for a complete refund and when deductions are permitted.

The distinction could affect customers who pay for immigration assistance and subsequently discover that their application cannot proceed.

### What the Documents Actually Establish

The published policies do not establish that AGENTS has collected government application fees improperly, retained money owed to customers, or processed visa applications through either website.

They do establish a failure to reconcile three separate representations:

1. Both websites claim to offer only unofficial qualification tests.
2. Their published refund policies describe paid visa representation and government application charges.
3. The LTR refund provisions fail to distinguish the free BOI endorsement stage from the subsequent paid visa-issuance process.

The July 2024 update dates also suggest that these documents have not been revised alongside the websites' more recently updated immigration guidance.

**AGENTS presents itself as an experienced immigration-services operator while maintaining contractual documents that leave customers unable to determine which services are actually being sold, when government charges arise, and what refund protections apply.**

The issue is especially relevant because both websites promote Thai Visa Centre and channel prospective immigration customers toward that affiliated business.

The unresolved question is whether these are obsolete legal templates left online without review or operative terms governing commercial visa-processing services that the websites' disclaimers do not acknowledge.

## Misleading Employment Rights and Foreign-Income Tax Guidance

The English-language homepage of `ltr.in.th`, displaying a last-updated date of November 24, 2026, contains misleading summaries of employment rights and tax benefits under Thailand's Long-Term Resident visa program.[^1]

### Work-Permit Eligibility for Investors and Pensioners

In its LTR category comparison, the website describes the Wealthy Global Citizen category as **"Not a work category"** and the Wealthy Pensioner category as **"Not for employment."**[^1]

However, the Board of Investment's official FAQ expressly identifies holders of both categories as eligible to apply for work permits.[^5]

| LTR category | Eligibility to apply for a work permit according to BOI |
| --- | --- |
| Wealthy Global Citizen | Eligible |
| Wealthy Pensioner | Eligible |
| Highly-Skilled Professional | Eligible |
| Dependent | Eligible, subject to applicable requirements |
| Work-from-Thailand Professional | Not eligible |

Eligibility to apply for a work permit is not automatic permission to undertake employment. Applicants must meet the relevant work-permit requirements and continue satisfying the conditions of their LTR category. In particular, employment income does not count toward the passive-income qualification for Wealthy Pensioners.

The website correctly distinguishes the Work-from-Thailand Professional category, which does not provide access to a Thai work permit. Its separate employment guidance also describes how LTR holders can apply for work permits.[^1]

Nevertheless, **the category comparison obscures the distinction between qualifying for an LTR visa on the basis of employment and being eligible to obtain permission to work after receiving the visa.** Neither investor nor pensioner status is a categorical exclusion from work-permit eligibility under the BOI's published guidance.[^5]

### Foreign-Income Exemption Does Not Extend to Highly-Skilled Professionals Under Section 5

In its employment guidance, the website places a discussion of the overseas-income exemption within a table row specifically labeled **"Highly-Skilled tax."** It describes the preferential 17% employment-income tax rate and then states that LTR holders may also receive an exemption on overseas income, without identifying the categories eligible for that exemption.[^1]

Sections 3–5 of Royal Decree No. 743 B.E. 2565 (2022) distinguish two separate tax benefits:[^13]

| LTR category | Foreign-source income exemption under Section 5 | Preferential 17% employment-income arrangement under Sections 3–4 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wealthy Global Citizen | Eligible | Not under this category |
| Wealthy Pensioner | Eligible | Not under this category |
| Work-from-Thailand Professional | Eligible | Not under this category |
| Highly-Skilled Professional | Not under this category | Eligible |
| Dependent | Not under this category | Not under this category |

Section 5 concerns qualifying assessable income earned abroad in a previous tax year and brought into Thailand. Section 6 makes the benefits subject to the prescribed qualifications and procedures. The 17% arrangement concerns qualifying employment income in targeted industries, not every form of income earned by a Highly-Skilled Professional.[^13]

**Highly-Skilled Professionals are not among the three categories expressly eligible for the foreign-income exemption established by Section 5.** Other exemptions or treaty provisions may apply independently of LTR status, depending on individual circumstances.

The website acknowledges that tax benefits have conditions and recommends consulting a tax adviser. Its general statement that some LTR holders can receive an overseas-income exemption is not inherently false. The problem is its placement within the Highly-Skilled tax guidance without specifying the statutory category restriction.

### Thai Visa Centre's Own Legal Archive Identifies the Correct Categories

Thai Visa Centre's separate legal archive on foreign-sourced income explicitly identifies Wealthy Global Citizens, Wealthy Pensioners, and Work-from-Thailand Professionals as the categories benefiting from the foreign-income exemption under Royal Decree No. 743.[^14]

The agency's legal archive therefore provides the category distinction missing from the associated LTR website's employment-tax summary.

These discrepancies are particularly consequential because `ltr.in.th` promotes Thai Visa Centre's commercial immigration services while offering prospective applicants guidance on visa selection. Readers could make substantial employment, investment, or tax-planning decisions on the basis of incomplete or misleading category comparisons.

The reviewed material does not establish that the operator intentionally misrepresented these requirements or that a customer suffered a financial loss. It establishes specific deficiencies in the website's presentation of official eligibility rules and statutory tax benefits.

## Contradictory Income Requirements for Remote Workers

The November 25, 2026 revision of `ltr.in.th` provides updated eligibility guidance for applicants seeking the Work-from-Thailand Professional category of Thailand's LTR visa.[^15]

Although the website correctly identifies the two-year average income requirement, it subsequently states:

> One strong year does not erase a year under the line.

This additional restriction is not supported by Section 3.3.1 of BOI Announcement Por. 3/2568, effective February 4, 2025.[^16]

The official Thai announcement expressly uses the term เฉลี่ย (average) when establishing the income thresholds.

For example, an applicant earning US$70,000 in one qualifying year and US$90,000 in the other would mathematically satisfy the US$80,000 average annual income threshold, subject to all remaining eligibility and evidentiary requirements.

The same distinction applies to the reduced US$40,000 average income threshold available to applicants with specified additional qualifications.

### Commercial Qualification Testing

The discrepancy is relevant because the website offers an unofficial qualification test and promotes Thai Visa Centre's commercial immigration services.

The reviewed guidance could cause prospective applicants with fluctuating annual earnings to incorrectly conclude that they fail the income requirement.

The website does not identify a separate government provision requiring the applicable threshold to be met independently in each year.

Whether its automated qualification test implements the same restrictive interpretation has not been independently established.

## Misleading Annual Reporting and Continuing Eligibility Guidance

### Incorrect Annual Reporting Deadlines

The November 26, 2026 revision of `ltr.in.th` describes annual immigration reporting as occurring once a year from the date permission to stay was granted.[^17]

However, the Board of Investment explicitly states that international travel resets the reporting period. Following re-entry into Thailand, the next report becomes due one year after the most recent arrival.[^18]

The official instructions also establish that:

- Reporting is required after one year of continuous residence.
- LTR holders submit Form TM.95 instead of the conventional TM.47 90-day report.
- An authorized representative may submit the report.
- Existing TM.30 residence-notification obligations remain applicable.[^5][^18]

The commercial website's reporting summary omits these distinctions, potentially causing frequent international travelers to misunderstand their reporting obligations.

### Understating the Consequences of Losing Qualifying Employment

The website states that if employment ends, investments are sold, or qualifying financial or insurance arrangements lapse, the BOI can refuse the next qualification endorsement.[^17]

Although the website elsewhere acknowledges that eligibility conditions must be maintained throughout the visa's validity, its explanation of the consequences focuses on future endorsement rather than possible termination of an existing visa.

The BOI's official FAQ expressly states that Highly-Skilled Professionals changing employers must notify the LTR unit at least one month before ending their previous employment contract.

If the new employer does not satisfy the relevant eligibility criteria, the existing LTR visa must be terminated.[^5]

The BOI separately publishes procedures for terminating an LTR visa before its expiration and promptly cancelling associated work permits.[^19]

**Loss of qualifying employment can therefore have immediate immigration consequences rather than merely affecting eligibility at the next five-year review.**

### Commercial Significance

These omissions are particularly relevant because `ltr.in.th` offers prospective applicants qualification testing while promoting Thai Visa Centre's paid immigration services.

Accurate explanations of continuing eligibility, visa termination, and reporting obligations are essential for applicants making long-term employment, investment, and relocation decisions.

## LTR Renewal Guidance Omits the BOI's Recommended Application Window

The English-language homepage of `ltr.in.th`, updated November 26, 2026, repeatedly instructs Long-Term Resident visa holders to apply for renewed qualification endorsement at least 20 working days before their existing permission expires.[^20]

Although this accurately reflects the regulatory minimum established by BOI Announcement Por. 4/2568, the website omits the considerably earlier application window recommended by the BOI itself.

### Regulatory Minimum Versus Practical Application Window

The BOI's official frequently asked questions recommend submitting five-year renewal applications between **120 and 60 days before the expiration of existing permission to stay**.[^5]

Sections 4–6 of BOI Announcement Por. 4/2568 distinguish three procedural requirements:[^21]

- Qualification endorsement applications must be submitted at least 20 working days before expiration.
- The BOI has 20 working days to consider the application.
- Applicants must subsequently obtain their immigration extension before their existing permission expires.

**Submitting an application at the regulatory minimum could therefore leave insufficient time to complete the subsequent immigration procedure.**

### Commercial Significance

The omission is particularly relevant as the earliest LTR visa holders approach their initial five-year renewals in 2027.

Accurate renewal guidance should distinguish the minimum regulatory deadline from the government's recommended application window, allowing sufficient time for additional documentation, qualification assessment, and immigration processing.

While `ltr.in.th` accurately reproduces the regulatory minimum, its practical application instructions remain incomplete compared with the BOI's current guidance.

## Incomplete Investment Eligibility and Asset Qualification Guidance

The November 26, 2026 revision of `ltr.in.th` provides prospective Long-Term Resident visa applicants with detailed financial qualification tables while promoting Thai Visa Centre's commercial immigration services.[^20]

However, its investment guidance omits several material restrictions established in the Board of Investment's published application checklists.

### Cryptocurrencies and Luxury Assets Are Excluded

The website describes the Wealthy Global Citizen category as requiring at least US$1 million in global assets, without explaining several important exclusions.

The BOI's official checklist expressly excludes cryptocurrencies, digital tokens, gold futures, artwork, amulets, designer goods, watches and jewelry from the qualifying global asset calculation.[^22]

A prospective applicant whose wealth is concentrated in these assets could therefore incorrectly conclude that they satisfy the financial qualification threshold.

### Leasehold Investments Require Ten Remaining Years

The website identifies Thai property as an eligible investment without explaining the minimum remaining duration applicable to leasehold arrangements.

The BOI's official FAQ and application checklists establish that qualifying leases must have at least ten years remaining when the applicant applies.[^5][^22][^23]

The application checklists also require recent documentation issued by the Department of Lands.

**A substantial financial investment in Thai leasehold property does not automatically satisfy the LTR investment requirement.**

### Publicly Traded Shares Have a Minimum Holding Period

The commercial website identifies investments in Thai public companies as qualifying assets without explaining the applicable holding period for shares listed on the Stock Exchange of Thailand.

The BOI's checklists require broker certification demonstrating that the applicant has held the relevant shares for at least one year before applying.[^22][^23]

Purchasing sufficient shares immediately before submitting an LTR application would therefore not necessarily satisfy the documentary requirements.

### Commercial Significance

These omissions affect both the US$500,000 investment requirement for Wealthy Global Citizens and the US$250,000 investment alternative for qualifying Wealthy Pensioners.

The website correctly explains the five-year remaining maturity requirement for government bonds but does not provide comparable explanations of the restrictions governing leasehold property or publicly traded shares.

**AGENTS promotes Thai Visa Centre's professional immigration services and offers unofficial qualification testing while omitting financial eligibility restrictions that could materially affect substantial investment decisions.**

The website should distinguish qualifying assets from general personal wealth and explain the applicable investment holding periods before encouraging prospective applicants to assess their eligibility.

The available evidence does not establish that any applicant has suffered financial losses or purchased unsuitable investments because of these omissions.

## Contradictory Dependent Eligibility Advice Within Traveler Discussions

As of November 27, 2026, `ltr.in.th` advertised a live discussion containing 307 comments alongside its promotion of Thai Visa Centre's commercial immigration services.[^24]

However, its purported traveler discussions contain advice that contradicts both the website's own legal explanations and the government's published eligibility requirements.

### Misleading Advice Concerning Elderly Parents

A participant displaying an email-verification designation described plans to relocate to Thailand with her husband, two children and elderly mother.

The participant assumed that BOI Announcement Por. 3/2568 had already implemented expanded dependent eligibility.

Another participant responded with instructions concerning separate applications, relationship documentation, health insurance and the US$25,000 deposit alternative.

Neither participant addressed the outstanding Ministry of Interior requirements.

The prospective applicant subsequently expressed relief regarding the deposit alternative because of the difficulty of obtaining health insurance for her 74-year-old mother.

### Government Still Publishes Restricted Dependent Eligibility

The BOI's official website continues to identify eligible dependents as lawful spouses and children younger than 20, subject to a maximum of four dependents per principal visa holder.[^25]

The BOI's January 15, 2025 announcement expressly made the expansion of dependent rights conditional upon an additional Ministry of Interior announcement.[^26]

Independent legal analysis published in November 2026 similarly identifies the outstanding implementing announcement and continuing restrictions.[^27]

Furthermore, the prospective applicant's proposed family contains four dependents, not more than four. The principal obstacle under the earlier framework is the mother's relationship to the applicant, rather than the numerical limit.

### Contradiction With the Website's Own Legal Guidance

The commercial website itself acknowledges the unresolved distinction between BOI qualification rules and Ministry of Interior immigration requirements.

Its November 27, 2026 guidance advises prospective applicants to confirm eligibility with the authorities before submitting applications for parents or additional dependents.[^24]

However, the discussion exchange proceeds as though the mother's eligibility has already been established.

**AGENTS publishes legal warnings concerning unresolved dependent eligibility while simultaneously displaying purported traveler discussions that disregard those warnings.**

### Thai Visa Centre's Administrator Contradicts Published Reporting Guidance

A separate discussion contains a response from Thai Visa Centre's verified administrator correctly explaining that international travel resets the annual immigration reporting period.

Nevertheless, two tables on the same website continue describing annual reporting as calculated from the original grant of permission to stay.

The administrator's explanation agrees with the BOI's official reporting instructions.[^18]

This demonstrates that correct information is already present within the website's own discussions, while contradictory guidance remains in its principal immigration publication.

### Commercial Significance and Discussion Reliability

These inconsistencies matter because the website presents its discussions as a resource for prospective immigrants while advertising Thai Visa Centre's professional assistance.

Families relying on inaccurate eligibility advice could incur unnecessary insurance, documentation and relocation expenses.

The operator should reconcile its published guidance, correct misleading discussion contributions, and clearly distinguish verified email addresses from independently verified immigration experiences.

The available evidence does not establish that AGENTS fabricated the comments or that any participant suffered financial losses.

## Incomplete Health Insurance Eligibility and Application Guidance

As of November 27, 2026, AGENTS' LTR information website continued publishing health insurance qualification guidance alongside its promotion of Thai Visa Centre's commercial immigration services.[^24]

Although the website correctly identifies the minimum coverage amount of US$50,000, its guidance omits several important conditions established by the Board of Investment.

### Travel Insurance Is Not Accepted

The BOI's official Wealthy Pensioner application checklist, dated November 6, 2025, expressly excludes travel insurance and life insurance without health insurance coverage.[^28]

The commercial website does not identify these exclusions in its dedicated health insurance section.

Consequently, applicants could incorrectly assume that a long-duration travel insurance policy satisfies LTR requirements merely because its medical coverage exceeds US$50,000.

### Group Insurance Coverage

The BOI also specifies that group health insurance must provide at least US$50,000 in coverage for each insured person.[^28]

AGENTS' guidance does not explain this distinction, despite separately discussing health insurance requirements for spouses and dependent children.

A shared policy's aggregate coverage amount does not necessarily establish that each applicant independently satisfies the qualification requirements.

### Deferred Submission of Insurance Documents

The commercial website advises applicants to resolve missing health coverage before filing their applications.

However, the BOI permits applicants to defer submitting insurance documentation by providing a signed Document Request Acknowledgement Form. The required documentation is subsequently requested during government consideration of the application.[^28]

This procedural alternative could be particularly useful for applicants arranging expensive international health insurance coverage.

It does not remove the underlying insurance qualification requirement.

### Commercial Significance

**AGENTS offers unofficial LTR qualification testing while omitting insurance eligibility restrictions and documentary alternatives that could materially affect applicants' purchasing decisions.**

These omissions are also relevant to the company's historical and continuing involvement in insurance distribution, documented in the separate [AGENTS CO., LTD. investigation](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md).

The available evidence does not establish that Thai Visa Centre has recommended unsuitable insurance to LTR applicants or that customers have incurred unnecessary insurance expenses.
