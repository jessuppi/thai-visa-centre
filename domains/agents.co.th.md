# agents.co.th

`agents.co.th` presents itself as the corporate website of [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md), a Thai company offering tourism technology and travel-assistance services.[^1]

The website promotes Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) submissions, eSIM distribution, and immigration reporting services. It links to [tdac.agents.co.th](tdac.agents.co.th.md) for TDAC applications and [90day.in.th](90day.in.th.md) for 90-day reporting services.[^1][^2]

In a [May 2025 ASEAN NOW discussion](../threads/2025-05-01-asean-now-fake-tdac-emerges.md), [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md) publicly identified both tdac.agents.co.th and [tdac.in.th](tdac.in.th.md) as websites operated by Agents Co., Ltd.[^3]

The company's website identifies **AGENTS CO., LTD.**, registered in Thailand on **December 12, 2019**, under company registration number **0115562031107**. These details are provided by the company itself and do not independently establish historical domain ownership or government affiliation.[^1]

## Formal THNIC Domain Abuse Complaint (November 2026)

On November 12, 2026, Jesse Nickles submitted a formal complaint to [THNIC](../brands/thnic.md) requesting an investigation into the use of `agents.co.th` and its lower-level domain, [tdac.agents.co.th](tdac.agents.co.th.md). The complaint concerned a privately operated Thailand Digital Arrival Card submission service associated with [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md), including an $8 early-submission option and the collection of travelers' passport details, identity information, and travel records.[^4]

Nickles requested that THNIC verify the registered holder of `agents.co.th` and determine whether that holder was responsible for the subdomain's operation. He cited **Section 12.11**, which requires registered domains and lower-level domains to be used in good faith, alongside Sections 12.4, 12.5, and 17 concerning investigations, enforcement, and information-security threats.[^4]

The complaint acknowledged that private businesses may legitimately offer immigration assistance and that the current service discloses its lack of government affiliation. Nevertheless, Nickles requested a formal assessment of the domain holder's responsibilities, the handling of travelers' sensitive information, and the relevance of official Immigration Bureau warnings concerning unofficial TDAC services.[^4]

**The central unresolved question is whether THNIC independently verifies and assesses the responsibilities of registered domain holders when potentially problematic services operate through their subdomains.** Nickles requested a separate case reference and written policy determination.[^4]
