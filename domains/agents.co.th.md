# agents.co.th

`agents.co.th` presents itself as the corporate website of [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md), a Thai company offering tourism technology and travel-assistance services.[^1]

The website promotes Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) submissions, eSIM distribution, and immigration reporting services. It links to [tdac.agents.co.th](tdac.agents.co.th.md) for TDAC applications and [90day.in.th](90day.in.th.md) for 90-day reporting services.[^1][^2]

In a [May 2025 ASEAN NOW discussion](../threads/2025-05-01-asean-now-fake-tdac-emerges.md), [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md) publicly identified both tdac.agents.co.th and [tdac.in.th](tdac.in.th.md) as websites operated by Agents Co., Ltd.[^3]

The company's website identifies **AGENTS CO., LTD.**, registered in Thailand on **December 12, 2019**, under company registration number **0115562031107**. These details are provided by the company itself and do not independently establish historical domain ownership or government affiliation.[^1]

## Formal THNIC Domain Abuse Complaint (November 2026)

On November 12, 2026, Jesse Nickles submitted a formal complaint to [THNIC](../brands/thnic.md) requesting an investigation into the use of `agents.co.th` and its lower-level domain, [tdac.agents.co.th](tdac.agents.co.th.md). The complaint concerned a privately operated Thailand Digital Arrival Card submission service associated with [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md), including an $8 early-submission option and the collection of travelers' passport details, identity information, and travel records.[^4]

Nickles requested that THNIC verify the registered holder of `agents.co.th` and determine whether that holder was responsible for the subdomain's operation. He cited **Section 12.11**, which requires registered domains and lower-level domains to be used in good faith, alongside Sections 12.4, 12.5, and 17 concerning investigations, enforcement, and information-security threats.[^4]

The complaint acknowledged that private businesses may legitimately offer immigration assistance and that the current service discloses its lack of government affiliation. Nevertheless, Nickles requested a formal assessment of the domain holder's responsibilities, the handling of travelers' sensitive information, and the relevance of official Immigration Bureau warnings concerning unofficial TDAC services.[^4]

**The central unresolved question is whether THNIC independently verifies and assesses the responsibilities of registered domain holders when potentially problematic services operate through their subdomains.** Nickles requested a separate case reference and written policy determination.[^4]

## TDAC Application Portal Uses Outdated Legal Documents and Conflicting Customer Protections

AGENTS CO., LTD. operates a private Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) application form through `agents.co.th/tdac-apply/`.

The service collects travelers' immigration information and offers paid early-submission assistance outside the government's ordinary registration window.[^5]

Its publicly accessible legal agreements identify AGENTS CO., LTD. as the contracting business.

However, those agreements contain outdated references to Thailand's abandoned Electronic Travel Authorization (ETA) system, contradictory customer age restrictions, inaccurate descriptions of the services being sold, and substantial limitations on financial liability.

**AGENTS CO., LTD. processes immigration applications through a commercial website whose customer-facing legal documentation contains several inconsistencies relevant to privacy, refunds, and contractual accountability.**

### The Application Form Collects Detailed Immigration Information

The AGENTS application form clearly identifies itself as a private service rather than Thailand's official government portal.[^5]

It provides a link to the Immigration Bureau's official TDAC website.

Nevertheless, the company's own application form requests detailed information from travelers, including:

- Full names and passport numbers.
- Passport country and date of birth.
- Email addresses and telephone numbers.
- Country of residence and occupation.
- Visa category and travel purpose.
- Arrival and departure information.
- Flight or vehicle numbers.
- Accommodation addresses in Thailand.
- Countries visited during the preceding two weeks.

These are not merely newsletter subscriptions or ordinary marketing contacts.

They are identity and travel records associated with international border crossings.

The form informs users that their information will be submitted to official government systems.

**Customers therefore provide sensitive immigration information to AGENTS CO., LTD. as an intermediary before the information reaches Thailand's Immigration Bureau.**

That commercial arrangement is not inherently unlawful.

However, it increases the importance of accurate legal agreements describing the service, the responsible company, data-processing arrangements, and customers' rights.

### Legal Pages Retain the Abandoned ETA System's Name

The current TDAC application form links directly to AGENTS CO., LTD.'s Terms & Conditions, Privacy Policy, Data Protection Policy, Disclaimer, and Refund Policy.

Although the visible headings correctly identify the types of legal documents, their browser-page titles continue identifying the service as **Thailand Electronic Travel Authorization (ETA)**.[^6][^7][^8]

This is outdated terminology.

Thailand originally proposed an Electronic Travel Authorization system for visa-exempt visitors.

However, the government subsequently adopted the Thailand Digital Arrival Card instead.

In February 2026, Thailand's Government Public Relations Department expressly confirmed that the Immigration Bureau developed TDAC to replace the proposed ETA system, effective May 1, 2025.[^9]

The two systems were not simply different names for an identical immigration document.

ETA was proposed as a travel-authorization procedure.

TDAC is a digital arrival-information declaration, not a visa or independent permission to enter Thailand.

**AGENTS CO., LTD.'s legal pages retain the name of an abandoned immigration system more than a year after the replacement system took effect.**

This discrepancy does not establish that the company currently processes ETA applications.

Its actual form clearly identifies TDAC.

The problem is that legally significant documentation connected to the current application service still contains obsolete terminology.

### The Documents Permit Users Aged 16 but Restrict Personal Information Below 20

The Terms & Conditions establish an age requirement of 16 years.[^6]

They state that customers must be at least 16 to use the website and its services.

However, the Privacy Policy establishes a different restriction.

Its section addressing children's privacy instructs users younger than 20 not to submit personal information through the website.[^7]

The two rules produce an obvious inconsistency:

| Legal document | Published instruction |
|---|---|
| Terms & Conditions | Users aged 16 or older may use the service |
| Privacy Policy | Users under 20 should not submit personal information |
| TDAC application form | Requires personal and passport details |
| Official Immigration Bureau guidance | TDAC applies to children and infants |

An individual aged 16, 17, 18, or 19 therefore satisfies the Terms & Conditions but is simultaneously instructed not to supply the information necessary to use the service.

The Privacy Policy acknowledges that parents may provide information concerning their children.

However, it describes that situation in the context of visa applications and does not clearly reconcile the general age restrictions.

Thailand's Immigration Bureau confirms that TDAC declarations are required for foreign travelers of all ages, including children and infants.[^10]

The official rules do not make the declaration optional merely because the traveler is younger than 20.

**AGENTS CO., LTD. publishes conflicting customer eligibility instructions for younger travelers using a service that explicitly processes immigration information for children.**

The available evidence does not establish that the company improperly processes children's data.

It establishes that the published agreements do not consistently explain who may submit information and under what authorization.

### The Refund Policy Misidentifies TDAC as a Visa Application

The company's Refund Policy introduces a separate factual error.

Under its section describing the TDAC Early Submission Service, the policy states that the service enables customers to submit visa applications well ahead of their travel dates.[^8]

However, a TDAC is not a visa application.

Thailand's Immigration Bureau describes TDAC as an electronic replacement for the former paper arrival card.[^10]

The declaration collects information used during entry procedures.

It does not grant a visa, establish a visa category, or independently authorize admission to Thailand.

The distinction matters because customers may misunderstand what the company's paid service actually provides.

AGENTS CO., LTD. accepts information before the official registration window and arranges later submission of the arrival declaration.

It does not obtain early government approval of a visa.

**The refund agreement describes a service involving visa applications even though the company is selling assistance with a digital arrival declaration.**

This error is particularly conspicuous because the company publishes extensive articles explaining the differences between TDAC, visas, and unofficial commercial application services.

### Refund Conditions Do Not Address Every Pending-Application Scenario

The same Refund Policy provides two express refund conditions for the company's TDAC Early Submission Service.[^8]

Customers may obtain a full service-fee refund if their trip is cancelled entirely.

They may also obtain a full refund when cancelling the service more than seven days before arrival.

However, the policy does not explicitly explain what happens when:

1. A customer changes their arrival date without cancelling the trip.
2. A traveler cancels the paid service between three and seven days before arrival.
3. The company fails to submit an advance application when the government registration window opens.
4. An application contains an error attributable to the intermediary.
5. A traveler decides to use the free official government service instead.

Some of these situations may be addressed through customer support or other contractual provisions.

The published refund policy does not establish that the company would refuse compensation in every such case.

Nevertheless, it leaves important practical questions unanswered.

**Customers purchasing advance TDAC assistance are not given a comprehensive explanation of their refund rights when the promised submission service is delayed, changed, or not performed.**

This matters because early submissions can remain pending in the company's system until the official government registration window becomes available.

The customer may therefore pay before the underlying government filing occurs.

### The Terms Attempt to Limit Liability to One Dollar

AGENTS CO., LTD.'s Terms & Conditions contain a substantial limitation of liability.[^6]

The agreement attempts to restrict aggregate liability to the greater of:

- US$1.
- The amount the customer paid to the company during the one-month period preceding the event giving rise to liability.

The document also attempts to exclude several categories of consequential and indirect damages.

The significance of this provision depends on the applicable circumstances and law.

A customer paying shortly before a relevant incident may have a contractual limit corresponding to the amount paid.

However, customers paying well in advance could face a different calculation if the relevant problem occurs outside the preceding payment window.

**The company's published agreement attempts to cap liability at potentially one dollar even though its services involve sensitive immigration information and time-dependent government submissions.**

The provision expressly operates only to the maximum extent permitted by applicable law.

The investigation has not established whether a Thai court would enforce that limitation in a particular dispute.

Nevertheless, the clause is relevant to assessing the contractual protections offered to paying customers.

### The Privacy Policy Separately Reserves Marketing Uses

The company's Privacy Policy permits processing personal information for purposes extending beyond submitting arrival declarations.[^7]

These include customer-account management, service improvement, product updates, feedback requests, testimonials, and marketing.

The policy also permits information sharing with related companies under common ownership or control.

Its Terms & Conditions separately acknowledge that customer information may be shared with sister companies for marketing and promotional purposes.[^6]

These provisions are commercially significant because the same application form collects passport and immigration information.

However, the Privacy Policy does not clearly distinguish which categories of information may be used for affiliated-company marketing.

**Customers are not given a sufficiently specific explanation of whether passport and travel-history information collected for government declarations is excluded from the company's broader marketing permissions.**

This is a transparency concern, not evidence that passport information has actually been used for marketing.

The available documentation does not establish unauthorized data transfers or improper commercial use of customer records.

### The Data-Controller Explanation Adds Uncertainty

The Privacy Policy identifies AGENTS CO., LTD. as a data controller when it determines how personal information is collected and processed.

However, the document also states that when a user submits information through the website, the company acts as a data processor and the user becomes the controller.[^7]

That explanation requires qualification.

Under Thailand's Personal Data Protection Act, controller and processor roles depend on the actual purposes and decision-making responsibilities of the parties.

An ordinary traveler does not automatically become the controller of an immigration agency's processing simply by completing an online form.

AGENTS CO., LTD. independently determines important aspects of its service, including account administration, marketing, payment handling, technical infrastructure, and affiliated-company disclosures.

The company may legitimately act as a processor for some categories of customer information.

However, its published policy does not adequately distinguish those situations from its independent commercial processing.

**The contractual description of customers as data controllers is difficult to reconcile with the company's separately disclosed authority over service operation and marketing decisions.**

The relevant legal classification would require examination of the actual processing arrangements.

The published wording alone does not establish a PDPA violation.

### Repeated Legal Templates Across the Business Network

The inconsistencies become more significant when viewed alongside the wider Thai Visa Centre business network.

The investigation has already identified similar contractual provisions on other associated websites.

These include:

- Age restrictions that differ between terms and privacy policies.
- Broad affiliated-company marketing permissions.
- Liability limits calculated using the greater of US$1 and recent customer payments.
- Unclear descriptions of whether ordinary customers act as data controllers.
- Legal documents that inadequately distinguish different immigration services.

The repetition suggests that AGENTS CO., LTD. and its associated operations use substantially similar legal provisions across multiple services.

Using standardized legal documentation is not inherently improper.

However, identical provisions may be unsuitable when the underlying services have materially different characteristics.

A digital arrival declaration, visa application, paid 90-day immigration report, and quarantine hotel booking involve different procedures, customer obligations, and personal-information requirements.

**Reusing generalized contractual provisions without adequately adapting them to the service being sold can create meaningful gaps in consumer disclosures.**

The continued ETA terminology and inaccurate visa-application language provide concrete examples.

### What the Evidence Establishes

The reviewed public documents establish that:

1. AGENTS CO., LTD. operates a private TDAC application form.
2. Its linked legal pages retain obsolete ETA browser titles.
3. Its Terms & Conditions and Privacy Policy contain inconsistent age restrictions.
4. Its Refund Policy misdescribes TDAC processing as visa-application submission.
5. Its refund provisions leave important pending-service scenarios unexplained.
6. Its contractual liability limit may be as low as US$1.
7. Its privacy disclosures permit commercial processing and affiliated-company marketing beyond the immediate arrival-declaration procedure.

These findings do not prove that the company has submitted incorrect arrival declarations, mishandled customer information, improperly refused refunds, or violated Thai law.

They do establish weaknesses in the accuracy and clarity of its public customer agreements.

### Questions AGENTS CO., LTD. Should Answer

The company should explain why its TDAC application policies continue carrying ETA titles after the government's replacement of that system.

It should reconcile its minimum service age of 16 with the Privacy Policy's restriction on submissions from users younger than 20.

The Refund Policy should distinguish TDAC declarations from visa applications and explicitly address application errors, submission failures, travel-date changes, and cancellations after payment.

The company should also clarify which categories of customer information are eligible for affiliated-company marketing.

Finally, it should explain how its contractual liability limit applies to advance submissions, particularly when payments occur more than one month before the relevant event.

**AGENTS CO., LTD. advertises professional assistance with Thailand's digital arrival requirements while publishing legal documentation containing obsolete immigration terminology, conflicting eligibility instructions, incomplete refund explanations, and restrictive liability provisions.**

Those discrepancies warrant correction because travelers relying on the company's services must understand both the government procedure being performed and their contractual rights if something goes wrong.

## Group TDAC Submissions and Indirect Personal Data Collection

As of November 27, 2026, AGENTS CO., LTD. continued promoting its TDAC submission platform to travel agencies and tour operators, emphasizing group administration, individual document delivery and enhanced privacy.[^11]

However, the company's published policies do not adequately distinguish customers directly using its website from individual travelers whose information is submitted by another business.

### Authorization to Submit Is Not Authorization to Market

Thailand's Immigration Bureau expressly permits travel agencies to submit TDAC applications on behalf of authorized clients.[^10]

However, authorization to complete a government arrival declaration does not automatically establish authorization to process the same information for unrelated commercial purposes.

AGENTS' Privacy Policy permits using personal information to market its own products and services and those of affiliated companies.[^7]

The policy does not specifically explain how these marketing provisions apply to travelers whose passport information, contact details and travel records were supplied by a tour operator.

### Statutory Obligations When Collecting Information Indirectly

Section 25 of Thailand's Personal Data Protection Act regulates personal information obtained from sources other than the individuals concerned.[^12]

Where consent is required, the controller must notify the affected individual without undue delay and within the applicable statutory period. The legislation also provides exceptions and specific notification requirements concerning subsequent communications and disclosures.

These provisions are particularly relevant to commercial TDAC group submissions because the person accepting AGENTS' website policies may not be the individual whose information is being processed.

**Acceptance of AGENTS' Privacy Policy by a tour operator does not, by itself, establish that every traveler has authorized affiliated-company marketing.**

### Right to Object to Direct Marketing

Section 32 of the PDPA establishes an individual's right to object to the processing of personal information for direct marketing at any time.[^12]

Once that right is exercised, the controller must discontinue the relevant processing.

AGENTS' Privacy Policy refers generally to opting out and provides a support email address. However, it does not specifically explain how travelers can exercise their direct-marketing objection rights without deleting their accounts or interfering with pending arrival-card submissions.[^7]

The position is particularly unclear for travelers who never created their own AGENTS accounts.

### Contrast With Government Data Processing

The Immigration Bureau's official FAQ describes TDAC information as being used for government border-control purposes.[^10]

AGENTS' commercial platform separately reserves broader marketing and affiliated-company disclosure rights.

Although individual document delivery may provide a genuine privacy advantage when distributing group confirmations, it does not resolve the broader questions concerning subsequent commercial processing.

**AGENTS advertises privacy-focused group submissions while leaving important aspects of indirect personal-data collection and affiliated-company marketing unexplained.**

The company should clarify how individual travelers receive privacy notices, distinguish immigration submission authorization from marketing permissions, and provide an accessible mechanism for exercising statutory objection rights.

The available evidence does not establish that AGENTS has marketed services to unauthorized recipients or unlawfully processed information obtained from travel agencies.
