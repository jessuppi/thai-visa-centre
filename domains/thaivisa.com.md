# thaivisa.com

`thaivisa.com` was the original domain of **ThaiVisa.com**, a Thailand-focused expatriate news and discussion forum established in 2002. The website became a prominent source of information about Thai immigration, visas, and expatriate services.[^1]

The domain now redirects to [ASEAN NOW](../brands/asean-now.md) at `aseannow.com`.[^2]

## Ownership and Rebranding

On September 1, 2015, [Choice Group Asia Co., Ltd.](../companies/choice-group-asia-co-ltd.md) announced its acquisition of ThaiVisa.com and associated media platforms from Thaivisa Expat Group Co., Ltd.[^3]

On **July 2, 2021**, the website officially rebranded as ASEAN NOW, expanding its coverage beyond Thailand to other Southeast Asian countries. The announcement assured existing members that its discussion forums would continue operating.[^4]

In April 2024, [The Thaiger](../brands/the-thaiger.md) acquired ASEAN NOW's digital assets from Choice Group Asia.[^5]

## Historical Immigration Discussions

ThaiVisa.com's archived discussions contain early public accounts of [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md), including questions about retirement-visa requirements, unsolicited advertising, and the agency's response to the August 2020 police investigation.

These discussions remain accessible through ASEAN NOW, preserving material originally published under the ThaiVisa.com name.

ASEAN NOW subsequently developed commercial sponsorship arrangements with Thai Visa Centre. Its moderation policies restrict criticism of advertisers and sponsors, as documented in the separate [ASEAN NOW investigation](../brands/asean-now.md).

## Commercial Advertising Campaigns Targeting Visa Agents in 2020

On August 14, 2020, just ten days after Thailand's Immigration Bureau chief publicly announced the arrests involving Thai Visa Centre's founders, ThaiVisa.com's commercial sales account, now identified as **ASEAN NOW Sales**, published an advertising offer specifically targeting visa agencies.[^6][^7]

The publisher identified the approaching expiration of Thailand's COVID-era immigration amnesty as a commercial opportunity to attract prospective visa-agency customers.

For **7,500 baht**, the publisher offered a 45-day advertising campaign comprising:

- Two email newsletter placements reaching a claimed 335,000 subscribers.
- Two Facebook promotions reaching a claimed 245,000 followers.
- Two discussion forum posts targeting geographical communities selected by the advertiser.

### Thai Visa Centre Advertorial

On September 15, 2020, the same sales account published an advertorial promoting [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md) as a trustworthy immigration-services provider.[^8]

The advertisement contained no mention of the recent police investigation, although it disclosed that the relationship between ThaiVisa.com and Thai Visa Centre was commercial rather than an affiliation beyond the advertisement.

The resulting discussion subsequently accumulated approximately 40,100 views and 205 replies, including 53 contributions from Thai Visa Centre's own account.

### Paid Placement Within Community Discussions

On October 5, 2020, ThaiVisa.com published additional advertising packages explicitly selling prominent placement within its discussion forums.[^9]

| Monthly price | Selected promotional benefits |
| --- | --- |
| 6,900 baht | Pinned advertorial, newsletter placements and Facebook promotions |
| 11,900 baht | Pinned advertorial, increased newsletter and Facebook exposure, LINE notifications |
| 16,900 baht | Pinned advertorial, forum sponsorship banner, additional promotional placements |

The publisher also advertised access to its Inspire Bangkok newsletter as part of the higher-priced packages.

**These historical sales offers establish that prominent visibility within ThaiVisa.com's expatriate discussion communities was a commercially purchasable service.**

The arrangements are particularly relevant to the subsequently documented [sponsor-protection rules](../brands/asean-now.md), which restrict criticism of advertisers while permitting sponsors to promote their businesses directly within immigration discussions.

The combination of paid discussion placement, preferential promotional access, and restrictions on unfavorable comments creates a structural conflict between the publisher's commercial interests and its role as a source of independent immigration information.
