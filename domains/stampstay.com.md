# stampstay.com

`stampstay.com` is a commercial Thailand immigration risk-assessment website offering automated travel-history analysis, visa guidance, and paid entry-risk reports.[^1]

The website claims to have analyzed more than **500 immigration cases** to identify patterns associated with entry refusals and visa scrutiny.[^1]

## Unverified Immigration Risk Statistics

StampStay advertises a **$79 immigration risk analysis** that includes an estimated probability of entry, primary risk factors, and recommended strategies. Priority reports cost $149.[^1][^2]

Its articles also publish precise statistics about immigration refusals, including claims that **42% involve travel-pattern abuse** and **19% involve insufficient documentation**.[^3]

However, the website does not publish the underlying case records, sampling methods, or independent validation needed to assess the reliability of these figures.

The service explicitly acknowledges that it has no official access to Thai immigration authorities and cannot guarantee the accuracy of its predictions.[^4]

## Inconsistent Pricing Information

StampStay's current Terms of Service advertise reports costing **$79 or $149**, while its Legal Disclaimer still describes a **$12 Sanity Check** service.[^2][^4]

The discrepancy raises questions about whether its published legal documentation reflects the services currently being sold.

## Customer Data Collection

StampStay's Privacy Policy describes collecting names, nationalities, travel histories, employment information, and income details from paying customers.

It states that personal information is retained for 12 months, anonymized travel-pattern data indefinitely, and payment records for seven years.[^5]

## Unidentified Legal Operator, Unspecified Jurisdiction, and Conflicting Privacy Disclosures

StampStay's Terms of Service and Privacy Policy, both dated March 23, 2026, identify the service by its trading name but do not identify the natural person or registered company legally responsible for its operations. The published contact details consist of a Gmail address and the website URL. Neither document specifies a registered business address or country of incorporation.[^2][^5]

### Contractual Obligations Without an Identified Jurisdiction

StampStay charges $79 for standard assessments and $149 for priority delivery. Its terms seek to limit liability to the purchase amount, exclude responsibility for immigration outcomes and consequential losses, and require customers to indemnify its operators and affiliates in specified circumstances.[^2]

Section 10 refers to the law of the jurisdiction where StampStay operates and provides for binding arbitration or proceedings in its local courts if negotiations fail. However, it never names the jurisdiction.

**Customers cannot determine from the published terms which country's laws or courts the service purports to select, or which legal person is accepting their payment and assuming contractual obligations.**

This omission does not establish that the service is unregistered or that its contractual provisions are unenforceable. It prevents prospective customers from assessing those questions using the published documents alone.

### Inconsistent Privacy Disclosures

The homepage promotes its free risk checker using the assurance **"No personal data required."**[^1] The About page nevertheless explains that the tool collects nationality, visa history, entry frequency, duration of stay, and other travel-pattern information, with an optional email address. It says risk-check records are retained for up to 12 months before anonymization.[^6]

These statements need to distinguish the absence of mandatory identifying details from the collection of information about individual travelers. Travel histories may constitute personal data when linked to an identifiable individual. The paid service expressly collects additional identifying information under its Privacy Policy.[^5]

| Subject | About page | Privacy Policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Email delivery | Sender.net | Resend |
| Individual records | Assurance against third-party sharing | Discloses payment, email, storage, analytics, and analysis service providers |
| Email retention | Subscription records kept until unsubscribed | Names and email addresses retained for 12 months, then deleted |

The different email providers could serve different functions, and newsletter subscriptions could be subject to a separate retention arrangement. Likewise, processing by contracted service providers is distinct from selling data or permitting recipients to use it for their own purposes. However, the documents do not clearly reconcile these distinctions.[^5][^6]

The Privacy Policy also acknowledges possible transfers to the United States and European Union and claims to use contractual and technical safeguards for international processing. It does not identify the legal person responsible for administering these arrangements.[^5]

### GDPR Transparency and Controller Identification

StampStay expressly invokes GDPR compliance and offers privacy rights to EU and UK users.[^5][^6]

Article 13(1)(a) of the EU General Data Protection Regulation requires an applicable controller collecting personal data directly from individuals to disclose its identity and contact details, along with those of its representative where applicable.[^7]

A contact mailbox provides a means of communication but does not, by itself, identify the person or entity determining the purposes and means of processing.

Whether the EU GDPR applies depends on the circumstances of the processing, including the territorial-scope provisions in Article 3. StampStay's published commitment to GDPR principles does not independently establish its legal compliance. The lack of a clearly identified operator makes that commitment difficult to assess.

### Express Denial of Affiliation With Chad Scira

StampStay's About page contains an independence notice expressly denying affiliation with visa agents, visa agencies, and individual visa consultants, including Chad Scira. It also states that the service does not act as an intermediary or submit visa applications.[^6]

This denial is relevant to the scope of the repository's investigation and should be preserved alongside the website's other public representations.

**The reviewed documents do not establish any connection between StampStay and Thai Visa Centre or Chad Scira.** Nor does the inclusion of a specific denial provide evidence of such a connection.

The substantive concerns established here are the lack of a clearly identified legal operator, the unspecified governing jurisdiction, and unreconciled privacy disclosures. These issues matter independently of any alleged affiliation with another immigration business.

## Unsubstantiated Approval Rates and Conditional Refund Protection

StampStay's commercial visa-analysis page advertises three additional performance metrics alongside its paid immigration risk-assessment services:[^8]

- More than 487 analyses delivered.
- A 94% approval rate.
- An average customer rating of 4.9 out of 5.

However, the website does not identify the calculation methodology, reporting period, number of confirmed immigration outcomes, or independent review platform supporting these figures.

### Undefined Immigration Approval Statistics

StampStay sells assessments covering several distinct immigration situations, including entry through immigration checkpoints, Destination Thailand Visa applications, and border crossings.

Its advertised approval rate does not distinguish between these categories or explain whether customers who never subsequently traveled or applied for a visa are excluded from the calculation.

The website also does not explain how it verifies customer outcomes or accounts for customers who do not report their results.

**A numerical approval rate cannot establish the predictive accuracy of an immigration risk-assessment service without an adequately documented methodology.**

StampStay's Legal Disclaimer acknowledges that its information is derived partly from online community reports, aggregated user experiences, and publicly available information rather than official access to immigration records.[^4]

### Unsubstantiated Re-Entry Restrictions

The commercial sales page identifies a 60–90-day re-entry restriction and permanently reduced future entry flexibility among the consequences of being denied entry to Thailand.[^8]

However, it does not identify a government regulation or immigration order establishing such a restriction as a general consequence of entry refusal.

Thailand's official government guidance explains that immigration officers may refuse admission based on the grounds established by the Immigration Act.[^9]

An ordinary refusal of admission should not automatically be equated with a formal prohibition on future entry.

The website also estimates the average financial cost of an entry refusal at $1,500–$3,000 without identifying the underlying data supporting that calculation.

These claims appear within promotional material encouraging travelers to purchase a $79 risk assessment before booking their flights.

### Conditional Money-Back Guarantee

Section 4.3 of StampStay's Terms of Service advertises a full satisfaction-based refund guarantee but requires customers to submit their refund requests within seven days of receiving their analysis and explain why the service did not meet their expectations.[^2]

The contract separately addresses customers who follow StampStay's recommendations but are subsequently denied entry to Thailand.

Under that provision, the operator reviews the circumstances and determines whether its original analysis was sufficiently inaccurate to justify a refund.

The relationship between this special refund provision and the general seven-day deadline is not clearly explained.

This ambiguity is commercially significant because StampStay encourages customers to purchase assessments before booking their flights, potentially weeks or months before their planned arrival.

**A customer may not discover whether the purchased assessment accurately predicted their immigration outcome until after the ordinary refund period has expired.**

Section 6 also disclaims warranties concerning the accuracy, completeness, and reliability of the company's analysis.

The available evidence does not establish that StampStay has improperly denied refunds, fabricated customer testimonials, or deliberately manipulated its approval statistics.

It establishes that the website markets precise performance figures and potentially costly immigration consequences without publishing sufficient supporting evidence, while its contractual documents leave important questions about refund eligibility unresolved.

## Commercial Exploitation of Travel Disruptions and Omission of Government Relief Measures

StampStay prominently advertises commercial immigration risk assessments to travelers affected by the Middle Eastern conflict that began on February 28, 2026.

Its dedicated Thailand travel-disruption guide, dated March 2, 2026, promotes a US$79 professional risk assessment covering emergency extensions, overstay penalties, alternative departure strategies, and future immigration consequences.[^10]

A separate international disruption guide, published on March 15, 2026, directs readers toward the same commercial service.[^11]

### Omission of Official Overstay Fine Waivers

On March 2, 2026, Thailand's Immigration Bureau published temporary relief measures for foreign nationals unable to depart Thailand because of Middle Eastern airspace closures.[^12]

The Tourism Authority of Thailand reiterated these measures on March 4.[^13]

The government announced:

- Waiver of overstay fines upon departure for qualifying stranded travelers whose permission to stay expired on or after February 28, 2026.
- Temporary extensions of stay of up to 30 days per application.
- An alternative procedure allowing immigration officers to record applicants' circumstances when an embassy supporting letter could not be obtained.

Travelers whose permitted stay had already expired and who instead sought an extension remained subject to the applicable fine-settlement procedure.

Neither of StampStay's reviewed crisis guides mentions the departure-related fine waiver.

Its March 15 publication appeared almost two weeks after immigration authorities announced these measures.

### Unsubstantiated Emergency Extension Statistics

StampStay's Thailand guide additionally publishes precise purported approval probabilities:

| Circumstances | Claimed approval probability |
| --- | --- |
| Application at least seven days before expiry | 70–85% |
| Application three to six days before expiry | 40–60% |
| Application supported by an embassy letter | 70–85% |

The website does not provide underlying datasets, sample sizes, immigration statistics, or a reproducible methodology supporting these figures.

Its commercial assessment is advertised as drawing upon more than 500 documented crisis scenarios.[^10]

### Commercial Significance

StampStay encourages stranded travelers to purchase personalized assessments comparing extension costs, overstay penalties, alternative flights, and future immigration risks.

However, the omission of an officially announced financial relief measure materially undermines the accuracy of those comparisons.

**The website promotes paid immigration risk assessments to travelers experiencing an emergency while failing to disclose a directly relevant government measure designed to alleviate their financial hardship.**

Both guides remained publicly accessible when examined on November 26, 2026.

No independently verified evidence establishes a corporate relationship between StampStay and Thai Visa Centre, or whether customers incurred unnecessary expenses as a result of these publications.

## Outdated Immigration Risk-Scoring Claims

On March 7, 2026, StampStay published an article explaining how Thai immigration officers supposedly assess individual travelers' entry risks.[^14]

The article categorically denies that Thailand's immigration authorities operate a computerized system assigning numerical risk scores to travelers.

This guidance remained publicly accessible on November 27, 2026.

### Official Disclosure of Automated Risk Scoring

The Immigration Bureau's THIM Privacy Notice, version 3.0, dated October 9, 2026, expressly identifies automated processing for:

- Biometric identity verification.
- Document-authenticity assessment.
- Security risk scoring.
- Digital service credentials.

Section 14.2 additionally establishes human oversight, manual overrides, appeal procedures and algorithmic auditing.[^15]

The government therefore acknowledges automated risk-scoring capabilities that StampStay's published explanation fails to address.

### Commercial Significance

StampStay sells personalized immigration risk assessments for $79, with priority delivery costing $149.[^2]

Its educational content promotes the supposed predictability of immigration officers' assessments while encouraging travelers to purchase its proprietary risk analysis.

**StampStay continues monetizing immigration risk predictions without adequately accounting for the government's subsequently disclosed automated risk-scoring capabilities.**

The operator should update its published guidance and explain the limitations of its proprietary assessments in light of government systems whose internal scoring methodology is not publicly disclosed.

The official Privacy Notice does not establish that every traveler receives a numerical admission-risk score or that automated assessments independently determine admission decisions.

The available evidence also does not establish any corporate relationship between StampStay and Thai Visa Centre.

## Unverified Immigration Dataset Composition and Predictive Accuracy

StampStay's free Immigration Risk Checker advertises more than 500 real analyses as evidence supporting its assessment capabilities.[^16]

However, its About page confirms that every completed risk check contributes to its growing immigration intelligence dataset.[^6]

Completing a risk questionnaire does not independently establish that the traveler subsequently attempted to enter Thailand or experienced the predicted immigration outcome.

### Unverified Statistical Methodology

StampStay's Privacy Policy cites a 35% denial rate among travelers with at least 180 days in Thailand and three or more border runs as an example of the intelligence its dataset produces.[^5]

However, the company does not disclose how many records contain confirmed immigration outcomes, how duplicate submissions are excluded, or how it distinguishes hypothetical travel plans from completed journeys.

Its Legal Disclaimer also acknowledges reliance on publicly available community reports rather than official immigration records.[^4]

### Commercial Accountability

**StampStay promotes the size of its immigration intelligence dataset without establishing how much of that dataset consists of independently verified immigration outcomes.**

The number of completed questionnaires or delivered assessments is not equivalent to the number of documented admission or refusal decisions.

The operator should distinguish submitted travel profiles, community reports and confirmed immigration outcomes when presenting statistical probabilities to prospective paying customers.

The available evidence does not establish that StampStay's statistics are fabricated or that records without confirmed outcomes are necessarily included in its denial-rate calculations.

## Contradictory Comparisons With Professional and Community Immigration Advice

As examined on November 28, 2026, StampStay's commercial immigration assessment page presents its $79 service as a preferable alternative to community discussions and professional immigration lawyers.[^8]

### Criticizing Its Own Information Sources

The sales page criticizes Reddit and Facebook discussions for providing unreliable anecdotes that may lead travelers to underestimate their immigration risks.

However, StampStay's About page acknowledges that its immigration pattern database originated substantially from answering questions within Reddit communities.[^6]

Its Legal Disclaimer also identifies community reports, forums and social media among its principal information sources.[^4]

Aggregating community reports may provide additional analytical value, but the operator does not publish sufficient methodological evidence demonstrating that its paid assessments overcome the reliability limitations attributed to those sources.

### Discouraging Professional Consultation

The commercial sales page claims immigration lawyers charge between $300 and $500 per hour and characterizes their services as unnecessary for ordinary entry-risk assessments.[^8]

No comparative pricing research is supplied.

Meanwhile, StampStay's Legal Disclaimer explicitly instructs users to consult licensed immigration lawyers or authorized visa agents before making immigration-related decisions.[^4]

The operator also acknowledges that it is not a licensed immigration consultant and does not provide professional legal advice.

### Unverified Comparative Outcomes

The sales page presents a successful tourist visa entry as an example of its assessment capabilities, suggesting that the customer avoided possible adverse immigration consequences.[^8]

However, a successful entry following a recommendation does not establish what would have happened had the traveler followed their original plans.

### Commercial Significance

StampStay should distinguish the demonstrated capabilities of its informational assessments from unverified claims of superiority over free community resources and professional immigration services.

The available evidence does not establish that its assessments provide no value or that its published customer experiences are fabricated.

## Misrepresentation of Statutory Immigration Appeal Rights

On March 7, 2026, the StampStay Research Team published an article addressing the options available to travelers refused entry into Thailand.[^17]

The article categorically states that no formal appeal mechanism exists at the border and presents subsequent visa applications as the principal avenue for resolving entry refusals.

### Contradiction of Thai Immigration Law

Section 22 of the Immigration Act B.E. 2522 establishes a statutory appeal procedure against qualifying orders requiring foreigners to leave Thailand.[^18]

The legislation provides for:

- Written notification of the immigration officer's order.
- Appeals to the responsible minister, except in cases falling under Sections 12(1) and 12(10).
- A filing deadline of 48 hours following notification.
- Suspension of expulsion while a properly submitted appeal awaits determination.
- A statutory consequence favorable to the appellant if no ministerial decision is issued within seven days.

Thai immigration authorities also publish Form TM.11 specifically for this appeal procedure.[^19]

### Potential Harm to Travelers

StampStay advises rejected travelers to arrange their departure and seek subsequent immigration remedies from outside Thailand.

Travelers relying on this guidance could consequently miss the statutory deadline for challenging an eligible refusal.

### Commercial Significance

The publication promotes StampStay's immigration risk assessment services while providing materially inaccurate information about existing statutory protections.

The operator should distinguish the legal availability of an appeal from its practical difficulty, eligibility restrictions and likelihood of success.

The available evidence does not establish that any traveler has forfeited an appeal because of StampStay's advice, or that immigration authorities consistently facilitate the appeal process in practice.
