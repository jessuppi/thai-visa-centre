# asq.in.th

The terms and conditions published on `asq.in.th`, dated July 16, 2024, identify [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md) as the contracting company and list `contact@aq.in.th` as the contact address.

The email address provides a documented connection to [aq.in.th](aq.in.th.md), which [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md) has separately acknowledged operating during the COVID-19 pandemic.

The company's [agents.co.th](agents.co.th.md) website also identifies `asq.in.th` as its Ask Thailand community platform, providing another publicly acknowledged connection between the domain and Agents Co., Ltd.

These records establish the company's stated contractual role and the shared contact information. They do not independently establish legal ownership of either domain during the earlier pandemic period.

## Aggregation of Facebook Discussions and Reuse of Community Content

The `asq.in.th` website operates as **Ask Thailand**, a question-and-answer platform that republishes and organizes discussions from Thailand-focused online communities. Its About page claims to aggregate **millions of responses** from multiple communities covering immigration, visas, housing, banking, and expatriate life. The platform explicitly identifies [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md) as its maintainer and describes an associated community network with more than 200,000 combined members. These audience and content-volume claims are published by the operator and have not been independently verified.[^1]

The website reproduces historical discussions associated with Facebook communities, including conversations predating Thai Visa Centre's acquisition of the **Thai Visa Advice** group in March 2022. One archived discussion contains the former administrator Tod Daniels's announcement that the group had been acquired by Thai Visa Centre, followed by more than 100 responses from community members. Another reproduces a separate discussion questioning the acquisition's implications for independent immigration advice.[^2][^3]

These discussions are presented as standalone Ask Thailand question pages, with searchable titles, topic classifications, response summaries, engagement statistics, and extensive comment histories. The platform partially obscures participants' surnames while retaining their first names, original statements, discussion dates, and associated profile imagery. Some pages provide links back to Facebook conversations or groups, but the material is also independently accessible through the operator's own website.[^2][^3]

**The republication raises questions about authorization, consent, and the reuse of community contributions.** Participants who originally posted in Facebook groups may not have understood that their statements would subsequently appear on a separate immigration-information website operated by a commercial visa agency. The publicly available documentation does not establish whether individual participants consented to this secondary publication, whether permissions were obtained through group administrators, or whether particular material was originally publicly accessible.[^1][^2][^3]

The operator's Privacy Policy identifies [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md) as the responsible business and permits obtaining personal information from public databases and joint marketing partners. Its Data Protection Policy states that personal information should be collected through appropriate methods and generally not disclosed to third parties without consent, subject to applicable legal exceptions. However, these documents do not clearly explain the legal basis or consent arrangements governing the large-scale republication of historical Facebook discussions, particularly where participants never registered directly with Ask Thailand.[^4][^5]

The platform also introduces an additional layer of editorial control by generating independent question titles, summaries, classifications, and related-content recommendations around existing conversations. These editorial presentations can influence how readers interpret older discussions, including disputes about Thai Visa Centre itself. Although the platform acknowledges its commercial affiliation, readers may not distinguish between the original community contributions and summaries or organizational material subsequently added by the website operator.[^1][^2][^3]

**The central concern is the transformation of community-generated immigration advice into a searchable content resource controlled by a commercial immigration-services business.** The evidence establishes that Ask Thailand republishes historical discussions and identifies Thai Visa Centre as its maintainer. It does not establish unlawful data collection, copyright infringement, privacy violations, or manipulation of individual statements.

Nevertheless, the operation warrants closer examination of **publication permissions, personal-data handling, historical content preservation, editorial independence, and the commercial reuse of community discussions**.[^1][^4][^5]

## Asymmetric Content-Reuse Restrictions and Personal Data Obligations

Ask Thailand's published Disclaimer, dated July 16, 2024, imposes extensive restrictions on the reproduction and redistribution of content appearing on its website.[^6]

AGENTS CO., LTD. prohibits copying website content for commercial purposes and incorporating that content into other publications or websites without its express permission.

The Disclaimer also grants AGENTS unlimited and perpetual rights to reproduce, distribute, publish, and reuse information submitted to the company.

**These provisions are particularly remarkable because Ask Thailand itself aggregates and republishes extensive historical discussions originating in Facebook communities.**

The reviewed policies do not explain whether the original Facebook contributors granted AGENTS equivalent publication rights or whether the company obtained separate authorization to reproduce their contributions.

A contractual licence applicable to people submitting content directly to Ask Thailand does not automatically establish permission to reproduce material obtained from another platform.

### Obligations Under Section 25 of Thailand's PDPA

Section 25 of Thailand's Personal Data Protection Act B.E. 2562 (2019) specifically regulates the collection of personal information from sources other than the individuals concerned.[^7]

Where consent is required, the data controller must inform the affected individual without delay and within 30 days of collection and obtain consent. The legislation provides exceptions under Sections 24 and 26, alongside specified exceptions to notification requirements.

Ask Thailand's Privacy Policy acknowledges obtaining personal information from external sources, including public databases and marketing partners.[^4]

However, it does not specifically explain the legal basis for collecting and republishing identifiable Facebook contributions or how affected contributors are notified.

### Website Visitors Are Not Necessarily the Original Contributors

The platform's legal policies primarily address individuals accessing its website, submitting information, or purchasing services.

However, people whose historical Facebook contributions appear on Ask Thailand may never have visited the website or accepted its legal agreements.

Acceptance of Ask Thailand's terms by its visitors does not establish consent from the original contributors whose information has been republished.

### Commercial Control Over Community Content

The platform's Disclaimer additionally reserves the right to edit or remove submitted content and acknowledges that the website may generate affiliate commissions.[^6]

These provisions reinforce the distinction between an independent community archive and a commercially controlled publication maintained by Thai Visa Centre.

The available evidence does not establish that AGENTS lacks valid publication permissions or an applicable lawful basis for processing personal information.

Nevertheless, the company should clarify how its extensive republication of community discussions complies with the copyright, consent, notification, and personal-data obligations that apply to content obtained from external platforms.

**AGENTS expressly restricts third-party reproduction of its website content while leaving unresolved the permissions governing its own extensive reproduction of third-party community discussions.**

## Commercial Advertising Within Republished Community Discussions

Ask Thailand's republication of Facebook discussions also creates commercial advertising opportunities for its operator, Thai Visa Centre. The following examples are based on historical indexed page captures rather than a verified current advertising layout.

### December 2022: Guaranteed Entry and Border Runs

A discussion dated December 29, 2022 concerns a foreign national seeking a visa-run company capable of arranging guaranteed entry into Thailand.

One participant recommends contacting the Facebook group's owner for VIP airport-entry assistance and claims that the service can ensure successful entry.

The republished page incorporates additional promotional material identifying Thai Visa Centre as a trusted visa agency, displaying favorable customer testimonials and advertising its experience serving more than 100,000 expatriates.[^8]

### May 2025: Retirement Applicants Without Required Funds

A discussion dated May 12, 2025 concerns a prospective retirement visa applicant who explicitly states that he lacks the required 800,000-baht bank balance but can afford agency fees.

The discussion contains competing recommendations, criticism of agent-arranged financial assistance, and warnings about immigration compliance.

However, the republished page inserts a separate commercial resources section encouraging readers to contact Thai Visa Centre directly, complete the qualification test on [dtv.in.th](dtv.in.th.md), and join an associated Facebook community. It also displays favorable testimonials promoting the agency.[^9]

### Commercial Reuse of Historical Community Contributions

These examples demonstrate how historical discussions concerning immigration difficulties can be repackaged as pages promoting the operator's commercial services.

The advertising material is distinct from the original participants' contributions and should not be interpreted as an endorsement by those participants.

**Ask Thailand uses historical community discussions to attract prospective immigration customers while incorporating commercial recommendations for the visa agency responsible for maintaining the platform.**

The available evidence does not establish that individual contributors authorized this commercial reuse or that the advertisements appeared in the original Facebook discussions.

## Republication of Sensitive Personal Information

Ask Thailand's aggregation of historical community discussions extends beyond ordinary immigration questions to conversations involving medical conditions, treatment histories, criminal records, and previous convictions.

Publicly indexed examples include discussions concerning medical eligibility for Destination Thailand Visas and the immigration consequences of criminal convictions.[^10][^11]

Some contributors use anonymous identities, while the platform more generally retains partially obscured names, profile imagery, and historical discussion details.

### Enhanced Protection Under Thailand's PDPA

Section 26 of Thailand's Personal Data Protection Act establishes additional restrictions on collecting sensitive personal information, including health information and criminal records.[^7]

Unlike ordinary personal information, processing these categories generally requires explicit consent or a qualifying statutory exception.

Section 26 also establishes additional safeguards specifically concerning the collection of criminal-record information.

Although the legislation recognizes an exception for information publicly disclosed with the individual's explicit consent, participation in an online discussion does not automatically establish that this exception applies to subsequent commercial republication.

### Inadequate Explanation of Sensitive Data Processing

Ask Thailand's published Privacy Policy identifies ordinary account information, contact details, and basic personal information among the categories collected by the operator.[^4]

It also acknowledges collecting information from external sources.

However, the policy does not specifically explain the collection and republication of sensitive medical or criminal-record information obtained through historical community discussions.

Nor does it explain the safeguards applicable to this material or distinguish fully anonymized contributions from information that could remain attributable to identifiable individuals.

**Obscuring portions of a contributor's name does not necessarily establish that their personal information has been effectively anonymized.**

### Commercial Significance

Ask Thailand converts historical community discussions into independently searchable pages maintained by a commercial immigration-services business.

This creates an additional distribution channel for potentially sensitive information originally shared in a different context.

The operator should identify the applicable legal basis for this processing, explain its anonymization procedures, and provide an accessible mechanism through which original contributors can request removal.

The available evidence establishes the republication of sensitive discussion material but does not establish whether particular contributors remain identifiable or whether AGENTS has obtained appropriate consent.

## Obscured Government Links and Affiliated Immigration Referrals

An examination of Ask Thailand on November 26, 2026 identified examples of republished community discussions in which references to official government resources were obscured while links to commercially affiliated websites remained accessible.

### October and November 2026 TDAC Discussions

An October 18, 2026 discussion concerning TDAC fees contains several contributions recommending Thailand's free government registration service.[^12]

However, some links accompanying those recommendations are replaced with strings of asterisks.

A November 25, 2026 discussion concerning difficulties completing the TDAC contains similar obscured links, including links accompanying recommendations to use official government websites and mobile applications.[^13]

Both pages prominently display an operator-inserted notice directing readers to the privately operated `thim.in.th` website.

That website, in turn, prominently promotes AGENTS' commercial TDAC submission service, although it also provides a link to the official government portal.[^14]

### Contradiction With Published Verification Advice

Ask Thailand's About page advises readers to verify important immigration requirements through official Thai government sources.[^1]

Thailand's Immigration Bureau explicitly identifies `tdac.immigration.go.th` as its official registration website.[^15]

**Obscuring links accompanying community recommendations to use government resources while retaining prominent links to affiliated websites creates an inconsistent presentation of available immigration services.**

This arrangement can introduce an additional commercial intermediary between travelers and the government resources they are seeking.

### Duplicated Community Contributions

Both examined discussions also contain repeated comments attributed to the same displayed contributors.

The duplication raises questions about whether the platform's published response totals accurately represent distinct community contributions.

The available evidence does not establish whether these repetitions result from the original discussions, automated content-import procedures, or subsequent processing.

### Commercial Significance

Ask Thailand is not merely a passive archive of community conversations. Its operator adds summaries, classifications, related-content recommendations, and prominent referrals to affiliated immigration websites.

The operator should clarify its link-redaction policies, comment-deduplication procedures, and criteria for inserting commercial referrals into republished discussions.
