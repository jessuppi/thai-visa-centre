# thaivisareviews.com

`thaivisareviews.com` operates a Thai visa agency comparison website that aggregates customer reviews, publishes agency rankings, and displays corporate credentials and government accreditation badges.

The website describes itself as independent and claims to verify Thai business registrations, assess customer-review authenticity, and detect suspicious activity.[^1]

However, its profile of [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md) contains demonstrably incorrect corporate information and unsupported official accreditation claims.

## Incorrect Company Registration

Thai Visa Reviews assigns Thai Visa Centre the following credentials:

- **Company registration:** 0205562022140
- **Registered capital:** 5 million baht
- **Business history:** Seven years

The same profile identifies TVC's operating premises as The Pretium Bangna in Samut Prakan.[^2]

But registration number **0205562022140 belongs to THAIVISACENTER (THAILAND) COMPANY LIMITED**, a separately incorporated company registered in Chonburi on June 17, 2019.[^3]

The company associated with Thai Visa Centre's Bang Na operation is [TVC. Visa Agent Co., Ltd.](../companies/tvc-visa-agent-co-ltd.md), whose corporate records identify:

- **Company registration:** 0115563017485
- **Registered capital:** 6 million baht
- **Incorporation:** August 18, 2020

The corporate records also identify its registered location in Samut Prakan, matching the business premises advertised by TVC.[^4]

**The review directory combines the corporate registration and financial credentials of one company with the business identity and premises of another.**

It provides no explanation of the relationship between the two companies.

This is a significant discrepancy for a platform that explicitly claims to verify company registrations before presenting businesses as trustworthy visa providers.

## Unsupported Government Accreditation Badges

The same Thai Visa Centre profile displays two prominent accreditation badges:

- **Thailand Privilege Authorized Elite Visa Agent**
- **Thailand LTR Long-Term Resident Visa Certified Agency**

Neither credential is supported by the corresponding published government records.

Thailand's Board of Investment maintains an official list of certified LTR application agencies. The list includes Siam Legal, Thai Relo Services, and other approved providers, but **does not include Thai Visa Centre or TVC. Visa Agent Co., Ltd.**[^5]

Thailand Privilege's 2025 annual report also identifies 50 corporate General Sales and Services Agents (GSSAs). Neither company appears in that published list, although Siam Legal is explicitly identified as an authorized agent.[^6]

Thai Visa Reviews provides no government appointment reference, accreditation number, or supporting document for either badge.

The records do not rule out a subsequent appointment or accreditation under another legally identified entity. However, **the directory presents both credentials as established facts without supplying evidence that the listed business holds them**.

## Similar Accreditation Claims for Other Agencies

The problem extends beyond Thai Visa Centre.

Thai Visa Reviews also awards Triumph Property badges for Thailand Privilege authorization and LTR certification.[^7]

Yet Triumph Property does not appear on the BOI's published list of certified LTR agencies.[^5]

This second example raises questions about how the directory assigns official-looking credentials and whether it checks them against the responsible government agencies.

## Contradictory Verification Claims

Thai Visa Reviews prominently advertises its ability to verify business registrations and detect fraudulent customer feedback.

Its website claims that agency rankings are determined independently, without payments or sponsorships, and that reviews undergo authenticity checks.[^1]

However, its Terms of Service expressly disclaim verification of individual reviewer identities and experiences. They also warn users that the platform cannot guarantee the accuracy of published reviews.[^8]

These disclaimers sit uneasily beside its marketing claims of independently verified businesses and reliable customer-review analysis.

Thai Visa Centre nevertheless receives a **98% UserScore**, favorable evaluation scores, and prominent accreditation badges despite the incorrect corporate identity published on its profile.[^2]

## Questions About Independence and Credibility

**Thai Visa Reviews presents itself as an independent authority on visa-agency credibility while publishing inaccurate corporate credentials and government accreditation claims that make Thai Visa Centre appear more established and officially qualified.**

The discrepancies are particularly significant because the directory's stated purpose is to help customers distinguish trustworthy immigration businesses from questionable operators.

The available records do not establish who beneficially owns the directory or whether Thai Visa Centre controls its content.

Nevertheless, the combination of incorrect company details, unsupported accreditation badges, and contradictory verification claims undermines the directory's presentation as a reliable independent evaluator.

Its ownership, commercial relationships, badge-assignment procedures, and handling of incorrect agency information warrant further investigation.

## June 2026 Award Contradicts Published Qualification Requirements

Thai Visa Reviews awarded [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md) its **Agency of the Month for June 2026**, presenting the agency as an independently evaluated industry leader.[^9]

The award features a 98% UserScore, thousands of customer reviews, and a lengthy endorsement of TVC's operations, customer service, and processing times.

However, the award's own qualification requirements expose a serious problem.

### Mandatory Government Accreditations

Thai Visa Reviews explicitly states that every Agency of the Month winner satisfies its editorial standards, including:

- Registration as a Thai company.
- Minimum registered capital of 2 million baht.
- At least five years in business.
- Thailand Privilege authorization.
- LTR certification.
- At least 100 verified reviews.
- No legal complaints on record.[^9]

**The directory awarded TVC this distinction despite providing no evidence that it satisfies two of its mandatory government-accreditation requirements.**

Thailand's Board of Investment does not list Thai Visa Centre or TVC. Visa Agent Co., Ltd. among its published LTR-certified agencies.[^5]

Thailand Privilege's 2025 corporate-agent list likewise does not identify either business, although it explicitly identifies Siam Legal and other authorized operators.[^6]

The possibility of a later appointment does not explain why the award provides no accreditation numbers, appointment documents, or government verification links.

### Corporate Verification Also Fails

The award claims its winners must be registered Thai companies.

Yet the associated Thai Visa Centre profile assigns the agency registration number **0205562022140**, belonging to the separately registered THAIVISACENTER (THAILAND) COMPANY LIMITED rather than the TVC. Visa Agent Co., Ltd. business associated with its advertised premises.[^2][^3][^4]

The supposed independent verification process therefore failed to reconcile even the legal identity of the business receiving the award.

### Promotional Endorsement Presented as Independent Recognition

The award goes beyond displaying customer-review statistics.

It publishes extensive promotional praise for Thai Visa Centre, describes its technology and customer service as exceptional, and includes a statement attributed directly to **the TVC team**.[^9]

That quotation indicates that the award publication incorporated material presented as coming from the agency itself. It does not establish payment, ownership, or improper influence.

However, **the directory presents the result as independent recognition while its published eligibility requirements remain unsubstantiated**.

Its Terms of Service separately disclaim verification of individual reviewer identities and experiences, despite the award promoting its large verified review base.[^8]

### Promoting Immigration Processing Without Personal Attendance

Thai Visa Reviews identifies nationwide passport collection and delivery as one of the reasons it selected Thai Visa Centre for its June 2026 Agency of the Month award.

Its editorial endorsement explicitly states that customers in Phuket and other provinces:

> never have to visit immigration in person.

The award also praises the agency's retirement-extension processing, citing passport turnaround times of 48 hours to five days.[^9]

These representations are significant because the Immigration Bureau's published retirement-extension handbook describes an ordinary application procedure involving personal attendance, document inspection, and photographic recording of the applicant.[^22]

The [Thai Visa Centre investigation](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md) separately documents a March 2026 customer testimonial describing repeated retirement extensions without visiting an immigration office.

Rather than examining how such arrangements comply with published immigration procedures, Thai Visa Reviews presents the absence of personal attendance as a reason to recommend the agency.

Its own homepage advises consumers to exercise caution when agencies claim special government connections and acknowledges that commercial intermediaries cannot accelerate official government processing times.[^1]

**The directory promotes unusually convenient immigration-processing arrangements without explaining the official requirements that ordinarily apply to those procedures.**

Applicable exceptions or subsequent procedural changes may permit certain applications without personal attendance. The available evidence does not establish that individual extensions were obtained unlawfully.

Nevertheless, a publication claiming to independently evaluate immigration agencies should distinguish ordinary administrative assistance from arrangements that appear to depart from published government procedures.

### Credibility of the Award

The problem is not simply that Thai Visa Centre received a favorable review.

**Thai Visa Reviews publicly certified the agency as meeting specific standards that its own published evidence does not establish.**

The unsupported accreditation claims, incorrect corporate registration, and promotional award presentation form a consistent pattern of unreliable verification.

For consumers comparing expensive immigration services, the distinction between an independently validated professional credential and a privately issued promotional award is material.

The directory should disclose the records used to establish TVC's eligibility, identify the corporate entity evaluated, and explain how its accreditation requirements were satisfied.

## Award Requirements Contradicted by Other Winners' Profiles

Thai Visa Reviews claims that every Agency of the Month winner satisfies mandatory professional accreditation requirements.

Its published selection criteria explicitly include both:

- Thailand Privilege authorization.
- Long-Term Resident (LTR) visa certification.

However, the directory's own records for several award winners fail to establish that they satisfy both conditions.

### Three Additional Examples

**TMT Thailand — July 2026**

The directory awarded TMT Thailand its July 2026 distinction while stating that all winners satisfy both accreditation requirements.[^10]

However, TMT Thailand's agency profile displays a Thailand Privilege authorization badge **without an accompanying LTR certification badge**.[^11]

**TVS Visa Services Pattaya — August 2026**

The August award repeats the same mandatory accreditation requirements.[^12]

Yet TVS Visa Services Pattaya's profile displays an LTR certification badge **without a Thailand Privilege authorization badge**.[^13]

**Thai Visa Expert — September 2026**

The September award again identifies both credentials as required.[^14]

But Thai Visa Expert's directory profile displays **neither accreditation badge**, despite prominently identifying its September award.[^15]

### Contradiction in the Verification Process

These examples expose a recurring problem with the directory's stated standards.

The awards declare that all winners satisfy both accreditation requirements, but the corresponding company profiles do not consistently identify those credentials.

The missing badges do not independently establish that the agencies lack authorization. They establish that **Thai Visa Reviews' own published records fail to substantiate the mandatory credentials it claims to have verified**.

The inconsistency also undermines the explanation that Thai Visa Centre's unsupported accreditation badges were merely an isolated data-entry error.

Across several months, the directory appears to apply a standardized award qualification statement without demonstrating that each winner meets its requirements.

### Credibility of the Awards

Thai Visa Reviews markets its awards as independent recognition based on rigorous evaluation.

Yet its published evidence does not consistently support the qualifications it claims every winner possesses.

**An award requiring two government accreditations cannot credibly function as proof of those credentials when the awarding platform itself does not consistently document them.**

This repeated discrepancy raises questions about whether the accreditation requirements are actually enforced or simply displayed as promotional criteria to make privately issued awards appear more authoritative.

## Republishing Criminal Allegations Against Negative Reviewers

Thai Visa Reviews reproduces Thai Visa Centre's responses to negative Trustpilot reviews, including accusations of coordinated harassment and claims involving a disputed criminal case.

The agency's profile includes two particularly revealing examples.[^2]

### Negative Reviews Met With Criminal Accusations

A reviewer identified as **Denis** complained about receiving unsolicited emails from someone named Grace.

Another reviewer, **Paul E.**, criticized Thai Visa Centre's responses to negative feedback, describing the agency's treatment of complaining customers as threatening.

Both reviews received substantially identical responses attributed to Thai Visa Centre.

Rather than addressing either complaint individually, the agency accused negative reviewers of participating in a coordinated harassment campaign.

Its responses cited criminal-case reference **BKK-443/2567**, claimed that the person responsible was subject to an arrest warrant, and asserted that thousands of fraudulent accounts had been created to attack the company.[^2]

Neither response provides evidence connecting the individual reviewer to the purported campaign.

### Repeating the Disputed Arrest-Warrant Narrative

The case reference matches the criminal allegations separately promoted through TVC's own website and other publications associated with its business network.

Those allegations are examined in the [tvc.co.th investigation](tvc.co.th.md), including discrepancies concerning the reported warrant's dates and the absence of independently verifiable court documentation.

**Thai Visa Centre is using ordinary customer-review responses to circulate the same criminal accusations it publishes in its reputation-defense material.**

The responses do not distinguish between a genuine customer complaint, a malicious review, and an individual allegedly responsible for a broader harassment campaign.

Instead, both reviewers receive the same narrative about fraudulent accounts and criminal proceedings.

### Role of the Supposedly Independent Directory

Thai Visa Reviews describes itself as an independent review-analysis platform that helps consumers identify reliable immigration agencies.[^1]

Its Terms disclaim verification of individual reviewer identities and experiences, while its promotional material emphasizes detecting suspicious reviews.[^8]

Yet the directory republishes TVC's criminal allegations without accompanying evidence substantiating the asserted warrant or establishing that the affected reviewers participated in coordinated abuse.

The directory labels the statements as agency responses, so their publication does not establish that its operator endorses the allegations.

However, **the republication extends the reach of TVC's disputed criminal narrative into another website presented as an independent consumer-review resource**.

### Implications for Review Independence

The problem is not that visa agencies are prohibited from responding to criticism.

It is that Thai Visa Centre repeatedly substitutes accusations of criminal harassment for substantive replies to consumer complaints, while an ostensibly independent review platform reproduces those accusations alongside favorable credibility scores and promotional awards.

The available evidence does not establish coordination between the directory operator and TVC.

Nevertheless, the arrangement raises questions about whether the directory meaningfully evaluates intimidating or unsupported business responses when assessing agencies' transparency, reputation, and treatment of customers.

**A review platform cannot reliably assess customer trust without examining how businesses respond when customers criticize them.**

## Denying Agency Endorsements While Publishing Official-Looking Awards

Thai Visa Reviews claims to operate as an independent review aggregation platform rather than an agency endorsement service.

Its Terms of Service explicitly state that the platform does not provide official recommendations or endorsements. Under its review-accuracy disclosures, it goes further, stating that it does not endorse any specific agency or service.[^8]

However, the directory's actual publications directly contradict that position.

### Certifying and Recommending Specific Agencies

The June 2026 award page formally certifies Thai Visa Centre as its **Agency of the Month**.[^9]

The publication does considerably more than summarize third-party customer reviews.

It selects TVC as an outstanding provider, praises its professional capabilities, and explains why the directory considers the business deserving of recognition.

The corresponding agency profile displays a **98% UserScore**, labels TVC an **"Exceptional Choice"**, and assigns favorable scores for expertise, online presence, response speed, and transparency.[^2]

These are explicit editorial judgments about the quality and credibility of a particular commercial service.

**Thai Visa Reviews claims it does not endorse individual agencies while publishing its own certifications, recommendations, and promotional awards for those same businesses.**

### Contradictory Consumer Disclosures

The distinction matters because the website encourages travelers to rely on its rankings when choosing immigration service providers.

Its marketing emphasizes independent analysis, verified businesses, and reliable customer feedback.

Yet its contractual disclosures tell visitors that the platform provides no endorsements and that they must independently verify the accuracy of the information presented.

The site does not explain how a formal award certifying a business as an outstanding provider differs from the agency endorsements it expressly disclaims.

### Relevance to Thai Visa Centre

The contradiction is particularly significant for Thai Visa Centre because its award accompanies the incorrect company registration and unsupported government-accreditation badges documented above.

Those discrepancies have not prevented the directory from awarding TVC prominent recognition or presenting its services as an exceptional consumer choice.

**The directory cannot credibly present its awards as authoritative evaluations while simultaneously denying that it endorses the businesses receiving them.**

The inconsistency raises further questions about the meaning of its certifications, the accountability behind its rankings, and the reliability of the assurances presented to consumers.

## Undisclosed Ownership and Data Controller Identity

Thai Visa Reviews claims that verifying company registrations and legal credentials is a central part of its independent agency-rating system.[^1]

However, the directory does not apply comparable transparency to itself.

Its published Terms of Service, Privacy Policy, and Contact page identify the business only as **Thai Visa Reviews**, without providing:

- The registered company or individual's legal identity.
- A company registration number.
- The jurisdiction in which the operator is established.
- An identifiable business office or responsible legal representative.

The website supplies several email addresses but no information allowing visitors to verify the business entity behind its supposedly independent evaluations.[^8][^16][^17]

### Collecting Personal Information Without Identifying the Operator

The Privacy Policy states that the platform collects names, email addresses, submitted reviews, contact messages, IP addresses, and browsing information.

It also processes customer reviews obtained from Google, Facebook, and Trustpilot.[^16]

The policy repeatedly claims compliance with the European Union's General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR).

However, **it never identifies the legal person or registered company responsible for processing that information**.

Article 13 of the GDPR requires organizations subject to its provisions to disclose the identity and contact details of the data controller.[^18]

A generic platform name and email address do not adequately explain which legal entity assumes responsibility for customer information.

The omission does not independently establish a GDPR violation, but it directly undermines the website's claims of transparent and accountable data handling.

### Unclear Legal Accountability

The directory's Terms also require disputes to undergo binding arbitration under unspecified international arbitration rules.[^8]

They do not identify the contracting legal entity, governing jurisdiction, arbitration institution, or location where disputes would be resolved.

This makes it difficult for reviewers, businesses, and consumers to determine who is legally responsible for inaccurate information, disputed reviews, or the website's own published credentials.

### The Verification Double Standard

**Thai Visa Reviews claims authority to investigate other businesses' legal identities while withholding the equivalent information about its own operation.**

That contradiction is especially significant given its incorrect corporate registration for Thai Visa Centre, unsupported government accreditation badges, and promotional awards based on unsubstantiated credentials.

The available evidence does not establish that Thai Visa Centre owns or controls the directory.

Nevertheless, **without a disclosed operator, the directory's claimed independence cannot be meaningfully verified**.

Its legal ownership, corporate registration, data-controller identity, and commercial relationships should be established before its rankings and awards are treated as credible independent assessments.

## Conflicting Review Totals and Unverifiable UserScore Calculations

Thai Visa Reviews markets its UserScore system as an independent assessment of customer satisfaction based on reviews aggregated from Google, Facebook, and Trustpilot.

However, several agency profiles publish inconsistent review totals without explaining how the figures relate to one another.

### Different Review Counts on the Same Pages

Four examples illustrate the discrepancy:

| Agency | Reviews analyzed | Reviews section | Difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Thai Visa Centre | 2,704 | 2,350 | 354 |
| TMT Thailand | 385 | 340 | 45 |
| TVS Visa Services Pattaya | 299 | 276 | 23 |
| Thai Visa Expert | 210 | 201 | 9 |

These figures appear on the respective agency profiles.[^2][^19][^20][^21]

The individual positive, neutral, and negative review counts generally add up to the larger *reviews analyzed* figures, not the smaller totals displayed above the review sections.

Yet each of these pages displays only **20 individual reviews**, with no additional reviews available through the visible listing.

### No Explanation of the Missing Reviews

The directory does not explain whether its different totals represent excluded submissions, unpublished reviews, historical records, or distinct data collections.

Nor does it provide enough individual review records for visitors to independently reproduce its calculations.

**Thai Visa Centre receives a 98% UserScore based on 2,704 supposedly analyzed reviews, but its review section identifies only 2,350 reviews and makes just 20 available for inspection.**

The discrepancy does not prove that reviews were fabricated. It demonstrates that the published information is insufficient to verify the advertised calculation.

### Outdated Evaluation Date

Thai Visa Centre's profile introduces another inconsistency.

Its agency evaluation assigns scores for expertise, online presence, response speed, and transparency, followed by an update date of **December 12, 2025**.[^2]

However, the same profile prominently advertises the agency's June 2026 award and incorporates customer reviews from later in 2026.

The page does not explain whether its evaluation scores were reassessed after December 2025 or how newer information affects those assessments.

### Reliability of the Rankings

These discrepancies matter because the directory uses its numerical scores to identify exceptional agencies and recommend businesses to prospective immigration customers.

**A platform claiming to independently analyze thousands of customer experiences should explain its sample sizes, exclusions, scoring calculations, and evaluation dates.**

Instead, Thai Visa Reviews publishes unexplained differences in review totals, offers only a small fraction of the underlying records for inspection, and displays favorable evaluation scores without a clear update history.

Combined with its previously documented incorrect corporate information and unsupported accreditation badges, these findings raise further questions about the reliability of its supposedly independent verification process.

## Incorrect LTR Employment Rights and Financial Eligibility Requirements

Thai Visa Reviews publishes a dedicated guide to Thailand's Long-Term Resident (LTR) visa program alongside its commercial agency rankings and recommendations.[^23]

However, several of its published eligibility requirements directly contradict guidance issued by Thailand's Board of Investment (BOI).

### Incorrect Work-Permit Guidance

The directory claims that Work-from-Thailand Professionals and Highly-Skilled Professionals receive digital work permits, while holders of the wealthy visa categories do not qualify for employment authorization.

The BOI's official FAQ establishes substantially different rules:[^24]

| LTR category | Eligibility to apply for a work permit |
| --- | --- |
| Wealthy Global Citizen | Eligible |
| Wealthy Pensioner | Eligible |
| Highly-Skilled Professional | Eligible |
| Dependent | Eligible |
| Work-from-Thailand Professional | Not eligible |

Eligibility to apply for a work permit does not automatically authorize employment. Applicants must satisfy the applicable employment and work-permit requirements.

**Thai Visa Reviews effectively reverses the employment restrictions applicable to remote workers and wealthy LTR visa holders.**

This error could have significant consequences for foreigners selecting a visa category based on their intended employment arrangements.

### Incorrect Financial Eligibility Requirements

The directory describes Wealthy Global Citizens as requiring either US$1 million in assets or US$80,000 in annual income.[^23]

However, the BOI currently requires both US$1 million in qualifying assets and at least US$500,000 in qualifying Thai investments. The previous minimum-income requirement is no longer included in the published criteria.[^25]

The directory also describes Wealthy Pensioners as requiring either US$40,000 in annual income or US$250,000 in investments.

In reality, the BOI requires either:

- At least US$80,000 in annual passive income; or
- At least US$40,000 in annual passive income combined with US$250,000 in qualifying Thai investments.[^25]

Investment alone does not satisfy the pensioner category's financial requirements.

**These discrepancies concern fundamental visa eligibility requirements rather than minor differences in terminology.**

### Unsubstantiated Success Rate

The guide prominently advertises a 95% LTR visa success rate without identifying the underlying application records, sample size, reporting period, or calculation methodology.[^23]

It does not explain whether this figure represents government approval statistics, outcomes reported by listed agencies, or an internally generated estimate.

The directory also advertises commercial agency fees ranging from 30,000 to 80,000 baht.

The BOI confirms that its application and qualification-assessment process is free, with the government visa issuance fee payable following approval.[^24]

Commercial assistance may legitimately involve additional charges, but the directory provides no evidence establishing that purchasing such assistance produces the advertised success rate.

### Implications for Agency Recommendations

These inaccuracies are particularly significant because Thai Visa Reviews presents itself as an independent evaluator of immigration agencies and their professional expertise.

Its LTR guide directs prospective applicants toward commercial service providers, while its agency profiles display purported government accreditation credentials.

The available evidence does not establish that customers suffered financial losses or immigration consequences because of the directory's guidance.

Nevertheless, **a publication claiming to verify immigration specialists should accurately represent the fundamental eligibility requirements of the services it recommends.**

## Misleading DTV Eligibility Requirements and Unsubstantiated Approval Statistics

Thai Visa Reviews' Destination Thailand Visa (DTV) guide advertises a 97% success rate and commercial agency fees ranging from 25,000 to 60,000 baht.[^26]

However, its published application instructions contain significant omissions and misleading generalizations concerning official visa requirements.

### Missing Permanent Residence Requirement

The directory recommends neighboring countries such as Laos, Vietnam, and Malaysia as potential application destinations for foreigners already visiting Thailand.

However, Thailand introduced additional supporting-document requirements on August 31, 2026, including proof of permanent residence in the jurisdiction where the application is submitted.[^27]

Although the directory includes criminal-record clearance in its document checklist, it omits the permanent residence requirement.

### Exaggerated Financial and Insurance Requirements

The directory categorically states that each applicant, including dependent children, must demonstrate 500,000 baht in their own bank account.

However, the Royal Thai Embassy in Bucharest's guidance, updated November 15, 2026, expressly recognizes sponsorship letters as an alternative form of financial evidence, including for dependent applications.[^28]

The directory also lists health insurance among the required DTV application documents, despite its absence from the embassy's published standard checklist.

Individual diplomatic missions may request additional documentation, but the directory fails to distinguish mission-specific requirements from general visa eligibility conditions.

### Unsubstantiated 97% Success Rate

The directory does not identify the application records, sample size, reporting period, or calculation methodology supporting its advertised 97% success rate.

It also does not establish whether this figure represents independent applicants, customers of commercial agencies, or an internally generated estimate.

These omissions are particularly significant because the directory recommends paid immigration agencies while presenting itself as an independent evaluator of professional visa services.

The available evidence does not establish deliberate deception or financial losses suffered by applicants. It does establish discrepancies between the directory's published guidance and official immigration requirements, alongside an unsubstantiated numerical approval claim.
