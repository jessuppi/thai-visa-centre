# aseannow.com

`aseannow.com` hosts [ASEAN NOW](../brands/asean-now.md), a Thailand-focused English-language expatriate news and discussion forum formerly known as ThaiVisa.com, acquired by [The Thaiger](../brands/the-thaiger.md) in April 2024. The forum lists [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md) as a commercial sponsor. Jesse Nickles has raised concerns about alleged promotional astroturfing, fabricated positive testimonials, and censorship of criticism targeting Thai Visa Centre and its related services. These allegations have not been independently established.

## Early Reports of Thai Visa Centre Marketing (2018)

On July 12, 2018, a discussion on ASEAN NOW's predecessor, ThaiVisa.com, questioned whether [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md) could legitimately arrange retirement extensions for applicants who lacked the required 800,000-baht bank balance.[^7]

Forum member **Tanoshi** reproduced an unsolicited promotional email attributed to the agency, advertising retirement extensions for 16,000 baht without requiring applicants to demonstrate bank deposits or pension income. The message claimed:

> We SPONSOR all your requirements[^7]

The email also described passport collection services and arrangements involving immigration officers allegedly working with the agency.

Tanoshi stated that they had never contacted Thai Visa Centre or requested information from a visa agency. They questioned how the business obtained their email address, noting that they had recently provided contact information to a document translation service. However, the source of the mailing list and any potential sharing of personal information were not established.

The discussion documents early public concerns about the agency's advertised financial arrangements and unsolicited marketing, years before the forum's later sponsorship and moderation controversies. The authenticity of the reproduced email and the legality of the described services were not independently established.

## Thai Visa Centre's Response to the 2020 Police Raid

On August 12, 2020, ThaiVisa.com members reported receiving promotional emails from [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md), just eight days after Thai immigration police publicly announced arrests connected to an alleged visa-forgery operation involving the agency. Police reported discovering forged documents and 55 government-agency rubber stamps during the investigation.[^8][^9]

One forum member reproduced an email dated August 10, attributed to Grace at Thai Visa Centre, reassuring customers that the agency remained operational. The message claimed:

> Immigration has already verified that we are a legitimate visa agent, which is why we are still open.[^8]

Forum participants questioned this assurance, noting the recent police allegations and asking whether the investigation had actually been resolved. Others raised concerns about unsolicited marketing emails and conflicting statements concerning Grace's continued involvement with the agency.

The discussion documents Thai Visa Centre's efforts to reassure potential customers and continue advertising its services immediately following the arrests. However, the reproduced email provided no independent evidence that Thai immigration authorities had formally cleared the agency of wrongdoing, and the discussion did not establish the eventual outcome of the investigation.

## Forum Moderator Dismissal of the 2020 Police Investigation

On November 21, 2020, a ThaiVisa.com member considering [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md) asked whether using a law firm would provide greater protection following the agency's reported legal troubles earlier that year. Global Moderator [DrJack54](../moderators/drjack54.md) dismissed those concerns, stating:

> Nothing resulted from unrelated police visit to TVC earlier this year.[^14]

He further claimed that immigration stamps and receipts obtained through agents were legitimate and dismissed critics of visa agents as uninformed. His assurances came approximately three months after Thai immigration police publicly announced arrests and allegations involving forged immigration documents.

On February 6, 2022, DrJack54 again recommended Thai Visa Centre to a member seeking a Bangkok visa agent. When another participant expressed surprise at the recommendation following the earlier controversy, DrJack54 replied:

> Wasn't a scandal.[^15]

He encouraged readers to consult the agency's favorable online reviews, comparing negative reports to ordinary customer complaints about other businesses.

These exchanges document a Global Moderator repeatedly dismissing concerns about Thai Visa Centre's reported police investigation while recommending its commercial services. His statements did not establish the investigation's outcome or provide independent evidence resolving the original allegations.

## Sponsorship and Censorship of Criticism

ASEAN NOW's published forum rules provide special protections for commercial sponsors. **Rule 37** states:

> You will not post disrespectful, dismissive, or off-topic comments about advertisements, sponsored content, or ASEAN NOW sponsors or Partners.[^1]

Rule 24 separately exempts forum sponsors from the general prohibition on commercial promotion, allowing paying advertisers to promote businesses in ways ordinary members cannot.[^1]

On November 5, 2025, ASEAN NOW administrator **Captain Flack** publicly confirmed removing a post criticizing [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md), describing it as an off-topic attack against a forum sponsor. He invoked Rule 37 and warned the member against posting negative comments about sponsors, an interpretation broader than the published rule's wording.[^2]

The discussion also featured favorable recommendations from forum moderators and promotional replies from Thai Visa Centre's official sponsor account. The incident raises questions about whether ASEAN NOW's commercial sponsorship arrangements compromise the independence of discussions concerning immigration services. The removed comment is no longer publicly visible, preventing independent assessment of its contents.

## Censorship of Visa-Agent Legality Discussions

On September 4, 2025, ASEAN NOW Global Moderator [DrJack54](../moderators/drjack54.md) announced the removal of posts questioning the legality of visa-agent arrangements during a discussion about obtaining retirement extensions without meeting the usual 800,000-baht bank deposit requirement:

> Off topic posts regarding legality or otherwise of agents removed along with reply. Off topic[^18]

During the same discussion, DrJack54 recommended [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md). On September 5, the agency's official **Forum Sponsor** account advertised retirement visa packages costing 60,000 baht for bank-account opening, a Non-O visa, and an annual extension, or 37,000 baht without bank-account opening. The agency also advertised expedited processing and invited customers to contact it directly.[^19]

On December 4, 2025, another member published a positive testimonial about Thai Visa Centre, praising the agency's handling of a retirement extension. When other members questioned the financial arrangements involved, DrJack54 again intervened:

> Posts re legality or otherwise of agents removed.[^6]

The original poster challenged the moderation decision, noting that he was sharing firsthand experience with a forum sponsor. The following day, he disclosed that his retirement extension had been issued through **Chai Nat province despite residing in Bangkok**, and confirmed that the agency offered financial assistance.

The pattern continued in May 2026, when another member sought Thai Visa Centre's assistance because he lacked the required 800,000-baht bank balance. DrJack54 referred him to the agency, whose sponsor account subsequently advertised annual extensions starting at approximately 16,000 baht. When participants questioned the legality of obtaining extensions without meeting the financial requirements, DrJack54 stated:

> Thread is not about legality or otherwise of use of agent.[^20]

He also confirmed removing off-topic posts and repeatedly discouraged further debate about the legality of such arrangements.

These incidents document repeated restrictions on discussions about visa-agent legality while favorable testimonials, commercial recommendations, and Thai Visa Centre's advertisements remained published. Although the deleted posts cannot be independently reviewed, the moderator's own statements confirm that questions concerning legality were among the subjects removed.

## Public Complaints on Trustpilot

As of November 2026, ASEAN NOW's Trustpilot profile displayed a **2.2 out of 5 TrustScore**, based on nine reviews, eight of which awarded one star. Reviewers raised complaints about censorship, forum bans, allegedly fake accounts, commercial advertising, and the reliability of published content.[^17]

On November 6, 2025, a reviewer identified as **Clive** reported being banned without warning after several years of participation. According to the review, the ban followed a question asking how forum sponsor [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md) obtained people's email addresses. The reviewer claimed that moderators treated the question as a negative attack against a sponsor.

The complaint appeared one day after ASEAN NOW administrator Captain Flack publicly acknowledged removing criticism of Thai Visa Centre under Rule 37. Although the timing and subject matter closely correspond, the available evidence does not establish whether the Trustpilot reviewer was involved in that particular forum incident.

Other reviews described similar dissatisfaction with moderation. In August 2025, a reviewer using the name **spud** alleged that a temporary ban referencing a four-year-old post became permanent after requesting an explanation. The reviewer also accused moderators of censoring private messages and criticized the forum's extensive advertising, sponsored articles, and advertorial-style content. Reviews published in May 2024 and October 2025 likewise complained about censorship and heavy-handed moderation.

On March 29, 2025, a reviewer identified as **Robbie** described ASEAN NOW as being populated by fake accounts, raising concerns about the authenticity of forum participation. The review did not identify specific accounts or provide evidence establishing coordinated activity.

On November 27, 2025, another reviewer, **Kristian**, alleged that ASEAN NOW relied on scraped and AI-generated material taken from other media outlets, alongside internally produced promotional articles. The reviewer argued that this approach resulted in incomplete or inaccurate reporting and blurred the distinction between journalism and advertising.

A February 2026 review by **Hon** separately alleged plagiarism and manipulated forum discussions, reinforcing earlier complaints about the authenticity and reliability of the website's content.

Taken together, these reviews document recurring public dissatisfaction with ASEAN NOW's moderation, advertising practices, editorial quality, and forum authenticity. Some allegations overlap with the sponsor-related moderation incidents independently documented in this article. However, claims concerning fake accounts, plagiarism, manipulated discussions, and AI-generated content remain unverified. The small number of Trustpilot reviews also limits their usefulness as a measure of overall member satisfaction.

## Moderator Accounts Promoting Thai Visa Centre

Multiple ASEAN NOW Global Moderators have publicly recommended services offered by forum sponsor [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md), including retirement visa extensions, airport entry assistance, and immigration reporting.

On August 31, 2024, [DrJack54](../moderators/drjack54.md) participated in a discussion concerning repeated visa-exempt entries and paid airport assistance. After fellow Global Moderator [BrandonJT](../moderators/brandonjt.md) identified Thai Visa Centre as a provider, DrJack54 directly invited the agency to explain its services:

> @ThaiVisaCentre can you advise.[^21]

On September 4, Thai Visa Centre responded through its official **Forum Sponsor** account, advertising airport fast-track and prescreening services costing approximately 2,000–8,000 baht, depending on the traveler's immigration history. The agency encouraged readers to contact it through LINE for individual quotations.[^21]

On September 10, DrJack54 acknowledged that repeated visa-exempt entries could result in questioning or refusal by immigration officers. Referring to the services discussed in the thread, he stated:

> Yes there are agents that can grease the wheels.[^22]

On May 4, 2025, BrandonJT directed forum users to Thai Visa Centre's airport "safe entry" service at `asq.in.th/thailand-fasttrack`, acknowledging that the agency had previously supplied the account with its booking link.[^3]

On April 22, 2026, BrandonJT recommended Thai Visa Centre's `90day.in.th` immigration reporting service to DrJack54, providing pricing information and details about the service.[^4]

These exchanges document moderators actively recommending a commercial sponsor, directly inviting the agency into discussions, and providing prospective customers with information about its paid services. The available evidence does not establish whether these recommendations were independently motivated, coordinated, or compensated, or whether the advertised airport arrangements operated as described.

## Moderator Claims About Immigration Payments and Temporary Bank Deposits

On November 8, 2025, ASEAN NOW Global Moderator [BrandonJT](../moderators/brandonjt.md) described how some visa agents allegedly arrange retirement extensions for applicants who cannot meet Thailand's financial requirements.

According to BrandonJT, agents accompany customers to a bank, temporarily deposit 800,000 baht into their accounts, obtain bank letters and updated passbooks, and subsequently withdraw the money. He further alleged:

> Part of the fee you pay the agent is passed on to the immigration office[^23]

He claimed these payments enabled applicants to obtain extensions despite failing to maintain the required bank balance. Fellow Global Moderator [DrJack54](../moderators/drjack54.md) subsequently acknowledged BrandonJT's explanation of how agents covered financial requirements.

On October 1, 2026, BrandonJT again described temporary agent-funded deposits, explaining that customers returned the 800,000 baht after the necessary bank documents were obtained. When another participant questioned how this arrangement satisfied the mandatory deposit-holding periods, BrandonJT claimed:

> Senior immigration officers can waive this requirement.[^24]

Thailand's published immigration guidance generally requires applicants using the bank-deposit method to maintain 800,000 baht for two months before applying and three months after approval, followed by a minimum balance of 400,000 baht during the remaining period.[^25]

These exchanges document a Global Moderator publicly describing arrangements that appear inconsistent with published immigration requirements, including an explicit allegation that visa-agent fees are passed to immigration offices. However, the statements provide no independent evidence establishing that such payments occurred or that immigration officers exercised the claimed authority.

Although BrandonJT has repeatedly recommended [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md) elsewhere on ASEAN NOW, neither discussion identifies the agency as participating in the alleged payments or temporary-deposit arrangements.

## Out-of-Province Visa Extensions Promoted by Forum Moderators

On August 13, 2026, an elderly ASEAN NOW member living in Korat sought advice about obtaining retirement extensions without personally attending immigration due to mobility difficulties. Global Moderator [DrJack54](../moderators/drjack54.md) directly tagged [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md), inviting the agency to assist.[^26]

The member subsequently explained that he had already contacted Thai Visa Centre but had been told the agency did not handle applications through Korat Immigration.

Fellow Global Moderator [BrandonJT](../moderators/brandonjt.md) disputed the apparent limitation, claiming:

> TVC can get you an extension anywhere in Thailand.[^26]

BrandonJT explained that when Thai Visa Centre did not work with an applicant's local immigration office, the agency could instead arrange an extension through an office in another province. He suggested that the applicant decide whether such an arrangement was acceptable.

DrJack54 separately suggested approaching Korat Immigration directly to explore alternatives, stating that this might require an arrangement to "grease the wheels." Other participants pointed out that assistance with immigration applications might be available without hiring a commercial agent.[^26]

Thailand's Immigration Bureau public handbook specifies that retirement-extension applicants should contact the immigration checkpoint responsible for the area where they reside.[^27]

The exchange documents forum moderators recommending a commercial sponsor while discussing arrangements that appear inconsistent with the published residence-based application procedure. However, BrandonJT did not explain how out-of-province processing would satisfy the applicable requirements, and the discussion does not establish that Thai Visa Centre actually arranged such an extension for this applicant.

## Claims of Guaranteed Airport Entry Services

On July 23, 2025, ASEAN NOW Global Moderator [BrandonJT](../moderators/brandonjt.md) publicly described paid airport "safe entry" services as **"100% reliable"**, claiming that agents could arrange guaranteed admission to Thailand through contacts with immigration officers.[^10]

The account described a process in which travelers submitted copies of every passport page for screening by airport contacts. According to BrandonJT, customers would also provide a photograph of their clothing before boarding so an immigration officer could identify them upon arrival, escort them to a counter, and personally facilitate their entry.

BrandonJT then directed readers to [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md), explaining how to purchase its VIP/Fast Track entry service through `tdac.agents.co.th`. Another forum participant questioned the legitimacy of the arrangement.[^10]

On June 23, 2026, Thailand's Immigration Division 2 announced an investigation into brokers allegedly charging foreigners 4,500–12,000 baht for guaranteed airport entry. Authorities established a fact-finding committee to examine possible misconduct involving immigration officers and warned of criminal and disciplinary consequences if wrongdoing was established.[^11]

The following day, BrandonJT responded to an ASEAN NOW discussion about the investigation by predicting that paid entry services would return to normal once public attention moved elsewhere. On June 25, he further mocked the prospect of effective enforcement:

> We've investigated ourselves and found no wrongdoing[^30]

On July 19, after other forum members reported that airport "safe entry" services had been suspended or restricted, BrandonJT claimed that they remained available at Don Mueang, Chiang Mai, and Phuket airports. He also mentioned an unconfirmed rumor that services had resumed at Suvarnabhumi.[^30]

By October 2026, however, BrandonJT offered a more restrictive assessment. Responding to a traveler who had previously been denied entry and used paid airport assistance, he stated that his latest information indicated **Krabi was the only airport where such services remained available**. Other participants discussed possible limited operations by different agencies at Suvarnabhumi.[^31]

These exchanges document a Global Moderator continuing to discuss and defend the availability of paid airport-entry arrangements following an official investigation into similar services. The changing availability claims suggest disruption to the broader market, although the extent of any restrictions was not independently established.

The official investigation did not publicly identify BrandonJT or Thai Visa Centre as subjects of the inquiry, and the forum discussion does not establish whether either was involved in the activities under investigation.

## Passport Data Collection and Airport Screening Claims

On May 7, 2025, ASEAN NOW Global Moderator [BrandonJT](../moderators/brandonjt.md) recommended [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md)'s airport "safe entry" service, directing a forum member to `asq.in.th/thailand-fasttrack`.[^28]

The member questioned the unfamiliar website, noting that it requested an upload of their passport information page without first providing a clear explanation of the service. They also asked whether the website actually belonged to Thai Visa Centre.

Rather than addressing the passport-data concerns, fellow Global Moderator [DrJack54](../moderators/drjack54.md) reproduced a Facebook testimonial from a traveler claiming to have been admitted into Thailand in approximately **20 seconds after using the airport entry service**, despite a history of numerous visa-exempt entries. DrJack54 then encouraged the member to contact the agent directly. The testimonial was not independently verified.[^28]

BrandonJT subsequently explained that the passport information was necessary because the agency needed to check the traveler's immigration history with contacts at the airport. According to the account, those contacts would identify potential problems affecting the price of the service.[^28]

The published terms and conditions for `asq.in.th`, dated July 16, 2024, identify [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md) as the contracting company. The terms also list `contact@aq.in.th` as the contact address.[^29]

On May 9, Thai Visa Centre's official **Forum Sponsor** account joined the same discussion and promoted `tdac.agents.co.th` as an alternative way to order its services, including optional paid early TDAC submissions.[^28]

The exchange raises questions about the transparency of commercial airport-entry services promoted by ASEAN NOW moderators, particularly the collection of sensitive passport information and claims of access to immigration records through airport contacts.

However, the forum discussion does not independently establish whether such airport screening arrangements existed, how submitted passport information was handled, or whether any customer data was improperly accessed or disclosed.

## Forum Promotion of Unofficial TDAC Websites

In May 2025, ASEAN NOW published a discussion warning travelers about unofficial Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) websites charging fees for a government service that was available free of charge. However, an **“Admin note”** added to the opening post explicitly described `tdac.in.th` and `tdac.agents.co.th` as:

> legitimate websites operated by AGENTS CO., LTD.[^5]

The note acknowledged that Thailand's official TDAC portal was `tdac.immigration.go.th`, while providing favorable assurances about the privately operated alternatives.

In the same discussion, Thai Visa Centre's official **Forum Sponsor** account criticized a competing commercial TDAC website for misleading travelers and inadequate business disclosures. The agency acknowledged operating `tdac.in.th`, initially described it as an informational website without paid services, and subsequently edited its post to advertise an $8 early-submission service.[^5]

The combination of favorable website assurances and direct commercial promotion by a forum sponsor raises questions about ASEAN NOW's separation of independent travel information from advertiser interests.

## Unsubstantiated Security Claims About the Official TDAC Website

On May 24, 2025, ASEAN NOW Core Administrator **CharlieH** published a promotional discussion advertising [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md)'s commercial Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) submission service, including paid early applications.[^12]

On July 9, Global Moderator [BrandonJT](../moderators/brandonjt.md) responded to a member concerned about personal-data security by claiming that information submitted through Thailand's official government TDAC website had a **"50% chance of being stolen by hackers"**, attributing the alleged risk to previous government data breaches.[^12]

The account provided no supporting statistics or evidence for this extraordinary claim. On August 9, BrandonJT subsequently defended the commercial platform's optional $8 early-submission fee, arguing that travelers might prefer paying for convenience rather than using the government's free service.[^12]

These statements contrast with the Thai Immigration Bureau's June 2025 public warning encouraging travelers to register exclusively through `tdac.immigration.go.th` to protect their personal information. The bureau emphasized that official TDAC registration was free and warned against unofficial websites requesting payments or personal information.[^13]

The discussion documents an ASEAN NOW administrator promoting a commercial TDAC service while a Global Moderator made an unsupported allegation about the security of the official government alternative. It raises further questions about the reliability and independence of immigration advice published alongside commercial sponsorships.

## Formal Consumer Complaint to Singapore Authorities (2026)

In November 2026, **Jesse Nickles** reported submitting a formal complaint to the Competition and Consumer Commission of Singapore (CCCS) concerning [The Thaiger](../brands/the-thaiger.md), ASEAN NOW, and their commercial relationship with [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md). The complaint was subsequently made publicly available.[^16]

The complaint alleged that ASEAN NOW's sponsorship arrangements and moderation practices could mislead consumers seeking independent immigration advice. It cited the suppression of criticism concerning commercial sponsors, favorable recommendations by forum moderators, and the promotion of unofficial Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) services.

Nickles requested regulatory scrutiny of these practices, raising concerns about whether commercial sponsorship was being adequately distinguished from independent forum discussions and consumer recommendations.

The complaint represents a formal escalation of the concerns documented in this article. Its allegations remain disputed or unverified, and no regulatory findings or enforcement decision have been established from the available information.
