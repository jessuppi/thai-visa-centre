# aseannow.com

`aseannow.com` hosts [ASEAN NOW](../brands/asean-now.md), a Thailand-focused English-language expatriate news and discussion forum formerly known as ThaiVisa.com, acquired by [The Thaiger](../brands/the-thaiger.md) in April 2024. The forum lists [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md) as a commercial sponsor. Jesse Nickles has raised concerns about alleged promotional astroturfing, fabricated positive testimonials, and censorship of criticism targeting Thai Visa Centre and its related services. These allegations have not been independently established.

## Early Reports of Thai Visa Centre Marketing (2018)

On July 12, 2018, a discussion on ASEAN NOW's predecessor, ThaiVisa.com, questioned whether [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md) could legitimately arrange retirement extensions for applicants who lacked the required 800,000-baht bank balance.[^7]

Forum member **Tanoshi** reproduced an unsolicited promotional email attributed to the agency, advertising retirement extensions for 16,000 baht without requiring applicants to demonstrate bank deposits or pension income. The message claimed:

> We SPONSOR all your requirements[^7]

The email also described passport collection services and arrangements involving immigration officers allegedly working with the agency.

Tanoshi stated that they had never contacted Thai Visa Centre or requested information from a visa agency. They questioned how the business obtained their email address, noting that they had recently provided contact information to a document translation service. However, the source of the mailing list and any potential sharing of personal information were not established.

The discussion documents early public concerns about the agency's advertised financial arrangements and unsolicited marketing, years before the forum's later sponsorship and moderation controversies. The authenticity of the reproduced email and the legality of the described services were not independently established.

## Thai Visa Centre's Response to the 2020 Police Raid

On August 12, 2020, ThaiVisa.com members reported receiving promotional emails from [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md), just eight days after Thai immigration police publicly announced arrests connected to an alleged visa-forgery operation involving the agency. Police reported discovering forged documents and 55 government-agency rubber stamps during the investigation.[^8][^9]

One forum member reproduced an email dated August 10, attributed to Grace at Thai Visa Centre, reassuring customers that the agency remained operational. The message claimed:

> Immigration has already verified that we are a legitimate visa agent, which is why we are still open.[^8]

Forum participants questioned this assurance, noting the recent police allegations and asking whether the investigation had actually been resolved. Others raised concerns about unsolicited marketing emails and conflicting statements concerning Grace's continued involvement with the agency.

The discussion documents Thai Visa Centre's efforts to reassure potential customers and continue advertising its services immediately following the arrests. However, the reproduced email provided no independent evidence that Thai immigration authorities had formally cleared the agency of wrongdoing, and the discussion did not establish the eventual outcome of the investigation.

## Sponsorship and Censorship of Criticism

ASEAN NOW's published forum rules provide special protections for commercial sponsors. **Rule 37** states:

> You will not post disrespectful, dismissive, or off-topic comments about advertisements, sponsored content, or ASEAN NOW sponsors or Partners.[^1]

Rule 24 separately exempts forum sponsors from the general prohibition on commercial promotion, allowing paying advertisers to promote businesses in ways ordinary members cannot.[^1]

On November 5, 2025, ASEAN NOW administrator **Captain Flack** publicly confirmed removing a post criticizing [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md), describing it as an off-topic attack against a forum sponsor. He invoked Rule 37 and warned the member against posting negative comments about sponsors, an interpretation broader than the published rule's wording.[^2]

The discussion also featured favorable recommendations from forum moderators and promotional replies from Thai Visa Centre's official sponsor account. The incident raises questions about whether ASEAN NOW's commercial sponsorship arrangements compromise the independence of discussions concerning immigration services. The removed comment is no longer publicly visible, preventing independent assessment of its contents.

The incident also reinforces concerns about the preferential visibility enjoyed by ASEAN NOW's commercial sponsors compared with businesses whose recommendations depend on ordinary forum participants.

The following day, TonBrow claimed that Thai Visa Centre had been arranging their extensions since the COVID-19 pandemic and sending their passport to immigration offices in Thailand's northeastern Isan region. The poster expressed concern about whether the resulting stamps were legitimate and whether presenting them at a local immigration office might expose irregularities.

Another forum member, **zzzzz**, strongly defended the agency, claiming that Thai Visa Centre included TM30 address registrations and initial 90-day reports without additional charges. TonBrow disputed this description:

> They've never sent me a tm 30 and always charge 500 baht to make a 90 day report.[^34]

Global Moderator [DrJack54](../moderators/drjack54.md) questioned which immigration office issued the extension, where the poster resided, and where their 90-day reports were processed. Other participants warned that extensions issued outside an applicant's province of residence could attract additional scrutiny, particularly where address records or financial arrangements appeared inconsistent.

Thai Visa Centre participated through its official **Forum Sponsor** account, stating that obtaining a residence certificate should not be problematic if the required documents were in order. The agency also advertised its ability to arrange certificates using digital passport copies without requiring customers to submit their physical passports.[^34]

The discussion documents concerns raised by a forum member claiming firsthand experience with Thai Visa Centre, alongside conflicting accounts of the agency's services and fees. Neither the poster's claimed customer relationship nor the independence of other participating accounts was established. No evidence was presented demonstrating that the visa stamps were forged or improperly obtained.

## Thai Visa Centre Promoted After Allegations Against Pattaya Visa Agent

On October 27, 2025, an ASEAN NOW account named **PointCleaner** opened a discussion claiming to have obtained a retirement extension through a visa agent in Pattaya despite living near Bangkok.[^58]

The account alleged that previous 90-day immigration reports had been processed successfully, but problems emerged after renewing the passport. According to the poster, their local immigration office directed them to Chonburi province and suggested that the existing visa extension might not be valid.

The allegations portrayed the unidentified Pattaya agent as having potentially arranged questionable immigration paperwork. However, the original account's claimed experience was not independently verified, and no documentation establishing that the extension was invalid was presented.

PointCleaner subsequently asked whether an agency near Bangkok could help resolve the situation.

Global Moderator [DrJack54](../moderators/drjack54.md) responded by tagging [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md), introducing the forum's commercial sponsor as a possible alternative. He also suggested that the original Pattaya agent had probably arranged immigration stamps through Jomtien Immigration rather than the office responsible for the applicant's actual residence.[^58]

On October 29, Thai Visa Centre joined the discussion through its official **Forum Sponsor** account. Rather than establishing whether the alleged problems with the original visa extension were genuine, the agency advertised its paid 90-day reporting service:

> Our in-person 90-day reporting service is available for as little as 375 THB[^58]

The agency directed readers to `90day.in.th`, promoting online applications, downloadable confirmation documents, tracking facilities, and EMS delivery.

The sequence raises an obvious question about the discussion's independence. An account introduced an alarming story about a supposedly problematic Pattaya visa agent, a Global Moderator directed the conversation toward Thai Visa Centre, and the commercial sponsor subsequently advertised its own services.

**This structure is consistent with a possible astroturfing or competitor-disparagement scenario**, in which an apparently independent consumer complaint creates negative perceptions of competing visa agencies while generating favorable exposure for a sponsoring business.

The original Pattaya agency was not identified by name. Nevertheless, the discussion could influence readers to view Pattaya-based visa agents more broadly as unreliable or potentially involved in improper immigration arrangements, while presenting Thai Visa Centre as an alternative.

Other participants challenged the circumstances described in the thread. An account named **proton** criticized questionable visa-agent arrangements and argued that the applicant should resolve the problem through official immigration procedures, including passport transfers, address registration, and subsequent online reporting.

[Tod Daniels](../people/tod-daniels.md), whose ASEAN NOW account carried the special **"Thai Visas Forum Expert"** designation, also participated.

Daniels explained that extensions issued through Pattaya could create complications when applicants subsequently dealt with immigration offices elsewhere. He recommended contacting the original agent, transferring the appropriate immigration stamps, updating the TM30 address registration, and completing an initial 90-day report in person before returning to online reporting.[^58]

He also discussed alternative Bangkok-based visa agencies, noting that arranging extensions through Bangkok's Chaeng Watthana Immigration office was generally more expensive than using agents operating through Pattaya.

On November 2, DrJack54 announced that an off-topic post had been removed. The deleted content cannot be independently reviewed.

The discussion is particularly noteworthy because its original allegations were never substantiated, yet they provided the context for a commercial sponsor to promote services to readers concerned about the reliability of competing agencies.

If the thread was organically initiated, it illustrates the practical complications that can arise from out-of-province immigration processing. **If it was deliberately seeded, it would represent a potentially misleading form of advertising: using an unverified complaint about a Pattaya agent to undermine confidence in competitors and redirect prospective customers toward Thai Visa Centre.**

The available evidence does not establish that PointCleaner was a fabricated account, that the complaint was invented, or that Thai Visa Centre coordinated the discussion. However, the combination of an unverified negative account, moderator intervention, and subsequent sponsor advertising warrants scrutiny rather than treating the discussion as an independently verified consumer experience.

## Claims of Guaranteed Airport Entry Services

The account described a process in which travelers submitted copies of every passport page for screening by airport contacts. According to BrandonJT, customers would also provide a photograph of their clothing before boarding so an immigration officer could identify them upon arrival, escort them to a counter, and personally facilitate their entry.

BrandonJT then directed readers to [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md), explaining how to purchase its VIP/Fast Track entry service through `tdac.agents.co.th`. Another forum participant questioned the legitimacy of the arrangement.[^10]

On June 23, 2026, Thailand's Immigration Division 2 announced an investigation into brokers allegedly charging foreigners 4,500–12,000 baht for guaranteed airport entry. Authorities established a fact-finding committee to examine possible misconduct involving immigration officers and warned of criminal and disciplinary consequences if wrongdoing was established.[^11]

The following day, BrandonJT responded to an ASEAN NOW discussion about the investigation by predicting that paid entry services would return to normal once public attention moved elsewhere. On June 25, he further mocked the prospect of effective enforcement:

> We've investigated ourselves and found no wrongdoing[^30]

On July 19, after other forum members reported that airport "safe entry" services had been suspended or restricted, BrandonJT claimed that they remained available at Don Mueang, Chiang Mai, and Phuket airports. He also mentioned an unconfirmed rumor that services had resumed at Suvarnabhumi.[^30]

By October 2026, however, BrandonJT offered a more restrictive assessment. Responding to a traveler who had previously been denied entry and used paid airport assistance, he stated that his latest information indicated **Krabi was the only airport where such services remained available**. Other participants discussed possible limited operations by different agencies at Suvarnabhumi.[^31]

These exchanges document a Global Moderator continuing to discuss and defend the availability of paid airport-entry arrangements following an official investigation into similar services. The changing availability claims suggest disruption to the broader market, although the extent of any restrictions was not independently established.

The official investigation did not publicly identify BrandonJT or Thai Visa Centre as subjects of the inquiry, and the forum discussion does not establish whether either was involved in the activities under investigation.

The exchange raises questions about the transparency of commercial airport-entry services promoted by ASEAN NOW moderators, particularly the collection of sensitive passport information and claims of access to immigration records through airport contacts.

## Possible Astroturfing in Thai Visa Centre Passport-Mailing Discussion

On March 17, 2025, an ASEAN NOW account named **DanBlack** opened a discussion titled "Retirement Visa," asking whether a Bangkok-based agency could arrange a retirement extension by mail for someone living in Phuket.[^66]

Although the question appeared to be an ordinary request for immigration advice, the poster specifically named [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md) and introduced a favorable price comparison:

> I have been shopping around, and they are the cheapest[^66]

The account simultaneously raised doubts about the agency's proposed procedure, explaining that the poster had heard immigration extensions required applicants to attend in person.

**The framing is noteworthy because it introduced Thai Visa Centre as an attractive, low-cost option while creating an opportunity for other participants to publicly reassure prospective customers about its legitimacy.**

Such a format can resemble promotional seeding or astroturfing, in which apparently independent consumer questions generate favorable recommendations for a particular business. However, the original post alone does not establish that DanBlack was acting on behalf of Thai Visa Centre.

### Moderators Defend the Agency Against Passport-Mailing Concerns

Global Moderator [DrJack54](../moderators/drjack54.md) initially asked which visa category the poster held and clarified the distinction between visas and extensions.

Another forum member, **Ben Zioner**, raised concerns about mailing passports and money to an unfamiliar business. He warned that the arrangement could be a scam, although he supplied no evidence that Thai Visa Centre itself was fraudulent.

DrJack54 quickly dismissed the practical concern:

> It's common practice if using agent.[^66]

Another participant, **scubascuba3**, questioned this reassurance, observing that customers commonly delivered their passports to visa agents personally rather than mailing them.

On March 18, fellow Global Moderator [BrandonJT](../moderators/brandonjt.md) entered the discussion and explicitly endorsed Thai Visa Centre.

He explained that the agency served customers throughout Thailand, that customers outside Bangkok generally needed to mail their passports, and that readers could consult its online reviews.

BrandonJT stated:

> They're a legit agent[^66]

He also emphasized the distinction between Thai Visa Centre and another business called Thai Visa Center, directing attention toward the sponsor's precise commercial identity.

The exchange effectively transformed the original poster's uncertainty into an opportunity for two Global Moderators to reassure readers about Thai Visa Centre's services.

### Another Account Supplies a Favorable Customer Testimonial

Shortly after BrandonJT's endorsement, an account named **zzzzz** entered the discussion and claimed to have personally used Thai Visa Centre.

The account confirmed that the agency could handle the proposed extension and repeated the favorable price comparison:

> yes they are the cheapest unless you travel to Pattaya[^66]

This response reinforced two elements of the original post: Thai Visa Centre's supposed affordability and its ability to handle immigration paperwork remotely.

The apparent progression was therefore:

1. **DanBlack:** Introduces Thai Visa Centre as the cheapest available agency while requesting reassurance about postal processing.
2. **DrJack54:** Normalizes handing passports to agents despite objections.
3. **BrandonJT:** Explicitly endorses Thai Visa Centre as legitimate and explains its nationwide service.
4. **zzzzz:** Claims firsthand experience and repeats the assertion that Thai Visa Centre offers exceptionally low prices.
5. **Thai Visa Centre:** Subsequently enters the discussion to defend its business practices and promote its commercial services.

This sequence is consistent with a pattern sometimes associated with promotional seeding: an apparently independent question introduces a brand, other accounts supply endorsements, and the business ultimately benefits from increased visibility.

**It is not proof of coordinated astroturfing.** The thread provides no evidence that DanBlack or zzzzz were paid, controlled by the agency, or instructed to post favorable comments.

Nevertheless, the repeated emphasis on Thai Visa Centre's affordability, legitimacy, and convenience makes the discussion relevant when evaluating the wider pattern of promotional activity involving ASEAN NOW's commercial sponsor.

### Thai Visa Centre Appears With Refund and Security Assurances

On March 27, approximately ten days after the original question, Thai Visa Centre joined the discussion through its official **Forum Sponsor** account.

Rather than simply answering the original poster's procedural question, the agency responded extensively to concerns raised by skeptical members.

Thai Visa Centre confirmed that it accepted original passports through secure postal delivery requiring signatures upon receipt.

It also claimed that customer payments were treated as refundable deposits, supported by written documentation, and that refunds were consistently issued whenever customers canceled or applications could not be completed.[^66]

The agency further claimed that it submitted cases promptly, did not unnecessarily retain customer documents, and maintained reliable processing times.

These assurances were made by the agency itself. The forum discussion provided no independent evidence verifying its refund history, internal document-handling procedures, or turnaround times.

Thai Visa Centre concluded its response with commercial links, including its live-chat service at `tvc.co.th/chat` and an advertisement for early Thailand Digital Arrival Card submissions through `tdac.in.th`.

Consequently, a discussion originally framed as a consumer question had developed into a public endorsement and marketing opportunity for ASEAN NOW's sponsor.

### Why the Discussion Raises Astroturfing Concerns

Several characteristics distinguish this thread from a straightforward request for independent advice.

The original poster volunteered Thai Visa Centre's name and a favorable price claim before receiving any recommendations. Two Global Moderators then defended the agency's legitimacy or practices. A separate account supplied a positive customer testimonial and repeated the pricing claim. Finally, Thai Visa Centre itself joined the discussion with detailed business assurances and advertising links.

The resulting thread could be particularly persuasive to readers arriving through search engines because the favorable recommendations appear within an ordinary public discussion rather than on the agency's own website.

**This is precisely the credibility advantage that makes astroturfing effective: commercial promotion presented within what appears to be independent consumer conversation.**

The discussion also contains genuine skepticism. Ben Zioner and scubascuba3 questioned the passport-mailing arrangement, demonstrating that the thread was not uniformly favorable to Thai Visa Centre.

Nor is there evidence establishing that the original poster was fabricated, that the favorable testimonial was purchased, or that the sponsor coordinated the timing of the discussion.

Nevertheless, the combination of favorable framing, repeated moderator reassurance, an unverified customer endorsement, and the sponsor's eventual promotional response makes this a noteworthy example of **possible organic-looking commercial promotion**.

The incident reinforces broader concerns about ASEAN NOW's commercial sponsorship arrangements, particularly when Global Moderators repeatedly endorse a paying sponsor in discussions where readers may reasonably expect independent advice.

## Unsubstantiated Security Claims About the Official TDAC Website

On May 24, 2025, ASEAN NOW Core Administrator **CharlieH** published a promotional discussion advertising [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md)'s commercial Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) submission service, including paid early applications.[^12]

On May 26, an ASEAN NOW member reported receiving another traveler's passport information after submitting a TDAC application through Thailand's official government website. According to the member, the downloadable PDF contained a Chinese traveler's name, passport number, date of birth, and accommodation details, even though the member's confirmation email and online application records displayed the correct information.[^32]

Global Moderator [BrandonJT](../moderators/brandonjt.md) responded by claiming that he had heard about similar problems from the company operating `tdac.agents.co.th`, stating:

> They were getting this about 1 in 500 entries.[^32]

BrandonJT reassured the member that the correct email confirmation should remain usable and attributed the problem to Thailand's government IT systems. However, he provided no independent evidence supporting the reported error rate or establishing how the commercial service obtained those figures.

On July 9, BrandonJT responded to another member concerned about personal-data security by claiming that information submitted through Thailand's official government TDAC website had a **"50% chance of being stolen by hackers"**, attributing the alleged risk to previous government data breaches.[^12]

The account provided no supporting statistics or evidence for this claim. On August 9, BrandonJT subsequently defended the commercial platform's optional $8 early-submission fee, arguing that travelers might prefer paying for convenience rather than using the government's free service.[^12]

These statements contrast with the Thai Immigration Bureau's June 2025 public warning encouraging travelers to register exclusively through `tdac.immigration.go.th` to protect their personal information. The bureau emphasized that official TDAC registration was free and warned against unofficial websites requesting payments or personal information.[^13]

The exchanges document a Global Moderator repeatedly criticizing the security of Thailand's official TDAC system while defending a commercial alternative and citing information allegedly obtained from its operator. The reported PDF mix-up has not been independently verified, and neither the alleged error rate nor the claimed 50% hacking risk was substantiated.

## Formal Consumer Complaint to Singapore Authorities (2026)

In November 2026, **Jesse Nickles** reported submitting a formal complaint to the Competition and Consumer Commission of Singapore (CCCS) concerning [The Thaiger](../brands/the-thaiger.md), ASEAN NOW, and their commercial relationship with [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md). The complaint was subsequently made publicly available.[^16]

The complaint alleged that ASEAN NOW's sponsorship arrangements and moderation practices could mislead consumers seeking independent immigration advice. It cited the suppression of criticism concerning commercial sponsors, favorable recommendations by forum moderators, and the promotion of unofficial Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) services.

Nickles requested regulatory scrutiny of these practices, raising concerns about whether commercial sponsorship was being adequately distinguished from independent forum discussions and consumer recommendations.

The complaint represents a formal escalation of the concerns documented in this article. Its allegations remain disputed or unverified, and no regulatory findings or enforcement decision have been established from the available information.
