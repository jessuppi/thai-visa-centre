# aseannow.com

`aseannow.com` hosts [ASEAN NOW](../brands/asean-now.md), a Thailand-focused English-language expatriate news and discussion forum formerly known as ThaiVisa.com, acquired by [The Thaiger](../brands/the-thaiger.md) in April 2024. The forum lists [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md) as a commercial sponsor. Jesse Nickles has raised concerns about alleged promotional astroturfing, fabricated positive testimonials, and censorship of criticism targeting Thai Visa Centre and its related services. These allegations have not been independently established.

## Early Reports of Thai Visa Centre Marketing (2018)

On July 12, 2018, a discussion on ASEAN NOW's predecessor, ThaiVisa.com, questioned whether [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md) could legitimately arrange retirement extensions for applicants who lacked the required 800,000-baht bank balance.[^7]

Forum member **Tanoshi** reproduced an unsolicited promotional email attributed to the agency, advertising retirement extensions for 16,000 baht without requiring applicants to demonstrate bank deposits or pension income. The message claimed:

> We SPONSOR all your requirements[^7]

The email also described passport collection services and arrangements involving immigration officers allegedly working with the agency.

Tanoshi stated that they had never contacted Thai Visa Centre or requested information from a visa agency. They questioned how the business obtained their email address, noting that they had recently provided contact information to a document translation service. However, the source of the mailing list and any potential sharing of personal information were not established.

The discussion documents early public concerns about the agency's advertised financial arrangements and unsolicited marketing, years before the forum's later sponsorship and moderation controversies. The authenticity of the reproduced email and the legality of the described services were not independently established.

## Thai Visa Centre's Response to the 2020 Police Raid

On August 12, 2020, ThaiVisa.com members reported receiving promotional emails from [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md), just eight days after Thai immigration police publicly announced arrests connected to an alleged visa-forgery operation involving the agency. Police reported discovering forged documents and 55 government-agency rubber stamps during the investigation.[^8][^9]

One forum member reproduced an email dated August 10, attributed to Grace at Thai Visa Centre, reassuring customers that the agency remained operational. The message claimed:

> Immigration has already verified that we are a legitimate visa agent, which is why we are still open.[^8]

Forum participants questioned this assurance, noting the recent police allegations and asking whether the investigation had actually been resolved. Others raised concerns about unsolicited marketing emails and conflicting statements concerning Grace's continued involvement with the agency.

The discussion documents Thai Visa Centre's efforts to reassure potential customers and continue advertising its services immediately following the arrests. However, the reproduced email provided no independent evidence that Thai immigration authorities had formally cleared the agency of wrongdoing, and the discussion did not establish the eventual outcome of the investigation.

## Sponsorship and Censorship of Criticism

ASEAN NOW's published forum rules provide special protections for commercial sponsors. **Rule 37** states:

> You will not post disrespectful, dismissive, or off-topic comments about advertisements, sponsored content, or ASEAN NOW sponsors or Partners.[^1]

Rule 24 separately exempts forum sponsors from the general prohibition on commercial promotion, allowing paying advertisers to promote businesses in ways ordinary members cannot.[^1]

On November 5, 2025, ASEAN NOW administrator **Captain Flack** publicly confirmed removing a post criticizing [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md), describing it as an off-topic attack against a forum sponsor. He invoked Rule 37 and warned the member against posting negative comments about sponsors, an interpretation broader than the published rule's wording.[^2]

The discussion also featured favorable recommendations from forum moderators and promotional replies from Thai Visa Centre's official sponsor account. The incident raises questions about whether ASEAN NOW's commercial sponsorship arrangements compromise the independence of discussions concerning immigration services. The removed comment is no longer publicly visible, preventing independent assessment of its contents.

On March 28, 2024, police spokesman Maj Gen Cherngron Rimpadee stated that immigration fast-track lanes at Bangkok's airports were reserved for eligible priority travelers and warned against online advertisements offering access for a fee.[^71]

The warning concerned a separate business, not Thai Visa Centre, and did not specifically address the agency's later "safe entry" claims. Nevertheless, it provides relevant official context for evaluating commercial promises involving preferential immigration processing.

On July 23, 2025, ASEAN NOW Global Moderator [BrandonJT](../moderators/brandonjt.md) publicly described paid airport "safe entry" services as **"100% reliable"**, claiming that agents could arrange guaranteed admission to Thailand through contacts with immigration officers.[^10]

The account described a process in which travelers submitted copies of every passport page for screening by airport contacts. According to BrandonJT, customers would also provide a photograph of their clothing before boarding so an immigration officer could identify them upon arrival, escort them to a counter, and personally facilitate their entry.

BrandonJT then directed readers to [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md), explaining how to purchase its VIP/Fast Track entry service through `tdac.agents.co.th`. Another forum participant questioned the legitimacy of the arrangement.[^10]

On June 23, 2026, Thailand's Immigration Division 2 announced an investigation into brokers allegedly charging foreigners 4,500–12,000 baht for guaranteed airport entry. Authorities established a fact-finding committee to examine possible misconduct involving immigration officers and warned of criminal and disciplinary consequences if wrongdoing was established.[^11]

The following day, BrandonJT responded to an ASEAN NOW discussion about the investigation by predicting that paid entry services would return to normal once public attention moved elsewhere. On June 25, he further mocked the prospect of effective enforcement:

> We've investigated ourselves and found no wrongdoing[^30]

On July 19, after other forum members reported that airport "safe entry" services had been suspended or restricted, BrandonJT claimed that they remained available at Don Mueang, Chiang Mai, and Phuket airports. He also mentioned an unconfirmed rumor that services had resumed at Suvarnabhumi.[^30]

By October 2026, however, BrandonJT offered a more restrictive assessment. Responding to a traveler who had previously been denied entry and used paid airport assistance, he stated that his latest information indicated **Krabi was the only airport where such services remained available**. Other participants discussed possible limited operations by different agencies at Suvarnabhumi.[^31]

These exchanges document a Global Moderator continuing to discuss and defend the availability of paid airport-entry arrangements following an official investigation into similar services. The changing availability claims suggest disruption to the broader market, although the extent of any restrictions was not independently established.

The official investigation did not publicly identify BrandonJT or Thai Visa Centre as subjects of the inquiry, and the forum discussion does not establish whether either was involved in the activities under investigation.

## Passport Data Collection and Airport Screening Claims

On May 7, 2025, ASEAN NOW Global Moderator [BrandonJT](../moderators/brandonjt.md) recommended [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md)'s airport "safe entry" service, directing a forum member to `asq.in.th/thailand-fasttrack`.[^28]

Rather than addressing the passport-data concerns, fellow Global Moderator [DrJack54](../moderators/drjack54.md) reproduced a Facebook testimonial from a traveler claiming to have been admitted into Thailand in approximately **20 seconds after using the airport entry service**, despite a history of numerous visa-exempt entries. DrJack54 then encouraged the member to contact the agent directly. The testimonial was not independently verified.[^28]

BrandonJT subsequently explained that the passport information was necessary because the agency needed to check the traveler's immigration history with contacts at the airport. According to the account, those contacts would identify potential problems affecting the price of the service.[^28]

The published terms and conditions for `asq.in.th`, dated July 16, 2024, identify [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md) as the contracting company. The terms also list `contact@aq.in.th` as the contact address.[^29]

On May 9, Thai Visa Centre's official **Forum Sponsor** account joined the same discussion and promoted `tdac.agents.co.th` as an alternative way to order its services, including optional paid early TDAC submissions.[^28]

The exchange raises questions about the transparency of commercial airport-entry services promoted by ASEAN NOW moderators, particularly the collection of sensitive passport information and claims of access to immigration records through airport contacts.

However, the forum discussion does not independently establish whether such airport screening arrangements existed, how submitted passport information was handled, or whether any customer data was improperly accessed or disclosed.

## Possible Astroturfing in Thai Visa Centre Passport-Mailing Discussion

On March 17, 2025, an ASEAN NOW account named **DanBlack** asked whether [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md) could arrange a retirement extension by mail for someone living in Phuket.[^66]

Although the poster questioned whether immigration rules permitted remote processing, they introduced the agency with a favorable price comparison:

> I have been shopping around, and they are the cheapest[^66]

The framing is noteworthy because an apparently independent consumer inquiry simultaneously introduced Thai Visa Centre as an inexpensive option and invited other participants to reassure readers about its legitimacy.

### Moderators and Another Account Endorse the Agency

When forum member **Ben Zioner** warned against mailing passports or money to an unfamiliar agent, Global Moderator [DrJack54](../moderators/drjack54.md) responded:

> It's common practice if using agent.[^66]

Another participant, **scubascuba3**, questioned this reassurance, noting that customers commonly delivered passports to visa agents personally rather than mailing them.

On March 18, fellow Global Moderator [BrandonJT](../moderators/brandonjt.md) explicitly endorsed Thai Visa Centre:

> They're a legit agent[^66]

He explained that the agency served customers throughout Thailand and that customers outside Bangkok generally mailed their passports. He also directed readers toward its online reviews.

Another account, **zzzzz**, subsequently claimed firsthand experience with Thai Visa Centre and repeated the original poster's favorable price comparison:

> yes they are the cheapest unless you travel to Pattaya[^66]

The apparent progression was therefore an independent-looking question introducing Thai Visa Centre, reassurance from two Global Moderators, and a favorable testimonial from another account.

This pattern could resemble promotional seeding or astroturfing, although the discussion does not establish that the original question or testimonial was coordinated with the agency.

### Thai Visa Centre Enters With Commercial Assurances

On March 27, Thai Visa Centre participated through its official **Forum Sponsor** account, responding directly to concerns about sending passports and payments to the agency.

The company claimed that passports were accepted through secure postal delivery requiring signatures, that payments were treated as refundable deposits, and that customers received written documentation confirming these arrangements.[^66]

It also asserted that refunds were consistently provided when applications were canceled or could not be completed, and that customer documents were processed without unnecessary delays.

These were assurances made by Thai Visa Centre itself. The discussion provided no independent verification of its refund practices, document handling, or processing times.

The sponsor concluded by promoting its commercial services through `tvc.co.th/chat` and `tdac.in.th`.

### Possible Astroturfing and Repeated Recommendations

The sequence illustrates how an apparently ordinary customer inquiry can develop into a favorable promotional discussion: a brand is introduced with a positive price claim, influential forum accounts provide reassurance, another participant contributes a testimonial, and the business eventually advertises directly.

**The involvement of zzzzz is particularly relevant because DrJack54 later publicly challenged the same account for repeatedly posting similar Thai Visa Centre recommendations in other discussions.** The account acknowledged that repetition.[^18]

Nevertheless, repeated recommendations do not establish that DanBlack or zzzzz were fabricated accounts, compensated promoters, or acting under instructions from Thai Visa Centre.

The original discussion also contained genuine skepticism about mailing passports, and there is no evidence that the agency mishandled the poster's documents.

What the exchange does document is **a potentially promotional pattern involving a commercial sponsor, favorable customer claims, and moderator endorsements**—all presented within an apparently independent consumer-advice discussion.

## Global Moderator Calls Out Repeated Thai Visa Centre Promotion by Forum Account

On September 4, 2025, an ASEAN NOW account named **NitJune** sought advice about a historic immigration overstay and potential difficulties obtaining a retirement visa.[^18]

DrJack54 challenged the repeated promotion:

The account acknowledged the repetition:

It then continued praising Thai Visa Centre as dependable, reliable, and inexpensive.

**The exchange is particularly significant because a Global Moderator publicly identified repeated promotion of ASEAN NOW's commercial sponsor by an account presenting itself as an ordinary forum member.**

### Repeated Recommendations From 2024 Through 2026

The account's recommendations extended across multiple discussions.

On December 3, 2024, zzzzz recommended Thai Visa Centre and Grace to someone seeking retirement-visa assistance near Khanom in southern Thailand, despite the inquiry concerning an area outside Bangkok.[^68]

On March 18, 2025, the account endorsed Thai Visa Centre in a discussion about mailing passports to the agency, claiming firsthand experience and repeating the original poster's assertion that its prices were exceptionally low.[^66]

On July 2, 2025, zzzzz again recommended the agency to a member seeking retirement-extension assistance without the required 800,000-baht bank balance:

> told you many times

The recipient, **simon43**, confirmed that he had already contacted Thai Visa Centre but explained that his concerns extended beyond obtaining an immediate extension.

These posts document recurring promotion of the same business across discussions involving different immigration needs, locations, and prospective customers.

### Possible Astroturfing and Sponsor Promotion

The repeated endorsements resemble a pattern associated with promotional seeding or astroturfing, where apparently independent community accounts consistently recommend a particular commercial provider.

However, **repetition does not establish that zzzzz was a paid promoter, fabricated account, or acting under instructions from Thai Visa Centre**. The account claimed to be a genuine customer, and that claim has not been independently disproven.

DrJack54's public challenge also demonstrates that his moderation was not uniformly favorable toward everyone promoting the sponsor.

Nevertheless, the broader September 2025 discussion raises additional concerns about commercial influence.

Thai Visa Centre participated through its official **Forum Sponsor** account, advertising retirement-visa packages costing **60,000 baht** with bank-account opening assistance or **37,000 baht** without it.[^19]

During the same discussion, DrJack54 announced that posts questioning the legality of visa-agent arrangements had been removed as off-topic.

The result was a discussion where repeated endorsements and the sponsor's commercial advertisements remained publicly visible while certain questions about visa-agent legality were removed.

**The combination of repetitive recommendations, direct sponsor advertising, and selective restrictions on discussion raises questions about ASEAN NOW's ability to distinguish independent consumer advice from commercial promotion.**

The available evidence does not establish coordination between zzzzz, Thai Visa Centre, or the moderators. It does, however, document a sustained pattern of favorable recommendations that even a Global Moderator considered repetitive enough to challenge publicly.

## Forum Promotion of Unofficial TDAC Websites

In May 2025, ASEAN NOW published a discussion warning travelers about unofficial Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) websites charging fees for a government service that was available free of charge. However, an **“Admin note”** added to the opening post explicitly described `tdac.in.th` and `tdac.agents.co.th` as:

> legitimate websites operated by AGENTS CO., LTD.[^5]

The note acknowledged that Thailand's official TDAC portal was `tdac.immigration.go.th`, while providing favorable assurances about the privately operated alternatives.

In the same discussion, Thai Visa Centre's official **Forum Sponsor** account criticized a competing commercial TDAC website for misleading travelers and inadequate business disclosures. The agency acknowledged operating `tdac.in.th`, initially described it as an informational website without paid services, and subsequently edited its post to advertise an $8 early-submission service.[^5]

On October 17, 2025, Thailand's Ministry of Tourism and Sports warned travelers against unofficial TDAC websites requesting payment, reiterating that registration through the government's official portal is free.[^72]

The Immigration Bureau's official FAQ separately allows travel agencies to submit TDAC applications on behalf of clients.[^73] This distinction is relevant to ASEAN NOW's promotion of Thai Visa Centre's privately operated platform, which offered paid early submissions for a government service otherwise available free of charge.

The combination of favorable website assurances and direct commercial promotion by a forum sponsor raises questions about ASEAN NOW's separation of independent travel information from advertiser interests.

## Unsubstantiated Security Claims About the Official TDAC Website

On May 24, 2025, ASEAN NOW Core Administrator **CharlieH** published a promotional discussion advertising [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md)'s commercial Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) submission service, including paid early applications.[^12]

On May 26, an ASEAN NOW member reported receiving another traveler's passport information after submitting a TDAC application through Thailand's official government website. According to the member, the downloadable PDF contained a Chinese traveler's name, passport number, date of birth, and accommodation details, even though the member's confirmation email and online application records displayed the correct information.[^32]

Global Moderator [BrandonJT](../moderators/brandonjt.md) responded by claiming that he had heard about similar problems from the company operating `tdac.agents.co.th`, stating:

> They were getting this about 1 in 500 entries.[^32]

BrandonJT reassured the member that the correct email confirmation should remain usable and attributed the problem to Thailand's government IT systems. However, he provided no independent evidence supporting the reported error rate or establishing how the commercial service obtained those figures.

On July 9, BrandonJT responded to another member concerned about personal-data security by claiming that information submitted through Thailand's official government TDAC website had a **"50% chance of being stolen by hackers"**, attributing the alleged risk to previous government data breaches.[^12]

The account provided no supporting statistics or evidence for this claim. On August 9, BrandonJT subsequently defended the commercial platform's optional $8 early-submission fee, arguing that travelers might prefer paying for convenience rather than using the government's free service.[^12]

These statements contrast with the Thai Immigration Bureau's June 2025 public warning encouraging travelers to register exclusively through `tdac.immigration.go.th` to protect their personal information. The bureau emphasized that official TDAC registration was free and warned against unofficial websites requesting payments or personal information.[^13]

The exchanges document a Global Moderator repeatedly criticizing the security of Thailand's official TDAC system while defending a commercial alternative and citing information allegedly obtained from its operator. The reported PDF mix-up has not been independently verified, and neither the alleged error rate nor the claimed 50% hacking risk was substantiated.

## Thai Visa Centre Promotes Private TDAC Service Despite Request for Official Government Website

On May 24, 2025, ASEAN NOW Core Administrator **CharlieH** published a promotional discussion introducing a Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) submission platform operated by [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md). The privately operated service offered free submissions within the standard 72-hour registration window and optional paid early submissions.[^12]

The discussion subsequently attracted questions about the platform's legitimacy, data security, and relationship with Thailand's official immigration system.

### Unverified Airline and Data-Security Claims

On August 8, 2025, an ASEAN NOW member named **Felt 35** reported encountering a TDAC form while completing an airline booking.

Global Moderator **Maestro** cautioned that advertisements appearing on airline websites were not necessarily endorsed by the airlines themselves.

On August 19, [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md) responded through its official **Forum Sponsor** account, claiming:

> Some airlines rely on our form[^12]

The agency suggested that its platform supported travelers whose countries or languages were inadequately accommodated by the official system.

However, Thai Visa Centre did not identify the airlines involved or provide evidence of any official partnerships. The discussion therefore did not establish whether the form encountered during the booking process was an airline-endorsed service or simply an advertisement.

Thai Visa Centre also claimed full compliance with Thailand's Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA), stating that users could delete their information after approval and prepare applications up to one year in advance.[^12]

These assurances were not independently verified.

On June 9, 2025, Thailand's Immigration Bureau had warned travelers to register through the official, free government website, `tdac.immigration.go.th`, to protect their personal information.[^13]

The warning did not establish that Thai Visa Centre's platform was fraudulent, but it highlighted the difference between official government registration and privately operated alternatives.

### Traveler Explicitly Rejects Commercial Services

On September 1, 2026, Felt 35 returned to the discussion requesting the genuine government TDAC website.

The member explicitly stated:

> Not interested in doing it early or use any agency.[^67]

Another participant, **treetops**, supplied the official government URL.

Nevertheless, Thai Visa Centre subsequently promoted its own platform through its **Forum Sponsor** account.

The agency advertised AI-assisted form completion, claimed to have processed more than **250,000 TDAC submissions**, and supplied its commercial link at `tvc.co.th/tdac`.[^67]

The claimed submission volume was not independently verified.

**The significant issue is that Thai Visa Centre continued advertising its private service after the traveler explicitly requested the official government website and rejected agency assistance.**

### Another Example of Unwanted Sponsor Promotion

The exchange resembles a September 2025 ASEAN NOW discussion in which a member repeatedly requested recommendations for visa agents other than Thai Visa Centre. Despite those requests, the sponsor eventually entered that discussion to advertise its services.[^63]

Both incidents demonstrate how independent-looking consumer discussions can become opportunities for commercial promotion even when participants expressly reject the advertised service.

The record does not establish that airlines maintained undisclosed relationships with Thai Visa Centre, that its platform mishandled personal information, or that the original inquiry was fabricated.

Nevertheless, **the unverified airline-use claim, self-reported security assurances, and continued advertising despite a clear request for government information raise questions about the influence of commercial sponsorship on ASEAN NOW's consumer discussions**.

## Formal Consumer Complaint to Singapore Authorities (2026)

In November 2026, **Jesse Nickles** reported submitting a formal complaint to the Competition and Consumer Commission of Singapore (CCCS) concerning [The Thaiger](../brands/the-thaiger.md), ASEAN NOW, and their commercial relationship with [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md). The complaint was subsequently made publicly available.[^16]

The complaint alleged that ASEAN NOW's sponsorship arrangements and moderation practices could mislead consumers seeking independent immigration advice. It cited the suppression of criticism concerning commercial sponsors, favorable recommendations by forum moderators, and the promotion of unofficial Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) services.

Nickles requested regulatory scrutiny of these practices, raising concerns about whether commercial sponsorship was being adequately distinguished from independent forum discussions and consumer recommendations.

The complaint represents a formal escalation of the concerns documented in this article. Its allegations remain disputed or unverified, and no regulatory findings or enforcement decision have been established from the available information.
