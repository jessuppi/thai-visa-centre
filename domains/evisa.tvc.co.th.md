# evisa.tvc.co.th

`evisa.tvc.co.th` is a private immigration-services portal operated by [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md) on behalf of [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md). It is not affiliated with Thailand's official e-visa system.[^1][^2]

## Immigration Documents and Customer Data

The portal allows customers to access visa applications, extensions, and 90-day reporting services using an email address or Thai telephone number.

Its operation involves processing potentially sensitive customer information, including identity documents, passport details, immigration records, and communications with visa agents.[^1][^3]

Thai Visa Centre identifies Agents Co., Ltd. as responsible for the platform, while its published privacy policies permit personal-data sharing with affiliated companies and service providers.[^2][^3]

## Government Website Confusion

The `evisa` subdomain resembles the terminology used by Thailand's official electronic visa service, operated by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs at `thaievisa.go.th`.[^4]

Unlike the government system, `evisa.tvc.co.th` is a commercial intermediary. Customers submitting immigration documents through the website are providing information to a private company rather than directly to Thai authorities.

**The principal concerns are the handling of sensitive immigration records, transparency about the responsible legal entity, and the distinction between private visa assistance and official government processing.**

## Client Portal Operator and Legal Agreements Identify Different Responsible Entities

Thai Visa Centre operates a private online client portal at `evisa.tvc.co.th`, allowing customers to manage visa applications, extensions, and immigration reporting.

The portal advertises access through an email address or Thai telephone number, along with professional assistance and ongoing communication with agency staff.[^1]

However, the publicly documented arrangements raise questions about which legal entity is responsible for customers' personal information, contractual obligations, and potential losses.

**Thai Visa Centre publicly identifies AGENTS CO., LTD. as the operator of the client portal, while its linked legal agreements identify the contracting business only as THAI VISA CENTRE, an unregistered trade name.**

The discrepancy becomes more significant when examining the company's contractual liability restrictions and privacy-policy representations.

### The Company Identifies AGENTS as the Portal Operator

On its reputation-defense page, Thai Visa Centre expressly distinguishes its physical immigration agency from its digital operations.[^2]

It identifies AGENTS CO., LTD., Thai registration number 0115562031107, as the operator of:

- `evisa.tvc.co.th`.
- `90day.in.th`.
- Its Thailand Digital Arrival Card services.
- Related website technology.

The disclosure establishes the company's own representation that AGENTS operates the digital infrastructure.

However, the client portal itself is branded as Thai Visa Centre.

Its publicly accessible sign-in page does not prominently identify AGENTS CO., LTD. as the responsible legal company.[^1]

Instead, it directs customers to the main Thai Visa Centre website for corporate information.

**The legal operator identified in the company's explanatory material is not the business name prominently presented to customers entering the portal.**

This distinction does not establish deceptive ownership.

Businesses routinely operate services under commercial brands.

The concern is whether customers receive a clear explanation of which registered company bears responsibility for the service.

### The Terms Identify an Unregistered Trade Name as the Contracting Party

Thai Visa Centre's published Terms & Conditions expressly describe the agreement as legally binding between the customer and THAI VISA CENTRE.[^5]

The document repeatedly uses that name when describing payments, obligations, third-party services, indemnification, and liability.

However, it does not identify TVC. VISA AGENT CO., LTD., AGENTS CO., LTD., or another registered Thai company as the contracting entity.

This omission matters because the present investigation has not identified a company registered in Thailand under the exact legal name Thai Visa Centre.

The business instead operates through a trade name associated with separately registered companies.

**A customer reading the agreement cannot determine from its text whether the counterparty is AGENTS CO., LTD., TVC. VISA AGENT CO., LTD., another company, or an individual operating under the trade name.**

Using a trade name does not automatically invalidate a contract.

Nevertheless, clear identification of the responsible legal person is important when customers need to seek refunds, enforce contractual obligations, or resolve disputes.

### The Terms Impose a Particularly Restrictive Liability Limit

Thai Visa Centre's Terms & Conditions contain a substantial limitation of liability.[^5]

The agreement attempts to exclude various categories of damages, including lost profits, business interruption, and consequential losses.

It then establishes an aggregate financial liability limit based on the greater of:

- US$1.
- The amount the customer actually paid to Thai Visa Centre during the month preceding the event giving rise to liability.

The practical significance depends on the circumstances.

A customer who has recently paid substantial fees may have a larger contractual recovery limit.

However, where the relevant event occurs after that payment window, the stated formula could produce a much smaller limit.

**The published contract attempts to restrict the financial liability of the business responsible for an immigration service to potentially as little as one US dollar.**

This provision is especially relevant because immigration services may involve original passports, government filing deadlines, visa eligibility, and substantial customer payments.

An application-processing error could produce consequences substantially exceeding the price of the agency's service.

The terms do not clearly explain whether the liability limit applies equally to the client portal, in-person immigration services, and activities performed by AGENTS CO., LTD.

The clause expressly operates only to the extent permitted by applicable law.

Its enforceability under Thai law has not been established by this investigation.

Nevertheless, **the existence of the limitation is a material contractual fact that customers should understand before relying on the agency to manage important immigration procedures**.

### Third-Party Services Introduce Further Responsibility Questions

The same Terms & Conditions contain provisions addressing third-party services.[^5]

These provisions broadly disclaim responsibility for external providers, including their data-handling practices.

They also authorize Thai Visa Centre to disclose customer information where necessary to enable or facilitate another service.

Those arrangements may be legitimate when an immigration agency uses payment processors, delivery services, translation providers, or other contractors.

However, the published agreements do not clearly explain where AGENTS CO., LTD. fits within this framework.

Thai Visa Centre describes AGENTS as the operator of its digital services, not merely an unrelated external provider.

**The documents leave unclear whether AGENTS acts as the customer's direct contracting business, a processor engaged by Thai Visa Centre, an affiliated service provider, or some combination of those roles.**

That uncertainty matters because the company publicly divides its operations between different businesses while maintaining legal agreements that do not identify those entities separately.

### The Privacy Policy Gives Conflicting Explanations of Data-Control Responsibility

Thai Visa Centre's Privacy Policy introduces a more specific problem.[^3]

The document explains that the company acts as a data controller when it determines the purposes and methods of collecting personal information.

That statement is consistent with the general definition of a data controller under Thailand's Personal Data Protection Act.

However, the same policy subsequently states that when a user submits personal information through its services, the company acts as a processor and the user submitting the information becomes the data controller.

**Submitting personal information to a commercial immigration agency does not, by itself, establish that an ordinary customer controls the purposes and methods of the agency's subsequent processing.**

Section 6 of Thailand's Personal Data Protection Act defines a controller according to actual decision-making authority over collection, use, and disclosure.[^7]

A processor instead handles data according to instructions from a controller.

The distinction depends on the parties' actual roles.

It cannot be conclusively determined merely by a statement in a website policy.

### The Operator Makes Independent Decisions About Customer Information

Thai Visa Centre's Privacy Policy expressly reserves numerous uses of customer information.

These include:

- Creating and managing customer accounts.
- Processing service orders.
- Improving products and services.
- Sending product and service updates.
- Requesting customer feedback.
- Publishing testimonials.
- Protecting the business against abuse.
- Responding to legal requests.

The policy also acknowledges obtaining personal information from public databases and joint marketing partners.[^3]

These activities involve decisions made by the business rather than simply following instructions from an individual customer.

A person submitting a passport copy for a visa application is not necessarily directing how the agency markets its services, selects testimonials, or operates its account system.

**Thai Visa Centre's own descriptions of its data-processing activities undermine the suggestion that customers automatically become data controllers simply by uploading information.**

The relevant legal roles may vary between operations.

For example, the agency could act as a processor for a separately contracted business customer while functioning as a controller for its own customer accounts and marketing.

But its published policy does not adequately distinguish those situations.

### A Separate Data Protection Policy Adds Another Layer

Thai Visa Centre publishes a Data Protection Policy in addition to its general Privacy Policy.[^6]

The separate document describes the company's responsibilities for protecting personal information and states that third-party disclosure generally requires consent unless an applicable legal exception exists.

It also promises internal procedures for information security, staff responsibility, and oversight of external processors.

These commitments are relevant to the company's claim that AGENTS operates the digital client portal.

However, the policy again identifies the responsible organization as THAI VISA CENTRE.

It does not separately describe AGENTS CO., LTD.'s responsibilities for the portal or identify a contractual arrangement governing the two businesses' respective access to customer information.

**The company publishes multiple privacy and data-protection commitments without clearly reconciling them with its public acknowledgment that a separately registered business operates its digital immigration infrastructure.**

The available documentation does not establish that the companies lack appropriate internal data-processing agreements.

It establishes that those arrangements are not explained in the reviewed public policies.

### Contradictory Rules for Customers Aged 16–19

The company's legal documents contain another directly observable inconsistency.

Its Terms & Conditions permit individuals aged 16 or older to use the website and services.[^5]

However, the Privacy Policy instructs individuals younger than 20 not to submit personal information through those same services.[^3]

The resulting discrepancy is straightforward:

| Document | Published age instruction |
|---|---|
| Terms & Conditions | Users must be at least 16 |
| Privacy Policy | Users under 20 should not submit personal information |

A customer aged 16, 17, 18, or 19 is therefore old enough to use the service under the Terms & Conditions but is simultaneously instructed not to submit the information ordinarily needed to create an account or purchase immigration assistance.

The Privacy Policy allows parents to submit information concerning their children as part of visa applications.

That exception is reasonable but does not resolve the inconsistency for younger users attempting to access the service independently.

**The business publishes incompatible age instructions without clearly explaining whether parental authorization is required for customers aged 16–19.**

This is particularly relevant to family visa applications and immigration procedures involving dependent children.

The contradiction does not establish unlawful processing of minors' information.

It establishes that the company's customer-facing instructions are internally inconsistent.

### The Portal's Legal Responsibilities Remain Unclear

The publicly accessible client portal does not provide sufficient information to determine whether users are presented with additional, portal-specific agreements after signing in.

The principal website's Terms & Conditions and Privacy Policy describe their scope as including related services, but the reviewed material does not establish precisely how those provisions are applied to each customer account.

The unresolved questions are therefore significant:

1. Which registered company enters into contracts with customers using `evisa.tvc.co.th`?
2. Does AGENTS CO., LTD. act as a data controller, processor, or joint controller?
3. What agreement governs the transfer of immigration information between AGENTS and Thai Visa Centre?
4. Does the published liability limit apply to cases managed through the portal?
5. Which company is responsible for correcting records, honoring deletion requests, and investigating data incidents?
6. Which age and consent requirements govern younger customers?

These questions arise from the company's own public descriptions of its operations and legal agreements.

The available evidence does not establish that customer records have been improperly disclosed, that the companies lack internal agreements, or that either business has violated the PDPA.

Nor does it establish that the contractual liability limit would necessarily be enforceable in every dispute.

**What is established is a mismatch between the named portal operator, the unidentified legal counterparty in the published agreements, and the company's inconsistent explanations of data-control responsibility and customer eligibility.**

### Relevance to the Wider Business Network

The investigation has already documented AGENTS CO., LTD.'s operation of privately branded immigration services, including TDAC applications and paid 90-day reporting.

The client portal provides another important connection between the registered digital-services company and the Thai Visa Centre trade name.

Unlike an informational website directing readers toward commercial services, this portal is specifically intended for customers managing ongoing immigration matters.

That increases the importance of identifying the business responsible for contractual performance and personal-data protection.

**Thai Visa Centre publicly acknowledges that AGENTS operates its digital immigration infrastructure while continuing to present customer-facing legal agreements under an unregistered trade name, with restrictive liability terms and unresolved privacy-policy contradictions.**

The company should identify the actual contracting entity, clearly distinguish the data-protection responsibilities of its associated businesses, and reconcile its published agreements with the services customers are purchasing.
