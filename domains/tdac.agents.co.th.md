# tdac.agents.co.th

In a [May 2025 ASEAN NOW discussion](../threads/2025-05-01-asean-now-fake-tdac-emerges.md), [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md) publicly identified `tdac.agents.co.th` and [tdac.in.th](tdac.in.th.md) as websites operated by [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md).[^1]

The company's [agents.co.th](agents.co.th.md) website also identifies `tdac.agents.co.th` as part of its Thailand Digital Arrival Card submission services.[^2]

The statement documents the agency's claimed operational relationship with the websites. It does not independently establish their legal ownership, precise launch dates, or any official government affiliation.

## Formal THNIC Domain Abuse Complaint (November 2026)

On November 12, 2026, Jesse Nickles submitted a formal abuse complaint to [THNIC](../brands/thnic.md) concerning `tdac.agents.co.th` and its parent domain, [agents.co.th](agents.co.th.md). The complaint identified [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md), registration number 0115562031107, as the publicly identified operator of a private Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) submission service offering paid assistance, including an $8 (270 THB) early-submission option. Applications submitted outside the government's permitted registration window remain pending until they can be forwarded for official processing.[^3]

The complaint raised concerns about the collection of travelers' passport information, identity details, and travel information through a commercial platform associated with a mandatory government immigration procedure. Nickles specifically invoked **Section 12.11 of THNIC's Domain Name Registration Policy**, which requires domain holders to use registered domains and lower-level domains in good faith. He additionally cited Sections 12.4, 12.5, and 17 concerning registrar investigations, potential enforcement, and information-security threats.[^3]

Nickles requested that THNIC verify the registered holder's responsibility for the subdomain, examine whether its operation complied with Section 12.11, assess any information-security concerns, and consider the Immigration Bureau's public warnings about unofficial TDAC services. **He requested a formal case reference and a written policy determination**, explicitly distinguishing this complaint from his unresolved April 2025 report concerning [tdac.in.th](tdac.in.th.md).[^3]

The complaint placed the responsibility of the domain holder and its lower-level domain under direct scrutiny, rather than limiting the investigation to the original `tdac.in.th` website. It also raised the question of how THNIC evaluates commercial services collecting sensitive traveler information under its published registration policies.[^3]

## Soliciting Google Ads Complaints Against Competitors

As of November 2026, the `tdac.agents.co.th` website operated by [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md) publishes a directory identifying **29 competing TDAC websites as scams or untrusted operators**, while presenting its own commercial service alongside Thailand's official government portal as a trusted submission option.[^4]

The website goes further than publishing consumer warnings.

Its individual competitor profiles identify Google advertiser accounts, reproduce advertising headlines, and provide direct links to report the advertisements through Google's Ads Transparency Center.[^5]

For example:

- **`ivisa.com`:** 61 advertisements attributed to Document Advisor, Inc.
- **`visasyst.com`:** Four advertisements attributed to U.S. Vital Documents.
- **`travelsmarttravelfast.com`:** Three advertisements attributed to Travel Smart Travel Fast LLC.

Each profile provides instructions telling visitors to select **"It promotes a restricted product or service"** when reporting an advertisement.

The website also supplies a prepared complaint describing the advertiser's alleged violations of Google's policies concerning government documents and travel authorizations.[^5][^6][^7]

### Commercial Interests Behind the Reporting Campaign

AGENTS CO., LTD. operates a competing TDAC submission service through `tdac.agents.co.th` and `tdac.in.th`.

Its published comparison advertises free submissions within 72 hours of arrival and an **$8 fee for earlier submissions**, while listing competing commercial providers as untrusted or fraudulent.[^4]

The company's competitor profiles also invite operators to request reconsideration if they stop charging fees for services that AGENTS considers to provide insufficient value.

**This creates an apparent commercial conflict of interest.** A company selling TDAC assistance is encouraging visitors to file advertising complaints against businesses offering competing assistance, while reserving for itself the ability to reconsider their negative classifications.

Some identified competitors may genuinely publish misleading advertisements or charge excessive fees. However, the company's allegations and prepared complaints are not independent Google policy determinations.

The available pages also do not establish that readers actually submitted the complaints or that Google removed the advertisements.

### Google Advertising Rules and Government Authorization

Google's government-documents advertising policy restricts direct advertising of specified government services and requires appropriate certification. Its October 2026 policy update also clarifies that ordinary business registrations and commercial licenses do not establish government authorization.[^8]

AGENTS promotes its Thai corporate registration prominently when distinguishing itself from foreign competitors.

However, **being a registered Thai company does not independently establish authorization from Thailand's Immigration Bureau** to provide an official TDAC service or satisfy Google's advertising-certification requirements.

The website provides no independently verifiable government accreditation establishing a privileged regulatory position over competing private TDAC intermediaries.

The concern is therefore not simply that AGENTS criticizes competitors. It is that the company **combines competitor blacklisting, prepared advertising complaints, and promotion of its own paid service**, without clearly separating independent consumer protection from its commercial interests.

## Omission of Mandatory Ebola-Related Entry Restrictions

As of November 25, 2026, the TDAC guide operated by AGENTS CO., LTD. presents detailed instructions concerning immigration health declarations, travel history, and yellow fever vaccination requirements.[^9]

Despite displaying a same-day update timestamp, the guide contains no reference to Ebola-related entry restrictions introduced by Thailand's public health authorities during 2026.

### Revised October 2026 Entry Restrictions

On October 15, 2026, the Civil Aviation Authority of Thailand published Aeronautical Information Circular AIC 09/26, implementing revised public health requirements effective October 16.[^10]

The circular establishes that:

- Travelers departing from the Democratic Republic of the Congo may enter Thailand only through Suvarnabhumi Airport.
- A mandatory 21-day quarantine period applies, calculated from departure from the affected areas.
- Travelers departing from Uganda may use standard immigration procedures but must present valid yellow fever vaccination certificates at the port health station.

The circular remains effective until further notice.

Thailand's Tourism Authority had previously published guidance concerning the introduction of Ebola-related screening measures and their relationship to TDAC registration in May 2026.[^11]

### Commercial Significance

The AGENTS guide promotes its commercial submission platform alongside detailed explanations of travel documentation and health requirements.

However, travelers relying exclusively on this guidance may remain unaware of restrictions affecting their airport selection, onward travel arrangements, and quarantine obligations.

The omission is particularly consequential given the website's prominently displayed update timestamp.

The available evidence does not establish whether the company's actual submission form provides additional warnings or whether customers have experienced disrupted travel arrangements.

Nevertheless, an apparently current commercial immigration guide should distinguish successful arrival-card submission from compliance with separate mandatory public health entry requirements.
