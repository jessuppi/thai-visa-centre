# tdac.agents.co.th

In a [May 2025 ASEAN NOW discussion](../threads/2025-05-01-asean-now-fake-tdac-emerges.md), [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md) publicly identified `tdac.agents.co.th` and [tdac.in.th](tdac.in.th.md) as websites operated by [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md).[^1]

The company's [agents.co.th](agents.co.th.md) website also identifies `tdac.agents.co.th` as part of its Thailand Digital Arrival Card submission services.[^2]

The statement documents the agency's claimed operational relationship with the websites. It does not independently establish their legal ownership, precise launch dates, or any official government affiliation.

## Formal THNIC Domain Abuse Complaint (November 2026)

On November 12, 2026, Jesse Nickles submitted a formal abuse complaint to [THNIC](../brands/thnic.md) concerning `tdac.agents.co.th` and its parent domain, [agents.co.th](agents.co.th.md). The complaint identified [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md), registration number 0115562031107, as the publicly identified operator of a private Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) submission service offering paid assistance, including an $8 (270 THB) early-submission option. Applications submitted outside the government's permitted registration window remain pending until they can be forwarded for official processing.[^3]

The complaint raised concerns about the collection of travelers' passport information, identity details, and travel information through a commercial platform associated with a mandatory government immigration procedure. Nickles specifically invoked **Section 12.11 of THNIC's Domain Name Registration Policy**, which requires domain holders to use registered domains and lower-level domains in good faith. He additionally cited Sections 12.4, 12.5, and 17 concerning registrar investigations, potential enforcement, and information-security threats.[^3]

Nickles requested that THNIC verify the registered holder's responsibility for the subdomain, examine whether its operation complied with Section 12.11, assess any information-security concerns, and consider the Immigration Bureau's public warnings about unofficial TDAC services. **He requested a formal case reference and a written policy determination**, explicitly distinguishing this complaint from his unresolved April 2025 report concerning [tdac.in.th](tdac.in.th.md).[^3]

The complaint placed the responsibility of the domain holder and its lower-level domain under direct scrutiny, rather than limiting the investigation to the original `tdac.in.th` website. It also raised the question of how THNIC evaluates commercial services collecting sensitive traveler information under its published registration policies.[^3]

## Soliciting Google Ads Complaints Against Competitors

As of November 2026, the `tdac.agents.co.th` website operated by [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md) publishes a directory identifying **29 competing TDAC websites as scams or untrusted operators**, while presenting its own commercial service alongside Thailand's official government portal as a trusted submission option.[^4]

The website goes further than publishing consumer warnings.

Its individual competitor profiles identify Google advertiser accounts, reproduce advertising headlines, and provide direct links to report the advertisements through Google's Ads Transparency Center.[^5]

For example:

- **`ivisa.com`:** 61 advertisements attributed to Document Advisor, Inc.
- **`visasyst.com`:** Four advertisements attributed to U.S. Vital Documents.
- **`travelsmarttravelfast.com`:** Three advertisements attributed to Travel Smart Travel Fast LLC.

Each profile provides instructions telling visitors to select **"It promotes a restricted product or service"** when reporting an advertisement.

The website also supplies a prepared complaint describing the advertiser's alleged violations of Google's policies concerning government documents and travel authorizations.[^5][^6][^7]

### Commercial Interests Behind the Reporting Campaign

AGENTS CO., LTD. operates a competing TDAC submission service through `tdac.agents.co.th` and `tdac.in.th`.

Its published comparison advertises free submissions within 72 hours of arrival and an **$8 fee for earlier submissions**, while listing competing commercial providers as untrusted or fraudulent.[^4]

The company's competitor profiles also invite operators to request reconsideration if they stop charging fees for services that AGENTS considers to provide insufficient value.

**This creates an apparent commercial conflict of interest.** A company selling TDAC assistance is encouraging visitors to file advertising complaints against businesses offering competing assistance, while reserving for itself the ability to reconsider their negative classifications.

Some identified competitors may genuinely publish misleading advertisements or charge excessive fees. However, the company's allegations and prepared complaints are not independent Google policy determinations.

The available pages also do not establish that readers actually submitted the complaints or that Google removed the advertisements.

### Google Advertising Rules and Government Authorization

Google's government-documents advertising policy restricts direct advertising of specified government services and requires appropriate certification. Its October 2026 policy update also clarifies that ordinary business registrations and commercial licenses do not establish government authorization.[^8]

AGENTS promotes its Thai corporate registration prominently when distinguishing itself from foreign competitors.

However, **being a registered Thai company does not independently establish authorization from Thailand's Immigration Bureau** to provide an official TDAC service or satisfy Google's advertising-certification requirements.

The website provides no independently verifiable government accreditation establishing a privileged regulatory position over competing private TDAC intermediaries.

The concern is therefore not simply that AGENTS criticizes competitors. It is that the company **combines competitor blacklisting, prepared advertising complaints, and promotion of its own paid service**, without clearly separating independent consumer protection from its commercial interests.

## Omission of Mandatory Ebola-Related Entry Restrictions

As of November 25, 2026, the TDAC guide operated by AGENTS CO., LTD. presents detailed instructions concerning immigration health declarations, travel history, and yellow fever vaccination requirements.[^9]

Despite displaying a same-day update timestamp, the guide contains no reference to Ebola-related entry restrictions introduced by Thailand's public health authorities during 2026.

### Revised October 2026 Entry Restrictions

On October 15, 2026, the Civil Aviation Authority of Thailand published Aeronautical Information Circular AIC 09/26, implementing revised public health requirements effective October 16.[^10]

The circular establishes that:

- Travelers departing from the Democratic Republic of the Congo may enter Thailand only through Suvarnabhumi Airport.
- A mandatory 21-day quarantine period applies, calculated from departure from the affected areas.
- Travelers departing from Uganda may use standard immigration procedures but must present valid yellow fever vaccination certificates at the port health station.

The circular remains effective until further notice.

Thailand's Tourism Authority had previously published guidance concerning the introduction of Ebola-related screening measures and their relationship to TDAC registration in May 2026.[^11]

### Commercial Significance

The AGENTS guide promotes its commercial submission platform alongside detailed explanations of travel documentation and health requirements.

However, travelers relying exclusively on this guidance may remain unaware of restrictions affecting their airport selection, onward travel arrangements, and quarantine obligations.

The omission is particularly consequential given the website's prominently displayed update timestamp.

The available evidence does not establish whether the company's actual submission form provides additional warnings or whether customers have experienced disrupted travel arrangements.

Nevertheless, an apparently current commercial immigration guide should distinguish successful arrival-card submission from compliance with separate mandatory public health entry requirements.

## Fast-Track Airport Services and Immigration Bureau Warnings

AGENTS CO., LTD.'s TDAC scam-warning page lists fast-track airport services among the commercial advantages of its own platform, identifying their absence as a disadvantage of competing TDAC providers.[^4]

However, the legitimacy of airport assistance depends on the nature of the service, the provider's authorization, and applicable immigration procedures.

### May 2026 Immigration Bureau Warning

On May 19, 2026, The Thaiger published an article by Chattarin Siradakul reporting an Immigration Bureau warning concerning purported paid fast-track immigration services advertised through Trip.com.[^12]

The authorities warned that unauthorized services could expose travelers to financial losses, fraud, and misuse of personal information.

ASEAN NOW republished the warning on the same day under the account snoop1130.[^13]

Neither publication identified AGENTS CO., LTD. or Thai Visa Centre as a subject of that particular warning.

### Integration With the Commercial TDAC Platform

As of November 25, 2026, the address `asq.in.th/thailand-fasttrack` redirects visitors to the AGENTS commercial TDAC application form.[^14]

The application's associated service description also advertises optional VIP airport assistance and transportation services.[^15]

The reviewed promotional pages do not identify the specific fast-track operator, its authorization arrangements, or the conditions governing access to priority immigration facilities.

### Distinguishing Legitimate Assistance From Unauthorized Immigration Services

Thailand's government recognizes legitimate priority immigration arrangements for certain eligible travelers, including qualifying BOI-promoted investors and executives.[^16]

Commercial airport assistance should therefore not automatically be classified as fraudulent merely because customers pay for it.

Nevertheless, AGENTS should clarify whether its advertised fast-track services consist of authorized airport assistance, priority-lane access, or another arrangement.

The available evidence does not establish that AGENTS provides unauthorized immigration services.

The company's commercial comparison is significant because it promotes fast-track availability as a measure of service superiority without explaining the regulatory distinctions highlighted by its associated media platforms.

## Omitted Government Assistance and Contradictory Group Submission Limits

As examined on November 26, 2026, AGENTS CO., LTD.'s commercial TDAC guide contains incomplete information concerning government assistance, reporting exemptions, and the capabilities of its own submission platform.[^17]

### Omission of Free Airport Registration Facilities

Thailand's Immigration Bureau provides TDAC registration kiosks at five international airports:

- Suvarnabhumi Airport.
- Don Mueang International Airport.
- Phuket International Airport.
- Chiang Mai International Airport.
- Hat Yai International Airport.

These facilities assist travelers who lack suitable electronic devices or internet access.[^18]

On February 3, 2026, the Royal Thai Embassy in Lima also confirmed that travelers could complete their TDAC before passing through immigration, including through airport registration facilities.[^19]

However, AGENTS' detailed commercial guide does not explain the availability of these kiosks.

Instead, it promotes its own advance submission, document-delivery, customer-support, and airport-assistance services.

### Thai Visa Centre's Own Legal Archive Identifies the Missing Information

Thai Visa Centre published an archived version of the Immigration Bureau's official TDAC guidance, captured on November 22, 2026.[^20]

The archive explicitly documents all five airport kiosk locations, assistance for travelers without internet access, and procedures for travelers who have not received their confirmation email.

It also identifies airline crew as exempt from TDAC registration.

AGENTS' commercial guide identifies transit passengers and Border Pass holders as exempt but omits the airline crew exemption.

**The missing government assistance options and exemptions are documented within the affiliated agency's own legal archive but remain absent from its commercial TDAC guide.**

### Contradictory Group Submission Limits

AGENTS repeatedly advertises unlimited group submissions as a commercial advantage over the official government portal.

However, its own TDAC guide describes a maximum of 100 travelers per submission.[^17]

Its separate commercial service description continues advertising unrestricted group sizes.[^21]

The operator does not explain whether larger groups are automatically divided into separate submissions or whether the advertised unlimited capacity is subject to operational restrictions.

This discrepancy is particularly relevant to travel agencies and tour operators purchasing advance submission services for large groups.

### Commercial Significance

Government registration facilities provide an alternative for travelers experiencing technical difficulties or arriving without completed arrival cards.

Although advance registration remains advisable, travelers should not be led to believe that purchasing commercial assistance is necessary to resolve these difficulties.

AGENTS should clearly identify available government assistance, accurately communicate registration exemptions, and reconcile its contradictory group-capacity claims.

## Contradictory Editing Claims and Last-Minute Correction Restrictions

As of November 27, 2026, AGENTS continued advertising superior TDAC editing capabilities compared with Thailand's official Immigration Bureau platform.[^17][^21]

Its commercial comparison describes the government system as providing partial editing support while identifying its own service as providing full support.

### Restrictions on Same-Day Corrections

Despite advertising the ability to modify submissions at any time, the company's own TDAC guide expressly states:

> Same-day edits are not permitted.

The guide also states that edits are permitted only when the arrival date is more than one day away.[^17]

These restrictions are not disclosed in the company's commercial feature-comparison table.

### Government Allows Corrections Before Entry

Thailand's Immigration Bureau expressly permits travelers to update incorrect information or submit a replacement arrival card before entering Thailand.[^18]

Its official FAQ specifically addresses changes to flights and accommodation arrangements.

Although certain personal identifiers cannot be modified through the government's editing function, travelers may submit a replacement declaration containing the correct information.

The most recent valid submission takes precedence.

**AGENTS advertises superior editing capabilities while imposing a documented restriction on last-minute corrections that is not identified in the government's published guidance.**

### Commercial Significance

Travelers purchasing advance TDAC assistance may subsequently experience flight cancellations, accommodation changes or other unexpected disruptions shortly before arrival.

The company's advertised editing advantage is particularly relevant in these circumstances.

AGENTS should disclose its editing restrictions prominently and explain that travelers can use the free government platform to submit corrected information before entry.

The available evidence does not establish that customers have been unable to obtain corrected arrival cards or suffered travel disruptions because of these restrictions.

Nevertheless, the published comparison does not accurately communicate the limitations of the commercial service.

## Unsubstantiated LTR Arrival Card Requirements in Commercial Advertising

As of November 27, 2026, AGENTS continued promoting individually generated TDAC documents as a significant commercial advantage over Thailand's official immigration registration platform.[^21]

The company's advertising specifically identifies the Long-Term Resident (LTR) visa program as requiring individual arrival-card documents.

### Unsupported Restriction on Government Group Submissions

The Board of Investment's official LTR visa issuance instructions require applicable applicants to provide their Thailand Digital Arrival Card and associated registration number.[^22]

However, the published instructions do not expressly prohibit documents generated through the government's group submission procedure.

AGENTS does not identify a specific government regulation or BOI instruction supporting its implied restriction.

### Free Individual Government Submissions

Thailand's Immigration Bureau expressly supports both individual and group arrival-card submissions through its free registration platform.[^18][^23]

Travelers requiring separately issued documents can therefore complete individual government submissions without purchasing commercial assistance.

AGENTS' feature comparison does not adequately communicate this alternative.

### Commercial Significance

**AGENTS invokes government visa-documentation requirements to promote its commercial service without adequately substantiating the claimed restriction or explaining the equivalent free government option.**

Although individual document distribution may provide legitimate convenience and privacy advantages, those benefits should be distinguished from mandatory immigration requirements.

The company should identify the official basis for its LTR-related advertising and clarify that travelers can obtain individual arrival cards directly from Thailand's Immigration Bureau.

The available evidence does not establish that the BOI accepts every format of group-generated arrival-card documentation or that any applicant has purchased unnecessary commercial assistance.

## VIP Airport Photograph Collection and Restrictive Refund Conditions

AGENTS' Refund Policy, last updated May 15, 2025, identifies selfie photographs among the documents that customers may be required to upload when purchasing VIP airport arrival services.[^24]

The policy excludes refunds where customers fail to supply required documentation following repeated requests and this prevents delivery of the purchased service.

### Incomplete Personal Data Disclosures

The associated Privacy Policy describes general categories of personal information but does not specifically address photographs collected for VIP airport assistance.[^25]

It does not explain whether these photographs are used exclusively for passenger identification, whether they are transferred to airport assistance providers, or whether separate retention periods apply.

Meanwhile, the company's Data Protection Policy permits certain information sharing with affiliated businesses for marketing purposes.[^26]

### Commercial Accountability

**Customers may face financial consequences for declining to supply photographs without receiving a clear, service-specific explanation of how those photographs will be handled.**

AGENTS should disclose the purpose of collecting photographs, the parties receiving them, applicable retention periods and mandatory documentation requirements before accepting payment.

The available evidence does not establish that AGENTS uses facial recognition technology, processes photographs for biometric identification, or distributes customer photographs for unrelated commercial purposes.

## Unnecessary TDAC Resubmission Referrals and Email Delivery Assurances

Historical traveler discussions remaining accessible on November 27, 2026, demonstrate how problems receiving government confirmation emails are used to recommend AGENTS' commercial TDAC platform.[^27]

In one German-language discussion, a traveler explained that the government system had successfully issued a TDAC number and downloadable QR code, although the confirmation email had not yet arrived.

An anonymous response recommended using AGENTS' submission platform instead.

The traveler subsequently confirmed that the original government confirmation emails had arrived.

### Government Confirmation Emails Are Not the Only Accepted Evidence

The Immigration Bureau's official FAQ confirms that travelers may present their downloaded TDAC document and QR code on an electronic device.[^18]

A delayed confirmation email therefore does not necessarily indicate an unsuccessful registration or require another submission.

### Unsubstantiated Email Delivery Assurances

In a separate Czech-language discussion on the same page, an anonymous response promoted AGENTS' platform by offering complete certainty of email delivery and continued document availability.[^27]

No independent delivery-performance records accompanied this assurance.

### Commercial Significance

**The discussion platform directs travelers toward AGENTS' commercial services even when their existing government registration may already be sufficient.**

Unnecessary duplicate submissions expose travelers' passport details and travel information to an additional private data processor.

The operator should distinguish successful government registration from email delivery problems and clearly identify circumstances in which resubmission is genuinely necessary.

The available evidence does not establish who authored the anonymous responses or whether any traveler unnecessarily resubmitted personal information because of them.
