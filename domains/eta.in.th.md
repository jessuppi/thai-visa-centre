# eta.in.th

`eta.in.th` is an unofficial Thailand Electronic Travel Authorization (ETA) information website operated by [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md), with [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md) identified in its published legal policies. The website continues describing ETA application requirements despite the Thai government's confirmation that the Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) replaced the proposed system. The domain has also been the subject of multiple abuse complaints submitted to [THNIC](../brands/thnic.md).[^1][^4][^5][^6][^8]

## Website Operation and Presentation

[Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md) publicly acknowledges operating `eta.in.th`, alongside other immigration-related websites including `dtv.in.th`, `tdac.in.th`, and `ltr.in.th`. The company describes these websites as unofficial information resources, denies allegations that they are deceptive, and maintains that their information is regularly updated and email subscriptions are voluntary.[^4]

The Terms & Conditions and Privacy Policy published on `eta.in.th` identify [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md) as the business responsible for the website's agreements. Both documents display a last-updated date of July 16, 2024. These first-party disclosures establish a publicly stated business connection, although they do not independently verify historical domain ownership or registration records.[^8][^9]

The Privacy Policy states that personal information, including names, email addresses, and telephone numbers, may be collected directly from visitors or obtained through public databases and joint marketing partners. Its permitted uses include product and service updates. These disclosures are relevant to Jesse Nickles's February 2025 complaint concerning unsolicited visa marketing, but do not establish how recipients' contact information was obtained or whether messages were sent without consent.[^1][^9]

On November 30, 2024, the **ThaiVisaCentre** account on ASEAN NOW publicly acknowledged operating `eta.in.th` after forum participants questioned its unofficial status and published ETA requirements. Participants specifically challenged the website's descriptions of required travel tickets, accommodation details, and proof of funds, including 10,000 THB for individual travelers and 20,000 THB for families. Thai Visa Centre responded that these requirements were not finalized, citing preliminary government discussions and stating that the website would be updated when further official information became available.[^10]

The website retains an **“Official Thailand ETA Links”** section pointing to older government publications and continues inviting visitors to subscribe for ETA notifications. Alongside its outdated application requirements, these features could mislead travelers about whether an ETA application system exists or remains planned.[^5][^6]

Although `eta.in.th` acknowledges that the Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) replaced the proposed ETA system, its prominent **“TDAC Information”** links direct visitors to [tdac.in.th](tdac.in.th.md), another privately operated immigration website, rather than directly to the Thai Immigration Bureau's official TDAC portal. The website also links to `thim.in.th`, an unofficial immigration guide that directs visitors to a third-party TDAC submission service at `agents.co.th`. These links document how visitors to `eta.in.th` are directed toward related private immigration websites and services.[^5][^7][^11][^12]

## Partial Corrections and Inconsistent Multilingual Guidance

As of November 25, 2026, the English-language homepage of `eta.in.th` describes Thailand's Electronic Travel Authorization (ETA) as a proposed system whose mandatory implementation remains conditional upon approval.[^5]

This represents a qualification of the website's earlier presentation. However, its Indonesian-language version continues describing the ETA as a forthcoming mandatory requirement for visa-exempt travelers.[^13]

The English-language website also retains an estimated approval time of 24 hours, despite acknowledging elsewhere that Thailand's Ministry of Foreign Affairs has not published an approval timeframe.

Thailand's government has already confirmed that the TDAC system replaced the proposed ETA and became operational on May 1, 2025.[^6]

**The website's partial corrections have not resolved its inconsistent presentation of a superseded immigration proposal across different languages.**

### Commercial Referrals in Response to Traveler Confusion

The website's public discussion section contains numerous questions from travelers attempting to locate the nonexistent ETA application procedure.

Repeated anonymous replies correctly explain that an ETA is not required but direct travelers to the commercial TDAC submission service at `agents.co.th/tdac-apply` rather than the government's free registration portal.[^5]

The identities and commercial affiliations of these anonymous contributors have not been independently established.

Nevertheless, the published exchanges demonstrate how confusion surrounding the proposed ETA can generate referrals to another immigration service operated by the same commercial network.

### Refund Terms for an Unimplemented Immigration System

The website expressly describes itself as providing unofficial travel information and newsletters.

However, its Refund Policy, dated July 16, 2024, describes paid visa assistance, applications submitted to embassies or consulates, government application charges, and refunds of agency fees following unsuccessful applications.[^14]

The policy also provides for transaction-fee deductions of 2–7% in certain cancellation circumstances.

Substantially similar contractual provisions appear on the affiliated [LTR website](ltr.in.th.md), despite that website also disclaiming direct application services.

The available evidence does not establish that AGENTS has accepted payment for nonexistent ETA applications. The refund provisions may represent an obsolete contractual template.

However, their continued publication raises questions about which commercial services the website actually provides and which customer transactions its legal policies govern.

**The continued operation of an outdated ETA information website creates a commercial referral channel for immigration services, even though the government has superseded the proposed system.**

## THNIC Abuse Complaints

On February 27, 2025, **Jesse Nickles** submitted a formal abuse complaint to [THNIC](../brands/thnic.md) concerning `eta.in.th`, [dtv.in.th](dtv.in.th.md), and [asq.in.th](asq.in.th.md). Nickles alleged that `eta.in.th` misleadingly presented itself as an official Thai government ETA website. His complaint also raised concerns about unsolicited visa marketing, the identities of the businesses operating these websites, and their domain-registration arrangements. He requested that THNIC investigate the reported websites and consider suspending their domains.[^1]

On May 1, 2025, Nickles again identified `eta.in.th` in correspondence addressed to THNIC, ThaiCERT, and Thailand's Technology Crime Suppression Division. He reiterated concerns about alleged government impersonation, misleading domain registrations, and commercial activities conducted through immigration-related websites.[^2]

On November 12, 2026, Nickles included `eta.in.th` among seven domains in a formal complaint requesting an investigation under **Section 10.3 of THNIC's Domain Name Registration Policy**. He requested verification of registered holders, examination of registration histories and earlier website presentations, and a written determination addressing whether potentially related registrations involved bad faith or other policy violations.[^3]

Despite the complaints submitted between February 2025 and November 2026, the available correspondence contains no documented response specifically addressing the original `eta.in.th` allegations, no confirmation that THNIC independently investigated the domain, and no final enforcement determination. This does not establish that no internal review occurred, but leaves the registrar's handling of the reported concerns unclear.[^1][^2][^3]

## Government Announcements Repurposed as Commercial Referral Content

On January 31, 2025, Thailand's Ministry of Tourism and Sports published an announcement concerning preparations for the introduction of the Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC).[^15]

AGENTS subsequently reproduced an English translation of this announcement on `eta.in.th`, attributing the material to the ministry.[^5]

However, the republished announcement incorporates five hyperlinks directing readers to the company's privately operated `tdac.in.th` website.

### Commercial Links Embedded Within Government Statements

The inserted hyperlinks appear in passages concerning:

- Government preparations for the TDAC launch.
- Development of the digital arrival card by the Immigration Bureau.
- Integration with airlines, disease-control authorities, and customs departments.
- Official Immigration Bureau informational brochures.
- Government instructional videos explaining the TDAC procedure.

The original Thai-language announcement contains no links to `tdac.in.th` and does not identify AGENTS CO., LTD. as a participant in the government project.[^15]

Nevertheless, the translated publication repeatedly associates the company's commercial domain with descriptions of government infrastructure and official public services.

### Conversion of Government Information Into Commercial Traffic

The linked domain currently redirects visitors to `tdac.agents.co.th`, where AGENTS promotes its private submission service, paid advance submissions, insurance, eSIMs, and airport assistance.[^16]

**The republication transforms an official government announcement into a commercial customer-acquisition channel while retaining the authority associated with the original government source.**

Although both private websites carry disclaimers concerning their unofficial status, those disclaimers do not explain that the hyperlinks embedded within the translated announcement were absent from the original publication.

This practice is particularly significant given the company's repeated criticism of competing TDAC intermediaries for misleading travelers about their official status.

## Obsolete Visa Exemption Guidance Following September 2026 Reforms

As of November 27, 2026, `eta.in.th` continued publishing guidance describing Thailand's 60-day visa exemption scheme for nationals of 93 countries and territories.[^5]

The website also describes extending these stays to 90 days and resetting permitted stays through border runs.

However, substantial changes to Thailand's visa exemption arrangements took effect on September 15, 2026.

### Abolition of the 60-Day Visa Exemption Scheme

Following publication of revised immigration measures in the Royal Gazette on August 31, 2026, Thailand's government confirmed the following changes:[^17]

- Abolition of the 60-day visa exemption scheme.
- A revised 30-day tourist visa exemption scheme covering 60 countries and territories.
- A new 15-day tourist visa exemption scheme covering two countries or territories.
- Reduction of visa-on-arrival eligibility to three countries or territories.

Travelers already admitted under the previous arrangements could remain until the expiration of their existing permission to stay.

Nevertheless, AGENTS continues presenting the superseded 60-day arrangement without explaining these changes.

### Consequences for Previously Eligible Nationalities

The outdated guidance is particularly significant for travelers whose visa exemption privileges were withdrawn entirely.

The Royal Thai Embassy in Mexico expressly confirms that ordinary passport holders from Mexico, Guatemala and Cuba require visas from September 15, 2026.[^18]

All three countries remain listed within the ETA website's published eligibility information.

The distinction is therefore not merely a reduction in permitted stay duration. Some travelers may now require advance visa approval despite previously qualifying for visa-exempt entry.

### Misleading Border Run Guidance

The website also presents departure and re-entry as a method of resetting a visitor's permitted stay.

However, on November 18, 2025, Thailand's Government Public Relations Department announced strengthened immigration screening targeting repeated visa runs without legitimate reasons.[^19]

The measures include restrictions on repeated visa runs and closer scrutiny of extension applications.

The Royal Thai Embassy in London also identifies a two-entry annual restriction for applicable visa-exempt land-border arrivals.[^20]

AGENTS' guidance does not adequately explain these restrictions or the discretionary nature of subsequent immigration admission.

### Commercial Significance

Thai Visa Centre publicly maintains that its affiliated immigration-information websites are regularly updated.[^4]

However, its ETA website continues publishing superseded entry requirements more than two months after the September 2026 reforms took effect.

**AGENTS maintains an immigration-information and commercial referral website for a superseded travel authorization proposal while also publishing obsolete visa exemption requirements and incomplete border run advice.**

The company should clearly identify the historical nature of its ETA material, replace outdated visa exemption guidance, and direct travelers to current official consular requirements.

The available evidence does not establish that any traveler has been denied boarding or entry because of this website.

## Incorrect Tax Residency Threshold and Speculative ETA Tax Integration

As of November 27, 2026, AGENTS' ETA information website continued suggesting a possible connection between the proposed electronic travel authorization system and taxation of foreigners spending more than 180 days annually in Thailand.[^5]

### Incorrect Statutory Residency Threshold

Section 41 of Thailand's Revenue Code establishes tax residency when an individual's cumulative presence in Thailand reaches 180 days or more during a tax year.[^21]

The distinction is significant because individuals spending exactly 180 days in Thailand satisfy the statutory domestic residency threshold.

The Revenue Department's general English-language personal income tax overview contains a similar wording discrepancy, but its published statutory text establishes the correct threshold.[^22]

### Thai Visa Centre's Own Legal Archive Contradicts the Guidance

Thai Visa Centre's legal archive contains a copy of the Revenue Department's statutory provisions captured on November 22, 2026.[^23]

Its explanation of Section 41 correctly identifies the cumulative 180-day threshold.

Nevertheless, the affiliated ETA website continues publishing its earlier inaccurate wording.

### Speculative Connection Between Immigration Registration and Taxation

The Ministry of Foreign Affairs' May 30, 2024 announcement described the proposed ETA as a long-term immigration measure intended to operate alongside the electronic visa system.[^24]

The announcement did not identify tax-residency enforcement as a function of the proposed system.

AGENTS does not identify an official source substantiating its suggested connection between ETA registration and taxation.

### Commercial Significance

**Thai Visa Centre maintains a legal archive containing the correct statutory tax-residency threshold while its affiliated immigration-information website continues publishing contradictory guidance and speculative regulatory connections.**

The operator should distinguish established tax legislation from speculation concerning proposed immigration technologies.

The available evidence does not establish that Thai immigration registration systems are incapable of supporting future tax enforcement, or that any traveler has incurred additional tax liabilities because of AGENTS' published guidance.
