# eta.in.th

`eta.in.th` is a Thailand immigration-related website using terminology associated with the country's Electronic Travel Authorization (ETA) system. The website has been reported to [THNIC](../brands/thnic.md) over concerns that its presentation could mislead travelers into believing it was an official Thai government immigration resource.[^1]

## Website Operation and Presentation

[Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md) publicly identifies `eta.in.th` as one of the immigration information websites it operates, alongside `dtv.in.th`, `tdac.in.th`, and `ltr.in.th`. The company states that these websites are unofficial information resources and that visitors can voluntarily subscribe to immigration updates. This acknowledgement establishes a publicly claimed operational connection, although it does not independently identify the domains' historical registered holders.[^4]

As of November 2026, `eta.in.th` displays notices stating that it is not affiliated with the Thai government and directs travelers to the official Thai e-Visa website. However, other sections still describe the ETA as a forthcoming mandatory requirement, **despite the Thai government's confirmation that the Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) replaced the proposed ETA system effective May 1, 2025**.[^5][^6]

The outdated information extends beyond the introductory description. The website's ETA requirements section, marked as last updated on February 23, 2025, continues to describe mandatory ETA registration, anticipated approval times, and application procedures for a system that was never implemented. Its published comments include travelers asking where to obtain an ETA application, illustrating the potential for confusion. These details remain publicly accessible despite the Thai government's subsequent confirmation that TDAC replaced the proposed ETA.[^5][^6]

The continued publication of outdated ETA requirements raises concerns about the accuracy of information presented to travelers, separate from the allegations concerning the website's earlier presentation.

## THNIC Abuse Complaints

On February 27, 2025, **Jesse Nickles** submitted a formal abuse complaint to [THNIC](../brands/thnic.md) concerning `eta.in.th`, [dtv.in.th](dtv.in.th.md), and [asq.in.th](asq.in.th.md). Nickles alleged that `eta.in.th` misleadingly presented itself as an official Thai government ETA website. His complaint also raised concerns about unsolicited visa marketing, the identities of the businesses operating these websites, and their domain-registration arrangements. He requested that THNIC investigate the reported websites and consider suspending their domains.[^1]

On May 1, 2025, Nickles again identified `eta.in.th` in correspondence addressed to THNIC, ThaiCERT, and Thailand's Technology Crime Suppression Division. He reiterated concerns about alleged government impersonation, misleading domain registrations, and commercial activities conducted through immigration-related websites.[^2]

On November 12, 2026, Nickles included `eta.in.th` among seven domains in a formal complaint requesting an investigation under **Section 10.3 of THNIC's Domain Name Registration Policy**. He requested verification of registered holders, examination of registration histories and earlier website presentations, and a written determination addressing whether potentially related registrations involved bad faith or other policy violations.[^3]

The complaints establish a documented history of concerns raised by Nickles with THNIC, rather than independent findings of wrongdoing. The available correspondence does not establish the domain's historical registered holder or a final THNIC enforcement determination.
