# eta.in.th

`eta.in.th` is an unofficial Thailand Electronic Travel Authorization (ETA) information website operated by [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md), with [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md) identified in its published legal policies. The website continues describing ETA application requirements despite the Thai government's confirmation that the Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) replaced the proposed system. The domain has also been the subject of multiple abuse complaints submitted to [THNIC](../brands/thnic.md).[^1][^4][^5][^6][^8]

## Website Operation and Presentation

[Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md) publicly acknowledges operating `eta.in.th`, alongside other immigration-related websites including `dtv.in.th`, `tdac.in.th`, and `ltr.in.th`. The company describes these websites as unofficial information resources, denies allegations that they are deceptive, and maintains that their information is regularly updated and email subscriptions are voluntary.[^4]

The Terms & Conditions and Privacy Policy published on `eta.in.th` identify [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md) as the business responsible for the website's agreements. Both documents display a last-updated date of July 16, 2024. These first-party disclosures establish a publicly stated business connection, although they do not independently verify historical domain ownership or registration records.[^8][^9]

The Privacy Policy states that personal information, including names, email addresses, and telephone numbers, may be collected directly from visitors or obtained through public databases and joint marketing partners. Its permitted uses include product and service updates. These disclosures are relevant to Jesse Nickles's February 2025 complaint concerning unsolicited visa marketing, but do not establish how recipients' contact information was obtained or whether messages were sent without consent.[^1][^9]

On November 30, 2024, the **ThaiVisaCentre** account on ASEAN NOW publicly acknowledged operating `eta.in.th` after forum participants questioned its unofficial status and published ETA requirements. Participants specifically challenged the website's descriptions of required travel tickets, accommodation details, and proof of funds, including 10,000 THB for individual travelers and 20,000 THB for families. Thai Visa Centre responded that these requirements were not finalized, citing preliminary government discussions and stating that the website would be updated when further official information became available.[^10]

As of November 2026, `eta.in.th` identifies itself as an unofficial immigration information website and includes a February 23, 2025 notice acknowledging that the proposed ETA system may have been replaced by the Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC). However, further down the same page, it continues describing ETA registration as mandatory for visa-exempt travelers, outlines application procedures, and displays an estimated approval time of 24 hours. These contradictory statements remain published despite the Thai government's confirmation that TDAC replaced the proposed ETA system effective May 1, 2025.[^5][^6]

The website retains an **“Official Thailand ETA Links”** section pointing to older government publications and continues inviting visitors to subscribe for ETA notifications. Alongside its outdated application requirements, these features could mislead travelers about whether an ETA application system exists or remains planned.[^5][^6]

Although `eta.in.th` acknowledges that the Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) replaced the proposed ETA system, its prominent **“TDAC Information”** links direct visitors to [tdac.in.th](tdac.in.th.md), another privately operated immigration website, rather than directly to the Thai Immigration Bureau's official TDAC portal. The website also links to `thim.in.th`, an unofficial immigration guide that directs visitors to a third-party TDAC submission service at `agents.co.th`. These links document how visitors to `eta.in.th` are directed toward related private immigration websites and services.[^5][^7][^11][^12]

## THNIC Abuse Complaints

On February 27, 2025, **Jesse Nickles** submitted a formal abuse complaint to [THNIC](../brands/thnic.md) concerning `eta.in.th`, [dtv.in.th](dtv.in.th.md), and [asq.in.th](asq.in.th.md). Nickles alleged that `eta.in.th` misleadingly presented itself as an official Thai government ETA website. His complaint also raised concerns about unsolicited visa marketing, the identities of the businesses operating these websites, and their domain-registration arrangements. He requested that THNIC investigate the reported websites and consider suspending their domains.[^1]

On May 1, 2025, Nickles again identified `eta.in.th` in correspondence addressed to THNIC, ThaiCERT, and Thailand's Technology Crime Suppression Division. He reiterated concerns about alleged government impersonation, misleading domain registrations, and commercial activities conducted through immigration-related websites.[^2]

On November 12, 2026, Nickles included `eta.in.th` among seven domains in a formal complaint requesting an investigation under **Section 10.3 of THNIC's Domain Name Registration Policy**. He requested verification of registered holders, examination of registration histories and earlier website presentations, and a written determination addressing whether potentially related registrations involved bad faith or other policy violations.[^3]

Despite the complaints submitted between February 2025 and November 2026, the available correspondence contains no documented response specifically addressing the original `eta.in.th` allegations, no confirmation that THNIC independently investigated the domain, and no final enforcement determination. This does not establish that no internal review occurred, but leaves the registrar's handling of the reported concerns unclear.[^1][^2][^3]
