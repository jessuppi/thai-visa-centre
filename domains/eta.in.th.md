# eta.in.th

`eta.in.th` is a Thailand immigration-related website using terminology associated with the country's Electronic Travel Authorization (ETA) system. The website has been reported to [THNIC](../brands/thnic.md) over concerns that its presentation could mislead travelers into believing it was an official Thai government immigration resource.[^1]

## Website Operation and Presentation

[Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md) publicly identifies `eta.in.th` as one of the immigration information websites it operates, alongside `dtv.in.th`, `tdac.in.th`, and `ltr.in.th`. The company states that these websites are unofficial information resources and that visitors can voluntarily subscribe to immigration updates. This acknowledgement establishes a publicly claimed operational connection, although it does not independently identify the domains' historical registered holders.[^4]

As of November 2026, `eta.in.th` displays notices denying any affiliation with the Thai government and directs travelers to the official Thai e-Visa website. However, its ETA requirements section, marked as last updated on February 23, 2025, still describes mandatory ETA registration, anticipated approval times, and application procedures. **This contradicts the Thai government's confirmation that the Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) replaced the proposed ETA system effective May 1, 2025.**[^5][^6]

The website also contains visitor comments asking where to obtain an ETA application, illustrating how the outdated information could confuse travelers. These concerns about the accuracy of its current content are separate from the allegations concerning its earlier presentation.[^5]

The website also retains an **“Official Thailand ETA Links”** section directing visitors to older government publications for information about applying for an ETA, despite there being no implemented ETA application system. Its homepage continues inviting visitors to subscribe for ETA notifications. These features extend the outdated information beyond the requirements section, potentially giving travelers the impression that an ETA application process still exists or is forthcoming.[^5][^6]

Although `eta.in.th` acknowledges that the Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) is now required, its prominent **“TDAC Information”** links direct visitors to [tdac.in.th](tdac.in.th.md), another privately operated immigration-related website, rather than directly to the Thai Immigration Bureau's official TDAC portal at `tdac.immigration.go.th`. While these links are presented as information resources rather than official application links, they direct travelers seeking current entry requirements to another website that has been the subject of complaints to THNIC.[^2][^5][^7]

## THNIC Abuse Complaints

On February 27, 2025, **Jesse Nickles** submitted a formal abuse complaint to [THNIC](../brands/thnic.md) concerning `eta.in.th`, [dtv.in.th](dtv.in.th.md), and [asq.in.th](asq.in.th.md). Nickles alleged that `eta.in.th` misleadingly presented itself as an official Thai government ETA website. His complaint also raised concerns about unsolicited visa marketing, the identities of the businesses operating these websites, and their domain-registration arrangements. He requested that THNIC investigate the reported websites and consider suspending their domains.[^1]

On May 1, 2025, Nickles again identified `eta.in.th` in correspondence addressed to THNIC, ThaiCERT, and Thailand's Technology Crime Suppression Division. He reiterated concerns about alleged government impersonation, misleading domain registrations, and commercial activities conducted through immigration-related websites.[^2]

On November 12, 2026, Nickles included `eta.in.th` among seven domains in a formal complaint requesting an investigation under **Section 10.3 of THNIC's Domain Name Registration Policy**. He requested verification of registered holders, examination of registration histories and earlier website presentations, and a written determination addressing whether potentially related registrations involved bad faith or other policy violations.[^3]

Despite the complaints submitted between February 2025 and November 2026, the available correspondence contains no documented response specifically addressing the original `eta.in.th` allegations, no confirmation that THNIC independently investigated the domain, and no final enforcement determination. This does not establish that no internal review occurred, but leaves the registrar's handling of the reported concerns unclear.[^1][^2][^3]
