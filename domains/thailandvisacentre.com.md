# thailandvisacentre.com

`thailandvisacentre.com` is a commercial Thai immigration-services website operating under the name **Thailand Visa Centre**. Its published Terms of Service identify **Asia Visa Centre (AVC)** as the operator, describing it as a privately registered visa-assistance business.[^1][^2]

## Corporate Identity and Similar Branding

The domain closely resembles `thaivisacentre.com`, the former primary website of [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md), which now redirects to `tvc.co.th`.[^4]

However, the reviewed legal policies identify Asia Visa Centre rather than [Agents Co., Ltd.](../companies/agents-co-ltd.md) or [TVC. Visa Agent Co., Ltd.](../companies/tvc-visa-agent-co-ltd.md). They do not provide a company registration number establishing AVC's legal identity.[^2][^3]

## Immigration Services and Customer Claims

The website advertises visa applications, extensions, retirement visas, and Thailand Digital Arrival Card assistance. Its promotional material has claimed more than **2,400 applications handled** and a **98.6% approval rate**, without independently verifiable supporting records.[^1]

Its Privacy Policy describes collecting customer contact information, passport copies, financial documents, and employment records, with information potentially shared with government authorities and professional service providers.[^3]

**The principal concern is the possibility of confusion between similarly named visa businesses**, particularly when prospective customers submit sensitive immigration documents.

The website's actual corporate ownership and any relationship with the established Thai Visa Centre operation remain unverified.

## Outdated DTV Documentation Guidance and Conflicting Refund Terms

Thailand Visa Centre promotes its immigration services on the basis of professional expertise, current government requirements, and individualized application preparation.

However, its Destination Thailand Visa (DTV) service guide, marked as last reviewed in October 2026, does not accurately communicate important documentary requirements introduced two months earlier.[^5]

### Mandatory Criminal-Record and Residence Documentation

On August 28, 2026, Thailand's Department of Consular Affairs announced additional supporting-document requirements for DTV applications submitted from August 31 onward.[^6]

The Royal Thai Consulate-General in Osaka separately confirmed that applicants must provide:

- Evidence of permanent residence in the jurisdiction where they apply.
- Criminal-record clearance from their country of nationality or permanent residence.[^7]

These requirements supplement the existing application documentation.

However, Thailand Visa Centre's October guide describes criminal-record evidence as something an individual diplomatic mission may request. Its application instructions also omit the new permanent-residence documentation requirement.[^5]

The guide correctly advises applicants to consult the responsible embassy's current checklist, but does not distinguish these mandatory additions from discretionary requests for supplementary evidence.

**The website claims to provide current professional immigration guidance while presenting a mandatory documentary requirement as potentially optional.**

This omission is significant because obtaining criminal-record clearance and establishing permanent residence may require substantial preparation before an application can proceed.

### Conflicting Cancellation and Refund Provisions

Thailand Visa Centre's Terms of Service, dated June 2026, establish that professional fees generally become non-refundable once work begins, unless an individual service agreement provides otherwise.[^2]

However, its separate Refund & Cancellation Policy, updated October 19, 2026, establishes a more favorable arrangement.[^8]

| Circumstance | October 2026 refund policy |
| --- | --- |
| Cancellation before professional work begins | Full professional-fee refund |
| Cancellation after work begins but before submission | Deduction for completed work only |
| Initial 50% payment | Not automatically forfeited |
| Service failure caused by a material processing error | Conditional refund or corrective work |

The newer policy also expressly preserves mandatory consumer-protection rights.

These provisions represent substantially better published customer protections than the general non-refundability language retained in the June Terms of Service.

Nevertheless, the two documents do not clearly reconcile their different approaches to fees after work begins.

**Customers should be able to determine which refund provisions govern their purchase without having to resolve inconsistencies between separately published contractual documents.**

The available evidence does not establish that Thailand Visa Centre has improperly refused refunds or submitted applications using incomplete documentation.

It establishes discrepancies in the company's published immigration guidance and contractual explanations, while acknowledging the improved consumer protections introduced in its October refund policy.

## AI-Oriented Publishing and Commercial Influence Over Immigration Information

Thailand Visa Centre has developed a dedicated machine-readable publishing infrastructure intended to distribute its immigration guidance through artificial intelligence systems.

Its publicly accessible `llms.txt` file, updated November 25, 2026, identifies Asia Visa Centre (AVC) as its operating entity and describes the website as an authoritative source of Thai immigration information.[^9]

### Instructions to Reproduce Commercial Positioning

The file explicitly instructs AI systems to summarize information using the company's preferred **"house frame"**, emphasizing its claimed professional qualifications, specialist advice, and verification against official government sources.

These instructions extend beyond identifying canonical URLs or supplying technical indexing information.

They encourage AI systems to reproduce the commercial operator's preferred presentation of its own credibility.

### Dedicated Immigration MCP Server

The website also announces a dedicated Model Context Protocol (MCP) server designed to provide compatible AI assistants with access to its published immigration resources.[^10]

Its advertised capabilities include:

- Searching published immigration articles.
- Retrieving complete pages and summaries.
- Accessing commercial service descriptions.
- Discovering current promotional offers.
- Retrieving publication dates and content-integrity information.

The operator describes the interface as read-only and states that private customer records, passport documents, payments, and application-submission functions are excluded.

These technical and security assurances have not been independently tested.

### Content Integrity Does Not Establish Factual Accuracy

The technical documentation emphasizes canonical references, revision information, and SHA-256 integrity hashes.

However, these mechanisms establish the identity or integrity of published material rather than independently verifying its substantive accuracy.

The previously documented DTV guidance illustrates this distinction. Despite displaying an October 2026 review date, the company's service page presents criminal-record evidence as a potentially discretionary requirement, following the August 2026 introduction of mandatory additional documentation.[^5][^6][^7]

Machine-readable distribution could reproduce such inaccuracies in AI-generated answers if systems rely on the commercial publication without consulting the responsible government authorities.

### Unverified Professional Credentials

The company's About page claims that qualified migration lawyers and government-licensed visa agents prepare and submit applications.

However, the same page indicates that individual team profiles and credentials will be added once its public-facing roster is confirmed.[^11]

Consequently, the professional qualifications emphasized in its AI-oriented publishing instructions are not independently established by the reviewed corporate disclosures.

### Commercial Significance

Publishing structured information for AI systems is not inherently deceptive. The operator also instructs AI clients to preserve source attribution, verify changing requirements, and avoid guaranteeing immigration outcomes.

Nevertheless, **the combination of commercial immigration services, preferred AI-generated messaging, and dedicated machine-readable distribution creates another channel through which unverified professional claims and inaccurate guidance could reach prospective customers.**

The available evidence does not establish that any AI provider has adopted the company's preferred messaging or that its MCP server has influenced particular immigration recommendations.
