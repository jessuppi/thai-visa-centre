# Retirement Visa Agency

- **Date:** July 12, 2018
- **Platform:** ASEAN NOW (formerly ThaiVisa.com)
- **Source:** https://aseannow.com/topic/1048018-retirement-visa-agency/

## Summary

On July 12, 2018, a discussion on ThaiVisa.com questioned the legitimacy of [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md), after the original poster reported finding the agency through Facebook and being offered a retirement visa extension despite not possessing the required 800,000 baht in a Thai bank account.

The original poster explained that they did not qualify for an extension under the normal financial requirements but had been assured that Thai Visa Centre could arrange one regardless. Other participants questioned whether such arrangements were lawful and warned about the potential consequences of obtaining immigration extensions without satisfying the official requirements.

A forum member named Tanoshi subsequently reproduced an unsolicited promotional email attributed to Thai Visa Centre. The message advertised retirement visa extensions for 16,000 baht, with an additional 4,000 baht for multiple-entry permission. According to the reproduced email, the agency did not require applicants to demonstrate a bank balance or pension income because it would sponsor their financial requirements.

The email also advertised processing within 3–7 business days, requiring only a passport and photographs, with complimentary 90-day reporting. Customers were offered several ways to submit their passports, including courier collection, postal delivery, visiting the Bangkok office, or handing documents directly to an immigration officer allegedly working with the agency.

Tanoshi stated that they had never contacted Thai Visa Centre or requested information from any visa agency. They questioned how the company had obtained their email address, explaining that they had recently provided their contact information to a translation service handling documents for the British Embassy and Thailand's Ministry of Foreign Affairs. The commenter raised concerns that customer contact information might have been shared or sold between unrelated businesses, although the source of the mailing list was not established.

Another commenter, using the account `tonyThrowAway`, claimed to have successfully used the agency the previous year after purchasing a condominium and no longer having sufficient funds in Thailand. The account also described Thai Visa Centre's Facebook messaging system and Bangkok office. This testimonial was not independently verified.

The discussion developed into a broader debate about visa agents circumventing financial requirements, the legitimacy of temporarily supplied bank funds, and allegations that some immigration officers accepted unofficial payments to approve otherwise ineligible applications. These claims reflected participants' interpretations rather than independently established wrongdoing by Thai Visa Centre.

The thread provides contemporaneous evidence of Thai Visa Centre's public promotion in 2018, including a reproduced email advertising visa extensions without ordinary financial documentation, alleged arrangements with immigration officers, and questions about unsolicited marketing and the provenance of customer contact information. The email's authenticity, the legality of the advertised arrangements, and the source of the recipient's email address were not independently established.
