## Summary

A June 2025 discussion on the Thai360 expatriate forum documents a customer's reported experience obtaining a retirement extension through [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md), including unusually rapid processing, discrepancies involving his registered residential address, and subsequent allegations concerning the removal of historical news coverage about the agency.

## Retirement Extension Obtained Two Days After Opening Bank Account

On June 30, 2025, forum member `think_too_mut` reported obtaining a Non-Immigrant O visa through the Royal Thai Consulate in Osaka, Japan.

The member initially intended to open a Thai bank account, deposit 800,000 baht, maintain the required balance for two months, and subsequently apply for a retirement extension without using an agency.

However, on July 9, the same member reported successfully obtaining a one-year retirement extension through Thai Visa Centre, providing the following timeline:

| Date | Reported activity |
| --- | --- |
| July 4, 2025 | Consultation, paperwork, and payment at Thai Visa Centre |
| July 7, 2025 | Bank account opened with assistance from agency staff |
| July 8, 2025 | Application processing |
| July 9, 2025 | Passport and bank passbook delivered by messenger |

The customer praised Thai Visa Centre's service and speculated that his application may have been included in a batch of applications processed together.

**The customer reported receiving his retirement extension only two days after opening the bank account, despite his earlier acknowledgment of the two-month financial seasoning requirement.**

The discussion does not establish how the financial requirements were satisfied, whether another qualifying financial arrangement was used, or whether immigration officials exercised an applicable exception.

Nevertheless, the timeline provides another publicly documented account of unusually rapid retirement-extension processing through Thai Visa Centre.

## Discrepancy in Registered Residential Address

On July 28, 2025, the customer reported leaving Thailand after successfully obtaining his bank account, retirement extension, and yellow house-registration book for his property.

However, he identified an outstanding task involving his residential address with immigration, explaining that the address required for his residence certificate differed from the address associated with his retirement visa.

The discrepancy raises additional questions about the residential information used during the agency's processing of his extension.

The discussion does not identify the immigration office responsible for issuing the extension or establish why the addresses differed.

## Allegations of Media Removal and Subsequent Republication

On August 13, 2025, the same customer made a substantially different observation about Thai Visa Centre:

> Thai Visa Centre are paying web sites to remove anything related.

He reported that an article he had attempted to share was promptly deleted and that the agency's prominent Facebook page had disappeared.

The member subsequently reproduced The Thaiger's August 5, 2020 article concerning the arrests of Chad Scira and Grace Scira following allegations of visa forgery and illegal cannabis cultivation.

The reproduced article also preserved The Thaiger's account of receiving threats from the administrator of the Facebook page identified in the police investigation, alongside Thai Visa Centre's denial that its customers' visas were fraudulent.

On August 14, the forum member reported that the original news article and the agency's Facebook page had reappeared. He suggested that the situation might involve a dispute between agencies or the targeting of a business owner.

He also reproduced material attributed to a February 2024 online publication containing further allegations about the removal of historical reporting and Thai Visa Centre's immigration-processing practices.

## Relevance to the Wider Investigation

The discussion is significant because the allegations concerning media removal originated from the same forum participant who had publicly praised Thai Visa Centre's retirement-extension services approximately one month earlier.

The reported disappearance and subsequent reappearance of historical news coverage also complement the documented [removal of the 2019 Udon Map discussion](2019-11-udon-map-visa-agency-immigration-database.md) following Thai Visa Centre's May 2024 complaint.

The Thai360 discussion does not independently establish that Thai Visa Centre paid publishers to remove articles, unlawfully obtained retirement extensions, or submitted false residential information to immigration authorities.

Nevertheless, it preserves a detailed firsthand customer account of rapid retirement-extension processing alongside contemporaneous allegations concerning the suppression of historical reporting about the agency.
