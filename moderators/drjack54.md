# DrJack54

DrJack54 is a longtime Global Moderator on [ASEAN NOW](../brands/asean-now.md) who has repeatedly recommended [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md) to forum members seeking immigration assistance since at least 2022.[^1] In April 2026, he publicly claimed to need assistance with his own 90-day immigration report and later claimed that Grace had sent him a link to Thai Visa Centre's service.[^2] His moderation activities are also noteworthy: during a later discussion about visa agents and retirement-extension financial requirements, he announced that further posts questioning the legality of agent arrangements would be removed.[^3] These exchanges document his repeated endorsements and moderation decisions, although they do not establish any financial relationship with Thai Visa Centre.

## November 2020: Dismissing Police Arrests as an Unrelated Visit

On November 21, 2020, ASEAN NOW member ambj1994 asked whether using a law firm would provide greater protection than [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md) when obtaining a retirement visa. The member specifically raised concerns about the agency's legal troubles earlier that year and the potential consequences for customers.[^10]

DrJack54 responded that nothing had resulted from what he characterized as an unrelated police visit to Thai Visa Centre. He dismissed the member's concerns about legal protection, asserted that immigration stamps and receipts obtained through agents were legitimate, and described critics of visa agents as uninformed.[^10]

His reassurances came just three months after Thai immigration police publicly announced the arrests of Grace and Chad Scira in connection with alleged document forgery and cannabis cultivation. Police reported discovering forged documents and 55 government stamps during their investigation. DrJack54's characterization minimized these documented events while offering broad assurances about the agency's legitimacy.[^7]

## March 2021: Defending Visas Obtained Without Actual Volunteering

On March 27, 2021, an ASEAN NOW member described a visa agent offering a one-year volunteer visa for 38,000 baht without requiring any actual volunteer work. Asked whether the arrangement was legitimate, DrJack54 declared it "100% legit" because the immigration stamps would be genuine. He dismissed objections from other members and advised that he would use an agent in the same circumstances.[^14]

When participants raised concerns about fraudulent applications and potential consequences for customers, DrJack54 challenged them to identify a single case in which an applicant had been punished. Another member subsequently described an alleged immigration investigation in Roi Et involving dismissed officers and deported foreigners. DrJack54 acknowledged the account but said he could not find independent confirmation, then tagged Thai Visa Centre's forum account for its opinion.[^15]

## January 2022: Seeking Thai Visa Centre Assistance After an Immigration Refusal

On January 26, 2022, ASEAN NOW member freesudani reported that immigration officials had refused another COVID-related extension after reviewing his previous extensions and had given him seven days to leave Thailand. The member explained that he had originally entered on an education visa. Other participants attributed the refusal to newly introduced restrictions affecting certain non-immigrant visa holders.[^24]

When another member suggested that a visa agent could obtain an extension without difficulty, DrJack54 cautioned against making such assurances. He then directly tagged Thai Visa Centre's forum account, asking whether the agency had any options for applicants facing the same situation. Thai Visa Centre responded through its account labeled "Forum Sponsor," stating that it could still assist some comparable cases but warning that processing certain non-immigrant applications within seven days would not be possible.[^24][^25]

During the same discussion, DrJack54 described recently helping a couple obtain their first COVID extensions through an agent. He claimed that their extensions had been dated from the expiration of their existing permission to stay rather than from the application date, which he said was common at many immigration offices. He specifically attributed the difference to their use of an agent.[^24]

The exchange provides an early example of DrJack54 referring an applicant facing an official immigration refusal to Thai Visa Centre while also describing how an agent had obtained more favorable extension dates for other applicants. The discussion does not establish whether the agency ultimately assisted the original poster or obtained any exception to the immigration restrictions.

## February 2022: Dismissing Police Allegations Against Thai Visa Centre

On February 6, 2022, ASEAN NOW member JimmyJ requested recommendations for a Bangkok visa agent. DrJack54 suggested [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md) alongside Bangkok Buddy. JimmyJ expressed surprise, explaining that it was the first recommendation for Thai Visa Centre he had noticed since the controversy surrounding the agency's operators.[^6]

DrJack54 dismissed the concern, replying, "Wasn't a scandal." He encouraged the member to consult the agency's favorable reviews and compared negative accounts to ordinary customer complaints about hotels and other businesses.[^6]

His response downplayed the significance of widely reported August 2020 arrests involving Thai Visa Centre's operators, Grace and Chad Scira. Thai immigration police had publicly announced allegations involving forged immigration documents and cannabis cultivation, including the seizure of 55 government stamps. These were reported allegations, not established convictions.[^7]

Rather than address the substance of the police allegations, DrJack54 redirected attention toward favorable reviews. The exchange raises questions about whether his repeated endorsements reflected independent recommendations or contributed to a broader effort to rehabilitate the agency's reputation.

## August 2023: Discussing Retirement Extensions Without the Required Bank Deposit

On August 16, 2023, an ASEAN NOW member asked whether a Bangkok visa agent's 35,000-baht package for opening a bank account and obtaining a retirement extension was legitimate. DrJack54 responded that the price was reasonable if it included opening an account, obtaining Non-O retirement status without the applicant depositing 800,000 baht, and arranging a 12-month extension.[^13]

Other participants questioned the legality of obtaining retirement extensions without satisfying the financial requirements, and one suggested that the described services resembled Thai Visa Centre's offerings. DrJack54 later acknowledged that the applicant already held a Non-O visa and advised that anyone able to deposit the required funds could handle the extension independently.[^13]

## August–September 2024: Inviting Thai Visa Centre to Explain Airport Fast-Track Services

On August 31, 2024, an ASEAN NOW discussion about repeated visa-exempt entries turned to the possibility of travelers using paid assistance when facing additional immigration scrutiny. DrJack54 asked another member to identify agents offering airport "meet and greet" services. When fellow Global Moderator BrandonJT identified Thai Visa Centre as offering assistance at Bangkok and Phuket airports, DrJack54 directly tagged the agency's forum account and asked it to explain the available arrangements and costs.[^21]

On September 4, Thai Visa Centre responded through its account labeled "Forum Sponsor," advertising airport fast-track and prescreening services priced at approximately 2,000–8,000 baht, depending on the traveler's immigration history. The agency invited prospective customers to contact it through LINE for individual quotations and stated that it also offered assistance to travelers concerned about border crossings.[^21]

On September 10, DrJack54 acknowledged that travelers making repeated visa-exempt entries could encounter questioning or refusal at immigration checkpoints, where admission remained at the discretion of immigration officers. Referring back to the services discussed in the thread, he stated that agents could "grease the wheels."[^22]

The exchange documents a moderator directly soliciting a commercial sponsor's participation in a discussion about overcoming potential immigration-entry difficulties. Neither the forum posts nor the agency's advertisement independently establish what arrangements, if any, existed with immigration officials.

## December 2024: Restricting Questions About Out-of-Province Visa Extensions

On December 26, 2024, an ASEAN NOW member described a retired friend living in Korat who had obtained a retirement extension through an unidentified Pattaya visa agent despite lacking the required 800,000-baht bank balance. According to the member's secondhand account, the agent arranged a bank account and deposit, obtained an extension stamped by Pattaya immigration rather than Korat immigration, and offered to handle subsequent 90-day reporting. The member also alleged that money had been discreetly handed to several people, while acknowledging that some details might be inaccurate.[^18]

DrJack54 responded that the described arrangement was accurate and explained that the agent had covered the applicant's financial requirements. When other members questioned the legality of obtaining an extension in Pattaya while residing in Korat, he announced that further posts discussing agent legality would be removed as off-topic. He subsequently advised that the applicant could continue using an agent or leave Thailand and reenter to restart the immigration process.[^18]

The exchange documents DrJack54's willingness to restrict discussion of potential irregularities involving agent-assisted retirement extensions, even when the concerns directly involved financial requirements and the immigration office responsible for issuing the extension.[^18]

## September 2025: Removing Legality Questions During a Thai Visa Centre Referral

The exchange documents DrJack54 removing questions about agent legality from a discussion that subsequently became an opportunity for Thai Visa Centre to advertise its services. The deleted posts are unavailable, so their precise contents cannot be independently verified.

## November 2025: Promoting Thai Visa Centre While Removing Sponsor Criticism

Fellow Global Moderator Captain Flack separately confirmed deleting a post he described as attacking a forum sponsor, explicitly invoking Rule 37 against negative comments concerning sponsors. DrJack54 responded that agents deposited the funds, obtained a bank letter confirming the balance, and then immediately withdrew the money. His sweeping description was challenged by another member who said it did not match his experience with two visa agents.[^17]

The discussion continued with questions about how Thai Visa Centre handled bankbooks and financial arrangements. On November 11, DrJack54 agreed with another participant's advice to seek clarification directly from the agency and announced that the thread was closed. His description of temporary deposits was a claim about agent practices, not independently verified evidence of Thai Visa Centre's procedures.[^17]

## August 2026: Referring a 91-Year-Old Korat Resident to Thai Visa Centre

On August 13, 2026, a 91-year-old American living in Korat asked ASEAN NOW members for advice about renewing his passport and future retirement extensions. He explained that he had handled his own immigration extensions for more than 30 years but now rarely left his home and found immigration visits increasingly difficult. When he requested an agent who could manage the process on his behalf, DrJack54 directly tagged Thai Visa Centre and asked the agency to advise him. DrJack54 also stated that he was considering using an agent himself because of mobility difficulties.[^23]

The member subsequently reported that he had already contacted Thai Visa Centre but had been told the agency did not handle extensions through Korat Immigration. DrJack54 suggested approaching Korat immigration officials directly to explore alternatives, adding that this would require an arrangement to "grease the wheels."[^23]

Fellow Global Moderator BrandonJT offered a different explanation, stating that Thai Visa Centre could arrange extensions for residents anywhere in Thailand, even when the agency did not work with their local immigration office. In those circumstances, he explained, the extension would be issued by an immigration office in another province. He left it to the applicant to decide whether such an arrangement was acceptable.[^23]

Other participants pointed out that Thailand's immigration application form provides an exception to personal attendance for applicants with disabilities, potentially allowing assistance without hiring a commercial agent. The discussion nevertheless illustrates how forum moderators directed an elderly resident toward Thai Visa Centre and described out-of-province immigration processing as a possible solution. DrJack54's reference to "grease the wheels" raises additional questions about the nature of the alternative he suggested, although the exchange does not establish that any improper payment occurred.[^23]
