# BrandonJT

*Last updated: November 1, 2026*

BrandonJT is a Global Moderator on [ASEAN NOW](../brands/asean-now.md) who joined the forum in late 2024 and appears to have received moderator privileges almost immediately afterward. Such a rapid promotion to a position of authority is highly unusual for a large, established discussion forum, particularly for a newly registered account with little posting history. Although the account presents itself as an expatriate living in the Chiang Rai area,[^1] BrandonJT has repeatedly recommended services offered by [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md), including paid airport "safe entry" arrangements[^2][^4][^7][^8] and retirement visa assistance.[^3][^5][^6] In May 2025, BrandonJT directly referred users to the agency's booking website at asq.in.th,[^12] while other posts have promoted its services through tdac.agents.co.th. These recommendations are particularly noteworthy given the account's unusually rapid elevation to a position of authority on a popular immigration forum.

## May 2025: Direct Contact with Thai Visa Centre

On May 4, 2025, BrandonJT told another ASEAN NOW user that Thai Visa Centre provided paid airport entry services at Suvarnabhumi, Don Mueang, and Phuket airports. He supplied the agency's booking URL at asq.in.th/thailand-fasttrack and explicitly acknowledged that Thai Visa Centre had "previously shared this link with me" for registering customers.[^13]

Three days later, on May 7, BrandonJT shared the same booking URL in another discussion, explaining that users could request a quote for the agency's airport "safe entry" service.[^12]

These exchanges are significant because BrandonJT acknowledged receiving a service-specific booking link from Thai Visa Centre before repeatedly directing forum users toward it. This suggests a more direct line of communication with the business than an ordinary forum member casually recommending a service.

## July 2025: Claiming Guaranteed Airport Entry Through Visa Agents

On July 23, 2025, BrandonJT responded to an ASEAN NOW user concerned about being denied entry to Thailand despite paying for an airport "safe entry" service. BrandonJT described these services as "100% reliable," claiming that agents would first obtain copies of every page of the customer's passport and submit them to contacts at the airport for screening and pricing.[^15]

He further claimed that customers would be instructed to send a photograph of their clothing before boarding their flight so that an immigration officer could identify them upon arrival. According to BrandonJT, the officer would personally meet the traveler, escort them to an immigration counter, and open it specifically to process their entry.[^15]

BrandonJT then directed the user to [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md), explaining how to access its paid VIP/Fast Track service through [tdac.agents.co.th](../domains/tdac.agents.co.th.md). Another forum participant questioned the legitimacy of the arrangement, describing BrandonJT's explanation as suspicious.[^15]

The exchange is significant because a Global Moderator of ASEAN NOW was publicly promoting a private visa agency while making extraordinary claims about guaranteed admission and direct cooperation from Thai immigration officers. The discussion provides no independent verification that the described arrangements operated as claimed.

## November 2025: Endorsing a Forum Sponsor While Criticism Was Removed

On November 4, 2025, BrandonJT publicly praised Thai Visa Centre in an ASEAN NOW discussion, claiming that virtually everyone he had seen discussing the agency reported positive experiences. In the same discussion, Thai Visa Centre participated under an account explicitly labeled "Forum Sponsor" and advertised its immigration services.[^3]

The thread also contained a moderation notice confirming that a post criticizing the sponsor had been removed. Another warning invoked ASEAN NOW's Rule 37, which restricted negative comments concerning advertisements and sponsors.[^3]

These actions are particularly noteworthy given ASEAN NOW's published forum rules. Rule 24 generally treats commercial promotion by non-sponsors as spam, while Rule 37 prohibits disrespectful, dismissive, or off-topic comments concerning sponsors and sponsored content.[^16]

This creates an apparent imbalance in which favorable recommendations for commercial sponsors can remain visible while criticism may face removal. BrandonJT's public endorsement of Thai Visa Centre is especially relevant given his Global Moderator status, although the available evidence does not establish that he personally removed the disputed post.

## June–July 2026: Downplaying an Investigation into Guaranteed Entry Services

On June 23, 2026, Thailand's Immigration Division 2 announced an investigation into brokers allegedly charging foreigners between 4,500 and 12,000 baht for guaranteed entry through Suvarnabhumi Airport. Authorities established a fact-finding committee to examine possible misconduct involving immigration officers and warned that any officials found responsible could face disciplinary and criminal proceedings.[^17]

The following day, BrandonJT responded to an ASEAN NOW discussion of the investigation by predicting that the services would return to normal once public attention moved elsewhere. On June 25, he further mocked the prospect of meaningful enforcement with the sarcastic remark, "We've investigated ourselves and found no wrongdoing."[^7]

On July 19, after other forum members reported that airport "safe entry" services had been suspended or restricted, BrandonJT stated that they remained available at Don Mueang, Chiang Mai, and Phuket airports. He also mentioned an unconfirmed rumor that the service had resumed at Suvarnabhumi.[^7]

These statements are noteworthy given BrandonJT's earlier promotion of paid airport entry services. Rather than expressing concern about the allegations under investigation, he appeared to treat the enforcement efforts as a temporary disruption. The official investigation concerned alleged broker activities and did not publicly identify BrandonJT or Thai Visa Centre as subjects of the inquiry.

## October 2026: Describing Temporary Bank Deposits for Retirement Visas

On October 1, 2026, BrandonJT explained how some Thai visa agents arrange retirement extensions for customers who do not have the required 800,000 baht available. According to his description, an agent helps the applicant open a Thai bank account, temporarily deposits 800,000 baht, obtains the necessary bank statements and immigration documentation, and then has the applicant "return the funds" to the agent.[^6]

However, Thailand's published immigration requirements generally mandate maintaining at least 800,000 baht in a Thai bank account for two months before applying for a retirement extension and three months after approval, with a minimum balance of 400,000 baht thereafter when using the bank-deposit qualification method.[^14]

Other forum participants questioned how the temporary deposits described by BrandonJT could satisfy these requirements. BrandonJT did not explain how the arrangement complied with the published rules or identify any applicable legal exemption.[^6]

The discussion documents a Global Moderator of ASEAN NOW presenting an arrangement involving temporary agent-supplied funds as a routine part of obtaining retirement extensions. Although BrandonJT frequently recommends Thai Visa Centre, this particular post did not identify which agents offered the arrangement.
