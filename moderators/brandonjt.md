# BrandonJT

*Last updated: November 1, 2026*

BrandonJT is a Global Moderator on [ASEAN NOW](../brands/asean-now.md) who joined the forum in late 2024 and appears to have received moderator privileges almost immediately afterward. Such a rapid promotion to a position of authority is highly unusual for a large, established discussion forum, particularly for a newly registered account with little posting history. Although the account presents itself as an expatriate living in the Chiang Rai area,[^1] BrandonJT has repeatedly recommended services offered by [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md), including paid airport "safe entry" arrangements[^2][^4][^7][^8] and retirement visa assistance.[^3][^5][^6] In May 2025, BrandonJT directly referred users to the agency's booking website at asq.in.th,[^12] while other posts have promoted its services through tdac.agents.co.th. These recommendations are particularly noteworthy given the account's unusually rapid elevation to a position of authority on a popular immigration forum.

## November 2024: Discussing Paid Airport Entry Services

On November 21, 2024, BrandonJT participated in an ASEAN NOW discussion about a traveler who had been refused entry to Thailand. When another user asked about the cost of airport "safe entry" services, BrandonJT explained that pricing depended on the applicant's immigration history. He estimated that services started at approximately 1,500–2,000 baht, with higher prices for applicants whose travel histories presented greater difficulties.[^26]

The exchange is an early documented example of BrandonJT discussing the pricing and limitations of paid airport entry arrangements. Although he did not identify a particular agency, his comments preceded his March 2025 endorsements of Thai Visa Centre and his May 2025 recommendation of its 2,000–4,000 baht airport entry service.[^19][^20][^26]

## March 2025: Publicly Endorsing Thai Visa Centre

On March 18, 2025, BrandonJT responded to an ASEAN NOW user in Phuket who asked whether Thai Visa Centre could arrange a retirement visa extension remotely. BrandonJT confirmed that the agency served customers throughout Thailand and explained that those outside Bangkok could mail their passports to its Bangkok office.[^19]

He explicitly described Thai Visa Centre as a "legit agent" and encouraged the user to look for online reviews. He also corrected the user's spelling of the company name, distinguishing "Thai Visa Centre" from "Thai Visa Center" and advising them to verify that they were contacting the intended business.[^19]

This exchange documents BrandonJT publicly endorsing Thai Visa Centre nearly two months before his May 2025 posts directing users to booking links that the agency had shared with him.[^13][^19]

## March 2025: Describing Thai Visa Centre's Bank Account Services

On March 20, 2025, BrandonJT participated in an ASEAN NOW discussion about obtaining a Non-O marriage visa and opening a Thai bank account. After another Global Moderator suggested contacting Thai Visa Centre, BrandonJT explained that most Bangkok visa agents, including Thai Visa Centre, did not offer standalone bank-account opening services. According to him, these agencies generally assisted with opening accounts only when customers were also obtaining a visa or extension through them.[^23]

The following day, Thai Visa Centre responded in the same discussion through its account identified as a "Forum Sponsor." The agency confirmed that it assisted customers opening bank accounts as part of Non-O retirement, marriage, family, and DTV applications. It also stated that customers receiving banking assistance had to be accompanied by its staff.[^23]

The exchange is noteworthy because BrandonJT provided specific details about Thai Visa Centre's banking arrangements before the agency publicly confirmed them. Although this demonstrates familiarity with its services, the discussion does not establish whether BrandonJT obtained that information directly from the company or through other sources.[^23]

## March 2025: Allegations About Visa Agents and Bank Accounts

On March 26–28, 2025, BrandonJT participated in an ASEAN NOW discussion about retirement visa agents reportedly losing access to certain Bangkok Bank branches. He claimed that some large agencies had been opening dozens of bank accounts for foreign customers each day, attracting government scrutiny and causing banks to stop working with them.[^18]

When another forum member challenged his explanation as speculation, BrandonJT maintained that the largest agencies were the ones affected and suggested they were moving their business to different bank branches. He also accused some affected agents of engaging in questionable practices, although he provided no official banking records or enforcement findings to substantiate those allegations.[^18]

The exchange is relevant to BrandonJT's later discussions of agent-arranged retirement extensions and temporary bank deposits. However, the discussion does not establish that Thai Visa Centre was among the agencies affected.

## May 2025: Direct Contact with Thai Visa Centre

On May 4, 2025, BrandonJT told another ASEAN NOW user that Thai Visa Centre provided paid airport entry services at Suvarnabhumi, Don Mueang, and Phuket airports. He supplied the agency's booking URL at asq.in.th/thailand-fasttrack and explicitly acknowledged that Thai Visa Centre had "previously shared this link with me" for registering customers.[^13]

Three days later, on May 7, BrandonJT shared the same booking URL in another discussion, explaining that users could request a quote for the agency's airport "safe entry" service.[^12]

On May 26, 2025, BrandonJT again recommended Thai Visa Centre in a discussion about a traveler being denied entry to Thailand. He tagged the agency's forum account and claimed it was offering airport "safe entry" services for 2,000 baht, increasing to 4,000 baht for more difficult cases. He directed users to tdac.agents.co.th and explained that the service could be selected as an add-on after completing the initial entry form.[^20]

These exchanges are significant because BrandonJT acknowledged receiving a service-specific booking link from Thai Visa Centre before repeatedly directing forum users toward it. This suggests a more direct line of communication with the business than an ordinary forum member casually recommending a service.

## May 2025: Citing Agency Reports of TDAC Passport Data Mix-Ups

On May 26, 2025, an ASEAN NOW user reported that the official Thai government TDAC website had supplied a downloadable PDF containing another traveler's passport information. Although the user's confirmation email and online records displayed their own correct details, the PDF reportedly exposed the other person's name, passport number, date of birth, and accommodation information.[^27]

BrandonJT responded that he had heard about similar incidents from the agency operating tdac.agents.co.th, claiming that approximately one in every 500 submissions was affected. He reassured the user that their correct email confirmation should remain usable and attributed the problem to Thailand's government IT systems.[^27]

The exchange is noteworthy because BrandonJT cited a specific error rate that he claimed to have learned from the operator of a commercial TDAC service he had repeatedly promoted. However, the discussion provides no independent verification of that figure or explanation of how he obtained it. The original user's reported incident occurred through the official government TDAC website, not necessarily through tdac.agents.co.th.[^27]

## July 2025: Claiming Guaranteed Airport Entry Through Visa Agents

On July 23, 2025, BrandonJT responded to an ASEAN NOW user concerned about being denied entry to Thailand despite paying for an airport "safe entry" service. BrandonJT described these services as "100% reliable," claiming that agents would first obtain copies of every page of the customer's passport and submit them to contacts at the airport for screening and pricing.[^15]

He further claimed that customers would be instructed to send a photograph of their clothing before boarding their flight so that an immigration officer could identify them upon arrival. According to BrandonJT, the officer would personally meet the traveler, escort them to an immigration counter, and open it specifically to process their entry.[^15]

BrandonJT then directed the user to [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md), explaining how to access its paid VIP/Fast Track service through [tdac.agents.co.th](../domains/tdac.agents.co.th.md). Another forum participant questioned the legitimacy of the arrangement, describing BrandonJT's explanation as suspicious.[^15]

The exchange is significant because a Global Moderator of ASEAN NOW was publicly promoting a private visa agency while making extraordinary claims about guaranteed admission and direct cooperation from Thai immigration officers. The discussion provides no independent verification that the described arrangements operated as claimed.

## July–August 2025: Defending Paid TDAC Services While Questioning Government Data Security

On July 9, 2025, BrandonJT participated in an ASEAN NOW discussion promoting AGENTS CO., LTD.'s commercial TDAC submission service. When another user questioned whether the service was legitimate and expressed concerns about personal-data theft, BrandonJT confirmed that the service was genuine and explained that early submissions required a fee. He then claimed that information submitted through Thailand's official government TDAC system had a 50% chance of being stolen by hackers, citing unspecified previous government data breaches.[^28]

On August 9, another forum member questioned why anyone would pay for early submission when the official TDAC could be completed free of charge within three days of arrival. BrandonJT defended the optional $8 fee, arguing that some travelers preferred paying for convenience rather than handling the application shortly before departure. Thai Visa Centre subsequently participated in the discussion through its "Forum Sponsor" account, confirming that the AGENTS service was free within the standard submission window and charged only for early processing.[^28]

These exchanges are noteworthy because Thailand's Immigration Bureau had already encouraged foreign travelers to register exclusively through its official TDAC website to protect their personal information.[^29] BrandonJT offered no supporting evidence for his claimed 50% risk of data theft, while publicly defending a commercial alternative. The official government application remained free, and use of the third-party service was optional.[^28][^29]

## November 2025: Endorsing a Forum Sponsor While Criticism Was Removed

On November 4, 2025, BrandonJT publicly praised Thai Visa Centre in an ASEAN NOW discussion, claiming that virtually everyone he had seen discussing the agency reported positive experiences. In the same discussion, Thai Visa Centre participated under an account explicitly labeled "Forum Sponsor" and advertised its immigration services.[^3]

The thread also contained a moderation notice confirming that a post criticizing the sponsor had been removed. Another warning invoked ASEAN NOW's Rule 37, which restricted negative comments concerning advertisements and sponsors.[^3]

These actions are particularly noteworthy given ASEAN NOW's published forum rules. Rule 24 generally treats commercial promotion by non-sponsors as spam, while Rule 37 prohibits disrespectful, dismissive, or off-topic comments concerning sponsors and sponsored content.[^16]

This creates an apparent imbalance in which favorable recommendations for commercial sponsors can remain visible while criticism may face removal. BrandonJT's public endorsement of Thai Visa Centre is especially relevant given his Global Moderator status, although the available evidence does not establish that he personally removed the disputed post.

On November 7, 2025, BrandonJT participated in another discussion about obtaining a Non-O retirement visa and opening a Thai bank account. He stated that some visa agents could still arrange bank accounts when customers were also applying for visas or extensions, adding that he knew "for a fact" Thai Visa Centre continued offering this service. He also tagged the agency's forum account.[^25]

The statement is noteworthy because BrandonJT expressed certainty about Thai Visa Centre's current services, rather than simply recommending the agency based on online reviews. He did not explain how he obtained that information. Thai Visa Centre had previously confirmed offering similar bank-account assistance in March 2025.[^23][^25]

## November 2025: Referring a Blacklist Inquiry to Airport Safe Entry Services

On November 26, 2025, an ASEAN NOW user asked for a reliable visa agent who could help determine whether a friend might be blacklisted or encounter problems when returning to Thailand. Another participant had raised the possibility that the friend might have an outstanding arrest warrant. BrandonJT responded by linking directly to a discussion about airport "safe entry" services.[^30]

The exchange is another documented instance of BrandonJT directing forum users toward airport entry arrangements, this time in response to concerns about a potentially serious immigration or legal issue. His reply consisted solely of the link; he did not specifically recommend Thai Visa Centre or claim that any agent could verify outstanding arrest warrants.[^30]

## November 2025: Claiming Visa Agent Fees Are Passed to Immigration Offices

On November 8, 2025, BrandonJT explained how some visa agents allegedly obtain retirement extensions for customers who cannot meet Thailand's financial requirements. He claimed that agents accompany customers to a bank, temporarily deposit 800,000 baht into their accounts, obtain the necessary bank letters and updated passbooks, and then withdraw the money.[^24]

BrandonJT went further, claiming that part of the agency's fee is passed to the immigration office so officials overlook the failure to maintain the required bank balance. Another Global Moderator subsequently characterized his explanation as describing how agents "cover" the financial requirements.[^24]

The statement is particularly significant because BrandonJT explicitly alleged payments to immigration offices in exchange for disregarding financial requirements. His post did not identify any specific agency or immigration officer, however, and provided no independent evidence that such payments occurred. Thai Visa Centre was not named in this particular exchange.[^24]

BrandonJT repeated his description of temporary agent-supplied bank deposits in October 2026, but that later post omitted his earlier allegation about payments to immigration officials.[^6][^24]

## April 2026: Referring a Fellow Moderator to Thai Visa Centre's 90-Day Reporting Service

On April 22, 2026, ASEAN NOW Global Moderator DrJack54 asked for an agent who could handle his 90-day immigration report because he was unable to submit it in person or by mail, and online reporting was unavailable following his return from abroad. BrandonJT responded by directing him to Thai Visa Centre's dedicated reporting website, 90day.in.th.[^31]

BrandonJT stated that Thai Visa Centre had launched the service approximately six months earlier, recalled an initial price of 375 baht, and explained that the completed reporting confirmation would be mailed to the customer. He acknowledged that he did not know the current price.[^31]

Thai Visa Centre separately confirmed that its service cost 500 baht for an individual report, with a discounted rate of 375 baht per report when purchasing four or more credits.[^3]

The exchange documents BrandonJT recommending another specific Thai Visa Centre service to a fellow Global Moderator, extending his public endorsements beyond visa extensions and airport entry arrangements.[^31]

## May 2026: Claiming Immigration Officers Can Waive Financial Requirements

On May 17, 2026, BrandonJT defended the use of visa agents to obtain retirement extensions without satisfying the usual financial-documentation requirements. Responding to concerns that such arrangements might circumvent Thai immigration law, he claimed that immigration officers possessed "full discretion and leeway" to determine eligibility and could approve extensions even without the normally required financial proof.[^5]

Another forum participant immediately challenged this interpretation, arguing that the governing Royal Thai Police order did not grant immigration officers unlimited discretion to disregard financial requirements or authorize additional payments in exchange for doing so.[^5]

Thailand's published immigration guidance specifies financial eligibility requirements for retirement extensions, including minimum income or bank deposits and mandatory holding periods for applicants relying on deposited funds.[^14]

The exchange documents a Global Moderator of ASEAN NOW publicly defending an expansive interpretation of immigration officers' authority in the context of commercial visa-agent services. BrandonJT did not identify a specific legal provision supporting such a broad exemption, leaving the accuracy of his claims unresolved.

## June–July 2026: Downplaying an Investigation into Guaranteed Entry Services

On June 23, 2026, Thailand's Immigration Division 2 announced an investigation into brokers allegedly charging foreigners between 4,500 and 12,000 baht for guaranteed entry through Suvarnabhumi Airport. Authorities established a fact-finding committee to examine possible misconduct involving immigration officers and warned that any officials found responsible could face disciplinary and criminal proceedings.[^17]

The following day, BrandonJT responded to an ASEAN NOW discussion of the investigation by predicting that the services would return to normal once public attention moved elsewhere. On June 25, he further mocked the prospect of meaningful enforcement with the sarcastic remark, "We've investigated ourselves and found no wrongdoing."[^7]

On July 19, after other forum members reported that airport "safe entry" services had been suspended or restricted, BrandonJT stated that they remained available at Don Mueang, Chiang Mai, and Phuket airports. He also mentioned an unconfirmed rumor that the service had resumed at Suvarnabhumi.[^7]

These statements are noteworthy given BrandonJT's earlier promotion of paid airport entry services. Rather than expressing concern about the allegations under investigation, he appeared to treat the enforcement efforts as a temporary disruption. The official investigation concerned alleged broker activities and did not publicly identify BrandonJT or Thai Visa Centre as subjects of the inquiry.

## August 2026: Suggesting Visa Extensions Through Other Provinces

In August 2026, an ASEAN NOW member living in Korat explained that they had contacted Thai Visa Centre for assistance with a retirement extension but were told the agency did not handle applications through Korat Immigration.[^21]

BrandonJT responded that the member might have asked the wrong question. He claimed that Thai Visa Centre could arrange extensions anywhere in Thailand, explaining that if the agency did not work with the applicant's local immigration office, the extension would instead be issued through an office in another province.[^21]

The exchange is noteworthy because BrandonJT was explicitly recommending the use of immigration offices outside an applicant's home province through a commercial visa agency. He did not explain how such arrangements would satisfy applicable residence or immigration-office requirements, or whether Thai Visa Centre would actually accept the member's application.[^21]

## October 2026: Describing Temporary Bank Deposits for Retirement Visas

On October 1, 2026, BrandonJT explained how some Thai visa agents arrange retirement extensions for customers who do not have the required 800,000 baht available. According to his description, an agent helps the applicant open a Thai bank account, temporarily deposits 800,000 baht, obtains the necessary bank statements and immigration documentation, and then has the applicant "return the funds" to the agent.[^6]

However, Thailand's published immigration requirements generally mandate maintaining at least 800,000 baht in a Thai bank account for two months before applying for a retirement extension and three months after approval, with a minimum balance of 400,000 baht thereafter when using the bank-deposit qualification method.[^14]

Other forum participants questioned how the temporary deposits described by BrandonJT could satisfy these requirements. BrandonJT did not explain how the arrangement complied with the published rules or identify any applicable legal exemption.[^6]

The discussion documents a Global Moderator of ASEAN NOW presenting an arrangement involving temporary agent-supplied funds as a routine part of obtaining retirement extensions. Although BrandonJT frequently recommends Thai Visa Centre, this particular post did not identify which agents offered the arrangement.

## October 2026: Reporting Further Restrictions on Airport Safe Entry Services

In October 2026, an ASEAN NOW user who had previously been denied entry to Thailand asked whether paid airport "safe entry" services were still operating. The user explained that they had used such a service before and wanted to know which providers remained available.[^22]

BrandonJT responded that, according to his latest information, Krabi was the only location where the service remained available. Another participant subsequently suggested that a different visa agency might have resumed limited operations at Suvarnabhumi Airport.[^22]

BrandonJT's statement contrasts with his July 2026 claims that these services remained available at several major airports. The exchange suggests that availability continued to change following the official investigation, although neither the extent of the restrictions nor their effect on Thai Visa Centre was independently established.[^7][^22]
