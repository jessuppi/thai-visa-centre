# Agents Co., Ltd.

**AGENTS CO., LTD.** (บริษัท เอเจนท์ จำกัด) is a Thai limited company incorporated on **December 12, 2019**, under registration number **0115562031107**. Thai corporate records list the company as active, with registered capital of **10 million baht**.[^1]

Its registered business activities include brokerage and agency services involving visas and work permits for foreign nationals. Its latest filed business description concerns website programming and online hotel reservation services.[^1]

## Connection to Thai Visa Centre

[Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md) has publicly identified AGENTS CO., LTD. as the operator of websites used for its immigration-related services. In a [May 2025 ASEAN NOW discussion](../threads/2025-05-01-asean-now-fake-tdac-emerges.md), the agency's forum account named AGENTS CO., LTD. as the operator of [tdac.in.th](../domains/tdac.in.th.md) and [tdac.agents.co.th](../domains/tdac.agents.co.th.md), and described a paid early-submission TDAC service.[^2]

The agency's own website also describes AGENTS CO., LTD. as its digital operations arm, responsible for TDAC, e-visa, and 90-day reporting platforms.[^7]

In a [May 2026 Reddit discussion](../threads/2026-05-reddit-thailand-90-day-online-reporting.md), Thai Visa Centre acknowledged charging 375–500 baht for assisted immigration reporting through [90day.in.th](../domains/90day.in.th.md).[^6] A THNIC WHOIS record independently identifies AGENTS CO., LTD. as the registered holder of that domain.[^3]

## Associated Domains

A THNIC WHOIS record dated October 2, 2026 identifies AGENTS CO., LTD. as the registered domain-holder organization for [90day.in.th](../domains/90day.in.th.md).[^3]

A THNIC WHOIS record dated October 24, 2026 also identifies AGENTS CO., LTD. as the registered domain-holder organization for [thim.in.th](../domains/thim.in.th.md), which was registered on May 29, 2026.[^5]

The company's website describes 90day.in.th as an immigration reporting service and identifies [asq.in.th](../domains/asq.in.th.md) as its Ask Thailand community platform.[^4]

## Expansion Into Wholesale Telecommunications and Conflicting Corporate Identities

In 2025, AGENTS CO., LTD. expanded into wholesale telecommunications through [esim.agents.co.th](../domains/esim.agents.co.th.md), a platform supplying electronic SIM cards to hotels, travel agencies, tour operators, and other businesses serving international travelers.

The platform advertises partner margins of 60%, automated eSIM issuance within two seconds, and direct relationships with Thai telecommunications operators.[^10][^14]

Although the website displays AGENTS CO., LTD.'s corporate registration number and travel-business licence 11/10602, its Terms of Service and Privacy Policy identify a different company, **eSIM Agents Co., Ltd.**[^14][^15][^16]

The Privacy Policy expressly describes eSIM Agents Co., Ltd. as a registered Thai company and the responsible data controller. However, the reviewed documents provide no separate corporate registration number for that entity.

This discrepancy is particularly consequential because the platform accepts prepaid wholesale customer deposits and collects corporate registration documents and identity documents from authorized business representatives.

The platform also claims compliance with Thai telecommunications regulations, although its published documentation does not clearly identify the licensing arrangements or division of responsibility for individual subscriber verification.

**The conflicting corporate identities extend the legal-accountability concerns documented across AGENTS' immigration platforms into its telecommunications business.**

The available evidence does not establish that the platform operates without required authorization or that eSIM Agents Co., Ltd. is an unregistered company. It does establish uncertainty concerning which legal entity receives customer funds, controls personal information, and assumes contractual and regulatory responsibilities.

## Contradictory Corporate History

Thai Visa Centre's official website describes AGENTS CO., LTD. as its digital subsidiary and claims the company was **originally registered during COVID-19** to develop hotel-booking systems for Thailand's Alternative State Quarantine (ASQ) program.[^8]

However, AGENTS CO., LTD. was incorporated on **December 12, 2019**, before the COVID-19 outbreak became an international public-health emergency in January 2020 and before the pandemic declaration in March 2020.[^1][^9]

The company may subsequently have developed quarantine-booking technology, but Thai Visa Centre's account of why and when it was originally registered conflicts with the corporate record.

### Claiming a Community's Earlier History

AGENTS CO., LTD.'s own website lists **2018** as the beginning of its expatriate-community services and advertises:

- 150,000+ community members
- 50,000+ questions
- 700,000+ responses

The company presents these figures alongside its privately operated [Ask Thailand](../domains/asq.in.th.md) website.[^10]

Yet AGENTS CO., LTD. was not incorporated until December 2019.

Furthermore, on March 17, 2022, Facebook group administrator Tod Daniels publicly announced that [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md) had **acquired the Thai Visa Advice group**, confirming that the community existed under different ownership before that transaction.[^11]

The company does not explain how much of its advertised community history and activity predates its involvement.

**AGENTS presents the history of an established expatriate community as part of its corporate track record without distinguishing activity generated before and after Thai Visa Centre's acquisition.**

### Unsubstantiated Thailand Pass Recognition

AGENTS also claims its quarantine-booking confirmations were officially recognized by Thailand Pass and that its systems enabled expedited approvals through the government program.[^10]

Its website attributes the following volumes to its pandemic-era operations:

| Advertised service | Claimed volume |
|---|---:|
| Quarantine hotel bookings | 200,000+ travelers |
| COVID insurance comparison | 100,000+ travelers |
| Thailand Pass integration | 40,000+ travelers |

These are company-published figures. AGENTS does not provide independently audited transaction records or explain whether individual travelers are counted across multiple services.

Thailand Pass was a government-operated entry-registration system introduced on **November 1, 2021**. The Ministry of Foreign Affairs published procedures for travelers and qualifying accommodation documents.[^12]

However, AGENTS does not provide a government announcement, formal partnership agreement, or other independently verifiable document substantiating its claim of official recognition or preferential processing.

**Acceptance of a hotel reservation during Thailand Pass registration is not, by itself, evidence that the booking platform received official government accreditation or special processing privileges.**

The detailed [AQ.in.th investigation](../domains/aq.in.th.md) further documents the company's historical use of travel-business licence 11/10602 and distinguishes hotel-reservation authentication from government application approval. The same licence now appears on its wholesale eSIM platform, establishing continuity in the company's published commercial credentials across substantially different business activities.

### Relevance to Current Immigration Services

These historical claims now form part of the credibility narrative behind AGENTS CO., LTD.'s commercial TDAC platform, 90-day reporting services, and other immigration-related websites.

The company's promotional material has also been distributed through [The Thaiger](../brands/the-thaiger.md) and [THAI.NEWS](../domains/thai.news.md), extending the reach of its claims about government recognition and large-scale processing experience.[^13]

The documented incorporation-date contradiction, retroactive community milestone, and unsupported official-recognition claim undermine the reliability of the experience credentials used to promote the company's current immigration services.

## Cross-Service Incentives and Commercial Customer Acquisition

AGENTS CO., LTD. operates several interconnected travel and immigration services, using preferential pricing and complimentary benefits to encourage participation in its wider commercial network.

Its published pricing distinguishes between ordinary travelers, existing immigration-service customers, and commercial hospitality partners.[^10][^17]

| Customer category | Advance TDAC submission |
| --- | --- |
| Ordinary travelers | US$8 (270 baht) |
| Existing 90-day reporting customers | Complimentary |
| Participating hotel partners | Complimentary |

The company's corporate website explicitly promotes complimentary advance TDAC submissions as a benefit available to customers using its paid [90-day reporting service](../domains/90day.in.th.md).

### Immigration Services as a Commercial Distribution Channel

AGENTS also promotes travel insurance, electronic SIM cards, airport transportation, and fast-track services through its TDAC platform.[^17]

Its corporate website describes a wholesale eSIM distribution system targeting hotels, travel agencies, tour operators, and online travel platforms, advertising partner margins of 60% and integration through an application programming interface.[^10]

These arrangements establish a commercial model extending beyond fees charged for immigration assistance itself.

Complimentary or discounted immigration services can serve as customer-acquisition incentives for other products and business partnerships.

### Different Personal-Data Processing Arrangements

Thailand's Immigration Bureau permits authorized travel agencies to submit TDAC declarations on behalf of their customers.[^18]

However, the government's published privacy assurances restrict official TDAC information to immigration and border-security purposes.

AGENTS' commercial Privacy Policy permits substantially broader processing, including marketing its own products and services and those of affiliated companies.[^19]

The absence of a direct submission fee therefore does not make the commercial platform equivalent to the government's free registration service in terms of personal-data processing.

### Commercial Transparency

Bundling services and offering complimentary benefits are not inherently unlawful.

However, the company's published arrangements raise questions about referral compensation, marketing permissions, and the extent to which customer information is used across its different commercial platforms.

The available evidence does not establish unauthorized data transfers or undisclosed payments from particular hotel partners.

**It establishes that AGENTS operates a commercial ecosystem in which immigration assistance, hospitality partnerships, telecommunications, and additional travel services are deliberately interconnected.**

### Contractual Liability Restrictions and Consumer Protection

The Terms & Conditions governing AGENTS CO., LTD.'s commercial TDAC submission platform, last updated April 2, 2025, attempt to limit the company's aggregate liability to the greater of one dollar or the amount the customer paid in cash during the month preceding the event giving rise to liability.[^24]

**For travelers using the company's free TDAC submission service without making other qualifying payments, the contractual liability ceiling would therefore be just $1, insofar as legally enforceable.**

The agreement also excludes various categories of consequential damages, including those arising from negligence and breaches of statutory duties.

These restrictions are particularly significant because AGENTS actively encourages travelers, hotels, travel agencies, and tour operators to use its platform to process passport information and immigration declarations, including unlimited group submissions.[^21]

#### Commercial Partnerships Without Corresponding Accountability

The agreement acknowledges that AGENTS may receive affiliate commissions from purchases made through external links.

However, it simultaneously requires customers to waive claims against AGENTS concerning third-party services, including matters involving their privacy practices and data security.[^24]

The company therefore reserves the ability to benefit financially from commercial referrals while attempting to restrict its responsibility for the associated risks.

#### Statutory Compensation Under Thai Law

Thailand's Unfair Contract Terms Act B.E. 2540 (1997), particularly Sections 4 and 8, restricts the enforceability of unreasonable contractual exclusions and limitations of liability.[^25]

Furthermore, Sections 77 and 78 of Thailand's Personal Data Protection Act B.E. 2562 (2019) establish civil compensation provisions for damages resulting from violations of the Act, subject to specified statutory exceptions.

Courts may also award additional punitive damages of up to twice the actual compensation.[^26]

**AGENTS' contractual liability ceiling should not be interpreted as overriding these statutory protections.**

The company's commercial model combines extensive personal-information collection, affiliated-business marketing, and third-party product distribution with contractual provisions attempting to minimize its financial accountability.

This arrangement warrants scrutiny by Thailand's consumer-protection and personal-data protection authorities.

## Insurance Distribution Partnerships and Regulatory Accountability

On September 24, 2025, a sponsored article published by The Thaiger identified several commercial partnerships integrated into AGENTS CO., LTD.'s Thailand Digital Arrival Card submission platform.[^20]

The article identified:

- Tune Protect as the travel insurance provider.
- Bolttech as the technology and insurance distribution partner.
- TrueMove as the telecommunications distribution partner.

These disclosures establish publicly advertised commercial relationships with businesses outside the immediate immigration-services network, although the underlying partnership agreements have not been independently examined.

### Historical Insurance Operations

AGENTS' corporate website identifies a COVID-era insurance comparison platform as one of its earlier commercial operations, claiming to have assisted more than 100,000 travelers.[^10]

Its subsequent integration of insurance purchases into TDAC submissions represents a continuation of this commercial strategy.

Rather than attracting customers exclusively through conventional insurance advertising, the company offers insurance products alongside assistance with mandatory immigration procedures.[^21]

### Insurance Brokerage and Licensing

Thailand regulates insurance agents and brokers through the Office of Insurance Commission (OIC).

Bolttech's Thai customer portal identifies Bolttech Insurance Broker (Thailand) Co., Ltd. and publishes insurance licensing information.[^22]

AGENTS' corporate website, meanwhile, prominently displays travel-business licence 11/10602 but does not identify an insurance brokerage licence.[^10]

The available disclosures do not explain whether AGENTS acts solely as a marketing intermediary, receives referral commissions, collects insurance premiums, or performs other regulated distribution activities.

A travel-business licence does not independently establish authorization to conduct insurance brokerage.

The involvement of a licensed insurance partner may provide an appropriate regulatory arrangement, but the precise allocation of responsibilities remains unverified.

### Upcoming Insurance Advertising Requirements

On September 15, 2026, Thailand's OIC issued amendments to its insurance sales and advertising framework, scheduled to take effect on January 1, 2027.

According to a September 24 legal analysis by Tilleke & Gibbins, the amendments strengthen requirements concerning third-party advertising, seller identification, promotional disclosures, and insurer oversight.[^23]

These developments are relevant to commercial insurance promotions integrated into immigration-assistance websites.

### Commercial Use of Immigration Information

The sponsored article also describes telecommunications services linked to travelers' verified TDAC profiles.[^20]

This establishes an advertised integration between immigration information and additional commercial services.

AGENTS should clarify which information is transferred to its commercial partners, how customers authorize those transfers, which company receives insurance payments, and whether referral compensation is involved.

The available evidence does not establish unlicensed insurance brokerage, unauthorized information sharing, or regulatory misconduct by any of the named partners.

Nevertheless, the documented partnerships demonstrate that AGENTS' immigration platforms also function as distribution channels for regulated commercial products.

## International Travel Industry Promotion and Government Platform Confusion

### June 2025: Promotion Through Travel Daily Media

On June 17, 2025, international travel-industry publication Travel Daily Media published an article promoting AGENTS CO., LTD.'s commercial TDAC submission platform under the byline of Marga Manlapig.[^27]

The article advertised discounted advance-submission fees ranging from US$0.50 to US$8 per traveler, depending on group size.

It also identified AGENTS' privately operated websites as official TDAC resources and repeated substantially identical performance and regulatory compliance claims appearing in The Thaiger's June 13, 2025 press release.[^13]

This provides further evidence of the distribution of AGENTS' promotional material beyond Thailand's expatriate media network.

### 2025: Rainbow Tours Customer Instructions

Polish tour operator **Rainbow Tours S.A.** published a detailed Polish-language TDAC submission guide on its corporate website, `r.pl`.[^28]

The document instructs customers to visit Thailand's official government TDAC website, `tdac.immigration.go.th`.

However, the accompanying screenshots illustrate AGENTS CO., LTD.'s privately operated submission platform, including:

- Its Polish-language homepage, displaying a June 23, 2025 update date.
- Its commercial contact and passport information form.
- Its per-traveler billing interface.
- Its group document-download functionality.

The guide also incorrectly states that completing the arrival card more than three days before the tour begins will incur a fee.

Thailand's government system does not offer a paid advance-submission option. Such fees are associated with private intermediaries rather than the official registration procedure.[^18]

**Rainbow's instructions therefore combined the official government website address with the application workflow and pricing conditions of a private commercial intermediary.**

### Subsequent Correction

Rainbow's revised Thailand entry guidance, displaying a March 11, 2026 update date, directs customers to the official government portal and correctly identifies TDAC registration as free.[^29]

Its linked replacement instruction document also uses screenshots of the genuine government application instead of AGENTS' commercial interface.[^30]

However, the replacement PDF retains the earlier warning concerning fees for premature applications.

The historical AGENTS-based instruction document also remained publicly accessible on Rainbow's website when examined on November 26, 2026.

### Commercial Significance

The Rainbow documentation demonstrates that confusion between AGENTS' commercial platform and Thailand's official immigration service extended into customer instructions published by an established international tour operator.

The documents do not establish a contractual partnership, referral compensation, or the number of Rainbow customers who submitted personal information through AGENTS.

Nevertheless, they provide concrete third-party evidence of the commercial platform being incorporated into international travel guidance without a clear distinction between government registration and private immigration assistance.

## June–July 2025 Press Release Syndication Campaign

AGENTS CO., LTD.'s promotion of its commercial TDAC platform extended beyond The Thaiger, ASEAN NOW, and Travel Daily Media into international press-release distribution networks and Thailand's national media.

### June 16, 2025: International Press Release Distribution

Web and IT News and AWNews published substantially identical promotional articles announcing AGENTS' commercial TDAC platform.[^31][^32]

Both publications attribute the material to PressReleaseCC and identify the following media contact:

- Company: AGENTS CO
- Contact person: Thomas
- Country: Thailand
- Website: `tdac.agents.co.th`

The articles repeat substantially identical wording, pricing, performance statistics, and commercial advantages appearing in The Thaiger's June 13, 2025 publication.[^13]

The syndicated copies provide additional documentary evidence that the promotional material originated from a coordinated corporate publicity campaign rather than separate independent evaluations of the platform.

### July 6, 2025: Promotion Through The Nation

The Nation subsequently published an article titled *AGENTS unveils new TDAC platform, offering flexible and efficient processing* within its PR News section.[^33]

The publication repeated several of the company's earlier promotional claims, including:

- A reported success rate exceeding 99% during the May 7 government website outage.
- Compliance with Thailand's Personal Data Protection Act.
- Indefinitely retained application drafts unless deleted.
- The supposed advantages of individual TDAC documents for LTR visa applicants.
- Thai domain extensions as indicators of legitimate providers.

The article also described AGENTS' private websites using official TDAC terminology while separately identifying the government's actual registration portal.

### Commercial Significance

The repeated publication of substantially similar corporate promotional material across multiple media outlets should not be interpreted as independent corroboration of AGENTS' performance statistics, regulatory compliance, or reliability claims.

The distribution campaign demonstrates that the company's publicity strategy extended beyond its established relationships with Thailand's expatriate publishing network.

The reviewed publications do not disclose the financial arrangements governing placement of the promotional material.
