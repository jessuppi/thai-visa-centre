# Issa Systems Co., Ltd.

**Issa Systems Co., Ltd.** (บริษัท อิสสะ ซิสเต็มส์ จำกัด) is a Thai company incorporated on **December 2, 2022**, under registration number **0105565194414**.

Its registered capital increased from **1 million baht to 4 million baht on May 21, 2026**. The company remains active.[^1]

## Conflicting Business Classifications

Thai corporate records identify immigration consulting among the company's registered activities.

However, its latest financial-statement business description concerns **training employees for restaurants and other service businesses**, classified under conference organization rather than immigration consulting.[^1]

The discrepancy raises questions about how the company's reported activities correspond to its commercial visa services.

## Issa Compass Operations

Issa Systems is one of two companies publicly identified as operating **[Issa Compass](../brands/issa-compass.md)**, alongside Singapore-registered [Issara Platforms Pte. Ltd.](issara-platforms-pte-ltd.md).[^2]

Issa Compass advertises visa applications, document preparation, immigration consultations, and related services across Southeast Asia.

## Customer Data and Singapore Operations

Issa Compass's Privacy Policy identifies **Issara Platforms Pte. Ltd. as the controller of customers' digital visa files**, while Issa Systems handles in-person services and physical documents in Thailand.[^3]

The policy states that customer data is stored primarily in Singapore and accessed from both Singapore and Thailand. Collected information includes passport copies, financial documents, and potentially sensitive health or criminal records.[^3]

This arrangement places responsibility for digital immigration records with the Singapore company rather than the Thai operating entity.

## Cross-Border Data Transfers and Unclear Processing Responsibilities

Issa Compass's Privacy Policy, effective August 23, 2026, expressly distinguishes between the operational responsibilities of its Thai and Singaporean companies.[^3]

Issara Platforms Pte. Ltd. controls digital visa files, while Issa Systems Co., Ltd. conducts in-person consultations, maintains physical documentation, and accesses the Singapore company's digital infrastructure.

However, the policy does not expressly classify Issa Systems as a separate data controller, joint controller, or processor acting exclusively on behalf of the Singapore company.

This distinction matters because the Thai company directly interacts with immigration customers and handles original documentation.

### International Data Transfer Requirements

Sections 28 and 29 of Thailand's Personal Data Protection Act establish requirements governing international transfers of personal information.

Additional regulations concerning international transfers became effective on March 24, 2024, providing mechanisms for lawful transfers through appropriate safeguards, recognized protections, and applicable statutory exceptions.[^4]

Singapore imposes corresponding obligations under Section 26 of its Personal Data Protection Act and Regulations 10–12 of its Personal Data Protection Regulations 2021.[^5][^6]

These provisions generally require organizations transferring personal information overseas to ensure comparable protection through legally recognized mechanisms.

Issa Compass states that it uses appropriate contractual safeguards for international processing. However, it does not identify the particular transfer mechanisms or describe how responsibilities are divided between its two operating companies.

**Storing customer records primarily in Singapore does not automatically remove the Thai company's responsibilities under Thailand's data-protection legislation.**

### Information Used Beyond the Original Visa Application

Section 6.3 of Issa Compass's Terms of Service permits customer information to be used for additional immigration compliance procedures beyond the original visa application.[^7]

However, the provision does not enumerate those additional procedures or explain whether they require separate customer authorization.

This is significant because the platform collects passport copies, financial evidence, employment documentation, and potentially sensitive medical or criminal-history records.

Further immigration assistance may represent a legitimate continuation of an existing customer relationship. Nevertheless, a general contractual authorization does not independently establish an appropriate legal basis for every subsequent use of sensitive information.

### Responsibility for Physical and Digital Records

The company's Privacy Policy provides an account-deletion mechanism and identifies exceptions for records retained for legal and financial purposes.

However, it does not separately explain how physical records maintained by Issa Systems are destroyed, returned to customers, or reconciled with corresponding digital records held in Singapore.

The available documentation therefore leaves several operational questions unresolved:

- Which company determines the purposes of processing physical immigration documents?
- Which company authorizes access by Thai employees and external contractors?
- What safeguards govern transfers between the two companies?
- Does account deletion also initiate the destruction or return of physical documentation held in Thailand?

The published policies do not establish unlawful international transfers, unauthorized secondary processing, or inadequate internal safeguards.

**They establish a cross-border immigration-data processing arrangement whose division of legal and operational responsibilities is not fully explained in the customer-facing documents.**

## Singapore Jurisdiction and Cross-Border Consumer Accountability

Although Issa Systems Co., Ltd. provides in-person immigration assistance in Thailand, Issa Compass's Terms of Service identify Singapore-registered Issara Platforms Pte. Ltd. as the contractual service provider.[^7]

Sections 16–18 establish the following dispute-resolution arrangements:

- Singapore law governs the agreement.
- Singapore courts have exclusive contractual jurisdiction.
- Customers must first attempt informal dispute resolution, with the company undertaking to attempt resolution within 30 days.
- Formal contractual notices are directed to the Singapore office.

The Privacy Policy separately identifies Issa Systems as the company handling physical documentation and in-person customer interactions in Thailand.[^3]

### Practical Implications for Customers

This corporate arrangement raises questions about responsibility when disputes arise from activities performed at the company's Bangkok office.

For example, customers may experience disputes concerning physical documents, staff instructions, application preparation, or payments associated with locally delivered services.

The published documents do not clearly distinguish which obligations Issa Systems assumes independently and which it performs on behalf of Issara Platforms.

**Customers receiving immigration assistance in Thailand are nevertheless presented with contractual terms directing disputes to Singapore.**

### Singapore's Small Claims Tribunals

Singapore's judiciary provides a relatively accessible mechanism for resolving qualifying consumer and service-contract disputes through its Small Claims Tribunals.[^8]

The published eligibility requirements include:

| Requirement | Condition |
| --- | --- |
| Ordinary claim limit | S$20,000 |
| Extended limit with mutual consent | S$30,000 |
| Filing deadline | Two years from the relevant cause of action |
| Respondent | Must be served in Singapore |

Identifying the correct contracting entity is therefore particularly important.

A qualifying claim against the Singapore company may fall within the tribunal's jurisdiction, whereas a claim solely against the Thai company could encounter jurisdictional restrictions.

### Mandatory Consumer Protections

Section 35 of Singapore's Consumer Protection (Fair Trading) Act prevents businesses from contractually removing protections conferred by that legislation, subject to its applicable provisions and exceptions.[^9]

The company's choice of Singapore law does not independently establish that every contractual restriction is enforceable or that mandatory Thai consumer protections cease to apply to locally delivered services.

### Unresolved Corporate Responsibilities

Issa Compass should clarify which company receives customer payments, employs or contracts the personnel delivering immigration assistance, assumes responsibility for physical documents, and administers refunds.

The available evidence does not establish that the company has improperly prevented customers from pursuing legal remedies.

Nevertheless, its cross-border corporate structure makes clear identification of contractual responsibilities particularly important for customers seeking financial redress.
