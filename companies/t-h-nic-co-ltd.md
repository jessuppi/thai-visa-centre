# T.H.NIC Co., Ltd.

**T.H.NIC Co., Ltd.** is Thailand's sole registrar for `.th` and `.ไทย` domain names, appointed by the [THNIC Foundation](../brands/thnic-foundation.md) on April 1, 2008. Operating under the [THNIC](../brands/thnic.md) name, the company administers domain registrations and is responsible for applying the Foundation's registration policies, including provisions governing domain abuse, bad-faith use, and suspension or revocation.[^1][^2]

Beginning February 27, 2025, T.H.NIC Co., Ltd. received repeated domain abuse complaints from Jesse Nickles concerning websites associated with [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md) and [Agents Co., Ltd.](agents-co-ltd.md). The complaints involved alleged government impersonation, misleading domain registrations, unsolicited commercial advertising, and disputed publications about Nickles. Although THNIC representatives acknowledged several complaints and promised reviews, the available correspondence left substantial questions unanswered concerning the registrar's investigations, enforcement decisions, and application of its published policies.[^3]

On November 12, 2026, Nickles submitted four additional formal complaints requesting policy reviews concerning [tdac.in.th](../domains/tdac.in.th.md), [agents.co.th](../domains/agents.co.th.md) and its TDAC subdomain, [90day.in.th](../domains/90day.in.th.md), and seven immigration-related domain registrations. He separately requested that the [THNIC Foundation](../brands/thnic-foundation.md) independently examine the registrar's handling of complaints dating back to February 2025. These submissions sought documented policy determinations, clarification of enforcement powers, and accountability for previously unresolved cases.[^4]

## Registrar Discretion and the Limits of Court-Order Requirements

The THNIC Foundation formally designated two separate commercial entities on April 1, 2008:

- Thai Name Server as the registry operator.
- T.H.NIC Co., Ltd. as the registrar.[^1]

The Foundation's Domain Name Registration Policy, revised in March 2024 and effective July 1, 2024, distinguishes the registrar's administrative and enforcement responsibilities from the registry operator's technical responsibilities.[^2]

### Interim Suspension During Unresolved Disputes

Section 15.2 provides for withholding action pending a final judicial determination or another dispute-resolution process.

However, Section 15.3 expressly permits interim suspension of domain services while disputes remain unresolved.

Sections 12.4 and 12.5 separately provide discretionary enforcement and investigative powers concerning domain registration and subsequent use.

**The policy distinguishes resolving the underlying dispute from temporarily restricting domain services while that dispute remains unresolved.**

This distinction is relevant to THNIC's November 2026 response concerning `tvc.co.th`, in which the registrar maintained that suspension required an explicit court or competent government order.[^3]

### Limitations on Registrar Responsibility

Section 22.2 limits the registrar's responsibility for verifying domain usage and involvement in related legal proceedings.

These provisions should be considered alongside its discretionary enforcement powers rather than omitted from the analysis.

The policy does not establish a universal obligation to investigate every complaint or suspend every disputed domain.

Nevertheless, the existence of discretionary suspension powers warrants an explanation of why those powers were considered inapplicable to the reported circumstances.

### Policy Enforcement Versus Judicial Adjudication

Determining whether disputed publications constitute criminal defamation is different from assessing whether a registered domain is being used consistently with its registration conditions.

Similarly, referring allegations of phishing or other information-security threats to police does not resolve whether the registrar considered its separate enforcement authority under Section 17.

The available correspondence does not establish that THNIC was legally required to suspend any particular domain.

However, it leaves unresolved how the registrar reconciled its stated court-order requirement with the discretionary powers contained in its own registration policy.

The Foundation's English translation expressly identifies the Thai-language version as legally authoritative. Any definitive legal interpretation must therefore account for the original Thai text.
