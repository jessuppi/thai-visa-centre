# T.H.NIC Co., Ltd.

**T.H.NIC Co., Ltd.** is Thailand's sole registrar for `.th` and `.ไทย` domain names, appointed by the [THNIC Foundation](../brands/thnic-foundation.md) on April 1, 2008. Operating under the [THNIC](../brands/thnic.md) name, the company administers domain registrations and is responsible for applying the Foundation's registration policies, including provisions governing domain abuse, bad-faith use, and suspension or revocation.[^1][^2]

Beginning February 27, 2025, T.H.NIC Co., Ltd. received repeated domain abuse complaints from Jesse Nickles concerning websites associated with [Thai Visa Centre](../brands/thai-visa-centre.md) and [Agents Co., Ltd.](agents-co-ltd.md). The complaints involved alleged government impersonation, misleading domain registrations, unsolicited commercial advertising, and disputed publications about Nickles. Although THNIC representatives acknowledged several complaints and promised reviews, the available correspondence left substantial questions unanswered concerning the registrar's investigations, enforcement decisions, and application of its published policies.[^3]

On November 12, 2026, Nickles submitted four additional formal complaints requesting policy reviews concerning [tdac.in.th](../domains/tdac.in.th.md), [agents.co.th](../domains/agents.co.th.md) and its TDAC subdomain, [90day.in.th](../domains/90day.in.th.md), and seven immigration-related domain registrations. He separately requested that the [THNIC Foundation](../brands/thnic-foundation.md) independently examine the registrar's handling of complaints dating back to February 2025. These submissions sought documented policy determinations, clarification of enforcement powers, and accountability for previously unresolved cases.[^4]

## Registrar Discretion and the Limits of Court-Order Requirements

The THNIC Foundation formally designated two separate commercial entities on April 1, 2008:

- Thai Name Server as the registry operator.
- T.H.NIC Co., Ltd. as the registrar.[^1]

The Foundation's Domain Name Registration Policy, revised in March 2024 and effective July 1, 2024, distinguishes the registrar's administrative and enforcement responsibilities from the registry operator's technical responsibilities.[^2]

### Interim Suspension During Unresolved Disputes

Section 15.2 provides for withholding action pending a final judicial determination or another dispute-resolution process.

However, Section 15.3 expressly permits interim suspension of domain services while disputes remain unresolved.

Sections 12.4 and 12.5 separately provide discretionary enforcement and investigative powers concerning domain registration and subsequent use.

**The policy distinguishes resolving the underlying dispute from temporarily restricting domain services while that dispute remains unresolved.**

This distinction is relevant to THNIC's November 2026 response concerning `tvc.co.th`, in which the registrar maintained that suspension required an explicit court or competent government order.[^3]

### Limitations on Registrar Responsibility

Section 22.2 limits the registrar's responsibility for verifying domain usage and involvement in related legal proceedings.

These provisions should be considered alongside its discretionary enforcement powers rather than omitted from the analysis.

The policy does not establish a universal obligation to investigate every complaint or suspend every disputed domain.

Nevertheless, the existence of discretionary suspension powers warrants an explanation of why those powers were considered inapplicable to the reported circumstances.

### Policy Enforcement Versus Judicial Adjudication

Determining whether disputed publications constitute criminal defamation is different from assessing whether a registered domain is being used consistently with its registration conditions.

Similarly, referring allegations of phishing or other information-security threats to police does not resolve whether the registrar considered its separate enforcement authority under Section 17.

The available correspondence does not establish that THNIC was legally required to suspend any particular domain.

However, it leaves unresolved how the registrar reconciled its stated court-order requirement with the discretionary powers contained in its own registration policy.

The Foundation's English translation expressly identifies the Thai-language version as legally authoritative. Any definitive legal interpretation must therefore account for the original Thai text.

For the detailed complaint chronology, see the [THNIC investigation](../brands/thnic.md).

## Operational Guidelines for Misleading Domains and Registrant Verification

T.H.NIC Co., Ltd. publishes separate Domain Name Registration Guidelines describing how its personnel implement the THNIC Foundation's registration policies.[^5]

These guidelines establish additional administrative procedures relevant to complaints concerning immigration-related websites.

### Misleading Domain Names as Grounds for Rejection

Section 2.5 expressly identifies domain names that mislead or confuse the public as grounds for rejecting or cancelling domain-related applications.

The Foundation's underlying Domain Name Registration Policy also establishes relevant naming restrictions:

- Section 7.9 addresses similarities to existing domains that could mislead the public concerning their respective holders.
- Section 7.11 addresses names resembling well-known names or trademarks in ways that could misrepresent the origin or ownership of services.
- Sections 7.14–7.15 establish the registrar's authority to examine registration rights, reject applications, and withdraw registered domains.[^2]

These provisions are directly relevant to the registration of domains resembling the names of Thai government immigration programs.

The general availability of .in.th registrations does not exempt applicants from the remaining naming restrictions.

### Seven-Day Registrant Information Requirements

Section 2.6 of the registrar's operational guidelines permits T.H.NIC to request updated information or verify existing information concerning domain registration and usage.[^5]

Following such a request, the domain holder or relevant administrator must supply the requested information or update the registration system within seven days of receiving notification.

The registrar also reserves the right to cancel registrations subsequently found to contain information inconsistent with its policies.

Section 2.2 separately identifies failure to respond within a prescribed period, or inability to contact responsible parties, as circumstances potentially resulting in domain suspension or deletion.

**The registrar therefore possesses a documented administrative mechanism for verifying domain registration information without first obtaining a judicial determination.**

### Relevance to the November 2026 Complaints

On October 29, 2026, THNIC confirmed that it had contacted the registered holder of `tvc.co.th` concerning disputed website content.[^3]

However, the available correspondence does not establish whether this communication included a formal registration-information request under Section 2.6.

The subsequent November 12 complaints also explicitly requested verification of the responsible domain holders and examination of potentially related registrations.[^4]

T.H.NIC should clarify whether it exercised its information-verification powers, whether any formal response deadlines were imposed, and whether the resulting information established compliance with its naming and registration requirements.

The seven-day deadline applies to registrants responding to qualifying registrar requests, not to the registrar's obligation to answer abuse complaints.

Nevertheless, these operational guidelines identify specific administrative procedures through which the registrar could examine reported registration irregularities independently of disputes concerning website content.

## Reserved Domain Safeguards and Preventive Registration Controls

Section 4.4 of T.H.NIC Co., Ltd.'s Domain Name Registration Guidelines, revision 3.2, dated May 21, 2025, establishes a mechanism for maintaining a reserved domain-name list.[^5]

The registrar's registration system blocks applications for reserved names. The registrar may also add names to this list following consideration of registration applications.

### Relevance to Government Immigration Programs

This mechanism provides a potential preventive safeguard against confusing registrations associated with newly announced government immigration programs.

The complaints concerning eta.in.th, dtac.in.th, dtv.in.th and other immigration-related domains raise questions about whether the registrar considered reserving sensitive government program names before permitting private registrations.

The registrar should clarify whether it consults relevant government agencies when establishing or updating its reserved-name list.

### Preventive Measures Versus Retrospective Enforcement

**THNIC possesses mechanisms for preventing certain domain registrations before disputes arise, rather than relying exclusively on subsequent complaints and judicial proceedings.**

The available evidence does not establish that any of the reported immigration-related domains were previously reserved or that their registration violated Section 4.4.

Nevertheless, preventive reservation deserves consideration as a means of reducing future confusion between government immigration services and privately operated commercial websites.
